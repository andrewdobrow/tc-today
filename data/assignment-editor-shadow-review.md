The tornado became a waterspout at one point.

Will Ulrich, a meteorologist with NWS Melbourne, said the event was unique because it was on the ground for 10 to 15 minutes, which is not typical during summer months. The tornado was generated by a typical summer afternoon thunderstorm and had winds of 60 to 70 mph. Ulrich said the event serves as a reminder that Mother Nature will always throw curveballs regardless of the time of year.

The National Weather Service issues tornado warnings once it is able to detect a tornado. The agency recommends having multiple ways to receive warnings to keep residents and their loved ones safe.
