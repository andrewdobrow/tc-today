Fort Pierce's city commission is spending $31,000 in taxpayer money to hire an outside search firm after nearly the city's entire legal team resigned, leaving Fort Pierce with no attorneys. The mass departure followed the commission's decision not to renew the current city attorney's contract, which expires in September. City staff confirmed that aside from one paralegal, the entire legal department has left.

City Commissioner Michael Broderick called the situation a self-inflicted wound during a heated special meeting Monday. Broderick said he is not aware of any complaints, disciplinary action or poor performance reviews that would lead to the city attorney's departure. The resignations included at least three city attorneys. Commissioner Chris Dzadovsky suggested the resignations were orchestrated, saying members of the city attorney's office chose not to renew contracts and resign, though he did not say who he believed orchestrated the mass resignation.

Dzadovsky disagreed, arguing the absence of legal staff would actually slow development down because it will take longer to have a city attorney legal review. Taxpayer Kevin Keene criticized the $31,000 expense during public comment, saying the city had advance notice the position would become vacant and nevertheless failed to orderly execute a succession process.

City Human Resources Manager Jared Sorenson defended hiring an outside firm, saying the independence is what the city was looking for. The commission approved the $31,000 contract and appointed Deputy City Manager Camille Wallace as interim city attorney while the permanent search is conducted. The city has not yet answered why the city attorney's contract was not renewed.
