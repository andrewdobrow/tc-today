Martin County authorities confirmed that a body found in the mangroves near the House of Refuge on Hutchinson Island is that of Michael Anthony Debevec, who was reported missing last week after visiting Chastain Beach. Investigators with the Martin County Sheriff's Office found Debevec's body Tuesday after an extensive search of the area. Detectives reported they have not found any apparent signs of foul play. The Medical Examiner has not yet determined Debevec's cause of death.

 The search began after a Good Samaritan found a wallet Saturday belonging to Debevec and went to Debevec's home in an attempt to return it, prompting his family to report him missing. Investigators later found Debevec's vehicle at Chastain Beach, more than two miles from where his body was ultimately discovered.

 Sheriff John Budensiek said investigators determined Debevec traveled from the Port St. Lucie area to Martin County at about 3 a.m. August 27 and parked at Chastain Beach. Phone records showed Debevec's phone was in use Friday morning and moving between Chastain Beach and the House of Refuge, according to investigators.

 On Tuesday morning, investigators found a backpack belonging to Debevec hanging high in a tree with his computer inside. A deeper search into the mangroves led investigators to the body. The Martin County Sheriff's Office thanked the community members who shared information about Debevec and helped with the search. Debevec's family has been notified of his death.

### Variant B

**Hero:** St. Lucie County votes to remove license plate readers from county roads over privacy concerns
**Hero source choice:** Source #1: St. Lucie County votes to remove license plate readers over privacy concerns - WPBF
**Urgency:** 8

St. Lucie County commissioners voted Tuesday to remove automated license plate readers, including Flock cameras, from county-owned roads and property, while also halting the issuance of new permits and potentially revoking existing ones. The vote follows earlier restrictions imposed by the St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office limiting license plate reader use to forcible felony investigations and missing person cases.

 The motion, introduced by Commissioner James Clasby, passed 3-2. Commissioner Erin Lowry said during the meeting, "I feel like everywhere you turn now in our community is a Flock camera." Clasby emphasized the need to protect residents' privacy, saying, "And I believe that we need to protect our citizens and order the removal from our right-of-way. It's actually mind-boggling. It's almost impossible to go anywhere without being tagged by at least one."

 Commissioner Cathy Townsend and another member voted against the measure, expressing a desire for input from local law enforcement before making a decision. Commissioner Jamie Fowler questioned the handling of data collected by the cameras during the discussion. In July, Paris Lewbel, a spokesperson for Flock Safety, explained that data collected by the cameras is controlled by the agency using them and is deleted after 30 days by default. Every search within the Flock platform is recorded in an immutable audit log, which tracks who conducted the search, the reason, and the parameters.

 St. Lucie County Sheriff Richard Del Toro released a statement saying his office would comply with the state order to remove cameras from state highways and tighten internal rules but did not call for their removal from county roads. Port St. Lucie Police Chief John Bolduc also announced an operational pause on automated license plate readers. The decision by the county commissioners goes beyond the Florida Department of Transportation's directive to remove cameras from state highways, extending the removal to county-controlled public rights of way.
