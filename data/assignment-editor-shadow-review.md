**Hero source choice:** Source #2: National Weather Service confirms EF0 tornado touchdown in Port St. Lucie - WPTV
**Urgency:** 8

The National Weather Service confirmed Monday that an EF0 tornado touched down Sunday evening in St. Lucie County, tracking 2.1 miles and damaging 20 to 30 homes with winds of 65 to 75 mph. Will Ulrich, warning coordination meteorologist with the National Weather Service Melbourne office, announced the findings during a press conference at Jessica Clinton Park following a damage survey of the affected areas. No injuries were reported from the tornado.

The tornado, classified as a landspout, first touched down around 6:10 p.m. near Southeast Kitching Cove Lane and Southeast Morningside Boulevard, close to the Tarpon Bay Yacht Club. The tornado produced minimal structural damage, including roof damage, soffit damage and pool enclosures lifted from their foundations. It then moved over the St. Lucie River, where it became a waterspout for approximately five to seven minutes, meandering slowly from north to south over the water before moving back onshore in the Bay St. Lucie community around 6:20 p.m.

The strongest wind gusts occurred in the Bay St. Lucie community, Ulrich said, where the tornado caused numerous downed trees and large branches, along with damage to well-built homes with tile roofs. The tornado continued west into Port St. Lucie communities near the Southbend area, where it caused discontinuous damage including downed trees, blown-over fences, and damaged or destroyed pool screen enclosures before dissipating. The entire event lasted approximately 14 to 15 minutes. Approximately 20 to 30 percent of roof covers were removed from some homes, though no homes were destroyed.

Ulrich explained that landspouts form differently than traditional tornadoes, developing closer to the surface along the leading edge of thunderstorm updrafts rather than from mesocyclones. Residents reported it was not raining during the tornado itself, with heavy rain arriving only after the tornado passed through. Ulrich said the tornado resulted from boundary interactions from daily afternoon showers and thunderstorms, noting that abnormally warm conditions create more vigorous boundary collisions. The National Weather Service worked alongside the City of Port St. Lucie Emergency Management and St. Lucie County Emergency Management to conduct interviews with residents and piece together the timeline of the event.
