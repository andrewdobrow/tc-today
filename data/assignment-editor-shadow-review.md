The data center issue will return to the council for further discussion.

Detectives searched Ruma's home and seized two phones, and a forensic review of the devices produced evidence that advanced the investigation.

 Ruma is employed as a Lyft driver and Uber Eats driver. Currey stressed that investigators have not alleged criminal conduct occurred while Ruma was working for either service, but released the information as they seek potential victims or people with concerning interactions involving him. Police also said the investigation uncovered complaints and law enforcement contacts involving unwanted communications and similar behavior dating back several years.

 Anyone who believes they may have been victimized, received altered or sexually explicit images from Ruma, or has information for detectives is asked to contact Vero Beach police Detective Greg Matakaitis at 772-978-4665.

### Variant A

**Hero:** DeSantis pledges to campaign for Amendment 3 as local leaders warn of service cuts and fee hikes
**Hero source choice:** Source #1: DeSantis says he'll help sell Amendment 3 as local opposition grows
**Urgency:** 8

Governor Ron DeSantis said Monday he will help persuade Floridians to approve Amendment 3, a property-tax proposal that would increase the homestead exemption for non-school property taxes to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, with inflation adjustments afterward. Under the amendment's ballot language, the expanded exemption would not apply to school district taxes.

 The proposal would also lower the annual cap on assessment increases for non-homestead properties, including businesses and rental properties, from 10 percent to 5 percent, and restrict how counties and municipalities use property-tax revenue. DeSantis said he has not settled on how he will participate and would not lead a political committee's statewide fundraising effort, but added he would be involved because he thinks the amendment is important.

 The governor rejected warnings from local leaders that the amendment would leave communities unable to provide basic services. DeSantis said he would support state funding to help rural counties with limited tax bases absorb revenue losses, though any state assistance would depend on lawmakers approving the funding. Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine Cava launched a South Florida campaign Wednesday to defeat Amendment 3, joined by local leaders, labor organizations and business and civic groups. She warned that communities could face service cuts, higher fees or some combination of both, and said Miami-Dade could lose about 10 percent of its general fund revenue in the first year and 20 percent in the second.

 Levine Cava said renters and businesses could shoulder additional costs without receiving the expanded homestead exemption. Supporters have potentially encouraging polling numbers. A Sachs Media survey conducted August 18 randomly assigned 800 Florida voters to groups reviewing either the original ballot language or a court-ordered revision.

 Support was 65 percent among those who read the revised wording, compared with 60 percent among those who saw the original. The difference was not statistically significant.

 The Florida Policy Institute estimates that public hospital districts and health systems would lose approximately $323 million over three years under Amendment 3. The institute warns that reduced funding could affect staffing, access to care and programs serving uninsured and underinsured patients. Amendment 3 requires approval from at least 60 percent of voters casting ballots on the measure in November. If approved, it would take effect in January.

Source #2: Thieves stealing SNAP benefits from Florida families are using the tax dollars to buy baby formula

More than a dozen Florida families have reported stolen SNAP benefits connected to a single address in Glendale, California.  Victims described the financial toll of the theft. Shayna Houser Parra told the I-Team that she and her family still have not had all of their bills covered because of the theft. Cheyanne Smith said she has gone through three cards and that locks, canceling the card and changing her PIN have not worked.

 In July, one of the Florida victims posted the address the SNAP fraud is tied to in a Facebook group for DoorDash drivers in California. Someone responded, writing that they had delivered formula there like two or three times and each time it was like five to seven cans.

 The Glendale Police Department said it has received more calls from Florida law enforcement agencies since the I-Team's previous report, as its investigation continues. The Department of Children and Families has given no indication it is doing anything to investigate the fraud, instead directing people to contact law enforcement and increase the security on their accounts.

 Records show the U.S. Government Accountability Office also wanted to know what is being done to prevent SNAP benefit theft. However, there is no requirement for state agencies to report data on stolen benefits to the federal government. That authority ended in 2024, along with the Stolen SNAP Benefits Program that reimbursed victims of fraud, meaning the benefits stolen from families will not be returned.
