Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at Fort Pierce aquarium offers close encounters with Florida coastal life

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium invites visitors to explore Florida's underwater world at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce's waterfront district. Manager Bill Hoffman explains that the facility's approach to displaying live, complex ecosystem models has been a cornerstone since 1980 under Smithsonian direction, distinguishing it from typical public aquariums. "We feel like we're the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast, and we don't want to be," Hoffman told WPTV. He points out that the aquarium houses more species than many larger facilities across the country.

The centerpiece is the Atlantic coral reef exhibit, a large tank that recreates one of Florida's most important coastal ecosystems. Beyond the reef display, visitors encounter live representations of seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities. These exhibits demonstrate the complex connections between different organisms in each habitat. Even smaller creatures get attention here, including a barracuda that measures just four inches, illustrating how stable ecosystems depend on countless small organisms throughout the food chain.

The touch tank ranks among the most popular attractions at the facility. Exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran notes that this hands-on experience allows guests to handle marine species they've likely never encountered before, all under the guidance of trained staff members. The aquarium is planning special programming around its 25th anniversary celebration scheduled for later this year.

Working in partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station, the exhibit aims to educate the public about ocean threats while inspiring conservation efforts. Hoffman stresses that understanding how small organisms support entire ecosystems is central to the facility's mission. The aquarium operates year-round and welcomes visitors to discover why ocean health matters. Those interested in learning more can visit sms.si.edu or follow the Smithsonian Marine Station on social media channels.
