Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit in Fort Pierce offers close encounters with Florida's coastal habitats

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium invites visitors to explore Florida's underwater world through live, complex ecosystem models located in Fort Pierce's waterfront district. Manager Bill Hoffman explained that the facility's approach to displaying fully functioning ecosystems has roots dating back to 1980 under the Smithsonian Institution, setting it apart from typical public aquariums. "We feel like we're the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast, and we don't want to be," Hoffman told WPTV. He noted that the facility houses more species than many larger exhibits across the country.

The centerpiece of the exhibit is the Atlantic coral reef tank, a large display that recreates one of Florida's most important coastal ecosystems. Beyond the coral reef, visitors encounter live representations of seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities. These displays showcase how different organisms interact and depend on one another within their natural environments. Even small details make an impression, such as the four-inch barracuda on display that demonstrates the diversity of marine life sizes.

The touch tank stands out as a visitor favorite, according to exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran. In this interactive space, guests can handle unusual marine species under staff supervision. Corcoran noted that visitors often encounter animals they have never touched before, making the experience memorable and educational. The facility is planning upcoming programs that will connect to the aquarium's 25th anniversary celebration scheduled for later this year.

The exhibit operates through a partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station and focuses on educating the public about ocean conservation. Hoffman emphasized that healthy ecosystems rely on countless small organisms working together, a principle demonstrated through the facility's live displays. The exhibit serves as a powerful educational opportunity for visitors of all ages to connect with Florida's fragile marine environments and understand their importance. The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit is located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce and is open to the public year-round. For more information, visit sms.si.edu or check the Smithsonian Marine Station's social media channels.
