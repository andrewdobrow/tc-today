Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at St. Lucie County Aquarium showcases Florida coastal habitats

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium invites visitors to explore Florida's vibrant underwater world through live, complex ecosystem displays. Located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce's waterfront district, the facility has maintained a unique philosophy since 1980 by showcasing fully functioning marine habitats rather than static displays. Manager Bill Hoffman told WPTV that the approach sets the aquarium apart from most public facilities. "We feel like we're the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast, and we don't want to be," Hoffman said. Despite its modest size, the facility houses more species than many larger aquariums across the country.

The centerpiece of the exhibit is the Atlantic coral reef tank, a sprawling display that recreates one of Florida's most important coastal ecosystems in vivid detail. Beyond the coral reef, visitors encounter live models of seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities. Each habitat demonstrates the intricate relationships between organisms that make these ecosystems thrive. Even smaller creatures play critical roles, such as the facility's four-inch barracuda, which contributes to the complex food webs on display.

The touch tank has become a visitor favorite, offering hands-on experiences with marine species under staff supervision. Exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran notes that guests often encounter animals they have never seen before while handling creatures from the live displays. This interactive component helps visitors develop a deeper connection to the marine life they learn about throughout the exhibit. The facility plans to expand its programming offerings later this year as part of its 25th anniversary celebration.

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit operates through a partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station and focuses on educating the public about ocean conservation. Hoffman emphasized that stable marine ecosystems depend on countless small organisms working together, a lesson reinforced by the facility's live displays. With growing concerns about ocean health, exhibits like this one provide valuable educational opportunities for visitors of all ages to understand the fragile beauty and ecological importance of Florida's marine environments. The facility is open to the public year-round, and more information is available at sms.si.edu and through the Smithsonian Marine Station's social media channels.
