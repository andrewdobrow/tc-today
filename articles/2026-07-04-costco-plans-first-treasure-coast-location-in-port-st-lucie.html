Costco plans first Treasure Coast store in Port St. Lucie

Costco is moving forward with plans to build its first store on the Treasure Coast in Port St. Lucie. The retailer has announced the development of a new warehouse location in the city, marking the first Costco warehouse to serve Martin, St. Lucie, and Indian River counties. The membership warehouse shopping model will arrive in the Treasure Coast region for the first time with this new location.

The timing of Costco's expansion to Port St. Lucie aligns with significant infrastructure development underway in the city. Port St. Lucie is working to expand Midway Road and add a partial interchange to Florida's Turnpike to accommodate growth and improve accessibility throughout the region. The Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to give 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to assist with the interchange project. Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, noted that "there's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie." He explained that the interchange is designed to address accessibility needs and support the city's growth trajectory. "This is a way for us to try to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years," Samples said.

The Florida Department of Transportation is managing a $95 million Midway Road Widening Project to help reduce congestion and improve safety. Crews are currently working on the first phase, widening the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second widening phase, which will extend from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, is still in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027. Additionally, FDOT is improving the Turnpike bridge and working on a partial interchange that remains in the design phase.

While construction activity has been noticeable to residents, many recognize its necessity. Port St. Lucie resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged the disruption but saw the value in the improvements. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," she said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." Beyond road widening, the Midway Road project will also add bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks to improve multimodal transportation options. The city council is scheduled to vote on final approval to transfer the land at an upcoming meeting. These infrastructure improvements, combined with Costco's arrival, signal significant development momentum for Port St. Lucie.
