Port St. Lucie city council votes to distribute $24 million Waste Pro settlement via tax roll

Port St. Lucie city council voted 4-1 to distribute the $24 million settlement from former trash hauler Waste Pro by crediting the funds directly to properties via the tax roll. This method uses an established system to channel the money to residents rather than issuing individual checks. Council members also decided against reimbursing the city for emergency solid waste expenses that had been covered by the general fund during the service failures.

The settlement stems from years of trash collection problems that plagued the city during the pandemic. Waste Pro failed to pick up overflowing bins regularly, leaving trash piled up on residents' yards. Both the city and Waste Pro filed lawsuits against each other in 2021. Waste Pro blamed labor shortages for the service failures, while the city argued the company breached its contract by failing to maintain adequate service standards. The dispute ultimately resulted in the $24 million settlement agreement.

Kelly Lee, a Port St. Lucie resident, attended Monday's council meeting to weigh in on how the money should be distributed. She recalled the frustration neighbors experienced when their trash accumulated as "a stinky mess" on their yards due to missed pickups. However, Lee raised concerns about fairness in the distribution process. She questioned how the city would handle compensation for people who experienced the problems but have since moved away, as well as for newer residents who were not impacted by Waste Pro's failures. "People have moved, the residents are no longer here, some of them are brand new residents that didn't pay into waste pro," Lee said, asking where those who left would receive their fair share.

Scott Samples with the city acknowledged the complexity of the situation, noting that staff have been working to collect data and develop different distribution scenarios for council recommendations. He emphasized that the city's goal is to make the best possible decision that helps address the hardship residents endured during that period. Mayor Shannon Martin previously stated her commitment to ensuring residents receive compensation for their troubles. Lee urged city leaders to involve residents in the decision-making process. "Have us weigh in on something to at least feel part of the process. One size can't fit all," she said.
