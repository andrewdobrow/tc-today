England and Argentina renew fierce World Cup rivalry in semifinal

England and Argentina will meet in the World Cup semifinals on Wednesday in Atlanta, resuming one of international soccer's fiercest rivalries. The World Cup rivalry between the nations dates back to 1962, but intensified after England's 1-0 quarterfinal win in 1966 when Argentina captain Antonio Rattin, whose death was announced on Saturday, was sent off in a bad-tempered match. Twenty years later in Mexico, Diego Maradona scored the infamous Hand of God goal that helped Argentina to a 2-1 quarterfinal win. That same game saw Maradona score what many believe to be the greatest World Cup goal ever when he dribbled the ball from the halfway line before beating England goalkeeper Peter Shilton. England felt aggrieved again in 1998 when David Beckham was sent off for kicking out at Argentina midfielder Diego Simeone before losing on penalties. Beckham scored a penalty in a 1-0 win four years later that contributed to Argentina being eliminated at the group stage.

England coach Thomas Tuchel said the two shirts are iconic and the historic matches are iconic, but as a coach he treats it as just another football match. Argentina coach Lionel Scaloni said Maradona's goal will be forever in their hearts and anybody who loves football will remember that in the best way possible. The winner will play Spain in the final in East Rutherford, New Jersey, on Sunday.
