35 Foster Youth Graduate at ChildNet Celebration at Palm Beach State College

ChildNet, a nonprofit serving foster youth in Broward and Palm Beach counties, celebrated a significant milestone as 35 students graduated at an annual ceremony held at Palm Beach State College. The event recognized young adults who have received support from the organization through education, adoption services, and independent living resources. The graduates represent foster youth and young adults who have worked toward stability and success while navigating the challenges of the child welfare system.

Among those celebrating their accomplishments were Sabrina Diaz and Brian Saintfort, two graduates who both experienced the loss of a parent and turned to ChildNet for guidance and support. The organization provided them with essential resources to help them move forward during difficult times. Diaz addressed her peers with an encouraging message about perseverance. She told others in similar situations to believe in their potential, work hard, and stay committed to their education and dreams. She emphasized the importance of pushing through pain and continuing to try despite obstacles. Saintfort shared similar advice, urging others to maintain faith in their ability to succeed.

Larry N. Rein, CEO of ChildNet, spoke about the meaning of the celebration. He acknowledged that the evening represented the combined efforts of the entire community, including case workers from ChildNet and the Department of Children and Family Services, as well as the determination of the graduates themselves. Rein emphasized that the event was primarily a chance to recognize and celebrate the hard work and resilience demonstrated by these young adults.

Both graduates have already begun planning their futures. Diaz has enrolled in pre-med classes at Palm Beach State College with aspirations of becoming a doctor. Saintfort is pursuing a vocational program to become a plumber. Their plans reflect the diverse paths these young adults are taking as they transition to independence and build careers. The graduation celebration highlighted not only the individual achievements of these 35 students but also the critical role that organizations like ChildNet play in supporting vulnerable youth through education and life skills training.
