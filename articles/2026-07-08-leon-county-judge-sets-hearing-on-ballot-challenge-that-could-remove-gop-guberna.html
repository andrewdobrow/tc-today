Leon County judge sets hearing on ballot challenge that could remove GOP gubernatorial candidate James Fishback

A lawsuit that could remove James Fishback from the Republican gubernatorial ballot moved to a fast track Tuesday when Leon County Judge David Frank scheduled a two-day hearing for July 21 and 22. Lt. Jay Collins, a rival GOP candidate, filed the lawsuit arguing Fishback does not meet Florida's seven-year residency requirement to run for governor. The complaint points to records tied to Fishback's voter registration and residency in Washington, D.C. Judge Frank warned attorneys there is an extreme shortage of time before the August 18 primary and noted it is likely already too late to stop ballots from being printed. Frank said if Fishback is removed, the court would issue an injunction to the Department of State directing supervisors of elections not to count votes for Fishback and to post notices. Fishback's attorney, former state representative Anthony Sabatini, told the judge the case turns on more than paperwork and that Fishback's intent will be central to determining residency.

The courtroom fight comes days after the issue erupted during last week's GOP primary debate featuring Fishback, Collins and former House Speaker Paul Renner. Frontrunner Byron Donalds skipped the event but still loomed large as rivals repeatedly invoked him. During the debate, Fishback pressed Collins over the lawsuit, telling him to drop the case and later accusing Collins of trying to steal the election with Byron Donalds. Collins responded that the challenge belongs in court and that he respects the judicial system. Witness and exhibit lists are due five days before the July 21 hearing.
