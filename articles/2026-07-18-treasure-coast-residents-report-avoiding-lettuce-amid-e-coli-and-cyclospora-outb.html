Treasure Coast residents change diets to avoid lettuce after E. coli and cyclospora outbreaks

Reports of contaminated produce continue to spread across the country, and many people in South Florida are changing what they eat and what they avoid. The latest concern centers around lettuce linked to outbreaks of E. coli and cyclospora, including lettuce connected to Taco Bell locations. Health officials said thousands across the U.S. have been affected, with confirmed cases also reported locally from the Palm Beaches into the Treasure Coast.

According to the Florida Department of Health, there have been five confirmed local cases of cyclospora since May: 2 in Palm Beach County, 2 in St. Lucie County, and 1 in Martin County.

At restaurants, grocery stores and fast-food chains, some families say they are steering clear of leafy greens altogether. Dakota and Brynlee Rhodes, while visiting Delray Beach with their mother Jenny, said they are trying to stay away from lettuce and stuff like that. Jenny Rhodes said she misses her salads and berries but believes it is better to be careful than ruin your day.

In downtown West Palm Beach, George Lorenz said he is also taking precautions while grabbing lunch with his father Brian on Clematis Street. Samantha Barone, dietitian for the bariatric program at Delray Medical Center, said consumers should focus on produce with protective outer layers. She recommends sticking to melon and things that have a harder skin that you can peel, but the trick is to still wash them before you cut into them and peel them.
