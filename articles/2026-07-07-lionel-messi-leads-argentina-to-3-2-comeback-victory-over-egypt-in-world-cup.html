Lionel Messi leads Argentina to 3-2 comeback victory over Egypt, advances to World Cup quarterfinals

Lionel Messi scored his eighth goal of the tournament and led Argentina to an improbable 3-2 victory over Egypt on Tuesday despite trailing by two goals with only 11 minutes of regulation time to play. The defending champions rallied from a 2-0 deficit to win their round of 16 match and advance to face either Switzerland or Colombia in the quarterfinals on Saturday in Kansas City, Missouri. Messi, who was in tears after the final whistle, scored his record-extending 21st goal at the World Cup in the 83rd minute to level the score at 2-2. Enzo Fernandez scored the winning goal in stoppage time to complete one of the great World Cup comebacks, with Cristian Romero starting the rally in the 79th minute.

Egypt had taken a surprising lead in the 15th minute when Yasser Ibrahim got ahead of Lisandro Martinez to meet Marwan Attia's cross and head the ball into the bottom corner. Argentina was quickly given the chance to level when Haissem Hassan tripped Nicolas Tagliafico in the box moments later. Referee François Letexier pointed to the penalty spot, but Egypt goalkeeper Mostafa Shobeir dove to his left to block Messi's shot for his second penalty miss of the tournament after also failing from the spot against Austria in the group stage. Messi later hit the post when the score was 1-0, and Shobeir pulled off another great save to stop Julian Alvarez from close range. Egypt thought it had doubled its lead in the second half when Mostafa Zico finished off a sweeping attack, but the wild celebrations were cut short when a foul earlier in the move was confirmed on video review and the goal was disallowed. That second goal for Egypt did come in the 67th from a similar break, and this time Zico's effort counted.

For much of the game, it looked like it would be a painful exit for the 39-year-old Messi in what might be the last of his six World Cups. Despite being the all-time leading scorer at World Cups, Messi has now missed four of eight penalty kicks at the tournament. Argentina is bidding to become the first team to win back-to-back titles since Brazil did so in 1958 and 1962.

Argentina coach Lionel Scaloni expressed his emotions after the match, saying he was so emotional and praising his group of players. Fernandez said the team never gives up and that four years have passed since Qatar, noting they have come to enjoy another World Cup and want to win it again.
