Port St. Lucie moves forward on Midway Road expansion and new Florida Turnpike interchange

Port St. Lucie is taking steps to improve traffic flow and regional connectivity as the city continues to experience growth. The City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to transfer 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation. This land will support the construction of a new partial interchange on the Florida Turnpike, a project that remains in the design phase.

Scott Samples, the city's strategic communications manager, explained the reasoning behind the interchange project. "There's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie," Samples said. The new interchange is designed to improve accessibility for residents and visitors as the area grows. According to Samples, the city is working "to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years." The City Council will vote on final approval to transfer the land at an upcoming meeting.

The interchange is part of a larger effort by FDOT to improve the region. The Florida Department of Transportation is overseeing the $95 million Midway Road Widening Project, which aims to reduce congestion and enhance safety. Currently, crews are working on the first phase of the project, expanding the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, which will widen the road from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, is still in the design stage. Construction on that phase is expected to begin in summer 2027.

The Midway Road Widening Project includes multiple improvements beyond just adding lanes. FDOT is working to improve the Turnpike bridge and will add bike and pedestrian paths alongside sidewalks to encourage alternative transportation. While construction can be disruptive for nearby residents, many understand its necessity. One Port St. Lucie resident acknowledged the inconvenience but recognized the benefits, saying "We definitely needed it to be expanded. It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." As the project progresses through its various phases and design stages, the improvements to Midway Road and the new Turnpike interchange are expected to better serve Port St. Lucie's current needs and support continued development in the coming decades.
