Port St. Lucie moves forward on Midway Road expansion and Florida Turnpike interchange

Port St. Lucie is advancing a major transportation initiative that will widen Midway Road and create a partial interchange connecting to Florida's Turnpike in St. Lucie County. The Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to transfer 7 acres of city land to the Florida Department of Transportation. This land donation will support the development of the new interchange, which city officials say is needed to address a significant gap in current Turnpike access points throughout the city.

The $95 million Midway Road Widening Project aims to reduce congestion and improve safety along this critical corridor. Currently, crews are focused on the first phase of construction, which involves expanding the roadway from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, extending from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design stage with construction expected to start in summer 2027, according to the FDOT project website. Beyond widening the road surface, the project also includes improvements to the Turnpike bridge and will incorporate bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks.

Scott Samples, the strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, emphasized that the new interchange addresses both current needs and future growth. "There's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie," Samples said. He added that the project is designed to "accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years." The partial interchange remains in the design phase, and the city council will vote on final approval of the land transfer at an upcoming meeting.

While construction activities have drawn attention from residents in the area, many recognize the project's importance. One nearby resident noted that the ongoing construction can be disruptive but acknowledged its necessity. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," she said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." As Port St. Lucie continues to grow, these infrastructure improvements represent a significant investment in the city's transportation network to support both current residents and future development.
