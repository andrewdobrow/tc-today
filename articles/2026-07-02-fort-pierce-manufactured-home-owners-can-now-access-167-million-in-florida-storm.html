Fort Pierce manufactured home owners can now access $167 million in Florida storm repair funding

Nearly 140 new Florida laws took effect on July 1st, and one of them could significantly help some of the state's most vulnerable homeowners. Senate Bill 594 opens up $167 million in state affordable housing funding to assist mobile and manufactured home owners with emergency repairs and storm-hardening retrofits. The change came in response to an investigation that revealed nearly 700,000 manufactured homes across Florida, about two-thirds of the state's total, were built before current federal safety standards and cannot withstand modern storms.

The push for this funding began after an EF-3 tornado destroyed the Spanish Lakes Country Club Community in Fort Pierce. The disaster obliterated 138 homes and killed six residents. Paula Richards, a Spanish Lakes resident, received repair estimates ranging from $10,000 to $15,000 but could not afford the costs. Many seniors living in older manufactured homes across Florida face the same financial barriers to making their structures safer. Their pleas for assistance eventually reached lawmakers, who responded by passing Senate Bill 594 in spring 2026.

Implementation of the program varies by county. Martin County Human Services reports that an online portal will open in August, with about 75 percent of the county's annual SHIP funding, typically $1 million to $1.5 million, now available to eligible mobile and manufactured homeowners. St. Lucie County is still waiting for final guidance from the state before announcing its application process. Indian River County officials will present the legislative change to their Board on August 18th, with a September 30th deadline to submit their implementation plan to the state. Okeechobee County is updating its local housing assistance plan to comply with the new law and plans to offer lot rent, rehabilitation and emergency repair assistance once the program opens.

People interested in applying should visit the Florida Housing Finance Corporation website to check income eligibility and learn how to apply. Applications can be submitted online or by mail. According to Julie Stellatos with Martin County Human Services, the funding operates on a first-come, first-served basis, making it important for residents to gather their paperwork quickly. While counties generally follow the same timeframe based on a calendar from the Florida Housing Coalition, the specific timeline varies by location. Beginning preparation now by visiting the state website and selecting your county will help you understand what funding is available and who to contact when applications open in your area.
