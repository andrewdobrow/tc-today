Hobe Sound mom fills community food pantry as neighbors struggle with rising costs

A Hobe Sound resident is supporting her neighbors during financially difficult times by establishing a Little Free Pantry outside her home. Jacquelyn Koester launched the initiative after posting in a local Facebook group, determined to make sure families in the area could access basic food items and necessities without embarrassment or judgment. What began as a straightforward request for community assistance has evolved into an ongoing resource that serves anyone facing hardship, regardless of their circumstances.

The pantry sits just behind the Hobe Sound Public Library, steps away from Koester's residence. Its shelves hold food and baby essentials intended for families struggling to manage their household budgets as inflation drives up everyday expenses. Koester observed that once fuel and grocery prices climbed, the pantry's contents disappeared much faster than she could replenish them. "When gas prices went up and food prices went up it was just emptying about faster than I can replace it," she explained. Despite the challenge of keeping up with demand, the overwhelming community response has sustained the effort.

After Koester reached out for help on Facebook, neighbors stepped up significantly to keep the pantry stocked. The contributions have been consistent and generous, ensuring the shelves remain filled for those who need them. One person even commented anonymously that they had driven from Jupiter specifically to visit the pantry and that access to its contents would help them survive the week. These messages of gratitude and stories of real impact fuel Koester's commitment to maintaining the resource and making sure it remains accessible to everyone in need.

Koester credits the tight-knit nature of Hobe Sound for the pantry's success. "I knew the second I posted on that group that Hobe Sound was going to show up for us because that's what we do, we are a tight knit community," she said. She reflects on how community support has evolved over time. "The saying it takes a village is so true and the villages look a lot different than they used to, but people still show up for each other and people want to help," Koester said. What now stands outside her home represents far more than a simple pantry. It has become a tangible symbol of neighbors caring for one another during uncertain economic times. Community members interested in supporting the pantry or accessing its resources can find more information through the Facebook group Koester created.
