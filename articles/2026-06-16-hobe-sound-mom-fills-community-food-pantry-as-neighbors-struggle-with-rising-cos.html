Hobe Sound mom creates community food pantry to help neighbors fight rising grocery costs

Jacquelyn Koester saw her community struggling with rising food and gas prices, so she decided to take action. She started a Little Free Pantry outside her home, stocked with food and baby essentials for anyone in the Hobe Sound area who needs help. What began as a simple Facebook post to connect neighbors has grown into a reliable resource for families trying to make ends meet. The pantry operates on a no-questions-asked basis, ensuring that anyone facing financial hardship can access the supplies they need.

Located behind the Hobe Sound Public Library, the pantry has become a visible symbol of community care. Koester knew from the start that her neighbors would respond to the call for support. "I knew the second I posted on that group that Hobe Sound was going to show up for us because that's what we do, we are a tight knit community," she said. The community response exceeded her expectations, with donations regularly arriving to keep the shelves filled. The demand has been so high that Koester has struggled to restock items as quickly as they disappear. "When gas prices went up and food prices went up it was just emptying about faster than I can replace it," she said.

The pantry's impact extends beyond simple food distribution. Stories from people who have used the pantry reveal its real-world importance to struggling families. One person posted anonymously in the Facebook group that they drove from Jupiter specifically to access supplies from the pantry, saying it would help them get through the week. These messages from grateful community members keep Koester motivated to maintain the effort and ensure the pantry remains accessible to those who need it most.

Koester has created a Facebook group where residents can find information about accessing the pantry and learn how to contribute donations. She sees the pantry as more than just a charitable effort. It represents how communities continue to support one another through changing economic times. "The saying it takes a village is so true and the villages look a lot different than they used to, but people still show up for each other and people want to help," she said. What sits outside her home has become a steady stream of community support for families working to afford groceries in an environment of rising costs.
