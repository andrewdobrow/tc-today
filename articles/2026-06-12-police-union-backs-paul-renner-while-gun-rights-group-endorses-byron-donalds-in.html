Florida 2026-07-04

Byron Donalds leads Florida GOP governor's race with $90 million raised as rival challenges eligibility of third candidate

U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds strengthened his position in the Republican primary for Florida governor, announcing that his campaign and affiliated political committee have raised more than $90 million this cycle, with more than $23 million coming in the second quarter alone. The campaign recently launched a $20 million statewide advertising buy as the August 18 primary approaches. Donalds, viewed as the front-runner to succeed term-limited Gov. Ron DeSantis, secured endorsements from Attorney General James Uthmeier and Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia, both appointed by DeSantis. He also has the backing of the two incoming legislative leaders, Senate President Jim Boyd and House Speaker Sam Garrison. These endorsements carry particular weight in a primary where DeSantis himself has not endorsed a successor, though several figures from his administration have begun supporting Donalds.

The race took a contentious turn when Lt. Gov. Jay Collins filed a lawsuit seeking to remove Republican candidate James Fishback from the ballot. Collins argues that Fishback does not meet Florida's constitutional requirement that a governor live in the state for at least seven years before being elected. The legal complaint points to Fishback's ties to Washington, D.C., including allegations that he claimed a D.C. property as a primary residence and voted there in 2020. "Election integrity matters," Collins said in explaining the challenge. Fishback has denied being ineligible, arguing he was born and raised in Florida. He accused Collins of attempting to damage his campaign and suggested, without providing evidence, that Collins is working with Donalds. At a news conference, Fishback announced, "The first motion we will file is for Congressman Donalds and Jay Collins to release all communications between their two campaigns."

Gov. DeSantis moved to use a new state law allowing Florida to designate organizations as terrorist groups. The law, which took effect July 1, allows the governor and Florida Cabinet to designate organizations based on recommendations from state domestic security officials. DeSantis said Florida will seek to designate the Council on American-Islamic Relations, the Muslim Brotherhood, and Antifa, along with more than 90 federally designated foreign terrorist organizations. If approved by the Cabinet, the designations could block taxpayer funding, contracts, and public support for listed groups and create penalties for providing material support. CAIR and civil-rights groups quickly sued in federal court, calling the move unconstitutional and warning it could threaten the organizations' ability to operate in Florida.

Florida also advanced restrictions on undocumented students. The State Board of Education voted to move forward with rules barring undocumented students from Florida's 28 state colleges and adult education programs, including GED preparation courses. The state university system's Board of Governors separately advanced a plan barring undocumented students from enrolling at Florida's more competitive public universities beginning with the 2027-28 academic year, though currently enrolled students would not be affected. Supporters argue taxpayer-backed institutions should serve only lawfully present students. Immigrant advocates pushed back, with student Alexander Vallejos saying, "We're just asking for an opportunity to earn our future through education."
