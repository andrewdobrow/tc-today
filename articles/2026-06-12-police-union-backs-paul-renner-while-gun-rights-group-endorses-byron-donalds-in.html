Florida 2026-07-03

Byron Donalds surges ahead in Florida GOP governor's race as Collins sues to remove rival from ballot

U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds strengthened his position as the Republican front-runner to succeed term-limited Gov. Ron DeSantis this week, announcing his campaign and affiliated political committee have raised more than $90 million this cycle, including over $23 million in the second quarter. The campaign recently launched a $20 million statewide advertising buy ahead of Florida's August 18 primary. Donalds picked up endorsements from two statewide officials appointed by DeSantis: Attorney General James Uthmeier and Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia. He also secured backing from the two incoming legislative leaders, Senate President Jim Boyd and House Speaker Sam Garrison. These endorsements carry particular weight in a primary where DeSantis himself has not endorsed a successor, though several figures from his administration have begun supporting Donalds.

While Donalds builds momentum with money and institutional support, Lt. Gov. Jay Collins filed a lawsuit this week to remove Republican candidate James Fishback from the ballot. Collins argues Fishback fails to meet Florida's constitutional requirement that a governor reside in the state for at least seven years before election. The legal complaint highlights Fishback's ties to Washington, D.C., including allegations that he claimed a D.C. property as his primary residence and voted there in 2020. Collins said the challenge was necessary because "election integrity matters."

Fishback denied the allegations, asserting he was born and raised in Florida and accusing Collins of attempting to damage his campaign. Without providing evidence, Fishback suggested Collins was working with Donalds and said his team's first motion would demand both Donalds and Collins release all communications between their campaigns. The legal battle adds uncertainty to the GOP primary as mail ballots prepare to go out and the primary date approaches.

In other Florida political developments this week, state officials moved forward with restrictions targeting undocumented students. The State Board of Education voted to advance rules barring undocumented students from the state's 28 colleges and adult education programs, including GED courses. This follows a separate action by the Board of Governors, which proposed barring undocumented students from Florida's more competitive public universities starting in the 2027-28 academic year, though currently enrolled students would not be affected. Additionally, Gov. DeSantis moved to use a new state law allowing Florida to designate organizations as terrorist groups. The state said it would seek to designate the Council on American-Islamic Relations, the Muslim Brotherhood, and Antifa. CAIR and civil rights groups quickly sued in federal court, calling the move unconstitutional and arguing it could threaten the organization's ability to operate in Florida.
