Florida 2026-07-03

Florida advances ban on undocumented students at state colleges as Byron Donalds leads GOP governor's race

Florida's State Board of Education voted this week to advance rules that would prevent undocumented students from enrolling in the state's 28 public colleges and adult education programs, including GED preparation courses. The action follows a separate decision by the state university system's Board of Governors to advance a plan barring undocumented students from Florida's public universities starting in the 2027-28 academic year. Students already enrolled would not be affected by the university proposal.

Supporters of the restrictions contend that taxpayer-funded institutions should prioritize students who are lawfully present in the United States. However, immigrant advocates and students have opposed the rules, arguing they would close educational doors for young people who grew up in Florida and graduated from Florida high schools. Alexander Vallejos, a student Dreamer, said "We're just asking for an opportunity to earn our future through education. Because when Dreamers succeed, Florida succeeds." The restrictions come after Florida previously eliminated in-state tuition rates for many undocumented students, including those protected under DACA.

The education restrictions emerged during a dominant week in Florida's Republican governor's race. Byron Donalds entered the week as the front-runner to succeed term-limited Governor Ron DeSantis. His campaign and affiliated political committee report raising more than 90 million dollars this cycle, including more than 23 million in the second quarter. The campaign recently launched a 20 million dollar statewide advertising buy. Donalds also received endorsements from Attorney General James Uthmeier and Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia, both appointed by DeSantis. He has backing from incoming Senate President Jim Boyd and House Speaker Sam Garrison.

Meanwhile, Lieutenant Jay Collins filed a lawsuit to remove Republican candidate James Fishback from the ballot, arguing Fishback fails to meet Florida's constitutional requirement that a governor live in the state for at least seven years before election. Collins' legal complaint cites Fishback's Washington D.C. ties, including claims he designated a D.C. property as his primary residence and voted there in 2020. Fishback denied the allegations and suggested Collins was working with Donalds. The legal battle adds uncertainty to the GOP primary as the August 18 primary date approaches and mail ballots prepare to begin distribution.
