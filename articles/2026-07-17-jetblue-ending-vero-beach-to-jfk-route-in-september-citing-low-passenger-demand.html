JetBlue ending Vero Beach to New York JFK route in September due to low demand

JetBlue is canceling its flight route between Vero Beach Regional Airport and New York's JFK Airport, with the last flights operating in September. The airline cited low demand as the reason for ending the route, which launched last December to excitement from Treasure Coast residents hoping for convenient travel options to the Northeast. Brandon Dambeck, interim airport director, said the decision reflects how the airline industry operates based on passenger numbers.

"It's just the way that that market works, and air carriers will fluctuate based off of demand," Dambeck said. He added that JetBlue "just wasn't seeing what they were hoping for" with the New York route.

Not everyone agrees the route had a fair shot at success. Charlie Ahern, who regularly uses JetBlue flights at Vero Beach Regional Airport, said he believes the route would have grown over time if given more opportunity. "I think if they hung in there, it would have worked.

I think it would have built up pretty good," Ahern said. He noted the convenience of flying out of Vero Beach, saying, "It's easy. It's close." His comments reflect concerns from local travelers who appreciated having direct access to New York without driving to larger airports in South Florida or Orlando.

The Boston route continues to perform well at Vero Beach Regional Airport, with Dambeck saying "Boston is seeing great demand." Grece Ezzi flew into Vero Beach on JetBlue from Boston on Friday for the first time, demonstrating continued interest in that northeastern connection. The contrast between the two routes highlights how different markets can perform differently even when serving similar geographic regions. While both Boston and New York are major northeastern cities, the Boston service has attracted enough passengers to remain viable while the JFK route struggled to build a sustainable customer base.

Dambeck said the door is not closed on the JFK route returning in the future, noting "They'll evaluate continuously. So just because it's gone now doesn't mean that it never will come back." This leaves open the possibility that JetBlue could restore service to New York if conditions change or demand increases. Airlines regularly reassess their route networks based on seasonal patterns, economic conditions, and shifting travel preferences. For now, Vero Beach travelers seeking to reach New York will need to connect through other cities or use alternative airports.
