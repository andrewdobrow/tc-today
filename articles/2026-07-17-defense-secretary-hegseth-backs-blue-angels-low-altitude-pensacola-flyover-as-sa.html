Defense Secretary Hegseth backs low-altitude Blue Angels flyover in Pensacola as safety reviews draw scrutiny

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth defended low-altitude military flyovers after a Navy Blue Angels jet flew so low over a crowded Pensacola beach Wednesday that chairs and tents went flying, sand kicked up and children held their hands over their ears. The Navy initially said it was conducting a thorough safety review, but Trump administration officials quickly praised the maneuver instead. Hegseth wrote on his personal X account that "the flyovers will continue until morale improves," while Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell posted "Carry on Patriots" alongside a photo showing a Blue Angels jet with a wingtip just feet above beachgoers' heads. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao, a Trump administration political appointee, said the Navy had no problem with the flight and there would be no reprimands or firings. Navy officials would not say if a routine flight debrief was the thorough safety review the service promised the day before. The White House tweeted a cartoon showing beachgoers photographing a Blue Angels jet with the words "Freedom" and "It's okay to love America."

The incident marks at least the third time Hegseth has voiced support for military maneuvers that drew scrutiny and safety investigations. In March, he ended an Army investigation into helicopters hovering near Kid Rock's Tennessee home, saying "No punishment. Carry on, patriots." Months later, he lifted suspensions of eight South Carolina National Guard helicopter pilots who conducted a low flyover during a July 4 event. In both prior cases, Hegseth's intervention led to the end of safety investigations. Pentagon spokesman Jacob Bliss said he had nothing further to provide when asked if the social media posts meant another investigation had been halted. Aviation safety consultant Jeff Guzzetti, who used to investigate crashes for the National Transportation Safety Board and Federal Aviation Administration, called the behavior reckless and said a cavalier attitude from top military leaders can only lead to accidents. Former Transportation Department Inspector General Mary Schiavo said Blue Angels demonstrations are exceedingly dangerous and not something to be performed over people.
