St. Lucie County wrongful death lawsuit filed after Florida Turnpike crash killed three workers

On August 12, 2025, a minivan carrying three workers became wedged underneath a semi-truck on the Florida Turnpike in St. Lucie County. The truck's driver, Harjinder Singh, allegedly made an illegal U-turn in a clearly marked "No U-turn" zone. Faniola Joseph, 37, Herby Dufresne, 30, and Rodrigue Dor, 54, all died in the crash. Joseph had been traveling south from Indiana to purchase goods for her retail business. Now her daughter, 22-year-old Angeline Dowdian, has filed a wrongful death lawsuit against Singh, his employer White Hawk Carriers, and freight broker C.H. Robinson.

The lawsuit alleges that C.H. Robinson knowingly hired White Hawk Carriers despite the company's serious safety violations. Attorney Dax Bello of Stuart represents Angeline and argues this was not a random accident but a foreseeable consequence of unsafe practices. "This is not an accident. It's a predictable end that came at the cost of three lives," Bello said. The case gains strength from a May 14 U.S. Supreme Court decision in Montgomery v. Caribbean Transport, which clarified that federal law does not shield freight brokers from state negligence claims when they place unsafe drivers on the road. Transportation expert James Lewis has described White Hawk's violations as "very egregious." Bello emphasizes that brokers and carriers have a responsibility to check publicly available safety records before putting drivers on the road.

Angeline lived abroad and had not seen her mother in six years, though they spoke daily through video calls. When the crash occurred, she missed a call from her mother. "She sent me a voice message. She said, 'Angeline, where are you?' This is the first message I go to every morning," Angeline said. She described losing her mother as losing everything. Her father had rejected her at birth, making her mother both parents to her. Visa restrictions have prevented her from visiting her mother's grave since the crash.

Singh faces criminal traffic homicide charges in Florida. White Hawk Carriers has since shut down, though Bello warned the company may reopen under a different name, a practice he said is common in the industry. Angeline's co-counsel Mark Brumer added that litigation will address not only who caused the crash but who could have prevented it. While no legal victory will undo her pain, Angeline hopes the lawsuit prevents other families from suffering as hers has. "I don't want nobody suffer like me," she said. "She worked her whole life so I could have a better one, and died in such a terrible way."
