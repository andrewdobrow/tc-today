Byron Donalds and Ashley Moody form unified ticket for Florida Governor and U.S. Senate races

Byron Donalds and Ashley Moody are coordinating their campaigns for Florida Governor and U.S. Senate, announcing joint appearances, mutual endorsements, and grassroots events designed to consolidate Republican support ahead of the November General Election. Moody endorsed Donalds, saying "He is a proven conservative leader who will fight to make Florida safer, healthier, and more affordable as Governor." She noted their shared work in Tallahassee and Washington on tax cuts, immigration enforcement, and prescription drug costs. Donalds reciprocated the endorsement, stating "Her proven leadership as Attorney General and now as a United States Senator makes her the right choice to continue serving our state." The campaigns plan to emphasize public safety, education, economic growth, and senior issues while contrasting themselves with the Democratic field.

Donalds, who represents Naples in Congress, entered the gubernatorial race in early 2025 with backing from former President Donald Trump. His campaign raised a record 22.2 million dollars in the first quarter for a non-incumbent Florida gubernatorial candidate and has now exceeded 67 million dollars overall. He has qualified for the ballot and secured endorsements from the Florida Farm Bureau's FarmPAC and legislative leaders. A former state Representative with a finance background, Donalds would become Florida's first Black Governor if elected. He faces primary opposition from Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins, former House Speaker Paul Renner, and businessman James Fishback, though Trump's endorsement and fundraising strength have positioned him as the frontrunner.

Moody, a fifth-generation Floridian and former federal prosecutor and Hillsborough Circuit Judge, served six years as Attorney General before Ron DeSantis appointed her to the U.S. Senate seat vacated by Marco Rubio, who became Secretary of State. During her time as Attorney General, she helped secure billions in opioid settlements for Florida. She was sworn in on January 21, 2025, has received Trump's endorsement, and has qualified for the Special Election to complete the remaining two years of Rubio's term.

On the Democratic side, former Republican Congressman David Jolly leads the gubernatorial field with former U.S. Representative Gwen Graham as his running mate. The Democratic Senate race remains uncertain after teacher Josh Weil withdrew due to a medical issue. The Primary Election is scheduled for Tuesday, August 18, with voter registration closing July 20. The General Election will follow on Tuesday, November 3, with registration closing October 5.
