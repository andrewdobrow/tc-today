President Trump's 250th anniversary rally overshadows congressional commemoration commission

President Trump's 250th anniversary rally on the National Mall has overshadowed the bipartisan, congressionally authorized commission supposed to coordinate commemorative events. Trump's face appears on banners across federal buildings in the nation's capital, and he plans to return to the National Mall on July Fourth for what he calls a "Trump rally." Since returning to office in January 2025, Trump has nominated one of his personal lawyers to serve as attorney general, ordered the Department of Justice to pursue his political enemies, deployed the U.S. Marines to the nation's second largest city and leveraged the presidency to enrich himself and his family. He has demanded that comedians who mock him be fired, slapped his name on the Kennedy Center, pushed to seize control of elections, filed lawsuits against news organizations whose coverage he disliked and sued his own government seeking $10 billion in taxpayer money.

Trump's actions have led to comparisons with King George III, the British monarch whose rule inspired the American Revolution. When King Charles III visited Trump this year, the official White House X account posted an image of the two men with the caption "TWO KINGS." At the start of his second term, Trump declared he had ended a New York City transportation program and posted: "LONG LIVE THE KING." Trump rejected the comparison, telling CBS' "60 Minutes" earlier this year: "I'm not a king. If I was a king, I wouldn't be dealing with you." Princeton University historian Julian Zelizer said the label fits differently on Trump, noting that the nation is celebrating founding principles rooted in fears of centralized power corruption.
