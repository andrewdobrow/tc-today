St. Petersburg redevelopment plan proposes restoring Booker Creek as natural feature

The Burg Bid's redevelopment proposal for Tropicana Field and the Historic Gas Plant District centers on Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre civic and cultural space that would anchor the $8.1 billion mixed-use district. The plan includes restoring Booker Creek, which was reduced to a drainage ditch between parking lots and the stadium, as a central natural feature of the former Gas Plant neighborhood. A reimagined Pinellas Trail would integrate with wide pedestrian paths and a community park along the restored creek. The proposal calls for nine bridges over Booker Creek, referencing the nine bridges that once stood in the Historic Gas Plant area, designed to restore physical connections across the site and neighborhood.

The development team, led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, proposes preserving the original gas tank footprint within Samuel Davis Park and weaving historic references throughout the Legacy Link, a diagonal pedestrian corridor. The plan emphasizes respect for the predominantly Black community that was razed decades ago to build Tropicana Field, with design elements intended to inform and educate future generations about the neighborhood's history. The proposal includes 3,600 income-restricted housing units over a 15-year buildout, with permanent homes for The Woodson African American Museum of Florida and a Museum of Public Art, along with cultural amenities, workforce training centers, and retail and entertainment space.
