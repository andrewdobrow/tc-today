Florida 2026-07-04

St. Petersburg selects $8.1 billion plan for Tropicana Field redevelopment centered on civic park and museum

Led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, The Burg Bid calls for purchasing 58 acres of city-owned land for $275 million, with the full land package valued at $405 million when factoring in $130 million in land the city would retain. The proposal includes a $20 million deposit upon execution of a purchase and sale agreement. The $8.1 billion plan transforms stadium parking and public land into a mixed-use district featuring housing, hotels, offices, retail, cultural institutions, workforce training, public art and entertainment space. Unlike the previous redevelopment deal centered on a new stadium, The Burg Bid organizes around Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre central park that would include The Woodson African American Museum of Florida, a Museum of Public Art, a Discovery Center, event lawns, a playground, a splash pad and a concert lawn. The park anchors 18.5 acres of total public park space on site.

The proposal includes 3,600 income-restricted housing units over a 15-year buildout, with 1,800 units in the Historic Gas Plant District and 1,800 on complementary sites elsewhere in St. Petersburg, plus homeownership opportunities at discounts to market value. The district would be built in three phases of approximately five years each, with early development beginning outside the Tropicana Field footprint before stadium demolition. The team's lease with the city ends after the 2028 season. Phase 1 would establish cultural and workforce anchors including the Woodson, the Collaboratorium workforce training center and a multimodal transportation hub.
