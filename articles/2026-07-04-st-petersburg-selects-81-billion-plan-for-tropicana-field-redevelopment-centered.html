St. Petersburg Selects $8.1 Billion Proposal to Redevelop Tropicana Field Site Around Civic Park and Museum

Led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, The Burg Bid calls for purchasing 58 acres of city-owned land for $275 million, with the full land package valued at $405 million when factoring in $130 million in land the city would retain. The proposal would be organized around Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre central park meant to become a civic and cultural space for the district. The park would be part of 18.5 acres of public park space and would include The Woodson African American Museum of Florida, a Museum of Public Art, a Discovery Center, event lawns, a playground, a splash pad, café areas, public art and a concert lawn. The proposal also includes plans for a surf wave attraction, recreation deck, multimodal hub, workforce and small-business center, and Resilience Event Center.

The plan calls for 3,600 income-restricted housing units over a 15-year buildout, with 1,800 units planned within the Historic Gas Plant District and another 1,800 on complementary sites elsewhere in St. Petersburg. The district would be built in three main phases, each expected to take about five years. Phase 1 would establish the district's cultural and workforce anchors, including The Woodson, the Collaboratorium workforce training and small-business center, the Legacy Link pedestrian corridor and a multimodal transportation hub. The proposal also calls for restoring Booker Creek, which was reduced to a drainage ditch between parking lots and Tropicana Field. The Rays' lease with the city ends after the 2028 season.
