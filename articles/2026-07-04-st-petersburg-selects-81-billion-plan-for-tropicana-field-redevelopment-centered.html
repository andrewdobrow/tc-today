St. Petersburg redevelopment plan proposes restoring Booker Creek and preserving gas tank footprint

The Burg Bid proposal, selected by St. Petersburg Mayor Ken Welch as the city's preferred path forward, centers on an $8.1 billion plan to transform Tropicana Field and the surrounding Historic Gas Plant District into a mixed-use development. Led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, the proposal calls for purchasing 58 acres of city-owned land for $275 million and would include 3,600 income-restricted housing units over a 15-year buildout. The district would feature Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre central civic and cultural space that would anchor the development. The park would be part of 18.5 acres of total public park space and would include a permanent home for The Woodson African American Museum of Florida, a Museum of Public Art, a Discovery Center, event lawns, a playground, a splash pad, and a concert lawn.

The proposal emphasizes respect for the Historic Gas Plant neighborhood, a predominantly Black community razed decades ago. The plan preserves the original gas tank footprint within Samuel Davis Park and weaves historic references throughout the Legacy Link, a diagonal pedestrian corridor connecting City Hall with the Manhattan Casino and the Deuces District. Nine bridges over Booker Creek would echo nine bridges that once stood in the area and restore physical connections across the site. The proposal also calls for restoring Booker Creek itself, which had been reduced to a drainage ditch between parking lots and Tropicana Field, with a reimagined Pinellas Trail integrating wide pedestrian paths and a community park along the creek.
