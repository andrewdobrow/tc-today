Indian River County fertilizer ban begins June 30 to protect waterways

Starting June 30, residents in Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties will need to stop applying fertilizers that contain phosphorus or nitrogen to their lawns and landscaping. The restriction takes effect during the rainy season when nutrient runoff poses the greatest threat to local waterways.

The ban is designed to reduce the amount of nutrient-rich runoff that flows into nearby water systems during heavy rainfall. According to Jordan Skaggs with Environmental Resources in St. Lucie County, this runoff can trigger algae blooms that damage waterways including the Indian River Lagoon and St. Lucie River. These blooms create multiple problems for aquatic environments by blocking out the beneficial plants that fish and wildlife depend on for survival. In some cases, harmful algae blooms become dense enough to release toxins that threaten human health, animal health and the overall health of ecosystems in the region.

Residents looking for alternatives during the fertilizer restriction period can turn to composting to maintain their landscapes. Grantly Ricketts, director of the University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences St. Lucie County Extension, explained that composting is an effective way to add nutrients back into soil and plants. He suggested using a small trash can as a simple and practical composting method that homeowners can easily implement during the ban period.

The three-county fertilizer restriction represents a regional effort to protect water quality during months when rainfall is heaviest and runoff is most likely to carry pollutants into nearby rivers and lagoons. By limiting the use of nitrogen and phosphorus-based fertilizers, local officials hope to prevent the nutrient buildup that fuels destructive algae blooms and maintains healthier conditions for fish, wildlife and human recreation in the area's waterways.
