Martin, St. Lucie and Indian River counties implement summer fertilizer ban through September

Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties have enacted a ban on fertilizers containing phosphorus or nitrogen from June 1 through September 30. The restriction applies to both residential homeowners and commercial landscaping operations throughout all three Treasure Coast counties.

The seasonal ban targets the summer months when heavy rainfall increases the risk of nutrient-rich runoff flowing into local waterways. These excess nutrients can trigger algae blooms that damage the environment and public health.

Jordan Skaggs, an environmental resources specialist with St. Lucie County, explained the reasoning behind the restriction. "It can shade out beneficial plants to fish and wildlife," Skaggs said. "At certain densities, harmful algae blooms can release toxins that can affect human health, wildlife health and overall ecosystem health."

The ban specifically protects sensitive waterways including the Indian River lagoon and St. Lucie River. During the rainy season, nutrient runoff from lawns and landscaping can wash into these ecosystems, accelerating algae growth and degrading water quality.

Residents and landscaping companies cannot apply nitrogen or phosphorus fertilizers to any lawns or landscaping during the four-month period. This applies regardless of whether the fertilizer is being used for residential gardens, commercial properties, or any other landscaping purpose.

Despite the restriction, property owners still have options for maintaining healthy landscapes during the summer months. Grantly Ricketts, director of the University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences St. Lucie County Extension, recommends composting as an effective alternative.

"It can be like a small trash can," Ricketts said. "That is the easiest and most practical way or means to add nutrients to your landscape."

Composting allows homeowners to create their own nutrient-rich soil amendment using kitchen scraps and yard waste. This approach maintains landscape health without introducing synthetic fertilizers into the environment. The composting method provides a sustainable solution for gardeners and landscapers who need to nourish their plants during the summer ban period.

The three-county restriction reflects growing efforts to protect Florida's waterways from nutrient pollution. By limiting fertilizer applications during the season when rainfall and stormwater runoff are heaviest, the counties aim to reduce algae blooms and preserve aquatic ecosystems.

Residents who need to maintain their landscaping during the ban should consult local extension services for approved methods and timing recommendations. The temporary restriction runs through September 30, when the rainy season typically begins to decrease.
