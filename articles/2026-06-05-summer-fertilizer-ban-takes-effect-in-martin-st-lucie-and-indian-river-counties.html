Fertilizer ban takes effect June 30 in Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties

Beginning June 30, residents across Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties must stop using fertilizers that contain phosphorus or nitrogen on their lawns and landscaping. The restriction is designed to protect local waterways during the rainy season when nutrient-rich runoff becomes a significant concern.

According to Jordan Skaggs with Environmental Resources in St. Lucie County, the ban targets a critical environmental problem. When fertilizers wash into waterways during heavy rains, they can trigger algae blooms that damage the Indian River Lagoon and St. Lucie River. These blooms create serious ecological consequences. "It can shade out beneficial plants to fish and wildlife," Skaggs explained. Beyond blocking sunlight, certain algae blooms at high concentrations produce toxins that threaten human health, wildlife populations and the broader ecosystem.

The rainy season creates ideal conditions for nutrient runoff to reach local waterways, making this the most effective time for the fertilizer restriction. County officials determined that limiting phosphorus and nitrogen during these months would reduce the amount of nutrients entering sensitive aquatic environments. This approach addresses the problem at its source by preventing excess nutrients from washing off treated lawns and landscaping during storms.

For residents concerned about maintaining their landscaping during the ban period, alternatives exist. Grantly Ricketts, director of the University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences St. Lucie County Extension, recommends composting as a practical solution. "It can be like a small trash can," Ricketts said, noting that composting represents "the easiest and most practical way or means to add nutrients to your landscape." This method allows homeowners to continue nourishing their plants and gardens using organic materials rather than commercial fertilizers containing the banned nutrients.
