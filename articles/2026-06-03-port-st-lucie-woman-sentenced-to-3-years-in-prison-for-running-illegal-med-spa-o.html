Port St. Lucie woman sentenced to 3 years in prison for running illegal med spa out of backyard shed

Rosa Mena, 50, of Port St. Lucie has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation after operating an unlicensed medical spa out of a shed behind her home on the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive. Mena ran the business, called Miracle Hand and Spa, performing Botox injections and other cosmetic procedures without a valid medical license.

A victim came forward to Port St. Lucie Police in May 2025 after experiencing partial facial paralysis following a session in which Mena administered 62 units of Botox. The victim had been referred to Mena by coworkers who had previously used her services. When the victim reported the paralysis, Mena refunded the $325 fee and asked her to return for another injection of vitamins to reverse the problem. The paralysis persisted, prompting the victim to request proof of Mena's medical credentials. Mena provided an altered phlebotomy certificate that had expired in February 2024. She also falsely claimed to be a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

A multi-agency investigation involving Port St. Lucie Police, PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax, and the Florida Department of Health led to a search warrant at Mena's residence. Detectives found medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines, and injection devices for fillers and Botox inside the backyard shed.

Mena was convicted on multiple charges including three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, fraud charges for obtaining property valued at $300 or more, and use of a two-way communication device to facilitate a felony. The case highlights the dangers of unlicensed cosmetic procedures and the importance of verifying medical credentials before receiving treatment.
