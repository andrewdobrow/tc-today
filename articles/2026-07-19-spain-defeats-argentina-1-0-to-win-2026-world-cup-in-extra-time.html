Spain Defeats Argentina 1-0 to Win 2026 World Cup in Extra Time

Spain defeated Argentina 1-0 to win the 2026 FIFA World Cup championship in a match that remained scoreless through regulation time. Ferran Torres scored the decisive goal in the 106th minute of extra time, pouncing on a bouncing ball in the box during the opening moment of the second 15-minute period. He used his left foot to bury a shot just under the crossbar and into the back of the net. The victory marks Spain's second World Cup championship and first since 2010.

Spain dominated possession and shot attempts throughout the match. The team took 15 shots in regulation compared to Argentina's zero and had nine corners to Argentina's one. Despite this overwhelming dominance, Lionel Messi and Argentina managed to hang on and force extra time.

Argentina goalkeeper Emiliano Martinez made 10 saves during the afternoon, the most ever in a World Cup final. His crucial saves kept Argentina in the match despite Spain's relentless attack.

The match turned in Spain's favor when Argentina's Enzo Fernandez received his second yellow card in second-half stoppage time, forcing Argentina to finish with only 10 players. Fernandez was carded for a reckless play that sent Spain defender Pau Cubarsi cartwheeling into the air, and the referee immediately pulled out the card. About two minutes after play resumed, another foul gave Lamine Yamal a chance to win it from about 22 yards out on a free kick.

Yamal's strike was curling toward the right post when Martinez dived to knock it away for his 10th save. He leaped to his feet, punching the air and imploring the crowd to get loud before the whistle sent the game to extra time.

With its victory, Spain became the first nation to simultaneously hold the World Cup crowns in both men's and women's soccer. The match was the 104th and last of the biggest World Cup ever, a 48-team event that played out over the United States, Canada and Mexico. Because there were more matches than any previous World Cup, it was no surprise that there were more goals than all other editions.

The tournament produced 308 goals in total. According to FIFA, the 2026 World Cup was officially the most attended ever.
