Palm Beach County schools earn third consecutive A rating from state

For the third consecutive year, the School District of Palm Beach County earned an "A" rating from the Florida Department of Education. This year, 143 district-operated schools, or 84 percent, received A or B ratings, up from 120 schools the previous year. The district achieved a record-setting 93.5 percent graduation rate, and district leaders reported that 86 percent of seniors participated in college-level coursework, including Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, Cambridge Advanced International Certificate of Education, or Dual Enrollment courses. Additionally, 144 recent graduates earned an associate degree alongside their high school diploma.

One of the district's biggest success stories came from S.D. Spady Elementary School in Delray Beach, which jumped from a C rating to an A this year. Principal Rona Tata, who has served 15 years as principal and five years as assistant principal, led the turnaround effort. Three years ago, staff began modifying the early childhood curriculum program, which starts at age 3 and combines with kindergarten. Many of those students are now in third grade and performing at higher levels. Spady Elementary was one of four district schools to improve from a C to an A rating this year, along with Dwight D. Eisenhower K-8, Glade View Elementary, and West Gate Elementary.
