Indian River County rental costs reach $1,222 for one-bedroom apartments

Indian River County residents face a Fair Market Rent of $1,222 for a one-bedroom apartment, according to 2026 data released by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development. This figure positions Indian River County in the mid-range of South Florida rental markets, higher than more affordable rural areas like Okeechobee County at $1,032 but substantially lower than premium markets in the region.

HUD's Fair Market Rent data shows significant price variations across South Florida counties. Belle Glade in Palm Beach County offers the lowest rates at $890 for a one-bedroom, while Wellington in Palm Beach County has the highest at $2,850. The data reflects current market conditions based on surveys, rental listings and market adjustments. These figures serve as benchmarks for housing assistance programs but do not impose rent control limits on landlords. St. Lucie and Martin areas fall between Indian River and Wellington, with rates of $1,467 for one-bedroom apartments.

Real estate professionals point to several factors driving these disparities. Talbot Sutter, president of Sutter and Nugent Real Estate, attributes Wellington's premium pricing to quality amenities and location advantages. "A lot of it has to do with school districts, especially Wellington. They have like top A-rated schools, lifestyle, they have so many aspects from equestrian to outdoor activities," Sutter said. He noted that demand for properties with more acreage and land has increased even in the rental market, particularly in wealthy enclaves like Wellington, western Lake Worth, Delray Beach and Boca Raton. Sutter's clients often include executives from New York City seeking luxury rentals before purchasing homes.

For renters navigating South Florida's challenging market, Sutter recommends conducting thorough research before committing to a lease. "Know the market, do your comps. You have to really, really know the area if you don't want to get ripped off paying rent," he said. Residents throughout the region report difficulty affording housing as rental prices continue climbing faster than income growth. The rental disparities highlight broader housing affordability challenges affecting South Florida communities across various income levels.
