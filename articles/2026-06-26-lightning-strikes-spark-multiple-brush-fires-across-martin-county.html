Lightning strike ignites one of two Martin County brush fires this week

Martin County experienced two brush fires this week, with lightning causing at least one of the blazes. Both fires are now fully contained and being monitored by the Florida Forest Service.

David Grubitch, a mitigation specialist and spokesperson with the department, said this is their peak season for such fires. He explained that the summertime heat is drying out fine dead fuels, making them receptive and ready to ignite. When lightning strikes these dried fuels, fires start quickly. Grubitch said rain often extinguishes these fires immediately, but sometimes they travel underground and surface days later.

The Florida Forest Service says the conditions that fuel these fires are not going away anytime soon, but residents should not be alarmed. The combination of summer heat and dry vegetation creates an environment where fires can easily ignite, particularly during thunderstorms when lightning strikes are common. While the two fires this week have been contained, officials continue to monitor the situation as weather conditions remain favorable for additional brush fires.

Grubitch said the public plays a crucial role in helping the Florida Forest Service do its job. He stressed that residents should never assume someone else has already called in a brush fire, saying that if you see something, say something, as it can make all the difference in how quickly crews are able to get a fire under control. Quick reporting allows firefighters to respond faster and prevent small fires from spreading into larger, more dangerous blazes that threaten homes and property.
