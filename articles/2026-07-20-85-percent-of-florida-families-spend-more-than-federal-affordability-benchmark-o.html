Florida families spend nearly 17 percent of income on childcare, far exceeding affordability benchmark

The cost of childcare in Florida far exceeds federal affordability standards, with families spending a median of 16.8 percent of their income on care, according to a Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta report. The study found 85.4 percent of Florida families are estimated to spend more than 7 percent of their income on childcare, the affordability benchmark established by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. The estimated weekly costs for the care of infants and toddlers and preschool-aged children in Florida are $268 and $208, respectively.

Childcare prices increased by 29 percent from 2020 to 2024, compared with a 22 percent rise in overall prices, according to the Atlanta Fed. The report said childcare costs now often far exceed the federal affordability benchmark. The study examined childcare costs in Georgia and Florida and found the median percentage of income families would need to pay for childcare is 13.9 percent across both states. The costs are putting significant financial pressure on parents and forcing difficult decisions on work hours and even leaving jobs, the study said.
