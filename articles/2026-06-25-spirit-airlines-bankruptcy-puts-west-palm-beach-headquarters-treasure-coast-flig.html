West Palm Beach entrepreneur bids to buy Spirit Airlines, rebrand as Florida Air Express

LeRoy Gillead II, a Treasure Coast entrepreneur, has submitted a proposal to purchase Spirit Airlines in its entirety, rehire its workforce, and relaunch the company as Florida Air Express, a low-cost carrier headquartered in West Palm Beach. According to court documents filed during Spirit's bankruptcy proceedings, Gillead is seeking to raise approximately $250 million from private investors to fund the acquisition. The deadline for all bids is scheduled for July.

The bankruptcy sale includes Spirit's valuable assets such as aircraft, airport gates, and potentially its headquarters building. Clint Henderson, managing editor of news at The Points Guy travel website, expressed doubt about whether Gillead's plan will come to fruition. Henderson believes it is far more probable that Spirit's assets will be distributed to the highest bidders, which are likely to be major established airlines rather than a new startup carrier. "This seems like a pie in the sky thing. If they can get the money together, maybe they'll establish a new low-cost carrier, but right now they don't fly any routes at all," Henderson said.

The disappearance of Spirit Airlines marks a significant shift in the budget travel market, leaving fewer options for cost-conscious passengers. Many travelers relied on Spirit for affordable fares during a time when travel expenses continue to rise. Henderson noted that the low-cost carrier model faces serious challenges in the United States, with existing budget airlines struggling to maintain profitability. Currently, only Frontier and Allegiant remain operating as low-cost carriers in the industry.

For consumers seeking affordable airfare following Spirit's collapse, Henderson recommends a flexible approach rather than airline loyalty. "Be airline agnostic; that's the best advice I have for consumers," Henderson said. "Go with the cheapest airline you can find. Use Google Flights and don't try to be loyal to one airline." This strategy requires travelers to invest additional time comparing prices across carriers but increases the likelihood of finding the best available fares. The Spirit Airlines bankruptcy signals broader challenges facing budget air travel in the United States, and the eventual fate of its distinctive yellow aircraft remains uncertain as they await potential repainting in new company colors.
