Treasure Coast entrepreneur proposes buying Spirit Airlines, basing Florida Air Express in West Palm Beach

A Treasure Coast entrepreneur has put forward a proposal to purchase Spirit Airlines out of bankruptcy and relaunch it as Florida Air Express, a low-cost carrier based in West Palm Beach. LeRoy Gillead II's plan includes rehiring Spirit's workers and keeping the airline operational. According to court filings, Gillead is seeking to raise up to $250 million from private investors to make the purchase. The deadline for bids on Spirit's assets is scheduled for July.

Spirit Airlines is currently in bankruptcy court selling off all of its assets, including aircraft, airport gates, and potentially its headquarters. These components represent significant value in the aviation industry. However, industry experts question whether Gillead's proposal will succeed in the competitive bidding process. Clint Henderson, managing editor of news at The Points Guy travel website, described the plan as "pie in the sky" and predicted a different outcome. "It would be much more likely in the end that you'll see the assets of Spirit divvied out to the highest bidder, and that's probably going to be major airlines," Henderson said.

The collapse of Spirit Airlines marks another loss for budget air travel in the United States. Many travelers relied on the airline because of its affordability at a time when travel costs continue to rise. Henderson noted that the low-cost carrier model is struggling across the industry. "I think the low-cost carrier model is kind of busted in the U.S. All the low-cost carriers really struggle to make money," he said. While Frontier and Allegiant remain in the low-cost category, these carriers face ongoing challenges in maintaining profitability.

For consumers seeking affordable airfare, Henderson recommends taking a different approach to finding deals. Rather than remaining loyal to a single airline, travelers should compare prices across all available options. "Be airline agnostic; that's the best advice I have for consumers. Go with the cheapest airline you can find. Use Google Flights and don't try to be loyal to one airline," he advised. As for Spirit's distinctive yellow aircraft, they will likely be repainted and rebranded by whatever airline or company eventually acquires them.
