U.S. men's soccer team faces Belgium in World Cup Round of 16 without suspended forward

The U.S. men's national team earned the program's first knockout stage victory since 2002 by dispatching Bosnia and Herzegovina 2-0 on Wednesday night, though the Americans played the final 26 minutes plus stoppage time with 10 men after forward Folarin Balogun was red-carded. Longtime referee Andy Davies acknowledged the decision was wrong, telling ESPN the contact was purely accidental and an unfortunate result from two players challenging for possession in a normal football movement. Coach Mauricio Pochettino said flatly it was never a red card, and the American camp noted that Lionel Messi made a nearly identical challenge against Algeria earlier in the tournament and received nothing.

Balogun, one of the team's top players, will watch Monday from the stands against Belgium at Lumen Field in Seattle. Belgium earned a Round of 16 win over the United States in the 2014 World Cup and has won six consecutive meetings against the U.S., including a 5-2 friendly rout in March, since the inaugural 1930 World Cup when the Americans recorded their lone victory in the head-to-head series. Despite the suspension and historical disadvantage, the U.S. demonstrated resilience by winning Wednesday without Balogun for the final half-hour. The team is well-coached and physically capable, playing at a venue where the home crowd will be as loud as any in the tournament.
