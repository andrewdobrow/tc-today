United States faces Belgium in World Cup Round of 16 with Florida fans watching

The United States men's national team takes the field Monday night against Belgium in the Round of 16 at Lumen Field in Seattle. The Americans earned the program's first knockout stage victory since 2002, having dispatched Bosnia and Herzegovina 2-0 on Wednesday night and playing the final 26 minutes plus stoppage time of that match with 10 men after Folarin Balogun was red-carded on what the American camp considered a maddening call. Longtime referee Andy Davies called the decision wrong, telling ESPN the contact was purely accidental and an unfortunate result from two players challenging for possession of the ball in a normal football movement. Coach Mauricio Pochettino said flatly it was never a red card, and the American camp was quick to note that Lionel Messi made a nearly identical challenge against Algeria earlier in the tournament and received nothing. Weston McKennie spoke for the locker room, saying the call was a bit bogus and adding that it is bogus.

Balogun, one of the team's top players, will watch Monday from the stands against a Belgian side that earned a Round of 16 win over the United States in the 2014 World Cup. Belgium has won six consecutive meetings against the U.S., including a 5-2 friendly rout in March, since the inaugural 1930 World Cup, the lone American victory in the head-to-head. The team is well-coached, physically capable, and playing at a venue where the home crowd will be as loud as any in the tournament.
