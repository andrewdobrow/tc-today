Fight to save rare deep-sea coral reef off Fort Pierce heads to final federal decision

Dozens of community members gathered at Blake Library in Martin County after a federally appointed council voted to roll back longstanding protections for the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef located just off the coast of Fort Pierce. The reef, found nowhere else on Earth, thrives in deep, dark waters hundreds of feet below the surface and is home to more than 2,000 marine species.

Two proposals would reopen parts of the reef to shrimp trawling, even though scientists say trawling destroyed as much as 90% of the reef before federal protections were put in place decades ago. Dr. Grant Gilmore, a scientist who previously dived the reef, warned that removing the habitat and food source the coral provides would eliminate the fish that depend on it. Mark Perry, executive director of the Florida Oceanographic Society, and other scientists urged the public to help save the reef, noting that corals worldwide are in trouble.

The proposals followed a Trump executive order issued in April 2025 that tasked eight regional councils nationwide to find ways to reinvigorate fishing industries. Bob Zales, executive director of the Southeastern Fishing Association and a generational shrimper, told reporters he believes reopening shrimping access to the Oculina Bank is essential for the struggling rock shrimp industry. Council data shows the rock shrimp industry has plummeted from over 700 trips per year in the mid-1990s to under 100 since 2010, with the number of active rock shrimpers dropping from 144 in 1996 to less than 30, with just 12 active boats in 2018.

Zales attributes the decline to high costs, competition from imported shrimp and closures of previously open fishing areas. But scientists warned reopening the reef could irreparably damage what little remains of the rare ecosystem. The federal agency is now preparing a final ruling on the fate of the Oculina Bank as the debate continues between conservation and industry interests.
