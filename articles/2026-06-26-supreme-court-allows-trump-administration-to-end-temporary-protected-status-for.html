Palm Beach County Haitians face deportation after Supreme Court eliminates protected status

The Supreme Court has sided with President Donald Trump's administration in eliminating Temporary Protected Status for thousands of people from Haiti and Syria. This decision puts hundreds of thousands at immediate risk of deportation, with significant impacts on South Florida. Temporary Protected Status allows foreign citizens already in the United States to remain, work, and support their families while their home countries experience crises such as natural disasters, political unrest, or violence. For Haitians and Syrians, this protection is now ending. The ruling affects approximately 350,000 Haitians and 6,000 Syrians nationwide.

Florida is home to the largest Haitian immigrant population in the United States. Within the state, Palm Beach County ranks third with 68,400 Haitian immigrants, behind Miami-Dade County with 78,800 and Broward County with 86,100. Attorney Byrnes Guillaume, who is Haitian and represents many people facing loss of TPS, explains the practical consequences. "It means that they have no status," Guillaume said. "It means that if you have a job you no longer are able to work. It means that you were once legal and now you have to go in the shadow." He raises concerns about families with American-born children: "For people with American-born kids, what you going to do, are you going to leave the kids here or are you going to take them to Haiti, a country they've never known? I do think lives will be at risk."

Stecko Dulcio, who owns a Haitian restaurant in Riviera Beach where he has served cuisine for five and a half years, became a U.S. citizen and considers himself fortunate to have security. He knows many friends with TPS and worries about their prospects. "That's going to be a disaster," Dulcio said. "Not only are their lives in danger, and even if they go they don't get killed, but they're going to have a miserable life, because there's no work, no job, and they lose everything they have." Dulcio notes that gangs have taken control in Haiti, seizing houses, controlling transportation, and perpetuating violence.

The Trump administration argues that TPS was designed as temporary relief during emergencies and that conditions in Haiti and Syria have sufficiently improved to no longer warrant the protection. Advocates strongly dispute this assessment. The Department of Homeland Security maintains it has sole discretion to determine when protections should end. Guillaume said no timeline for deportations has been established. Since January of last year, DHS has ended protections for people from more than 13 countries. Dulcio urges action: "Pray to the government of the United States to do something to help those people."
