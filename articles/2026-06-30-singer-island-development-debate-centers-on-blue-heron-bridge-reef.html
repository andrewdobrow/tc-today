Singer Island development proposal raises concerns over world-renowned Blue Heron Bridge reef

The Continuum Company, a New York-based developer, has proposed Singer Island Gateway, a mixed-use project valued at approximately $14 million at 2525 Lake Drive. The development would feature hundreds of residential units, commercial and public space, and a marina near the base of Blue Heron Bridge at the entrance to Singer Island in Palm Beach County. The main structure would rise 25 stories above the waterfront location.

The project has drawn attention because it would be situated above Phil Foster Park, an underwater ecosystem that attracts divers and snorkelers from around the world. Visitors to the area describe the marine environment in glowing terms. "A few stingrays, some like rainbow fish," one visitor noted. Another described "blue and yellow fish, and some pink coral," while a third mentioned seeing "yellow coral, which I've never seen before, and striped fish." One diver called it simply "beautiful, amazing." The reef's global reputation stems from the unique combination of marine life found in these shallow waters beneath the bridge.

Longtime resident Gary Heiser, who grew up in the area, opposes the development based on both its scale and potential environmental impact. He believes the 25-story tower is inappropriate for the location, saying "It's way too drastic for this location. Twenty-five stories would be okay in Chicago, but it doesn't work right here." Beyond aesthetic concerns, Heiser worries that construction activity, shade from the tall building, and the marina could harm the marine ecosystem. He expressed skepticism about the developer's environmental conclusions, stating "I find it hard to believe that there'd be no impact. I would like to see more research."

The developer has published an environmental study on its website in which a geoscientist concluded the project would have no impact on marine life in the Lake Worth Lagoon, with the ecosystem remaining unchanged. Not all area visitors share Heiser's concerns. One diver indicated his position would depend on whether the project actually threatens the reef, saying "If it doesn't disturb the reef, I don't see a problem with it."

The project has not yet received approval. The developer was contacted for additional comment, with a response pending. Meanwhile, residents have organized a petition against the proposal.
