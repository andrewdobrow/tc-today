Singer Island 25-story development proposal raises concerns over world-renowned Blue Heron Bridge reef

A New York-based developer, The Continuum Company, has proposed Singer Island Gateway, an approximately $14 million mixed-use development at 2525 Lake Drive in Palm Beach County. The project would include hundreds of residential units, commercial and public space, and a marina, with the main structure rising 25 stories at the entrance to Singer Island. The location places the development directly above the underwater reef at Phil Foster Park near Blue Heron Bridge, a destination that attracts divers and snorkelers from around the world.

The reef draws visitors for its remarkable marine ecosystem. People who visit the area describe seeing stingrays, rainbow-colored fish, blue and yellow fish, pink coral, yellow coral, and striped fish in the shallow waters. Louis Espinosa, a diver who frequents the location, described it simply as "beautiful, amazing" and called it one of the best spots he has visited. The combination of accessible shallow water and diverse sea life has made this area renowned among diving enthusiasts globally.

However, longtime resident Gary Heiser, who grew up in the area, has expressed serious reservations about the project's scale and placement. He believes the 25-story tower is inappropriate for the location, noting that while such a structure might work in cities like Chicago, it does not fit here. Beyond aesthetic concerns, Heiser worries that construction activities, the shade cast by the tall building, and the marina could harm the marine life below. "I don't think it's a good idea that this treasure gets impacted to such a great extent," he said. An environmental study posted on the developer's website presents a contrasting view. A geoscientist concluded that the development would have no impact on marine life in the Lake Worth Lagoon, with the ecosystem remaining unchanged. Heiser remains skeptical of these findings, stating he would like to see additional research before accepting such conclusions.

Opinions among reef visitors diverge on the matter. While Heiser opposes the project based on potential environmental damage, Espinosa indicated his position depends on whether there is genuine evidence of harm to the reef. "If it doesn't disturb the reef, I don't see a problem with it," Espinosa said. The proposal has not yet been approved. The developer has been contacted for comment, with a response still pending. Meanwhile, residents have launched a petition opposing the Singer Island Gateway project.
