Byron Donalds adds $688,000 in one week while GOP rivals struggle to raise campaign cash

With about 50 days remaining in the Republican primary to replace Ron DeSantis, Byron Donalds continues to vastly outpace his competition in fundraising. The gubernatorial front-runner added nearly $253,000 to his campaign account and $435,000 to his Friends of Byron Donalds political committee during the week ending June 19. This latest haul brings his total cash on hand to nearly $66 million, cementing his commanding position in the race. He has already booked a $20 million media buy for upcoming weeks, and even after spending that amount, he will maintain a substantial financial advantage over the other candidates in the field.

The contrast between Donalds and his opponents is stark. James Fishback raised only $4,825 to his campaign account and nothing to his Florida First political committee during the reporting period. As of June 19, Fishback had approximately $117,000 available to spend. During that same week, he spent more than $60,000 from his committee alone, including a payment to his running mate Sean Lozano. Jay Collins fared somewhat better, bringing in just over $11,000 to his campaign account and $1,500 to his political committee. However, Collins is burning through his resources quickly, with nearly $2 million from his committee going toward an ad buy in a single week. He currently has roughly $3.5 million available for the remainder of the race.

Former House Speaker Paul Renner continues to face serious fundraising challenges. He raised less than $30,000 to his campaign account for the second consecutive reporting period, along with $13,000 to his political committee. Renner has approximately $2.5 million to spend as of the June 19 closing date. Despite these modest figures, his resources still exceed those of Fishback, though he lags far behind both Collins and Donalds in available funds.

The candidates will not participate in a debate at Saturday's Florida GOP Sunshine State Showdown. Donalds and his allies contend that debates are unnecessary given the current state of the race. They argue that donors are unwilling to spend significant resources against what they describe as an inevitable nominee. While DeSantis and some members of the broader field have expressed interest in a debate format, it will not occur at this event. The fundraising numbers suggest that Donalds's assessment may have merit, as rival campaigns struggle to generate the financial support needed to mount a serious challenge to the front-runner.
