Byron Donalds extends commanding lead in Florida governor's race with $66 million war chest

Byron Donalds continues to pull away from his Republican rivals in the race to replace Ron DeSantis as Florida governor. The Naples congressman raised nearly $253,000 for his campaign account and $435,000 for his political committee, Friends of Byron Donalds, during the week ending June 19. With just over 50 days until the primary election, Donalds has accumulated nearly $66 million in available funds. Though a $20 million media buy will reduce his cash on hand in the next reporting period, his financial advantage over the rest of the field remains insurmountable.

The fundraising gap between Donalds and his opponents has grown into a decisive chasm. James Fishback raised only $4,825 to his campaign account and nothing to his Florida First political committee during the same week, leaving him with roughly $117,000 to spend. Notably, Fishback spent more than $60,000 from his committee during the period, including a $5,000 payment to his running mate Sean Lozano. Jay Collins raised just over $11,000 to his campaign account and $1,500 to his political committee, maintaining roughly $3.5 million in available funds. However, nearly $2 million of his committee money went toward an ad buy in the week. Former House Speaker Paul Renner has struggled with fundraising for two consecutive periods, raising less than $30,000 to his campaign account and $13,000 to his political committee. He has roughly $2.5 million to spend.

The weakness across the broader field reflects donor sentiment about the race's direction. The Republican Party of Florida declined to hold a gubernatorial debate at Saturday's Sunshine State Showdown, a decision that drew criticism from trailing candidates seeking opportunities to directly challenge Donalds. Donalds and his allies have argued that a debate is unnecessary given the lack of viable opposition in the race. The latest fundraising reports support this position, demonstrating that donors have not responded to pleas from other candidates to fund a competitive primary.

As Florida Republicans prepare to select their nominee, the contest appears increasingly decided. Donalds' financial dominance combined with his opponents' inability to gain traction with contributors suggests the primary outcome is largely predetermined. Without a debate platform to gain exposure and facing steep funding disadvantages, the trailing candidates appear unable to mount a meaningful challenge to the frontrunner in the remaining weeks of the campaign.
