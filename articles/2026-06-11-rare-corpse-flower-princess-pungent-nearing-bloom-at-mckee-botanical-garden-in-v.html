Rare corpse flower 'Princess Pungent' nearing bloom at McKee Botanical Garden in Vero Beach

A Titan Arum, commonly called the corpse flower, is expected to bloom soon at McKee Botanical Garden in Vero Beach. According to updates from the garden's Instagram account, the rare plant could flower within the next few days or weeks. When it does bloom, visitors will experience one of nature's most distinctive and unpleasant phenomena: the flower emits a powerful stench resembling rotting flesh. The bloom itself will be short-lived, typically lasting only 24 to 48 hours before the flower closes.

The Titan Arum originates from the rainforests of Sumatra and Indonesia, where it faces serious threats in its natural habitat. The species is considered endangered, making specimens like the one at McKee particularly valuable for conservation efforts and scientific study. The botanical garden received this individual plant in May 2026 and has been carefully monitoring its development ever since. The garden has affectionately nicknamed the flower "Princess Pungent" and has kept followers updated through social media posts documenting the plant's progress.

Recent signs suggest the bloom may arrive very soon. According to a Thursday update from McKee Botanical Garden, the flower has grown nine inches taller in recent days. Additionally, one of the plant's three remaining leaves has dropped off, which the garden identifies as an important indicator that blooming time is approaching. These physical changes typically precede the dramatic flowering event that makes corpse flowers so remarkable and sought-after by botanists and curious visitors alike.

The timing could work in visitors' favor. McKee Botanical Garden's annual Waterlily Celebration is scheduled for June 13, which falls within the window when Princess Pungent might bloom. This means guests attending the celebration could potentially witness the full flowering display and experience the distinctive odor firsthand. For those interested in following the plant's progress, McKee Botanical Garden continues to post regular updates on Instagram, allowing people to track when the rare bloom might occur. Whether the flower cooperates with the celebration's timing remains to be seen, but the garden's dedicated monitoring ensures that when bloom time arrives, news will spread quickly to those eager to witness this unusual botanical event.
