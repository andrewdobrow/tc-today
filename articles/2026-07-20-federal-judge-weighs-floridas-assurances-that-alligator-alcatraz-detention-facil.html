Federal judge weighs Florida's assurances that Alligator Alcatraz detention facility won't reopen

A federal judge heard arguments Monday about whether Florida's closure of the controversial Alligator Alcatraz immigration detention facility in the Everglades is permanent. Aerial photos filed in court show the remote airstrip where the facility sat for nearly a year is now empty, with only a few trucks scattered around where a sprawling complex of trailers, tents, generators, and lights once stood. Florida Division of Emergency Management officials said in sworn declarations that the images were captured over the weekend.

Immigration attorneys and people who had been detained at Alligator Alcatraz sued the state and federal government, arguing their civil rights were violated because of inadequate attorney-client access at the facility. The state and federal government now say the case is moot because the facility is closed. Nicholas Meros, an attorney representing Florida, told the judge there is no reasonable likelihood that the facility will reopen, though he said he could not speak for future administrations. The state used emergency powers to take the land for Alligator Alcatraz from Miami-Dade County, which confirmed Monday afternoon it has not gotten the land back.

Amy Godshall, an ACLU attorney representing the plaintiffs, argued that the photos and declarations were not enough assurance that immigrant detainees would not someday return to the Everglades. Godshall noted that when they first opened the facility, they set it up in 10 days, the state still controls the land, and they haven't given the land back. She also pointed to what she calls a lack of transparency regarding the circumstances under which Alligator Alcatraz closed.

While the state initially said in June that it was relocating the detainees for hurricane season, Gov. Ron DeSantis also made public statements that Alligator Alcatraz had served its intended purpose of adding bed space for immigration detainees.

The closure also came amid reports that the state had spent more than $1 billion on the facility and has not been reimbursed by the federal government. DeSantis announced in October that FEMA had approved a $608 million grant for Alligator Alcatraz, but court filings in a separate federal lawsuit revealed that FEMA had not disbursed that funding because of ongoing litigation.

Meros denied in court Monday that the facility had closed for financial reasons or to avoid discovery in court. An ICE spokesperson told WPTV on Monday that the agency is pursuing all available options to expand bedspace capacity, including partnering with states and local governments and private sector partners to house detainees at existing facilities.
