Federal judge weighs Florida's assurances that Alligator Alcatraz immigration facility won't reopen

A federal judge questioned Monday whether Florida can guarantee that the Alligator Alcatraz immigration detention facility will remain permanently closed after aerial photos showed the remote Everglades site completely dismantled. The images, filed in court by Florida Division of Emergency Management officials and captured over the weekend, show only an empty airstrip with a few scattered trucks where a sprawling complex of trailers, tents, generators, and lights stood for nearly a year. An attorney representing Florida told the judge that if the photos are not clear evidence of permanent closure, he did not know what would be.

The legal proceedings stem from a civil rights lawsuit filed by immigration attorneys and former detainees who argued they faced inadequate attorney-client access at the facility. Both state and federal officials now contend the case is moot because the facility has closed. Nicholas Meros, an attorney representing Florida, told the court during a Monday afternoon status conference there is no reasonable likelihood the facility will reopen.

However, ACLU attorney Amy Godshall argued the photos and sworn declarations provide insufficient assurance, noting the state set up the original facility in 10 days and still controls the land. The Miami-Dade Mayor's Office confirmed Monday afternoon it has not received the land back, which the state took using emergency powers.

The closure came after the state spent more than $1 billion on the facility without receiving federal reimbursement. Governor Ron DeSantis announced in October that FEMA had approved a $608 million grant for Alligator Alcatraz, but court filings in a separate federal lawsuit revealed FEMA never disbursed the funding due to ongoing litigation. FEMA has not answered direct questions about whether it will reimburse the Florida Division of Emergency Management.

A FEMA spokesperson said the agency reviews reimbursement requests to ensure costs are allowable and validated before releasing funding. FDEM did not respond to a request for comment.

An ICE spokesperson told reporters Monday the agency continues pursuing all available options to expand bed space capacity, including partnerships with states, local governments, and private sector partners. The state initially said in June it was relocating detainees for hurricane season, though DeSantis also stated publicly that Alligator Alcatraz had served its intended purpose of adding bed space for immigration detainees. Meros denied in court that the facility closed for financial reasons or to avoid discovery in the lawsuit, saying the state has no intention to reopen it while acknowledging he could not speak for future administrations.
