Russian national linked to Delray Beach Medicare fraud company charged in second billing scheme

A Russian national connected to a Delray Beach durable medical equipment company accused of Medicare fraud allegedly continued billing the federal health program through a new business after the original company lost its payments. Nika Machutadze was charged with conspiracy to commit money laundering in January 2026 and arrested in Texas the following month. He remains in jail while federal prosecutors in West Palm Beach move forward with the case. Business records for both Sunshine Senior Solutions and Centurion Superior Medical list Machutadze as an owner.

Mike and Connie Parrett were among dozens of Medicare recipients who reported fraudulent billing after Sunshine Senior Solutions charged Medicare for medical equipment Connie said she never needed or received. "I hadn't even been to a doctor for anything," Connie said about the charges. More than a year later, the Parretts received a new Medicare statement at their Ohio home, this time in Mike's name, from Centurion Superior Medical, a company based in Austin, Texas. "But it's the same kind of thing. It's billing catheters, and actually there were six different bills," Mike said. Federal investigators have connected Centurion to the same operators behind Sunshine Senior Solutions.

A criminal complaint alleges Centurion billed Medicare in 2025 after federal authorities suspended payments to Sunshine Senior Solutions in late 2024. Two of the six charges from Centurion on Mike Parrett's statement were denied, though all charges occurred during 2025. Experts warn that victims of one fraud scheme often become targets for additional schemes. "As we've seen in this case, the criminals work with lists of identities and oftentimes they will just share the identities with other fraudsters," said Dr. David Maimon, a cyberfraud expert who studied Sunshine Senior Solutions.

Maimon recommends that potential victims freeze their credit and consider purchasing identity protection plans. He also pointed to the Justice Department's new National Fraud Enforcement Division, which investigates Medicare fraud cases. "This is all suggesting my opinion that we definitely take the issue of fraud more seriously," Maimon said. The repeated targeting of the same individuals demonstrates the ongoing challenge Medicare fraud poses to vulnerable seniors and the importance of government oversight in protecting beneficiaries.
