St. Lucie County implements 45-day anchoring limits in Indian River Lagoon to combat abandoned vessels

St. Lucie County established new anchoring restrictions in the Indian River Lagoon and Fort Pierce Harbor near the South Causeway Bridge starting June 1. The regulations create anchor limitation areas where boat owners cannot keep vessels anchored for more than 45 consecutive days within any six-month period. Anyone wishing to anchor longer must leave all designated anchoring limitation areas for at least 24 hours before returning.

The county took this action in response to a growing problem with abandoned vessels in local waterways. During the previous year, the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission removed approximately two dozen derelict vessels from the area. According to Erick Gill with St. Lucie County, these restrictions will improve navigation in the waterways. "We need to make sure that highway for vessels is clear and free for other vessels to come and go," Gill said. The new rules aim to keep channels open and accessible for legitimate boating traffic.

Abandoned vessels pose significant environmental risks to the lagoon's ecosystem. James Beams with St. Lucie County Port, Inlet and Beaches explained that when vessels remain in the water for extended periods, they threaten water quality. Abandoned boats contain diesel tanks, oil tanks, and holding tanks that can leak pollutants into the surrounding water. Gill emphasized that the anchoring limitations will help prevent these contaminants from contaminating the lagoon. The regulations represent a proactive approach to protecting the waterway from environmental damage caused by derelict vessels.

The Indian River Lagoon supports an exceptionally diverse ecosystem with over 4,000 species of animals, plants, and birds. Gill stressed the importance of protecting this valuable natural resource. "We want to make sure we're protecting it," he said. The new anchoring restrictions serve as one tool in the county's broader effort to maintain the health and navigability of these important waterways. Boaters interested in the specific boundaries of the anchor limitation areas can access a site map online through St. Lucie County's official resources.
