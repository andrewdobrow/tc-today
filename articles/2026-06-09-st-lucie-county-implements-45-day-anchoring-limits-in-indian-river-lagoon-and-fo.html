St. Lucie County enacts 45-day anchoring limits in Indian River Lagoon to curb abandoned vessels

St. Lucie County put new anchoring rules into effect on June 1 to address the growing problem of abandoned vessels in local waterways. The county designated two anchor limitation areas in the Indian River Lagoon and Fort Pierce Harbor near the South Causeway Bridge. Under these new restrictions, boaters cannot anchor a vessel for more than 45 consecutive days within any six-month period without leaving all anchoring limitation areas for at least 24 hours. This requirement forces temporary departures that help prevent vessels from becoming permanently abandoned in the water.

The county has experienced an increase in derelict vessels over recent years. According to Erick Gill with St. Lucie County, the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission collected around two dozen abandoned vessels last year alone. Gill explained that the new anchoring limitations will improve navigation in these waterways. "We need to make sure that highway for vessels is clear and free for other vessels to come and go," he said. By preventing vessels from anchoring indefinitely, the county aims to keep channels and anchorages accessible for legitimate boating traffic.

Environmental protection is another key concern driving the new restrictions. James Beams with St.Lucie County Port, Inlet and Beaches noted that long-abandoned vessels pose serious risks to water quality. "There's a lot of environmental concern with the diesel and oil tanks and then holding tanks," Beams said. When derelict vessels remain in the water for extended periods, these fuel and waste systems can leak or fail, contaminating the lagoon. The new 45-day limit helps prevent this pollution from entering the water and damaging the ecosystem.

The Indian River Lagoon supports an extraordinarily diverse ecosystem. Gill emphasized that over 4,000 species of animals, plants, and birds depend on this waterway. By limiting how long vessels can anchor without leaving, the county is taking action to protect this sensitive environment from the pollutants and debris associated with abandoned boats. Boaters who want to anchor in these areas should be aware of the new rules and plan their trips accordingly. A site map showing the exact locations of the two anchor limitation areas is available for reference.
