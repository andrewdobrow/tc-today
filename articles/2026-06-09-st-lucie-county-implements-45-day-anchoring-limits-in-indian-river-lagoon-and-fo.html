St. Lucie County implements 45-day anchoring limits to combat abandoned vessels

St. Lucie County established new anchoring restrictions effective June 1 to address the growing problem of abandoned vessels in local waterways. The county designated two anchor limitation areas in the Indian River Lagoon and Fort Pierce Harbor near the South Causeway Bridge. Under these new rules, boaters cannot anchor a vessel for more than 45 consecutive days within any six-month period without departing all anchoring limitation areas for at least 24 hours.

The abandoned vessel problem has become increasingly visible in St. Lucie County's waterways. According to Erick Gill with St. Lucie County, the Florida Wildlife Commission collected approximately two dozen derelict vessels during the previous year. The new anchoring restrictions aim to keep the waterways clear and navigable for legitimate vessel traffic. "We need to make sure that highway for vessels is clear and free for other vessels to come and go," Gill said. By limiting how long vessels can remain anchored, the county hopes to prevent boats from being left in place indefinitely.

Environmental protection serves as another key motivation for the restrictions. James Beams with St. Lucie County Port, Inlet and Beaches explained that abandoned vessels pose significant environmental risks when left unattended for extended periods. Boats sitting in the water can leak diesel fuel, oil, and other contents from holding tanks, threatening water quality in the lagoon. These pollutants can harm the diverse ecosystem that depends on clean water. Gill emphasized that preventing these contaminants from entering the water is essential for protecting the lagoon's inhabitants. The area is home to over 4,000 species of animals, plants, and birds that rely on this aquatic environment.

To help boaters understand where these restrictions apply, St. Lucie County has published a detailed site map showing the anchor limitation areas. Boaters operating in the Indian River Lagoon and Fort Pierce Harbor should review this map to ensure compliance with the new regulations. The restrictions represent the county's effort to balance the needs of boaters who use the waterways with the imperative to protect the environment and maintain navigable channels for all water traffic.
