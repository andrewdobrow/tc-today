Breast cancer patient denied reconstruction surgery coverage hours before procedure

Beth Clark, 48, was diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma three months ago and chose to undergo a full double bilateral mastectomy with reconstruction. Less than 24 hours before her reconstruction surgery, her insurance company, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, denied coverage for the procedure. According to the denial letter, the surgery involved a surgical mesh called GalaFlex, which the insurer considered "investigational" with "no or not enough proof it improves health." In a 2023 letter to health care providers, the FDA stated that GalaFlex's "efficacy and safety for breast cancer patients have not been determined." Anthem responded to Clark's case by saying the mesh "does not currently meet the standards required for coverage by our medical policy" without FDA approval, though the company noted Clark retained the right to appeal the decision.

Dr. Alicia Billington, Clark's plastic surgeon, uses GalaFlex mesh on most of her breast reconstruction patients and says the product is less risky than other options with proven success. Despite Anthem's denial, Clark received her reconstruction surgery with GalaFlex mesh a little more than a week ago. Last year, after investigative reporting shared breast cancer patients' stories of denial, Cigna became the first and only major insurer to start approving GalaFlex for breast reconstruction. A new bill has been introduced to modernize the Women's Health and Cancer Rights Act to protect breast cancer patients from being denied treatments during their cancer journeys.
