Anthem Blue Cross denies breast cancer patient's reconstruction surgery hours before procedure

Beth Clark, 48, was diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma three months ago and underwent a full double bilateral mastectomy. Less than 24 hours before her reconstruction surgery, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield denied coverage for the procedure. According to her denial letter, the surgery involving a surgical mesh called GalaFlex is considered "investigational" and "there is no or not enough proof it improves health." While GalaFlex is FDA-approved for some uses in the body, it is not approved for breast reconstruction and remains an off-label, investigational, and experimental treatment. In a 2023 letter to health care providers, the FDA stated that the treatment's "efficacy and safety for breast cancer patients have not been determined."

Dr. Alicia Billington, Clark's plastic surgeon, uses GalaFlex mesh on most of her breast reconstruction patients and argues that insurance companies should reconsider their coverage policies. She says the product produces fewer complications than other options with proven success. Despite the denial, Clark received her reconstruction surgery with the GalaFlex mesh a little more than a week ago after Billington found a mechanism to make it happen. Anthem responded by stating it regularly reviews coverage policies to ensure they are based on medical evidence and that without FDA approval, the mesh "does not currently meet the standards required for coverage by our medical policy."
