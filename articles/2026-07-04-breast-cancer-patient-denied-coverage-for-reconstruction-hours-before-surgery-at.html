Breast cancer patient speaks out after Anthem Blue Cross denies reconstruction surgery hours before procedure

Beth Clark was diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma three months ago and chose a full double bilateral mastectomy with reconstruction. Less than 24 hours before her reconstruction surgery, her insurance company Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield denied coverage for the procedure involving GalaFlex surgical mesh, citing in her denial letter that the surgery is considered "investigational" and "there is no or not enough proof it improves health." GalaFlex is a synthetic surgical mesh approved by the FDA for some uses in the body but not approved for breast reconstruction. In a 2023 letter to health care providers, the FDA stated its "efficacy and safety for breast cancer patients have not been determined."

Dr. Alicia Billington, Clark's plastic surgeon, uses GalaFlex mesh on most of her breast reconstruction patients. While acknowledging no treatment is risk-free, Billington says the product is less risky than other options with proven success and calls it "the gold standard in breast reconstruction with implants" for many surgeons. A spokesperson for Anthem said the company regularly reviews coverage policies to ensure they are based on medical evidence and aligned with treatment standards, but noted that without FDA approval, the mesh does not meet the company's medical policy standards for coverage. Despite the denial, Clark received her reconstruction surgery with the GalaFlex mesh a little more than a week ago after Billington found a mechanism to make it happen. Both Clark and Billington are continuing to fight the denial.
