Two Palm Beach County women arrested, accused of human trafficking operation in Lake Worth Beach

Diana Chaparro Contreras, 33, and Sandra Peralta Aguilar, 50, are facing multiple charges including human trafficking and profiting from or arranging prostitution. Both women are currently held in the Palm Beach County Jail. Authorities say they operated a trafficking scheme out of a Lake Worth Beach residence where a California woman was recruited under false pretenses and forced into sexual exploitation.

The investigation began in January when the victim responded to a housekeeping job posting on Indeed and was offered $35 per hour. After accepting the position, she was flown to Florida and brought to a home on S. M Street in Lake Worth Beach. Upon arrival, her passport, Social Security card, and work permit were confiscated. The victim reported being threatened with deportation and coerced into sexual acts with men who visited the residence. She was confined to a room with boarded windows and allegedly forced to engage in sexual activity with seven men before managing to escape. The victim used a lamp to break through the plywood covering the windows. She also reported that a possible 15-year-old girl was present at the location.

Law enforcement tracked the operation over several months, observing multiple men entering and leaving the property. In April, authorities discovered the operation had relocated to another address approximately one mile away. Financial records revealed that Chaparro handled more than $177,000 in cash and bank transactions associated with the trafficking scheme.

Laura Cusack, an advocate with the Human Trafficking Coalition of the Palm Beaches, emphasized that such operations can occur anywhere in the community. "It doesn't just happen in a vacuum or in the underground world. It could happen in the house next door," Cusack said. She stressed that community awareness serves as one of the most effective tools for identifying trafficking activity. Cusack noted that neighbors and community members who recognize suspicious patterns, such as unusually high foot traffic, can make a critical difference in stopping exploitation. "These predators are in our community. They can target our children so it just takes one person to make the call, report what they see and that can make all the difference," Cusack said. Anyone who suspects human trafficking is encouraged to contact the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office or reach out to the Human Trafficking Coalition of the Palm Beaches at 561-267-5783 or htcoalitionpb@gmail.com.
