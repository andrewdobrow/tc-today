Boca Raton's George Snow Scholarship Fund awards record $7.5 million to 470 students

The George Snow Scholarship Fund in Boca Raton has distributed more than $7.5 million in scholarships and support services to 470 students throughout Palm Beach and Broward counties in 2026. President Tim Snow announced the record-breaking milestone to WPTV. Over its four-decade history, the nonprofit organization has awarded more than $45 million to over 4,100 students, establishing itself as the largest scholarship provider in Palm Beach County. The fund's core mission focuses on addressing the gap between traditional financial aid packages and what students and their families can realistically afford. Scholarship recipients receive four-year commitments, which Snow said helps provide financial stability throughout their college years.

The organization's support extends well beyond monetary scholarships. Vice President of Development Jay Brandt emphasized that GSSF offers mentorship, professional development opportunities, and educational resources including the College Compass podcast. These comprehensive services are designed to give scholars the tools they need to succeed academically and professionally. Among the 2026 recipients is Zya Chow, a Wellington Community High School graduate who earned two scholarships: the Royal Palm Hearing Aid Scholarship and the Davidowitz Family Caregiving Youth Scholarship. Chow plans to attend Georgetown University and expressed her appreciation for the organization's support.

One distinctive program within GSSF is the Doyle-Whelchel Scholars Boutique, directed by Scholar Services Coordinator Garvey Antoine. The boutique provides professional, formal, and casual clothing to help scholars prepare for important life transitions. Students can access appropriate wear for job interviews, internships, and their first days on campus. The boutique receives donations of both new and gently used clothing along with footwear and accessories from the community.

The George Snow Scholarship Fund's comprehensive approach to student support reflects growing recognition that removing financial barriers alone is insufficient for true educational equity. As college costs continue rising, organizations like GSSF are addressing multiple dimensions of student success. By combining financial aid with mentorship, professional development, and practical resources like clothing for interviews and campus life, the fund tackles obstacles that often prevent capable students from underserved communities from reaching their full potential. The organization's 2026 results demonstrate that South Florida communities continue to invest in the next generation's educational opportunities.
