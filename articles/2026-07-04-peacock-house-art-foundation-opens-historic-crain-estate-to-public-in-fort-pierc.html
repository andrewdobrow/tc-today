Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce into artistic destination with history and global artists

The Peacock House Art Foundation preserves the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain at the historic Crain Estate in Fort Pierce. Jaxson Crain was a tile worker by trade, and his craftsmanship is visible throughout the entire facility from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. Krystal Masters, the art director, said the estate is 90% the way it was when the Crains were there. The house retains original features including the smoking and bourbon room with man paneling, manly bathroom colors, pre-stained glass, and a bar on the front patio. Visitors can take guided tours of the lush gardens and vibrant architecture every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. The couple traveled the world nine times, collecting one-of-a-kind items for their Florida estate including a gazebo shipped from India that features tiny peacocks. The foundation has made a conscious effort to preserve the estate's original character and avoid commercialization.

The Peacock House gets its name from the birds that have roamed the property for decades. The Crains are believed to have introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, bringing in seven originally that have grown to over 100. Among the flock is a beloved bird named Bob, believed to be 60 years old, who walks with a limp and is hand-fed daily from his own bowls. The foundation's artist-in-residency program attracts artists from around the world, housing them in simple but elegant accommodations on the property. The art lab offers classes for children and adults of all skill levels. Masters leads one-on-one classes where students with zero experience walk out with a piece of art. Classes include sculpting and workshops like paint your pet in watercolor and rainbow.
