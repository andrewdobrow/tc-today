Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce into artistic destination with global artist program

The Peacock House Art Foundation operates from the historic Crain Estate in Fort Pierce, preserving the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain who once called the property home. Jaxson Crain was a tile worker by trade, and his craftsmanship is visible throughout the entire facility from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. The foundation has kept the estate 90% the way it was when the Crains lived there, including the smoking and bourbon room with its manly paneling and stained glass. The couple traveled the world nine times, collecting items for their Florida estate including a gazebo shipped from India that features tiny peacocks in its design. Visitors can take guided tours of the lush gardens and vibrant architecture every Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

The Crains are believed to have introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, originally bringing in seven birds that have since multiplied to over 100. Among the flock is Bob, believed to be 60 years old and recognizable by his limp. Bob lives inside the estate, has his own bowls, and is hand-fed every day. The foundation runs an artist-in-residency program that attracts artists from around the world with simple but elegant accommodations on the property. Art director Krystal Masters leads the art lab, which offers classes for children and adults of all skill levels. The majority of Masters' classes are one-on-one where students come in with zero experience and walk out with a piece of art.
