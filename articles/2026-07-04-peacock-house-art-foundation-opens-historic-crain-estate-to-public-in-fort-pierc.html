Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce estate into arts destination with global residency program

The Peacock House Art Foundation is transforming Fort Pierce into an artistic destination by preserving the historic Crain Estate and operating an international artist residency program. The nonprofit occupies the former home of Jaxson and Mary Crain, who traveled the world nine times collecting items for their Florida property. Jaxson Crain, a tile worker by trade, completed all the tiling throughout the facility from ground to walls to ceiling. The estate remains 90 percent unchanged from when the Crains lived there, including the smoking and bourbon room with its stained glass and front patio bar. The Crains collected a gazebo shipped from India that features tiny peacocks in its design. Guided tours of the gardens and architecture are offered every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

The Peacock House gets its name from the birds that have inhabited the property for decades. The Crains are believed to have introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, originally bringing seven birds that have grown to over 100. Among the flock is Bob, believed to be 60 years old and the oldest peacock on the property, who walks with a limp and is hand-fed daily from his own bowls. The foundation's artist-in-residency program houses artists from around the world in simple but elegant accommodations. Art director Krystal Masters leads the art lab, offering classes for children and adults of all skill levels in mediums including sculpting and watercolor, with one-on-one classes designed for complete beginners.
