Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce into artistic destination with global artists

The Peacock House Art Foundation is transforming Fort Pierce into an artistic destination by preserving the historic Crain Estate and connecting local and international artists. The non-profit organization honors the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, who once lived on the property. Jaxson Crain was a tile worker whose craftsmanship is visible throughout the facility, from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. Visitors can take guided tours of the lush gardens and vibrant architecture every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. The estate remains approximately 90 percent unchanged from the Crains' time there, including details like the smoking and bourbon room with its man paneling and prestained glass. The couple traveled the world nine times, collecting one-of-a-kind items for their Florida estate, including a gazebo shipped from India that features tiny peacocks in its design.

The Peacock House gets its name from the birds that have roamed the property for decades. The Crains are believed to have introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, starting with seven birds that have since grown to over 100. Among the flock is Bob, a beloved bird believed to be 60 years old and the oldest peacock on the property, who walks with a limp and is hand-fed daily. The foundation's artist-in-residency program attracts artists from around the world and offers simple but elegant accommodations. The art lab provides classes for children and adults of all skill levels, including one-on-one instruction in mediums like sculpting and watercolor.
