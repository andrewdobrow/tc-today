Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce historic estate into arts destination

The Peacock House Art Foundation is reshaping Fort Pierce's cultural landscape by operating out of the historic Crain Estate, a property that now serves as both a museum and active arts center. The nonprofit preserves the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, the original owners who filled the estate with their travels and personal touches. Jaxson Crain was a tile worker whose skill is evident throughout the property, with his tilework visible from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. The foundation has committed to maintaining the estate's original character rather than commercializing it, keeping approximately 90 percent of the home exactly as it was during the Crains' time there. Visitors can explore the lush gardens and distinctive architecture through guided tours offered every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

The couple's extensive travels shaped the estate's unique character and collections. They journeyed around the world nine times, acquiring one-of-a-kind items for their Florida home, including a gazebo shipped directly from India that features small peacock details. The property also showcases the smoking and bourbon room, featuring rich paneling, masculine bathroom colors, and ornate stained glass that opens onto a patio bar. According to art director Krystal Masters, the Crains' worldly acquisitions and personal style created a distinctive home that continues to captivate visitors today.

The property gets its name from the peacocks that have inhabited the grounds for decades. The Crains introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, initially bringing seven birds to the estate. From that original flock, the population has grown to over 100 peacocks. Among them is Bob, a celebrated resident believed to be 60 years old and recognizable by his distinctive limp. Staff hand-feed Bob daily using his own bowls, and the peacocks have become memorable highlights of tours, especially for young visitors who get to feed the birds directly. The Crains' animal collection once extended further, as local lore suggests that Jaxson Crain traveled to South America and returned home with a baby monkey carried in his pocket.

Today, the foundation operates an artist-in-residency program that draws artists from around the world, providing them with simple yet elegant accommodations on the property. The art lab offers accessible classes for both children and adults of all skill levels, with Masters leading one-on-one instruction designed for complete beginners. The lab features diverse mediums including sculpting and watercolor painting. Masters emphasizes that art serves as a therapeutic outlet, allowing students to disconnect from their phones and create something meaningful. She finds fulfillment in watching students leave feeling accomplished and fulfilled, understanding that art provides emotional and creative benefits beyond technical skill development.
