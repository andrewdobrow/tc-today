Peacock House Art Foundation preserves Crain Estate history and brings international artists to Fort Pierce

The Peacock House Art Foundation is establishing Fort Pierce as an artistic destination by preserving the historic Crain Estate and operating an artist-in-residency program that welcomes creators from around the world. The nonprofit organization maintains the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, who previously lived on the property in the Peacock Arts District. Jaxson Crain worked as a tile worker, and his skilled craftsmanship remains visible throughout the facility on floors, walls, and ceilings. The estate has retained approximately 90 percent of its original character from the Crains' occupancy, including a smoking and bourbon room featuring wood paneling, traditional masculine colors, and stained glass that opens onto a front patio bar. Visitors can experience the lush gardens and distinctive architecture through guided tours offered every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

The Crains developed their estate through extensive world travel, having journeyed internationally nine times and collected unique items for their Florida home. Among their acquisitions was a gazebo shipped from India, decorated with small peacocks. The couple is credited with introducing peacocks to Fort Pierce during the 1970s, initially bringing seven birds that have since multiplied to over 100. The estate's most famous peacock resident is Bob, believed to be 60 years old, who is recognizable by his limp. Bob receives special treatment, living in his own designated space with individual bowls and receiving hand-feeding from staff members daily. The foundation has deliberately prioritized preserving the estate's authentic character while avoiding excessive commercialization.

The foundation's artist-in-residency program draws international artists by offering simple yet elegant accommodations on the property. These spaces provide everything artists need while maintaining understated elegance. The art lab provides classes for both children and adults regardless of skill level or prior experience. Krystal Masters, the art director, teaches one-on-one classes specifically designed for beginners who arrive with no artistic background. "The majority of my classes are one on one classes you come in with zero experience and you walk out with a piece of art and it makes you feel good," Masters said. Classes cover various mediums including sculpting and watercolor, with current workshops such as "paint your pet in watercolor and rainbow" available to participants.

Beyond its focus on preservation and professional art, the foundation emphasizes art's therapeutic value. Masters views art as stress relief, noting that participants "leave happy and haven't been on their phone scrolling." The foundation's commitment extends to memorable experiences for all visitors, particularly children who interact with the peacocks during tours. The organization has also preserved stories of the Crains' eccentric lifestyle, including the anecdote that Jaxson Crain once returned from South America with a baby monkey he had carried in his pocket. Through these interconnected programs, the Peacock House Art Foundation continues to honor the Crains' adventurous spirit while connecting local and international artists within the broader Peacock Arts District community.
