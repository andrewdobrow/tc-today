Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce estate into artistic destination with global artists

The Peacock House Art Foundation is transforming Fort Pierce into an artistic destination by preserving the historic Crain Estate, the former home of Jaxson and Mary Crain. Jaxson Crain was a tile worker whose craftsmanship is visible throughout the facility, from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. The couple traveled the world nine times, collecting one-of-a-kind items for their Florida estate, including a gazebo shipped from India. According to art director Krystal Masters, the estate remains 90% the way it was when the Crains lived there, including the smoking and bourbon room with stained glass and a bar on the front patio. The foundation has made a conscious effort to preserve the estate's original character without commercializing it. Guided tours of the lush gardens and vibrant architecture are available every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

The Peacock House gets its name from the birds that have roamed the property for decades. The Crains are believed to have introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, originally bringing in seven birds, which have since grown to over 100. Among the flock is a beloved bird named Bob, believed to be 60 years old, who walks with a limp and has his own bowls. Bob is hand-fed every day and sometimes feeds out of children's hands during tours. The foundation's artist-in-residency program attracts artists from around the world and offers simple but elegant accommodations. The art lab provides classes for children and adults of all skill levels, with Masters leading one-on-one classes for complete beginners using mediums including sculpting and watercolor.
