Martin County free clinic sees surge in patients after enhanced ACA subsidies expire

Volunteers in Medicine, a free medical clinic serving low-income families in Martin County, is experiencing increased demand for its services following the expiration of enhanced financial assistance through the Affordable Care Act at the beginning of 2026. The end of the enhanced subsidy program has left thousands of Floridians without health insurance coverage and struggling to access medical care. According to Dr. Jordan Bromberg, medical director of Volunteers in Medicine, more than 200,000 people across Florida did not renew their ACA health insurance this year. While only about 20 patients have so far come to the clinic after losing ACA coverage, estimates suggest as many as 500 Martin County residents may have lost access to health insurance.

Rokaya McGarry represents one of the patients who turned to the clinic after the subsidies expired. She explained that she could no longer afford health insurance once the enhanced ACA subsidies ended. McGarry said she had begun viewing medical care as a luxury rather than a necessity until her situation became critical. After losing her mother, she fell severely ill with bronchitis and felt weak. In a moment of desperation, she reached out to Volunteers in Medicine, and the clinic took over her care within a day.

Dr. Bromberg emphasized the importance of early medical screening and intervention. He noted that many health conditions can be identified early through preventive care, potentially preventing serious outcomes. Bromberg expressed concern about the number of uninsured Floridians and stressed that there are far more people in the community who need the clinic's services than have sought them out so far. He highlighted that Volunteers in Medicine has the capacity to serve more patients than currently use its services.

The clinic provides free medical care without any strings attached. To qualify for services, individuals must earn under $39,990 annually, or families of four must earn under $82,500 per year. McGarry expressed deep gratitude for the care she received, saying she could not imagine where she would be without the clinic's assistance. As the clinic continues to support Martin County residents who lost insurance coverage, Dr. Bromberg's message remains clear: the facility has room for more patients and encourages those who qualify to seek the medical care they need.
