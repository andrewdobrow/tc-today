Martin County free clinic sees surge in patients after ACA subsidies expire, has capacity for more

Volunteers in Medicine in Martin County is treating more patients after enhanced subsidies through the Affordable Care Act expired at the start of 2026, leaving many people without health insurance. The clinic serves low-income families and provides free medical care with no strings attached.

Dr. Jordan Bromberg, medical director of Volunteers in Medicine, said more than 200,000 people in Florida did not renew their health insurance through the ACA this year. He estimates upwards of 500 people in Martin County lost access to health coverage. So far, about 20 patients have turned to the clinic after losing their ACA coverage. Rokaya McGarry is one of those patients. After the enhanced ACA subsidies expired, she could no longer afford health insurance. She fell very ill after losing her mother and began to see medical care as a luxury until it became a necessity. She called the clinic and was seen within a day.

Bromberg emphasized the importance of early medical screening. He said the expiration of the enhanced financial assistance program has left many people without any access to medical care. When health conditions are found early through screening, they can prevent serious outcomes. McGarry described her situation during that difficult time. "Panic set in because you know that the only way that you're going to be helped as far as health is to go to an Emergency," she said. After calling the clinic in her moment of desperation, she expressed deep gratitude for the care she received. "I can't even express how grateful I am to them because I don't know where I'd be," McGarry said.

To qualify for care at Volunteers in Medicine, individuals must earn under $39,990 per year, or under $82,500 for a family of four. Despite the influx of new patients, Bromberg said the clinic has room to serve more people from the community. "There are so many more out there that can come and see us," he said. "We have the capacity for more." The clinic continues to provide medical services at no cost to qualifying patients, making it an important resource for those who have lost insurance coverage and have limited financial means to pay for healthcare.
