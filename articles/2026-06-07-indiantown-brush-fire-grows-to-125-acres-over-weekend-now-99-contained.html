Indiantown brush fire grows to 125 acres, now 99% contained after weekend escalation

A brush fire that started Friday in Indiantown spiraled out of control over the weekend before firefighters brought it under near-total control by Sunday morning. The fire eventually consumed approximately 125 acres and reached 99% containment as of Sunday, according to Martin County Fire Rescue and the Florida Forest Service.

The incident began on Friday, June 5, when Martin County Fire Rescue responded to a rapidly developing brush fire in the 2600 block of Southwest Warfield Boulevard just before 3:30 p.m. Firefighters quickly contained the initial blaze at approximately 37.8 acres, with no reported injuries and no structures threatened. The Florida Forest Service assisted by establishing plow lines to prevent the flames from spreading further. Martin County Fire Rescue also deployed its drone team to identify remaining hotspots and confirm full containment before crews left the scene. Though the cause remained unknown at the time, early indications pointed to a holdover fire, in which a lightning strike could have smoldered underground for days before igniting visible flames. The Florida Forest Service has continued investigating the fire's origin.

By Saturday, June 6, the situation deteriorated dramatically when crews returned to the same area to fight a much larger 120-acre brush fire. Officials believe the fire rekindled from the previous day's incident. Shifting winds combined with dense vegetation fueled rapid fire growth, triggering a large-scale response that included Martin County Fire Rescue, the Florida Forest Service, and the Martin County Sheriff's Office. Firefighters focused on protecting nearby structures as smoke blanketed the surrounding area. Officials confirmed there was no broader threat to the public, though residents were warned to expect smoky conditions throughout the weekend as suppression efforts continued.

By Sunday morning, firefighters had achieved 99% containment of the fire, which reached a total size of approximately 125 acres. The Florida Forest Service remained on scene monitoring conditions to prevent any additional flare-ups. Authorities said the investigation into the fire's origin remains ongoing.
