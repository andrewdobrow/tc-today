West Boca Raton homeowner faces repeated delays on My Safe Florida Home grant despite completed work

Anne Bendel of West Boca Raton has encountered multiple obstacles in her attempt to receive a state grant designed to help homeowners strengthen their homes against storms. The persistent delays have tested her patience as she navigates the My Safe Florida Home program, which typically awards grants up to $10,000 to eligible homeowners.

Bendel first reached out to WPTV in May regarding her application, which was being delayed due to missing paperwork from her insurance company. After resolving that initial issue, representatives with My Safe Florida Home informed her in May that her application status was complete. However, just one week later, the program requested additional documentation, specifically a permit of completion for a large impact window that Bendel had installed. She promptly provided the requested document, but new questions then arose about dates on her paperwork. Her application has since been sent back for further review, leaving the status of her grant funding uncertain. She paid $8,000 out of pocket for the home improvements and is hoping to recover some of those costs through the grant.

The My Safe Florida Home program has been instrumental in assisting thousands of homeowners throughout Florida by providing financial support for storm-resistant upgrades and helping them qualify for insurance discounts. However, Bendel's experience has undermined her confidence in the program's reliability. She described the process as particularly challenging because she lacks access to a computer and must handle all communications through her phone without assistance from others. "It's gut-wrenching," she said about managing the application process under these constraints. Despite completing all her required work, obtaining necessary permits, and securing the insurance coverage the program asked for, Bendel believes the program continues to introduce new requirements. She expressed frustration about what she perceives as unfair treatment, suggesting the program may be creating additional obstacles once homeowners have already finished their projects.

WPTV contacted the Florida Department of Financial Services in Tallahassee to inquire about Bendel's case and the current status of her grant application. Bendel, who describes herself as persistent and thorough, maintains that she has fulfilled every requirement presented to her. Her situation highlights potential challenges within the grant application process that may warrant further examination by state officials overseeing the program.
