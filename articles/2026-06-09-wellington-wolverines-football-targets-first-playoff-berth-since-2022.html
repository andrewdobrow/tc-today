Wellington Wolverines football targets first playoff berth since 2022

Wellington High School's football team is focused on a singular objective this summer: capturing their first playoff berth in two years. The Wolverines haven't made the postseason since 2022, and the entire program is committed to reversing that trend as fall approaches. Through intensive summer conditioning, players and coaches are building the foundation they believe will propel them back to the playoffs.

Senior Brady Seider embodies the hunger driving the team's efforts. "It would mean everything you know. Everybody just looks at Wellington like we are a bunch of nobodies. We really wanna prove everybody wrong. We just wanna get there, see what it's like to win," Seider said. The message is clear: Wellington wants to silence doubters and demonstrate they belong among Florida's competitive programs.

Head coach Pete Walker, in his second year leading the Wolverines, arrives with impressive credentials. At Glades Day, he won three state championships, establishing himself as a proven winner. Walker sees the same potential in his current squad. "Well, first I had great coaches, which I have again on my staff. I think we're as good as anybody in Florida right now. The kids bought in through hard work and team and those types of things. So that's the things that we're concentrating on again, and they're moving in the right direction," Walker said. His confidence stems from the commitment he's seeing from his players during the offseason program.

Junior Danhoi Williams emphasized how the summer workouts serve a broader purpose beyond physical conditioning. "Just trying to get a winning season and try to go to the playoffs. You know, get them gelling, new teammates, new coaches. Just trying to see that everything is going right in the summer and head into the season with it," Williams said. Building chemistry among players is proving essential as the team incorporates new talent and assistant coaches. As Wellington continues its summer conditioning program, the organization appears positioned to make a serious playoff push when the season kicks off. The combination of experienced leadership, motivated players, and focused preparation suggests the Wolverines are ready to end their postseason absence.
