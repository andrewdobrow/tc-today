Federal Court Blocks Florida Enforcement Lawsuit Against Pediatricians Over Gender Care Advocacy

A federal court in Illinois has granted a preliminary injunction blocking Florida from pursuing an enforcement lawsuit against the American Academy of Pediatrics, dealing a significant blow to the state's efforts to penalize the medical organization for its support of gender-affirming care for transgender youth. The injunction halts Attorney General James Uthmeier's lawsuit, which accused the nonprofit pediatricians organization of deceptive trade practices, racketeering, and antitrust violations based on its medical guidance and policy advocacy.

The federal court found the academy plausibly demonstrated that Florida filed the lawsuit to punish and suppress its protected speech. In granting the injunction, the court determined that the Florida complaint contains significant misrepresentations of the academy's policy statements and rests on a weak legal theory that its medical guidance constituted commercial speech. The court concluded the state appears to have brought the case in bad faith without a reasonable expectation of success on the merits.

The ruling represents a major development in the ongoing legal and political battle over transgender healthcare in Florida. The American Academy of Pediatrics, a national organization representing pediatricians, has publicly supported gender-affirming care for transgender youth as medically appropriate treatment. Florida officials have taken an aggressive stance against such care, and the enforcement lawsuit represented an attempt to use consumer protection and antitrust laws to challenge medical organizations that advocate for treatment policies at odds with state positions.

The preliminary injunction means Florida cannot proceed with its enforcement action while the underlying case continues in federal court. The decision raises questions about whether other states might face similar legal obstacles if they attempt to use enforcement powers against medical organizations based on their advocacy and clinical guidance. The case highlights the intersection of free speech protections, medical professional standards, and state regulatory authority. The American Academy of Pediatrics can continue its advocacy and policy work without facing immediate legal consequences from Florida during the litigation.
