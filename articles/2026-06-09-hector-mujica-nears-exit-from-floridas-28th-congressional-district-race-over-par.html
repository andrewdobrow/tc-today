Hector Mujica nears exit from Florida's 28th Congressional District race over party registration issue

Hector Mujica is preparing to withdraw from the Democratic primary in Florida's 28th Congressional District following a party registration problem that makes him ineligible to run. The former Google executive wants federal prosecutor Robin Peguero, currently running in the neighboring 27th Congressional District, to switch races and take his place, according to multiple sources.

The eligibility issue stems from a party affiliation change in Mujica's voter registration records. Broward County records show that Mujica switched from Democrat to no party affiliation on June 4, 2025, then returned to Democrat on July 26, 2025. Florida law requires candidates to maintain continuous party membership for 365 consecutive days to run for a party's nomination. Democratic lawmakers unanimously passed this requirement earlier this year, and Governor Ron DeSantis signed it into law in April.

Mujica initially claimed the registration change was a clerical error and stated he was unaware of the switch until his opponent's campaign discovered it in late July. However, the Broward Supervisor of Elections rejected this explanation, saying their records review found no evidence of any administrative mistake. Mujica's inability to account for the change is notable given that he had recently run for U.S. Senate, raising over $717,000 before withdrawing about two weeks before that race's qualifying period. Retired Air Force Commander Phil Ehr, Mujica's primary opponent in the district, filed a complaint with the Florida Elections Commission and threatened to file a lawsuit on Friday, the final day of qualifying, if Mujica remains on the ballot.

Ehr's campaign manager Vanessa Brito confirmed that Mujica is attempting to recruit Peguero to replace him in the race. Peguero has raised $800,000 through March 31 and is currently in a Democratic primary against Eliott Rodriguez for the 27th District seat, which is being defended by Republican Maria Elvira Salazar. Brito expressed disapproval of the recruitment effort, saying Mujica should withdraw and wait until he becomes eligible to run. The 28th Congressional District covers portions of southwestern Miami-Dade County, including Florida City, Homestead, South Miami, and Sweetwater, along with the Florida Keys in Monroe County. The 27th District includes Miami, Coral Gables, and several other communities. Federal candidates must reside in Florida but not necessarily in their specific district.
