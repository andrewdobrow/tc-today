Treasure Coast reports 3 cyclospora cases as residents avoid lettuce, berries during outbreak

Health officials confirmed five local cases of cyclospora since May, with two in Palm Beach County, two in St. Lucie County and one in Martin County, according to the Florida Department of Health. The concern stems from outbreaks tied to contaminated produce, including lettuce connected to Taco Bell locations, that can cause severe stomach illness and intense diarrhea.

Thousands across the U.S. have been affected. At restaurants, grocery stores and fast-food chains, families say they are steering clear of leafy greens. George Lorenz said he is taking precautions while grabbing lunch with his father, Brian, on Clematis Street in West Palm Beach, opting for pizza from Elisabetta's instead. In Boynton Beach, Hendriette Peckens said she bought two big bags of oranges, bread and some apples, and planned to cook Alaskan salmon for dinner.

Samantha Barone, dietitian for the bariatric program at Delray Medical Center, said consumers should focus on produce with protective outer layers. Barone recommends foods like bananas, melons and avocados, noting that consumers should still wash them before cutting into them and peeling them. She also recommends washing all fruits and vegetables thoroughly before eating them and says beans and nuts are safe alternatives that still provide fiber and protein.

Barone warns to avoid bagged lettuce if possible and recommends buying whole heads of lettuce and peeling away the outer layers. She advises avoiding berries, especially raspberries, because they are difficult to clean properly.
