Treasure Coast Residents Report Five Cyclospora Cases Since May

The Florida Department of Health reported five confirmed local cases of cyclospora since May: two in Palm Beach County, two in St. Lucie County and one in Martin County. The cases are part of nationwide outbreaks of E. coli and cyclospora linked to contaminated produce, including lettuce connected to Taco Bell locations. Health officials said thousands across the U.S. have been affected, causing many South Florida residents to change their eating habits and avoid leafy greens altogether.

Families across the region are steering clear of salads and contaminated produce that can cause severe stomach illness and intense diarrhea. Dakota and Brynlee Rhodes, visiting Delray Beach with their mother Jenny, said they are avoiding lettuce and similar foods. Jenny Rhodes said she misses her salads and berries but believes it is better to be careful.

In West Palm Beach, George Lorenz said he has heard about the outbreak and is staying away from affected produce. In Boynton Beach, shopper Hendriette Peckens said she bought oranges, apples and bread instead of greens and planned to cook Alaskan salmon for dinner.

Samantha Barone, dietitian for the bariatric program at Delray Medical Center, said the outbreaks are not an excuse to abandon healthy eating. She recommended consumers focus on produce with protective outer layers, suggesting they stick to melon and foods with harder skin that can be peeled, including bananas, melons and avocados. However, she warned that people still need to wash these items before cutting into them and peeling them. Barone also said beans and nuts are safe alternatives that still provide fiber and protein.

Barone's biggest warning was to avoid bagged lettuce if possible. She recommended buying whole heads of lettuce and peeling away the outer layers. She also warned that berries, especially raspberries, are difficult to clean properly and should be avoided.

Barone recommended cleaning produce using a 1 to 3 part mixture of distilled white vinegar and water. Consumers should soak, massage and clean off produce and make sure to cook vegetables. She emphasized that washing all fruits and vegetables thoroughly before eating them is essential, and consumers should wash items with harder skins before cutting into them.
