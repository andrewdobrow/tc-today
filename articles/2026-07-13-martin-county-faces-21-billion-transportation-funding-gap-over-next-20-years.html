Martin County faces $2.1 billion transportation funding gap as needs outpace available money

Martin County is facing a $2.1 billion transportation funding shortfall that could shape how residents get around for decades. A PowerPoint obtained through a public records request shows the Martin County Metropolitan Planning Organization estimates the county will need approximately $2.8 billion for transportation improvements over the next 20 years. Only $701 million is currently available, meaning the county can fund just about one quarter of its identified needs. The cost analysis was emailed to county and Stuart officials last August and outlines transportation needs across roads, transit, aviation, freight, and other infrastructure categories through 2050. Of the $701 million currently available, all but $10 million comes from state and federal funding sources.

Nine roads the county identified as requiring widening to handle growing traffic remain unfunded. The total cost to widen those roads is approximately $213 million, leaving all nine projects out of the county's draft Cost Feasible Plan. The roads include SW Martin Downs Boulevard, SR-714/SW Martin Highway, SW 96th Street, SW Bridge Road, CR-714/SW Martin Highway, SW Allapattah Road/CR-609, SE Green River Parkway, SW Indiantown Avenue, and SE Bridge Road/CR-708. May and Maddy Brumlow travel Martin Downs Boulevard nearly every day and said the congestion is already noticeable. Beth Beltran, administrator for Martin County's Metropolitan Planning Organization, said State Road 710 is among the county's highest priorities due to fatal crashes and serious injury crashes, with improvements totaling about $300 million.
