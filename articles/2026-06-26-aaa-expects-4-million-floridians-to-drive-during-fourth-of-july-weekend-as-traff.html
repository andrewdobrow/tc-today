AAA expects 4 million Floridians to drive during Fourth of July weekend as traffic fatalities spike 44% on holiday

Nearly 4.6 million Floridians are expected to travel at least 50 miles from home during the Fourth of July weekend, representing a 3% increase from the previous year. About 4 million of these travelers will drive, while an additional 330,000 will fly and roughly 204,000 will use buses, trains, cruises, or other transportation methods. As one of the nation's top domestic travel destinations, Florida will experience significant traffic congestion on major highways including I-4, I-95, and roads leading to beaches and theme parks. Travel timing matters during this busy period. AAA and INRIX data recommend that drivers avoid afternoon and evening travel, particularly on July 2 when people are heading out and on July 5 and 6 when they return home. Early morning hours generally offer the best driving conditions.

The statistics surrounding holiday traffic safety paint a concerning picture. On a typical July day, Florida experiences an average of 7 to 8 traffic fatalities. However, the Fourth of July itself has historically produced a 44% spike in fatalities, averaging about 11 deaths on that single day based on data from 2017 through 2021. Throughout entire holiday weekends, the state has seen between 25 and 32 or more deaths, with many of these accidents linked to alcohol impairment and failure to use seatbelts.

Vehicle maintenance plays a critical role in preventing accidents during holiday travel. Benny Garcia at Palm Beach Tire Pros emphasizes the importance of checking tire pressure before long drives. To determine if tires need replacement, drivers can use the penny test by placing a coin upside down in the tire treads. If you can see the entire outline of Abraham Lincoln's head, the tires should be changed. Understanding tire age is equally important. The date code on a tire's sidewall provides this information, with the first two numbers indicating the week of manufacture and the last two numbers showing the year. Most tires typically last between 5 and 6 years.

Before embarking on Fourth of July travel, drivers should perform a complete vehicle inspection. Check tire pressure, battery condition, and all fluid levels, including oil. Pack an emergency kit in your vehicle in case of unexpected problems. Once on the road, maintaining focus is essential for safety. Put away your phone and eliminate distractions. Remember that fatigued driving poses dangers similar to impaired driving. By taking these precautions and planning your travel schedule carefully, you can help ensure a safer holiday weekend for yourself and your family.
