Stuart downtown dock to close Monday for $3 million renovation, will nearly double slip capacity

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down July 6 for a $3 million renovation expected to wrap up by next Fourth of July. The project will significantly expand the facility's capacity, nearly doubling the number of available slips from 18 to 44 for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The upgrades are funded through a state grant and transportation impact fees.

The renovation work will add a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock, and a smaller floating platform. According to Stuart Mayor Sean Reed, these improvements serve a critical purpose beyond just adding space. "They're doing it to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms," Reed said. "So the year of waiting will be worth it." With more than 1,400 boaters using the dock annually, the project represents a significant investment in the community's waterfront infrastructure.

The dock's closure has raised concerns for businesses that depend on the facility. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates tours from the dock, initially worried about continuing operations during construction. However, the city found a solution. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for the company, explained the arrangement: "They're allowing us to build a gate right here at the end of the downtown Stuart dock. This is the non-floating section, but still we're going to be able to open this up and board people right here." This accommodation will allow the tour company to continue serving customers during the renovation period.

Kuyrkendall emphasized the dock's importance to Stuart's identity and economy. "Stuart is not Stuart without the water," he said. He also noted the current limitations, especially during peak times. "On the weekend that's when it can get a little hectic at the dock and you know it's never great for us when we're squeezing in and trying to find spots." Boat captain Parker Fisher, who operates in Stuart, supports the improvements. "It's definitely needed some work over the years, so I'm glad they're putting money into it," Fisher said. Kuyrkendall shared this optimism about the modernization project, saying the expanded dock will benefit both the city and local businesses like his own.
