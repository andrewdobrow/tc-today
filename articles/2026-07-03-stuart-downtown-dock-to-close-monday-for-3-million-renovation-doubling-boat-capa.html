Stuart's downtown dock closes Monday for $3 million renovation to nearly double slip capacity

Stuart's downtown public dock will close July 6 for a $3 million renovation project expected to wrap by next Fourth of July. The upgrade will nearly double the facility's capacity, expanding available slips from 18 to 44 for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The project, funded by a state grant and transportation impact fees, will add a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock, and a smaller floating platform. With more than 1,400 boaters using the dock annually, Stuart Mayor Sean Reed said the improvements serve dual purposes: expanding capacity and strengthening the dock against severe weather. "They're doing it to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms," Reed said. "So the year of waiting will be worth it."

The city is working to accommodate businesses during the closure. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates passenger tours from the dock, was initially uncertain about continuing operations. The city found a solution by allowing the business to build a gate at the end of the non-floating section for passenger boarding. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, described the compromise as workable. He noted the dock's current limitations, particularly on weekends when demand peaks. "Stuart is not Stuart without the water," Kuyrkendall said, emphasizing the dock's importance to the city's identity and economy.
