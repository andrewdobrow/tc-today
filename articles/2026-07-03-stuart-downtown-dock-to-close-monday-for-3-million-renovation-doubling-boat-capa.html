Stuart downtown dock closes today for $3 million renovation to nearly double slip capacity

Stuart's downtown public dock closes July 6 for a $3 million renovation projected to be complete by next Fourth of July. The project will nearly double the dock's capacity from 18 to 44 slips for motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation is funded by a state grant and transportation impact fees and will include a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock and a smaller floating platform. Stuart Mayor Sean Reed said the upgrades are designed to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms. More than 1,400 boaters use the dock annually. Boat captain Parker Fisher said the dock has needed work over the years and he is glad they are putting money into it, expressing hope for more floating docks to be added.

During the closure, the city is working to accommodate businesses and boaters who rely on the dock. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates off the dock, initially had concerns about continuing to run during construction. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, said the city is allowing them to build a gate at the end of the downtown Stuart dock's non-floating section so they can board people at that location. Kuyrkendall said Stuart is not Stuart without the water and noted the dock can get hectic on weekends when his operation squeezes in and tries to find spots. He said modernizing and expanding the dock is really going to be great for the city and for his business.
