Stuart's downtown dock closes Monday for $3 million renovation doubling slip capacity

Stuart's downtown public dock will close July 6 for a $3 million renovation projected to be complete by next Fourth of July. The project will nearly double the dock's capacity, expanding from 18 to 44 slips for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation includes a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock and a smaller floating platform. The project is funded by a state grant and transportation impact fees. Stuart Mayor Sean Reed said the upgrades are designed to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms.

During the year-long closure, the city is working to accommodate businesses and boaters who rely on the dock. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates off the dock, received city approval to build a gate at the end of the downtown dock's non-floating section for passenger boarding. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, said the dock can get hectic on weekends when boats squeeze in to find spots. Boat captain Parker Fisher, who is based in Stuart, said the dock has needed work over the years and hopes the renovation includes more floating docks. More than 1,400 boaters use the dock annually.
