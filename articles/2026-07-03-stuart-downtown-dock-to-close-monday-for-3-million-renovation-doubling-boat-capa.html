Stuart's downtown dock closes today for $3 million renovation, capacity to nearly double

Stuart's downtown public dock closed July 6 for a $3 million renovation projected to be complete by next Fourth of July. The year-long project will nearly double the dock's capacity, expanding from 18 to 44 slips for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation is funded by a state grant and transportation impact fees and will include a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock and a smaller floating platform. More than 1,400 boaters use the dock annually. Stuart Mayor Sean Reed said the upgrades are designed to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms. Boat captain Parker Fisher, who is based in Stuart, said the dock has needed work over the years and he is glad the city is putting money into it.

During the closure, the city is working to accommodate businesses and boaters who rely on the dock for drop-off and pick-up. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates off the dock, initially had concerns about whether it could continue running during construction. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, said the city is allowing the company to build a gate at the end of the downtown Stuart dock's non-floating section for boarding passengers. Kuyrkendall said Stuart is not Stuart without the water. He said weekends can get hectic at the dock with boats squeezing in and trying to find spots, and that modernizing and expanding the dock will be great for the city and for Tiki Taxi & Cruises.
