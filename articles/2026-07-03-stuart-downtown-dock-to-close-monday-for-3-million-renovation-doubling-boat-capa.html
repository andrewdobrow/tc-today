Stuart downtown dock closes Sunday for $3 million renovation, nearly doubling slip capacity

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down July 6 for a major renovation project costing $3 million. The work is expected to wrap up by next Fourth of July, delivering what city officials say will be crucial improvements to the waterfront facility. The renovation will nearly double the dock's capacity, increasing available slips from 18 to 44 for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The project is being funded through a state grant and transportation impact fees.

The renovation will add substantial new infrastructure to the dock. Plans include a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock, and a smaller floating platform. Stuart Mayor Sean Reed emphasized that the upgrades serve a critical purpose beyond simply expanding capacity. "They're doing it to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms," Reed said. "So the year of waiting will be worth it." The improvements are designed to protect the dock and vessels during severe weather events.

More than 1,400 boaters rely on the downtown dock annually, making the temporary closure a significant disruption. The city has taken steps to minimize the impact on businesses and boaters who depend on the facility for boarding and drop-off services. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates from the dock, initially faced uncertainty about continuing operations during construction. The city found a solution by allowing the company to build a gate at the end of the non-floating section where they can board passengers. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, stressed the dock's importance to the community. "Stuart is not Stuart without the water," Kuyrkendall said. He also noted the current limitations the dock faces, particularly during peak times. "On the weekend that's when it can get a little hectic at the dock and you know it's never great for us when we're squeezing in and trying to find spots," Kuyrkendall said.

Local boaters welcome the improvements. Boat captain Parker Fisher, based in Stuart, has long recognized the need for maintenance. "It's definitely needed some work over the years, so I'm glad they're putting money into it," Fisher said. He expressed hope that the project would expand floating dock space. For Kuyrkendall and other dock users, the renovation represents a necessary step forward. "Modernizing and expanding this back dock is really going to be great for the city but also for us as well," Kuyrkendall said.
