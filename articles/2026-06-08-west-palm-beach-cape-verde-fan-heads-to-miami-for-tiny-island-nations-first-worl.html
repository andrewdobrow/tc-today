West Palm Beach man to cheer Cape Verde at World Cup in Miami

Eridjon Dacruz of West Palm Beach is traveling to Miami this month to watch Cape Verde compete in the World Cup, marking a historic moment for one of the world's smallest nations to ever qualify for the tournament. Dacruz grew up in Cape Verde, an archipelago consisting of 10 islands located off the African coast, and later relocated to the United States. The island nation, which gained independence from Portugal during the 1970s, has a population of approximately half a million people. As Dacruz prepared for the big game, he looked through his collection of soccer jerseys, pulling out his favorite national team gear from his home country.

Cape Verdeans have expressed tremendous excitement about their national team, called the Tubarão Azul or Blue Sharks, qualifying for the World Cup for the first time in history. Dacruz, who still has many family members living in Cape Verde, noted that the qualification has been a major topic of conversation throughout the islands. He expressed amazement at the achievement, saying he never thought it would happen during his lifetime given the country's small size. When reflecting on his past gaming experiences, Dacruz recalled searching for Cape Verde players in FIFA video games and finding only about two options available. He is confident in his team's potential, believing they can advance if they win a couple of matches.

Dacruz's trip comes courtesy of his girlfriend, who gave him World Cup tickets as an anniversary gift. During their anniversary dinner, she presented him with a handwritten note inside a small box that read "I'm going to the World Cup," surprising him completely. He will attend Cape Verde's match against Uruguay in Miami. The team opens the tournament against Spain, one of the tournament favorites. While waiting for his new Cape Verde national jersey to arrive in time for the game, Dacruz anticipates showing his support in person.

Cape Verde's World Cup qualification will provide significant financial benefits to the nation's soccer development. The soccer federation will receive more than 10 million dollars to help build the next generation of players. Beyond sports, Cape Verde remains frequently mentioned during hurricane season, when tropical systems form near the islands before tracking across the Atlantic toward the United States, according to meteorologist Sami Squires. These weather systems originating from the African coast occasionally develop into storms that move westward across the ocean, though forecasters hope major storms will stay away this year.
