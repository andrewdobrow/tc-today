Port St. Lucie 6,000-seat soccer stadium groundbreaking set for November, new renderings released

Professional soccer is still set to begin next year in Port St. Lucie, with the club releasing new renderings this week of its 6,000-seat stadium. Representatives for Port St. Lucie SC announced Friday that groundbreaking is anticipated later this year, with current plans targeting November. The stadium will be built at the Walton & One development, a 46-acre mixed-use redevelopment project located along U.S. 1 and Walton Road. While the club's inaugural season begins in 2027, the future stadium is scheduled to open in early 2028. The team has not announced where they plan to play next year while the facility is under construction. The club posted Thursday on social media that their home venue will be a place where soccer, community, and unforgettable moments will come together. The team said every space has been designed to create unforgettable experiences, from matchdays to concerts, festivals, and community events, calling it a destination built for everyone. The 6,000-seat venue will have the flexibility to grow in capacity and will serve as a year-round gathering place.

Port St. Lucie resident Zach Woods said he thinks there's nothing to do in PSL for the most part and this may be the first cool thing that we've ever actually brought here. However, Woods said you can't really even get out here on US-1 before this and it's a nightmare, so he thinks the additional traffic on gamedays will be huge. Focus Sanders, a barber at Sapp's Barbershop, said the venue offers more business because it's going to bring more people over here, including people coming from maybe out of town who didn't get a haircut. Sanders said he's not liking the traffic that's going to come, but he is liking the business traffic. Ticket deposits are currently available online. It was announced last October that the city had been awarded a United Soccer League League One Team, with city council members unanimously approving both the funding and operating agreements. The city said the stadium is an $80 million economic investment, and no taxpayer money will be used for its construction.
