Humane Society of the Treasure Coast uses Stuart thrift stores for animal care during Palm City shelter renovations

The Humane Society of the Treasure Coast is relying on its two thrift stores to maintain animal care operations while undergoing major renovations at its Palm City shelter. The nonprofit started construction on its adoption, customer service, and intake areas earlier this year, which forced the organization to find creative solutions for housing its animals. According to spokesperson Sarah Fisher, many of the shelter's cats have been temporarily moved to the thrift store locations. Fisher emphasized how important this transition has been, noting that with their shelter cages unavailable during construction, the stores have provided essential space for the animals.

The thrift stores are now serving dual purposes for the organization. Senior Manager Kelly Ellis explained that customers shopping for unique items can also browse adoptable cats while visiting either location. This approach keeps the animals visible to potential adopters while freeing up much-needed space at the main facility. Ellis stressed that every purchase made at the thrift stores directly supports the animals in their care. The organization welcomes donations of gently used goods, which shoppers can drop off while exploring the merchandise and meeting available pets.

While the stores house some animals during construction, the Humane Society is also actively seeking temporary foster homes for other animals. This two-pronged approach allows the organization to care for its full population while the renovation project continues. The strategy demonstrates how animal shelters can adapt when buildings require upgrades, maintaining both operations and community connections during challenging transitions.

The Humane Society's thrift stores are located at 3302 SE Federal Highway in Stuart and 1099 NW 21st Street in Stuart. For more information about store hours and guidelines for donating items, visit hstc1.org.
