Humane Society of the Treasure Coast thrift stores house cats during Palm City shelter renovation

The Humane Society of the Treasure Coast is finding creative solutions to keep its animal care operations running smoothly while undergoing major renovations at its Palm City shelter. The nonprofit began construction on its adoption, customer service, and intake areas earlier this year, which temporarily made many of the shelter's cages unavailable. To address this challenge, the organization has relocated many of its cats to its two thrift store locations throughout the renovation period.

According to spokesperson Sarah Fisher, the thrift stores have become essential spaces for housing animals during construction. "With our shelter cages unavailable, the stores have become a lifeline for our animals," Fisher explained. Senior Manager Kelly Ellis added that shoppers visiting either thrift store can now browse merchandise while also meeting adoptable cats. This dual-purpose approach allows the organization to continue its adoption efforts while maintaining its retail operations that support the animals' care.

The presence of adoptable cats in the thrift stores offers an unexpected benefit to the community. Ellis emphasized that purchases made at the stores directly support animal care. "Every treasure you find here helps provide for our animals," she noted. The organization welcomes donations of gently used goods from the community, which helps sustain operations during the construction period. Additionally, the Humane Society is actively seeking temporary foster homes for other animals while the renovation work continues at the main facility.

The thrift stores are located at 3302 SE Federal Highway in Stuart and 1099 NW 21st Street in Stuart. Community members interested in adopting a cat, shopping for treasured items, donating goods, or becoming a temporary foster can visit either location. More information about store hours and donation guidelines is available at hstc1.org. The solution demonstrates how local animal welfare organizations can adapt to challenges while maintaining both their adoption programs and community connections during facility improvements.
