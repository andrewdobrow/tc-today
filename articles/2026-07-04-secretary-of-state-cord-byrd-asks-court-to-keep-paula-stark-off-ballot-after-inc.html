Secretary of State Cord Byrd Seeks to Block Paula Stark From House District 47 Ballot After Filing Failure

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking a Leon Circuit Court to keep St. Cloud Republican Paula Stark off the ballot after she failed to submit required paperwork before the June 12 qualifying deadline. Stark filed her qualifying materials less than 30 minutes before the noon deadline, which state officials say prevented her from discovering a critical missing document. Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests required for all candidates, was never submitted by her treasurer and partner, Joel Davis.

Five state employees provided sworn written declarations stating that Davis never submitted Form 6 and was not accidentally handed it back when he submitted other paperwork during the final hour of the qualifying window. Election Records Division Bureau Chief Erica Ikay Reeve said in a court filing that three weeks after the deadline, Stark still has not submitted Form 6. According to Reeve, Stark was one of 12 candidates who failed to properly turn in the form, though half of them corrected the problems and qualified. Byrd argued that Stark and Davis knew Form 6 was required and could have discovered the missing document if they had not waited until the final hour to file their materials.

Reeve also documented a pattern of filing problems with Stark's campaign. The campaign has filed finance reports more than a month late, reported excessive contributions, and maintained an incorrect address on file. Audits in 2022 and 2024 found inconsistent transfers between campaign and office accounts. Additionally, a late report fine upheld by the Florida Elections Commission was referred to debt collection for nonpayment in November 2025. The state revoked Stark's Friends of Paula political committee in November due to over 16,000 dollars in unpaid fines from missed campaign finance deadlines.

If Stark remains off the ballot, the August Primary for House District 47 would be open to all voters to decide between Democrats Jorge Figueroa and Anthony Nieves. No other Republicans filed to run in the traditionally blue House District representing Osceola and Orange counties. Byrd emphasized the time constraints involved in the decision, noting that ballots must be mailed to overseas military and their families by July 4, with vote-by-mail ballots due out between July 9 and 16. The Secretary of State argued that reversing course would be costly and likely risk confusion in the results of this race and others on the ballot.
