Florida Secretary of State seeks to bar Rep. Paula Stark from ballot over missing financial disclosure

Florida Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking the court to keep Rep. Paula Stark off the ballot, disputing her account of a paperwork mix-up that left her disqualified from the race. Five state employees submitted sworn written declarations saying Stark's Treasurer and partner, Joel Davis, never submitted Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests, and was not accidentally handed it back when he submitted other paperwork in the final hour of the qualifying window on June 12. Stark's incomplete qualifying materials were timestamped less than 30 minutes before the noon deadline.

Byrd argued that if Stark or Davis had questioned why the Form 6 was allegedly handed back, the mistake would have been discovered while Davis was at the Division. Election Records Bureau Chief Erica Ikay Reeve said in a court declaration that three weeks after the deadline passed, Stark still has not submitted a Form 6. Reeve noted Stark was one of 12 candidates who did not properly turn in the form, though half of them corrected the problems and still qualified. Reeve also detailed issues with Davis's filings, including a campaign finance report filed over one month late, reported excessive contributions, and an incorrect address on file. Audits found inconsistent amounts transferred between campaign and office accounts in 2022 and 2024.
