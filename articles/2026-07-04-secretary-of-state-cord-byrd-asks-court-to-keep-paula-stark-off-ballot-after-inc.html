Secretary of State seeks to block St. Cloud Republican from ballot after last-minute filing error

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking courts to keep Paula Stark off the ballot for House District 47 after the St. Cloud Republican failed to properly submit required paperwork. In response to Stark's lawsuit seeking ballot access, Byrd's office argues that Stark's treasurer and partner, Joel Davis, never submitted Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests, to state employees. Nor was the form accidentally handed back when Davis submitted other paperwork in the final hour of the qualifying window on June 12. Stark's incomplete materials were timestamped less than 30 minutes before the noon deadline. Five state employees provided sworn written court declarations supporting the state's position that the form was never received.

Byrd said in his court response that Stark and Davis could have avoided this situation through basic care and caution. Had they questioned why the Form 6 was allegedly handed back, or had Davis not waited until the last minute to submit the documents, the missing form could have been caught and corrected before the deadline. Election Records Division Bureau Chief Erica Ikay Reeve noted that Stark was one of 12 candidates who did not properly submit Form 6, but half of them corrected the problems and still qualified. Three weeks after the qualifying deadline, Stark still has not submitted the form. Without seeing it, Reeve said, the state cannot review whether Stark would have met qualification requirements.

Reeve's court filing also detailed a pattern of problems with Stark's campaign paperwork over multiple election cycles. Davis has filed campaign finance reports more than a month late, reported excessive contributions, and has an incorrect address on file. In 2022 and 2024, audits found inconsistent fund transfers and balance issues. The state revoked Stark's Friends of Paula political committee in November due to more than 16,000 dollars in unpaid fines from missed campaign finance deadlines. A late report fine that was upheld by the Florida Elections Commission went unpaid and was referred to debt collection in November 2025.

The stakes are significant. If Stark remains off the ballot, the August Primary for House District 47 becomes open to all voters to decide between Democrats Jorge Figueroa and Anthony Nieves. No other Republicans filed to run in the traditionally blue House District representing Osceola and Orange counties. Byrd emphasized the tight timeline, noting that ballots must be mailed to overseas military and their families by July 4 and vote-by-mail ballots are scheduled to go out July 9-16. The Universal Primary Contest for House District 47 is already underway, and reversing course would be costly and likely create confusion, according to Byrd's filing.
