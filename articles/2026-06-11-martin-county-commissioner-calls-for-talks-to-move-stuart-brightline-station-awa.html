Martin County Commissioner seeks to move Brightline station away from downtown Stuart

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi has asked county staff to begin discussions with Brightline about relocating the planned train station from its current proposed location in downtown Stuart. The station was previously agreed to be built on a parking lot across from the courthouse in the downtown area. Ciampi suggested the fairgrounds as an alternative site, citing parking congestion and other issues at the downtown location as reasons to reconsider.

The commissioner's request comes in response to concerns raised by the FEC, which sent a letter to then-Stuart Mayor Christopher Collins stating it does not support a Brightline station in Stuart. According to the FEC, the proposed downtown station would violate U.S. Coast Guard rules because it would require the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge to remain lowered while the train stops at the station. Ciampi acknowledged that if the Army Corps of Engineers or U.S. Coast Guard determine the downtown location is too close to the bridge, the issue becomes moot for him. He argued that moving the station further south to the fairgrounds would eliminate the bridge proximity problem entirely.

The suggestion drew mixed responses from the community. Paul Gedwed, a resident, supported the idea, calling it a well-thought-out opinion given the congestion at the proposed downtown site. However, Steven Vitale, owner of the Colorado Inn, voiced concerns about moving the station away from downtown. He questioned why the county would build a train station that people cannot easily walk to, noting that most people would want to access downtown Stuart. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, expressed a different perspective, saying her downtown store would benefit either way the county decides, but urged officials to make a decision and proceed with the project regardless of location, as long as it remains in Martin County.

County officials clarified that Ciampi's comments do not constitute official direction for staff to implement changes. The board would need to formally discuss the relocation proposal and vote on it before staff could begin exploring alternative sites. Meanwhile, Martin County continues waiting for approval on its grant application to fund the Brightline station project. Until the funding is secured, the project cannot move forward with either location.
