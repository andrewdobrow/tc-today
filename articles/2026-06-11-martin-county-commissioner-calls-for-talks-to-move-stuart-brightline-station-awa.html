Martin County Commissioner pushes to relocate Brightline station away from downtown Stuart

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi is calling for discussions between county staff and Brightline leadership about moving the planned train station from its current downtown Stuart location. The station was previously agreed to be built on a parking lot site across from the courthouse in downtown Stuart. Ciampi suggested the fairgrounds as a potential alternative location, citing parking and other congestion concerns with the downtown spot. "I would ask our staff to begin a conversation and discussions with Brightline leadership for the possibility of changing the location from where it is currently, which creates parking issues and other issues," Ciampi said.

The commissioner's proposal comes after the Florida East Coast Railway sent a letter to then-Stuart Mayor Christopher Collins expressing opposition to a Brightline station in Stuart. The FEC stated that the proposed station would violate U.S. Coast Guard rules because the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge would need to remain lowered while the train stops at the station. Ciampi explained his reasoning for exploring alternatives, saying that if regulators like the Army Corps of Engineers or U.S. Coast Guard determine the station location is problematic, the downtown site becomes unviable. He argued that moving the station further south to the fairgrounds would eliminate this regulatory obstacle entirely.

Community members expressed divided opinions about the relocation proposal. Paul Gedwed, a resident, supported the idea as reasonable given the congestion issues at the proposed downtown location. However, Steven Vitale, who owns the Colorado Inn, disagreed, contending that most visitors would want to access downtown Stuart and questioned why the county would build a train station people cannot walk to from their destination. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, took a more pragmatic approach, indicating her downtown business would benefit either way and urged officials to move forward with construction regardless of the final location, as long as it remains in Martin County.

County officials emphasized that Ciampi's comments do not constitute formal direction to staff. Any action on exploring alternative locations would require the full board to discuss and vote on the matter before staff can proceed. The county is currently awaiting a decision on its grant application to fund the Brightline station project. Until that funding is secured, the relocation discussion remains secondary to obtaining the necessary financial resources to move forward with construction.
