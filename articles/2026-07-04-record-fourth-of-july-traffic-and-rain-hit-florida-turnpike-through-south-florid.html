Record Fourth of July travel brings heavy traffic and rain to Florida roads

A record number of Americans are traveling this Fourth of July weekend, with South Florida drivers facing heavy rain and crowded roads. Friday's wet weather created challenging conditions on the Florida Turnpike heading south of Orlando. "Really beautiful until it starts raining and then everyone forgets how to drive," driver Ed Budd said. Despite the conditions, some drivers managed the journey smoothly. Driver Jim Macfawn commented on the scale of travel this year, saying "It sounds like a lot. I'm surprised people can afford all the gas."

AAA estimates 72.2 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, marking an all-time high for the holiday. Of that number, 61.4 million are making the trip by car. Orlando and Miami rank among the top five travel destinations this Independence Day, according to AAA. Experiences varied among drivers: Carlos Borras and his wife made the round trip between Orlando and Miami on Friday and reported no traffic or accidents, while driver Fernando Amado described busy conditions, noting "It's been pretty busy, especially with the weather." AAA recommends hitting the road after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to avoid the worst congestion, with the heaviest traffic expected between 10 a.m. and noon on Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday.
