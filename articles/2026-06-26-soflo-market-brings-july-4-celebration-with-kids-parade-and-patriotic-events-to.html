SoFlo Market brings July 4 celebration with kids parade and patriotic events to Boca Raton City Hall

The SoFlo Market is transforming Boca Raton City Hall into a red, white and blue celebration this Independence Day. Founder and organizer Tatiana Freitas told WPTV that the free monthly market's mission is to uplift small local businesses and bring the community together. For the holiday edition, organizers are adding patriotic touches throughout the event. Highlights include a kids parade, live DJ, unique vendors, gourmet food offerings, and free gifts for the first 20 children who arrive.

Family entertainment takes center stage with activities designed for all ages. Children can enjoy face painting, a foggy bubble station, mini golf, and balloon twisting. The market will also feature its signature handmade crafts and desserts. A live appearance by the SoFlo Market's flamboyant flamingo mascot is expected to be a crowd favorite, adding to the festive atmosphere.

The event emphasizes community connections through local partnerships. Boca Raton firefighters and police officers have been invited to participate, reinforcing the family-focused environment. Freitas' son Kyle and husband Eddie are part of the organizing team working to ensure activities run smoothly. On-site generators will power the event, and strong cell service will allow visitors to share moments instantly on social media. The market's Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and YouTube channels will feature behind-the-scenes content throughout the day.

The SoFlo Market takes place Saturday, July 4, 2026 from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. at Boca Raton City Hall, located at 201 West Palmetto Park Road. Admission is free. While the market runs monthly, Freitas says the Independence Day edition always stands out as a particularly special event. Independent markets like SoFlo are increasingly important to local economies, offering exposure for small businesses while creating spaces where neighbors can connect. By combining the market experience with Fourth of July traditions, organizers are making a statement of community pride. Freitas emphasized the event's importance: "We want to make people proud to call this place home."
