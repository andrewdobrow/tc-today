SoFlo Market hosts Fourth of July celebration at Boca Raton City Hall

Founder Tatiana Freitas is preparing the SoFlo Market for a Fourth of July celebration at Boca Raton City Hall. The free monthly market event will draw families, food lovers and shopping enthusiasts from across Palm Beach County on Saturday, July 4. According to Freitas, the market's core purpose is to support small local businesses while strengthening community connections. For Independence Day, the organizing team is expanding the experience with patriotic themed activities and entertainment designed to appeal to all ages.

The celebration will feature a kids' parade, DJ entertainment, handmade crafts from local vendors, gourmet food options and desserts. Children will enjoy face painting, a foggy bubble station, mini golf and balloon twisting throughout the day. The first 20 children to arrive will receive free gifts. The SoFlo Market's flamingo mascot, known for its flamboyant personality, is expected to be a highlight for visitors. The market will run from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. at Boca Raton City Hall, located at 201 West Palmetto Park Road.

The event emphasizes community involvement and partnerships. Local firefighters and police officers have been invited to participate, reinforcing the family-focused atmosphere organizers aim to create. Freitas' son Kyle and husband Eddie are part of the team managing event operations. On-site generators will provide power for vendors and entertainment, while strong cell service will allow attendees to share their experience on social media. The market's social media channels, including Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and YouTube, will feature behind-the-scenes content and photos from the celebration.

While the SoFlo Market operates monthly, Freitas highlights the July Fourth edition as particularly significant. She emphasized the importance of such independent markets for local economies, noting they provide crucial visibility for small businesses and create gathering spaces for neighbors. "We want to make people proud to call this place home," she said. Admission to the event is completely free. For those interested in learning more about what makes their community special, Freitas encourages people to reach out and share their stories.
