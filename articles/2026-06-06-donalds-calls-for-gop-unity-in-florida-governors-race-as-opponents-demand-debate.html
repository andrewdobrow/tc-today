Byron Donalds calls for GOP unity ahead of Florida gubernatorial primary as opponents demand debates

Byron Donalds declared Friday that the general election has effectively begun following Jerry Demings' withdrawal from the Democratic primary for Governor due to a prostate cancer diagnosis. The Naples Republican, who has raised $81 million, argued that Republicans should unite behind his candidacy more than two months before the August 18 primary election. With Democrats having a presumptive nominee in the wake of Demings' departure, Donalds said he would rather direct his substantial financial resources toward the general election than continue competing in the Republican primary.

Donalds pointed to his complete and total endorsement from President Donald Trump and his lead of more than 40 points in multiple public polls as evidence of his viability as the party's nominee. He contrasted his record with that of his Democratic opponent, saying the differences between himself and David Jolly could not be more stark. Jolly would end the Florida Dream and transform the state into another Virginia, Illinois, California, or New York, Donalds said, referencing the migration of residents fleeing blue state governance. He called on the Republican party to unite and focus on defending Governor DeSantis's accomplishments and keeping Florida free.

Rather than participate in debates, Donalds said he would continue engaging voters through direct contact methods including town halls, community meetings, business roundtables, and door-to-door canvassing. He noted that all other candidates remain in single digits and argued that fighting amongst Republicans only helps Democrats achieve their goals. My campaign is committed to town halls, community meetings, business roundtables, and door knocking to take our message of conservatism and common sense directly to the people of Florida, Donalds said.

Donalds' debate avoidance has drawn criticism from opponents. Jay Collins launched The Collins Callout website Friday, writing that the only question left is whether Byron answers the bell, or keeps running. Former House Speaker Paul Renner posted a video to social media urging Donalds to stop ducking and get on stage, stating that Floridians deserve to see the differences between candidates. Renner also said neither Collins nor Donalds has been willing to debate him. Updated fundraising reports covering activity through May are due June 10. Through the end of March, Renner had raised more than $6 million while Collins had raised roughly $1.75 million.
