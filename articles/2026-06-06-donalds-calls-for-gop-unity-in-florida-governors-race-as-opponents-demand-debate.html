Byron Donalds holds 46-point lead in Florida GOP gubernatorial primary, new poll shows

Congressman Byron Donalds is dominating the Republican primary race for Florida governor, according to a new poll from the Associated Industries of Florida. The survey shows Donalds capturing 54 percent support among Republican primary voters, giving him a 46-point advantage over his nearest competitor. Conducted June 3-7 among 386 Republican primary voters, the poll found James Fishback at 8 percent, Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins at 5 percent, and former House Speaker Paul Renner at 2 percent. Nearly a third of respondents, 31 percent, remain undecided about the race.

Donalds demonstrates strong support across Florida's major population centers and demographic groups. In Fort Myers, he leads with 71 percent support, followed by 69 percent in West Palm Beach, 54 percent in Orlando, 53 percent in the Panhandle, and 51 percent in Tampa. Among white Republicans, Donalds draws 55 percent support, while earning 54 percent among Hispanic Republicans. Senior voters, who typically show the highest participation rates in primary elections, back Donalds at 60 percent. These results align with previous polling data showing his consistent strength. An Emerson College survey in April found Donalds at 46 percent in the primary, with Collins and Fishback each at 4 percent.

Donalds enters the primary race with significant financial and political advantages. He has raised more than 81 million dollars through his campaign and the Friends of Byron Donalds PAC from over 35,000 donors. He also carries the complete endorsement of President Donald Trump. The campaign has constructed an extensive field apparatus throughout Florida to prepare for early voting, which begins in two months. An April poll by Echelon Insights showed Donalds leading Democrat David Jolly 49 percent to 43 percent in a potential general election matchup.

Jeremy Sheftel, AIF vice president of Political Operations, said the polling demonstrates Donalds' commanding position heading into the primary. Ryan Smith, the campaign's chief strategist, emphasized that Florida Republicans are uniting behind Donalds and his message about making the state more affordable. Smith characterized Donalds as a common sense conservative who will defeat liberal candidates. The primary election is scheduled for August 18, with the Republican and Democratic nominees advancing to the November 3 general election. The Associated Industries of Florida will present the full poll findings at its Summer Political and Policy Retreat in Sarasota.
