Byron Donalds calls for GOP unity as primary opponents demand debates

Byron Donalds declared himself the front-runner in the Republican gubernatorial race Friday and urged the party to unite behind his candidacy more than two months before the August 18 primary election. The Naples congressman made his appeal for party cohesion hours after Democrat Jerry Demings withdrew from the gubernatorial contest due to a prostate cancer diagnosis. With Demings out of the Democratic primary, Donalds argued that the general election effectively begins now, making it imperative for Republicans to consolidate support rather than engage in divisive primary battles.

Donalds pointed to several factors as evidence of his strength and viability. He cited his complete and total endorsement from President Donald Trump and highlighted polling data showing him leading by more than 40 points in multiple public surveys. The congressman has raised $81 million for his campaign so far and indicated he would prefer to preserve those resources for the general election rather than spend them fighting rival Republicans in the primary. He stressed that all other candidates currently poll in single digits and contended that continued infighting among Republicans only benefits Democrats. "At this point, fighting amongst Republicans only helps the Democrats end the Florida Dream we all love and cherish," Donalds said. He pledged to communicate directly with voters through town halls, community meetings, business roundtables, and door-knocking efforts rather than through debate stage appearances.

However, two of Donalds' main competitors reject his call for unity and are demanding that he participate in debates. Jay Collins launched The Collins Callout website Friday, framing the challenge in confrontational terms. "The callout is on the table, the only question left is whether Byron answers the bell, or keeps running," the site stated. Former House Speaker Paul Renner went further, releasing a video on social media claiming that neither Donalds nor Collins is willing to debate him. "Floridians deserve to see the differences between the candidates. Stop ducking and let's get on stage," Renner said.

The candidates' fundraising totals through the end of March reveal significant gaps in their resources. Donalds has far outraised his opponents, while Renner had accumulated more than $6 million and Collins approximately $1.75 million. Updated fundraising reports covering activity through May will be due June 10, providing fresh indicators of financial momentum heading into the final stretch of the primary campaign. These figures underscore Donalds' substantial advantage in the field as the race enters its final phase.
