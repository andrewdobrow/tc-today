Donalds calls for GOP unity in Florida governor's race as opponents demand debates

Byron Donalds is calling on Republicans to unite behind his candidacy for Florida governor, arguing that the party should coalesce around him more than two months before the August primary election. The Naples Republican made this pitch for party unity on Friday, following Democrat Jerry Demings' announcement that he was withdrawing from the gubernatorial race due to a prostate cancer diagnosis. With Demings' departure, Donalds said "the general election starts today," signaling his view that the Democratic side is effectively settled with a presumptive nominee. He has raised $81 million to date and indicated he would prefer to spend those resources in the general election rather than continue competing in the Republican primary.

Donalds is positioning himself as the clear frontrunner based on his fundraising advantage and polling strength. He cites a "complete and total endorsement" from President Donald Trump and notes he leads by more than 40 points in multiple public polls. He argues that his opponents have not demonstrated comparable strength, with all other candidates polling in single digits. Donalds also contrasted his vision with that of Democrat David Jolly, saying their differences are stark and that Jolly would undermine Florida's appeal to people fleeing blue state governance. Donalds pledged to continue engaging voters through town halls, community meetings, business roundtables, and direct door-to-door canvassing, but notably refused to commit to participating in debates.

His opponents are pushing back against his debate avoidance. Jay Collins launched a website called The Collins Callout on Friday with messaging that acknowledges Donalds as the prohibitive frontrunner while challenging him to face off on stage. Former House Speaker Paul Renner went further, posting a video on social media in which he called out both Collins and Donalds for refusing to debate. "Floridians deserve to see the differences between the candidates. Stop ducking and let's get on stage," Renner said in his video appeal.

The financial picture shows a significant disparity among the Republican candidates. Through the end of March, Renner had raised more than $6 million and Collins approximately $1.75 million, according to the most recent available reports. Both candidates are expected to file updated fundraising reports covering activity through May on June 10, which should provide additional insight into the strength of their campaigns heading into the final stretch before the primary election.
