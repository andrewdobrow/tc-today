England's World Cup team trains in Palm Beach Gardens after win over New Zealand

England secured a 1-0 victory over New Zealand in a pre-World Cup friendly match held in Tampa over the weekend, with Harry Kane scoring the decisive goal with a header. Following this preparation match, the English national team has relocated to Palm Beach Gardens for additional training sessions before moving to their Kansas City base camp this weekend. The team also has another warm-up game scheduled in Orlando before the World Cup tournament begins. England's opening World Cup match is set for next Wednesday against Croatia.

Manager Thomas Tuchel emphasized the team's commitment to progressing during their time in Palm Beach Gardens. "We expect to push tomorrow physical and from intensity and style of play, ball speed and everything, want to take the next step," Tuchel said about the upcoming practice sessions. Tuchel highlighted the quality of the facilities available to the team during their stay in the area. Star defender Marc Guehi shared similar sentiments about the accommodations and venue, noting the positive experience the squad has had since arriving. "Great, fantastic. Everyone is super friendly and welcoming to us, felt at home straight away here, and in West Palm Beach. The quality of the pitch, really good so far," Guehi said. However, Guehi acknowledged that adapting to Florida's summer heat presented a challenge for the players. He explained that while the first day was extremely hot, the team has been gradually adjusting to the weather conditions.

The training facilities in Palm Beach Gardens employ specific techniques to optimize playing conditions. At Gardens District Park, staff water the fields moments before the team arrives for practice. Andrew Keller, Palm Beach Gardens Director of Agronomy, explained the reasoning behind this preparation. The moisture on the grass helps the ball move more quickly across the field rather than catching on the grass and rolling, allowing players to practice at speeds that replicate actual stadium conditions. Keller noted that professional stadiums also water their fields before matches.

England's presence in South Florida represents part of broader World Cup preparations happening in the region. After the English team departs for Kansas City, Portugal's national team will use the same facilities in Palm Beach Gardens for their own training sessions. This rotation of international teams demonstrates the area's importance as a training destination for World Cup participants preparing for the tournament.
