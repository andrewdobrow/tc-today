40-year-old driver killed in head-on crash on State Road 710 in Martin County

A 40-year-old man died early Sunday morning following a head-on collision on State Road 710 in Martin County, the Florida Highway Patrol reported. The crash occurred around 2 a.m. near the intersection of State Road 710, also known as Beeline Highway, and Southwest Silver Fox Lane. A northbound 2015 Kia Optima crossed into the southbound lane and struck a 2021 Toyota Tundra in a direct collision. The impact caused the Kia to burst into flames, and it came to rest on the grass shoulder facing north. The Tundra remained in the southbound lane, facing southeast. The Kia driver was pronounced dead at the scene. The Tundra driver, a 41-year-old man from Indiantown, was airlifted to the hospital by LifeStar medical helicopter.

The deadly crash is just the latest in a troubling pattern on the roadway. Martin County Fire Rescue has responded to 42 vehicle accident calls on State Road 710 this year alone. Following a fatal crash in January, Martin County Sheriff John Budensiek described the road as a "death trap." Residents and local officials have grown increasingly frustrated with safety conditions on the stretch of highway. Bill Townsend, who lives near the road, pointed to driver behavior as a contributing factor. "People don't pay a whole lot of attention to other people on the road, and it's kind of a dangerous road sometimes," Townsend said. "Everybody's always in a hurry... just living here makes me worry about the young people learning to drive and learning to drive on that road concerns me, because I have young kids."

State officials have long-term plans to address the road's safety issues. The Florida Department of Transportation intends to widen State Road 710 from two lanes to four lanes, though construction is not expected to begin until 2031. This timeline has drawn criticism from local leaders who question how many more fatalities will occur before improvements are completed. "The question will be how many more people have to die before we get that project going. Florida touts that they have the safest roads in the country but this road is one that clearly is not in that category," Budensiek said in January.

While residents await major construction, FDOT has launched interim improvements including pavement repairs, updated markings, new signage, and additional turn lanes. However, Townsend and other residents say these measures are insufficient. "It would be beneficial to the people that live and travel that road all the time if something happened faster," Townsend said, emphasizing the urgent need for more comprehensive safety improvements to prevent further tragedies.
