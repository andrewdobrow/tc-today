Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League urges hurricane prep for pets with microchips, emergency kits

As hurricane season intensifies across South Florida, Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League is encouraging residents to develop comprehensive disaster plans for their pets. Dr. Alyssa Comroe, CEO of Peggy Adams, emphasizes that pets are safest during hurricanes when they stay with their owners. The organization's expert team has outlined essential preparation steps that every pet owner should take before the next storm arrives.

The shelter recommends several critical safety measures starting with identification. Pets should wear collars with current ID tags and have updated microchips registered with accurate contact information. Equally important is preparing pets mentally for storms by introducing them to a designated safe room well in advance. This space might be a walk-in closet or downstairs bathroom. Practice runs inside the safe room before hurricane season arrives help reduce stress and confusion when an actual storm threatens.

Pet owners need to assemble a comprehensive emergency kit containing supplies for at least three days. The kit should include food and water stored in airtight containers, bowls, a sturdy carrier, bedding, towels, medications for two weeks, vaccination records, and first-aid supplies. Cat owners should add litter and a litter box. The kit should also contain leashes, harnesses, cool packs, wipes, waste bags, and multiple forms of visible identification. Keeping a printed list of local pet-friendly shelters and hotels in an easily accessible location ensures pet owners know where to go if evacuation becomes necessary. Recent photos of pets, ideally showing them with their owners, are invaluable for identification if pets become separated during the chaos of a storm.

The shelter also recommends conducting hurricane drills that incorporate pets' normal daily routines, including feeding, water access, and rest periods inside the safe room. These practice sessions help maximize comfort when an actual emergency occurs. Pet owners should remember that Florida's hurricane season runs from June through November, a period that coincides with extreme summer heat. Beyond storm preparation, year-round heat safety measures such as providing water enrichment, adequate shade, and avoiding midday walks help protect pets throughout the season. Taking time to prepare now significantly reduces risks for pets during evacuations and emergency situations.
