Cornell Art Museum in Delray Beach opens upcycled art exhibit running through September

The Cornell Art Museum at Old School Square in Delray Beach is showcasing "Persistence of Matter," a new summer exhibition dedicated to artists who transform discarded materials into compelling artwork. The show reflects the museum's commitment to blending artistic expression with positive community engagement, according to Marusca Gatto, Cultural Arts Director for the Delray Beach Downtown Development Authority. Guest curators Mavis Benson and Pamela Lambie selected artists creating sculptures, installations, and mixed media works entirely from reclaimed objects.

The exhibition highlights several notable artists, including Autumn Kioti, whose creations draw inspiration from both natural and urban environments. Another featured artist, Mike Silverman, presents his "Self-Reflection" series, which transforms discarded computer hard drive platters and printed circuit boards into reflective sculptures. Silverman explained that his pieces are designed to help viewers reconsider their relationship with technology and waste, inviting them to see themselves reflected in materials typically destined for landfills.

As environmental concerns grow across communities, this exhibition serves as a visual conversation about sustainability and responsible consumption. "Persistence of Matter" demonstrates how artistic innovation can highlight the ongoing life cycles of discarded materials while promoting environmental awareness through creative beauty. The show encourages visitors to think differently about waste and its potential for transformation into meaningful art.

The exhibition runs through September 10, 2026, and includes several programming opportunities for deeper engagement. Visitors can attend artist talks, participate in salon discussions, and watch film screenings throughout the exhibition period. The museum maintains regular hours: Wednesday noon to 5 p.m., Thursday and Friday noon to 7 p.m., Saturday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Sunday noon to 5 p.m. For more information about upcoming events and programs, visitors can call 561-654-2220.
