Cornell Art Museum in Delray Beach opens 'Persistence of Matter' exhibit featuring upcycled art

The Cornell Art Museum at Old School Square in Delray Beach has launched a new summer exhibition called Persistence of Matter that celebrates artists transforming waste materials into compelling artwork. The show aligns with the museum's commitment to combining artistic expression with meaningful community engagement, according to Marusca Gatto, Cultural Arts Director for the Delray Beach Downtown Development Authority.

Guest curators Mavis Benson and Pamela Lambie assembled a collection of artists working across sculptures, installations, and mixed media pieces created entirely from reclaimed and discarded materials. The exhibition showcases the work of artists like Autumn Kioti, whose creations blend natural and urban elements, and Mike Silverman, who developed his Self-Reflection series using components salvaged from old computer hardware, including hard drive platters and printed circuit boards. Silverman explained that his work encourages viewers to reconsider their own connection to technology and the waste it generates, inviting them to see these materials in a completely different way.

The exhibition runs through September 10, 2026, and offers visitors multiple opportunities to engage with the artists and themes. Programming includes artist talks, salon discussions, and film screenings designed to deepen conversations about the work on display. The museum welcomes visitors Wednesday through Sunday, with hours varying by day. Wednesday hours are noon to 5 p.m., Thursday and Friday from noon to 7 p.m., Saturday from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Sunday from noon to 5 p.m. Those interested in attending specific events can call 561-654-2220 for additional details.

As communities increasingly grapple with sustainability challenges, Persistence of Matter provides an artistic perspective on the enduring cycles of the materials people discard daily. The exhibition demonstrates how creative innovation can transform environmental concerns into beautiful, thought-provoking art. By presenting upcycled materials in gallery settings, the show promotes broader discussions about environmental responsibility and what it means to reconsider waste in contemporary society. The combination of striking visual presentations and educational programming encourages visitors to reflect on their relationship with consumption and disposal while appreciating the skill and vision artists bring to reimagining discarded objects.
