Port St. Lucie couple opposes rezoning of lot behind their home from residential to commercial

A Port St. Lucie couple is searching for answers after the city council voted unanimously to rezone the lot behind their home from residential to commercial, despite their months-long opposition. Joseph and Margaret Mucciolo say their backyard views of nature and sense of quiet relaxation that drew them to purchase the property are now under threat. Margaret Mucciolo told reporters she saw the beautiful setting and told the real estate agent to get ready to make an offer because it just felt like home. Joseph Mucciolo said that in six months or a year, the trees currently standing behind their yard are going to be gone.

The Port St. Lucie City Council approved changing the lot behind their yard where trees currently stand to be rezoned from residential to commercial. The council says the change is necessary to reduce traffic in the neighborhood. One council member explained that adding commercial closer to neighborhoods reduces the length of trips, which reduces overall traffic because if you have commercial closer to you, your trips are shorter and it reduces the burden on the overall network. The Mucciolos have fought the rezoning for months and feel their voices have gone unheard throughout the process.

The couple spoke at Monday's meeting voicing their concerns, but they feel the council left them unheard, leaving them searching for answers about where to go from here. Joseph Mucciolo questioned whether they can make an objection and have this looked at again. The council held its second reading Monday, and while there was public comment, it was what's called a quasi-judicial hearing. This means council members act as a panel of judges applying existing zoning rules to a specific property and making decisions based strictly on sworn testimony and factual evidence, not public opinion.

Margaret Mucciolo said the process needs to change until it's logical, transparent, caring, and provides hope for people. She emphasized that this is her home and expressed frustration with feeling ignored except by the news media. The couple says they will be exploring any kind of appeal process to change this outcome or to change the next one. Their fight represents a broader concern about how rezoning decisions impact longtime residents who purchased their homes based on the existing character of their neighborhoods.
