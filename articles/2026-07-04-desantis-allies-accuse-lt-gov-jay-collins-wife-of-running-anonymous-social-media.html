DeSantis allies accuse Lieutenant Governor's wife of running anonymous accounts attacking Governor

Several social media accounts were shut down after prominent members of Ron DeSantis' administration alleged that Layla Collins, wife of Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins, operated anonymous profiles used to criticize the governor. Christina Pushaw, a Communications Specialist in the Governor's Office, and Jeremy Redfern, Deputy Chief of Staff to Attorney General James Uthmeier, both presented evidence they said connected the accounts to the Second Lady. The accounts were deactivated shortly after the accusations became public.

Pushaw shared information from Promota CEO Robert Salvador showing that the account @FSUdude01670 appeared linked to a phone number and email address similar to those known to belong to Layla Collins. Florida Politics obtained screenshots from two-factor authentication attempts that displayed partial information for both an email address and phone number. Pushaw also identified a second account, @bobnamy1, which she claimed was tied to Layla Collins. According to Pushaw's posts, the account content focused exclusively on promoting Jay Collins and attacking those who did not endorse him, including the governor himself. Redfern confirmed the connection, stating that the FSUDude account was directly tied to Layla Collins' personal cell phone number.

The controversy has intensified during an already contentious gubernatorial race. DeSantis appointed Jay Collins as Lieutenant Governor last year, and he subsequently appointed Layla Collins to the state Board of Education. Both Pushaw and Redfern previously served as Press Secretary for DeSantis. Redfern currently works for Uthmeier, who has endorsed U.S. Representative Byron Donalds for governor. Meanwhile, Collins is struggling in the race, having raised only about $15,500 in hard money during the week through June 26 and holding less than $3.5 million total as of that date. Donalds has consolidated endorsements from major state officials including Attorney General James Uthmeier, Agriculture Secretary Wilton Simpson, and incoming Florida Senate President Jim Boyd.

Collins has not publicly responded to the allegations about his wife's social media accounts. Florida Politics requested comment from both Jay and Layla Collins but had not received a response as of publication. The incident adds significant complexity to DeSantis' final months in office and raises questions about dynamics within the administration as the primary election approaches. The situation represents an unusual breach of internal party harmony during what was supposed to be a primary focused on selecting DeSantis' successor.
