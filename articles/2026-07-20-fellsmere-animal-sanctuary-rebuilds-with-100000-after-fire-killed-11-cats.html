Fellsmere animal sanctuary rebuilds after fire with more than $100,000 in community support

For the Love of Paws, a Fellsmere animal sanctuary founded in 2014 by Ted Pankiewicz in memory of his late wife, is rebuilding after a devastating fire destroyed the facility on March 9. The blaze killed 11 cats and burned Pankiewicz's arms as he tried to save the animals. Since then, the community has raised more than $100,000 to help him rebuild.

Pankiewicz explained that starting a rescue or something similar was always something his late wife wanted to do. The sanctuary operates differently than a traditional rescue, serving as a permanent home for animals rather than a temporary shelter. For the Love of Paws supplies 12,000 to 15,000 pounds of pet food monthly to senior citizens, disabled individuals, and veterans who struggle to feed their pets. Pankiewicz told reporters that when pets arrive at the sanctuary, they stay for the rest of their lives and are cared for as family.

The new facility will be constructed entirely of steel and will include a security system that alerts the fire department directly when smoke is detected. An interior sprinkler system will also be installed. Pankiewicz said the smoke detectors will immediately alert the fire department and acknowledged the sprinklers will likely upset the cats but added that the safety measures are necessary.

The rebuilt sanctuary is expected to be a cage-free space with an outdoor catio where cats can roam freely. Pankiewicz described the community response as deeply emotional, explaining that the fire made them cry tears of sorrow while the outpouring of support brought tears of gratitude. The sanctuary, which serves some of the Treasure Coast's most vulnerable residents and their pets, continues to work toward reopening in its new steel structure with enhanced safety features following the March tragedy.
