Fourth of July cookout costs rise 4 percent as inflation hits holiday food prices

The Fourth of July tradition of cookouts, grilling and picnics comes with a higher price tag this year. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, a holiday meal for 10 now costs $73.82, up 4 percent from a year ago. The survey points squarely at inflation as the cause of rising prices. Ground beef is up 5.5 percent, and buns are up 7.7 percent. Strawberries, hit hard by a Florida frost, are up 12.4 percent. Ice cream is up 5.3 percent.

Not everything is more expensive, though. Potato prices are down this year, which is good news for potato chip and salad lovers. Corn prices have fluctuated, but locally grown corn from the Glades can offer the best deal. These lower prices on select items provide some relief for shoppers planning their holiday celebrations, though the overall trend shows an increase in costs compared to last year.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch said there are ways to keep costs in check despite the rising prices. "It's really important to shop savvier than ever, it is comparing the stores to see who has the best deals for the groceries on your list, the biggest thing for families is cutting down the food waste, and that begins with a meal plan," Woroch said. Her advice emphasizes the importance of strategic shopping and planning ahead to make the most of grocery budgets during the holiday.

Local shoppers described their plans for Fourth of July meals despite the higher costs. One shopper said Friday they were planning hamburgers, unfortunately not on the grill because they live in a condo, with coleslaw, chips and maybe some beer for lunch. For dinner, they planned burnt ends, mashed potatoes and fresh broccoli. Another shopper said they were cooking hamburgers, beef, steak and baked potato for their celebration. The varied menu plans show families are still committed to traditional holiday meals even as inflation affects grocery prices across multiple food categories.
