Port St. Lucie 9-year-old launches junior chess club to teach kids the game

Orion Jean Baptiste, a 9-year-old from Port St. Lucie, started a junior chess club because he wanted to share his enthusiasm for the game with other children in his community. Baptiste picked up chess at age 5 while playing with family members and developed a genuine passion for the sport. He recognized that many young people focus primarily on technology and wanted to encourage them to value board games instead. "I just wanted kids to learn about chess," Baptiste said. "Everybody's more focused on like tech stuff. I'm hoping that more people have respect for board games."

The club has grown quickly since its launch, attracting more than 36 junior members who gather every Friday at the Port St. Lucie library. Diana Cowan Kempton, who serves as the program director, believes chess offers significant benefits for young people's development. She explained that the game can help build the next generation of leaders by making them more focused, patient, strategic, and thoughtful in their decision-making. The club welcomes players of all skill levels, creating an inclusive environment for both beginners and experienced players.

Parents have responded positively to the club's arrival in the community. Thomas Furlong brought his children to participate because he saw it as an ideal opportunity for his daughter to learn chess while helping his son improve his existing skills. He also appreciated that the setting gave his children a chance to engage in friendly competition with each other. Francesca Furlong, one of the newest members, enjoys the social aspect most. "I like playing with other kids," she said about her favorite part of attending the club.

The Atlantis Chess Club is establishing itself as a fixture in the Port St. Lucie community through its weekly Friday meetings at the library. Looking ahead, the organization plans to expand its reach by launching two additional programs at the Fenn Center in Fort Pierce. This growth reflects the growing interest in chess among young people and suggests that Baptiste's initial vision of building respect for board games is resonating with families in the area.
