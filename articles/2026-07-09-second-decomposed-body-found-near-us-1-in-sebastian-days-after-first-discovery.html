Two bodies found near Sebastian remain under investigation, appear unconnected but suspicious, sheriff says

The Indian River County Sheriff's Office said Thursday that two bodies found near Sebastian this week appear unconnected but remain under investigation. James Thompson, 44, a Brevard County resident, was found Monday on the west shoreline near U.S. Highway 1 and the north county line by a person fishing in the area. On Wednesday shortly after 4 p.m., a second body, heavily decomposed, was located in shrubbery along the east shoreline near 14510 US Highway 1, just north of Sebastian, across the street from Monday's location.

Sheriff Eric Flowers said at a Thursday morning news conference that Thompson had been staying with his girlfriend at a hotel in Sebastian and was last seen Sunday walking northbound on U.S. Highway 1 around 1:30 p.m. Thompson had a history of fentanyl use and mental health issues and had made a purchase of methamphetamine on July 3, entering a manic state, according to Flowers. The sheriff said Thompson had been to mental health facilities in Brevard County many times. The second body was a 65-year-old man with a temporary address with his brother in Sebastian who was out of jail on pretrial release after being arrested by Plantation police on a charge of aggravated battery with a deadly weapon in Broward County. The man had a serious history of seizures and walked away from a hospital Sunday morning against medical advice. The man's brother took him to the hospital Sunday, and he was still wearing hospital socks when he left. His brother last saw him walking north at 7:30 a.m. Sunday.

Flowers said autopsies on both bodies revealed no stab wounds or evidence of violence. Thompson had only a minor contusion on his eyebrow. The sheriff said the investigation remains active until toxicology results determine the cause of death. Flowers said both cases are still considered suspicious, noting that any death that is not a natural death is suspicious. He warned that bad fentanyl or methamphetamine could be on the streets and urged drug users to seek treatment. Plantation police had obtained a warrant for the 65-year-old man for absconding on his pretrial release by being in Indian River County.

Jim Sullivan, a resident who lives in the area, said the community is very nice and has low crime. Sullivan said the two bodies were found about a football field's length apart and within a straight line of sight from his bedroom window. He said it was comforting to know the deaths appear unrelated but still disconcerting. Anyone with information in either case is asked to contact the Indian River County Sheriff's Office or Treasure Coast Crime Stoppers to remain anonymous.
