Byron Donalds skips Florida GOP governor debate, dominates discussion anyway as rivals split anti-frontrunner vote

U.S. Representative Byron Donalds, the Trump-backed frontrunner in Florida's Republican gubernatorial race, refused to participate in the state's first major GOP debate Thursday night but became the central focus of the two-hour event. Former House Speaker Paul Renner, Lt. Jay Collins and businessman James Fishback took the stage and mentioned Donalds by name at least 60 times as each tried to position himself as the strongest alternative to the absent candidate.

Moderator Patrick Bet-David opened the debate by calling Donalds "the elephant not in the room" and explained that Donalds declined to attend because he has raised roughly $100 million and polls between 48% and 58%. Bet-David said Donalds told him he would only consider debating once another candidate hits double digits in polling, summarizing Donalds' response as "Till then, I'm not showing up." Collins criticized Donalds for skipping the event, saying "We don't do kings in the United States of America. We sure don't do them in Florida." He added that if Donalds wants the governor's mansion, "you've got to grow a spine, you've got to stand up, and you've got to fight for the people of Florida." Renner called Donalds' absence disrespectful and argued that every voter in Florida deserves a debate. Fishback contrasted Donalds' fundraising advantage with his own campaign's claim to be the first Florida gubernatorial campaign to visit all 67 counties. Donalds told WPLG previously that "If the race was close, and we were battling it out, we would be debating right now."

The debate centered on property taxes, insurance, affordability and hyperscale data centers. Fishback promised to completely abolish property taxes in Florida and take a "chainsaw" to local government spending. Collins said cities and counties have a spending problem and need more accountability. Renner said the current property tax amendment does not go far enough and pitched his "Florida First" plan to eliminate most homestead property taxes while shifting burden to tourists, out-of-state investors and large corporations. The sharpest clash came over data centers, with Fishback promising to "reject and remove every experimental data center" in Florida and Renner saying he would call a special session to stop hyperscale projects. Collins urged local control with state guardrails, prompting Fishback to mock his answer as a "word salad."

The debate gave Renner, Collins and Fishback opportunities to draw contrasts and pressure Donalds over his absence, but the anti-Donalds lane remains split among three candidates. Renner emphasized his record as House Speaker, Collins highlighted his military service and role in the DeSantis administration, and Fishback positioned himself as an outsider. With Donalds maintaining advantages in polling, fundraising and endorsements, the debate appeared less like a campaign-changing event and more like an audition for which candidate deserves a one-on-one contest with the frontrunner.
