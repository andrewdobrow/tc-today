Juno Beach condo residents want fence replaced at JB-7 beach access, could cost town thousands

A missing fence at a Juno Beach public beach access point is sparking conflict between condo residents and summer beachgoers, with potential repair costs reaching into the thousands. Residents at the Colony Condos report that people using the JB-7 public walkway have been crossing through their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. With only bushes now marking the boundary between the public path and private land, residents say the situation has become increasingly problematic.

Lori Hill, a Colony Condo resident, described how the issue intensifies during summer months. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Residents previously tried confronting trespassers directly but stopped when encounters became hostile. "We used to confront them and tell them this is private property, and they would get pretty rude," Hill said. "So, now what we've just been doing is just calling the police, and they've been coming up and confronting them." Hill expressed hope that the boundary was clearer when the fence stood. "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking," she said.

However, not all beachgoers perceive the trespassing as a significant problem. Michael House, a visitor to JB-7, said he regularly walks to the access point from a public parking lot nearby and has not observed the issues residents describe. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that."

The matter was brought before the Juno Beach Town Council, which noted that the town is not legally obligated to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring more affordable options. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. The council agreed to address the fence question during its budget discussions scheduled for July 15.
