Juno Beach residents want fence replaced at JB-7 beach access as trespassing disputes escalate

Residents at the Colony Condos in Juno Beach are asking the town to reinstall a fence at the JB-7 public beach access after removing it in 2023 for safety reasons. The missing barrier has left only bushes between the public walkway and the residents' private property, prompting what condo dwellers say is an increase in trespassing and confrontations during busy summer months.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, described the recurring problem. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Hill said the situation has escalated beyond property damage. When residents initially tried to address the issue directly, she said visitors became defensive. "We used to confront them and tell them this is private property, and they would get pretty rude. So, now what we've just been doing is just calling the police, and they've been coming up and confronting them," Hill said. Hill expressed hope that the town would help restore the boundary, saying "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking."

The issue reached the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where officials acknowledged the town is not legally required to install a fence. However, Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring affordable alternatives. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. The town council agreed to address the fence question during its upcoming budget discussions scheduled for July 15.

Not all beachgoers perceive a significant problem. Michael House, who visits the beach regularly from a nearby public parking lot, said he has not observed the trespassing issues residents describe. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that." The disagreement highlights the ongoing tension between providing public beach access and protecting residential property rights in the coastal community.
