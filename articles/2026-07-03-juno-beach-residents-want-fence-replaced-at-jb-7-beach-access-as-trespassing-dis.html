Juno Beach residents seek fence restoration at JB-7 beach access as town considers $6,000 fix

Residents at the Colony Condos say people using the JB-7 public walkway are cutting through their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. Lori Hill, a Colony resident, said the problem peaks in summer with cars parking randomly and people pushing back bushes to cut through with coolers, fishing rods and lawn chairs. Hill said confrontations with trespassers have become recurring, and residents now call police instead of confronting people directly after receiving rude responses when telling trespassers the property is private.

The dispute came before the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where a town official noted the town is not obligated to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested a lower-cost solution may be worth exploring, proposing the town could help with a lower-cost fence or a combination of split rail and shrubs. Hill said residents hope the town will reinstall the fence to restore their landscaping and private parking. Beachgoer Michael House, who walks to JB-7 from a public lot a couple of blocks away, said he has not witnessed the problems residents describe and has never noticed anyone crossing the fence or shrubs. The council agreed to take up the fence question during its budget discussions on July 15.
