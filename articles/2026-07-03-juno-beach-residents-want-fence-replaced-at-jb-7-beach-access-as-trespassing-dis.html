Juno Beach residents demand fence replacement at JB-7 beach access as trespassing disputes escalate

A missing fence at the JB-7 public beach access point in Juno Beach has created tension between Colony Condos residents and summer beachgoers. The town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons, leaving only bushes to separate the public access path from private property. Lori Hill, a Colony resident, said the problem peaks in summer when cars park randomly and people push back bushes with their coolers, fishing rods and lawn chairs to cut through private land. Hill said residents used to confront trespassers and tell them it was private property, but people would get rude, so now they call the police instead.

The dispute came before the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where a town official noted the town is not obligated to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern said a lower-cost solution may be worth exploring, suggesting the town could help with a lower-cost fence or a combination of split rail and shrubs. Hill said residents hope the town will reinstall the fence so they can have their landscaping and private parking back. The council agreed to address the fence question during its budget discussions on July 15.
