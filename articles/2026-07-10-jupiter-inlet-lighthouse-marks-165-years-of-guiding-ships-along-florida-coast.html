Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse offers 165 years of history and panoramic views from 105-step climb

Standing at the base of the Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse, the striking red brick tower rises high above the inlet. Built in 1860, the lighthouse has guided countless ships along Florida's Atlantic coast for more than 165 years. The 105-step climb to the top leads up a narrow spiral staircase where lighthouse keepers once climbed night after night, keeping the powerful Fresnel lens shining for mariners. Reaching the gallery rewards climbers with breathtaking panoramic views.

The Atlantic Ocean stretches endlessly to the east, while the turquoise waters of the Jupiter Inlet meet the Loxahatchee and Indian rivers below. Boats glide through the channel, and the sea breeze makes warm Florida mornings feel perfect. After the climb, the museum grounds offer restored historic buildings where visitors can learn about the lighthouse's fascinating past, from its construction before the Civil War to its years as an active Coast Guard station.
