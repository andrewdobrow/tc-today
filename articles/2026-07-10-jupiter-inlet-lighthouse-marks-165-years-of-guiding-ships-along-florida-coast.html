Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse celebrates 165 years of guiding ships along Florida coast

The Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse has guided ships along Florida's Atlantic coast for more than 165 years. Built in 1860, the striking red brick tower stands at the Jupiter Inlet, where visitors can climb 105 steps to reach panoramic views at the top.

The climb to the top takes visitors up a narrow spiral staircase, the same steps that lighthouse keepers once climbed night after night to maintain the powerful Fresnel lens that warned mariners of the coastline. From the gallery at the top, the Atlantic Ocean stretches endlessly to the east, while the turquoise waters of the Jupiter Inlet meet the Loxahatchee and Indian rivers below. Boats glide through the channel as the sea breeze provides relief from Florida's warm weather.

The lighthouse grounds include a museum with restored historic buildings that detail the structure's fascinating history. The exhibits cover the lighthouse's construction before the Civil War and its years serving as an active Coast Guard station. Visitors can walk among the historic buildings and learn about the generations of keepers who maintained the beacon.

The landmark continues to stand as a testament to maritime history along Florida's coast. The lighthouse's powerful Fresnel lens has served mariners for generations, making it an enduring symbol of navigation and safety for ships traveling the Atlantic waters. The combination of historic significance and stunning coastal views makes the Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse a remarkable destination for those interested in Florida's maritime heritage.
