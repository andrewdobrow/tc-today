DEA warns meth surge spreading to Treasure Coast as cocaine prices rise

Federal agents in Palm Beach County pulled 54 kilos, nearly 120 pounds, of crystal meth off Palm Beach County streets in just two days. One seizure was laced with deadly fentanyl. Inside the DEA's West Palm Beach office, federal agents packed up 12 kilos, about 26 pounds, of pure crystal meth as part of the larger haul. Special Agent Kevin Bobbitt said the scale of what agents are seeing is unlike anything he has encountered in South Florida. "In South Florida, no. I've never seen this," Bobbitt said.

Bobbitt said the surge is tied directly to economics. As cocaine prices are high, with an ounce now selling for $600 to $1,000, more users are turning to meth as a cheaper alternative. An ounce of meth can cost less than half that, ranging from $280 to $500. "So, from a pure economic standpoint, it's much cheaper to get your hands on methamphetamine," Bobbitt said. The problem is not limited to trafficking. Bobbitt said a meth lab was recently busted in Palm Beach County, and the surge is beginning to spread beyond the county's borders. "We see it up in the counties in the Treasure Coast as well. To this volume, not yet, but I'm sure it's coming," Bobbitt said. Jim and Adrienne Tichy run a halfway house called The Lodge at Delray Beach and have witnessed the surge in meth firsthand. "Whole new animal with the methamphetamines," Jim Tichy said. According to a 2026 White House report, in some states overdose deaths involving meth have surpassed those from fentanyl.
