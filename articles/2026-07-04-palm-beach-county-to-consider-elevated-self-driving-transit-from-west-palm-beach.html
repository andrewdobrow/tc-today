California company proposes elevated self-driving network linking West Palm Beach to airport

Glydways, a California-based autonomous transit company, is proposing an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport. The company will present the proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for initial consideration. Small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers would travel along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads, bypassing traffic congestion entirely. Glydways estimates travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center.

The system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system, according to Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger, who referenced previous South Florida light rail discussions that carried estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile. Tuesday's County Commission meeting will determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal, not approve construction. If commissioners move forward, the county could seek competing bids and begin deeper discussions on routes, station locations, costs and financing. Gettinger said the service would be priced like public transit rather than rideshare and would require local employees to staff operations centers and maintenance facilities.
