Glydways proposes elevated self-driving transit connecting West Palm Beach to airport

Glydways, a California-based autonomous transit company, will present its proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for initial consideration. The elevated system would connect President Donald J. Trump International Airport to the convention center and nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations using small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. The system would bypass traffic congestion, potentially cutting the trip between downtown and the airport to less than 10 minutes, with estimated travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center.

Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system while offering comparable passenger capacity, referencing previous South Florida light rail estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile. The company described it as functioning like a "horizontal elevator," carrying passengers directly between destinations without intermediate stops. Potential funding sources include Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. Tuesday's County Commission meeting will determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal. If commissioners move forward, the county could seek competing bids and begin discussions on routes, station locations, costs and financing, with a planning and design contract potentially awarded later this year.
