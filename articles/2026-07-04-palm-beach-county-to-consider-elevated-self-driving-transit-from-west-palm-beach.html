California autonomous transit company proposes elevated network connecting West Palm Beach to airport

Glydways, a California-based autonomous transit company with projects underway in Atlanta and California, will present a proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for initial consideration. The company proposes an elevated network of self-driving vehicles that would connect downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport, the convention center, and nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations. Small autonomous electric vehicles carrying about four to six passengers would travel along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the concept is designed to function like a "horizontal elevator," carrying passengers directly between destinations without getting stuck in traffic or making multiple intermediate stops. Glydways estimates travel times of less than 10 minutes between downtown and the airport, seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport, and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center.

While Glydways has not disclosed a specific price tag, Gettinger said the system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system while offering comparable passenger capacity. He referenced previous South Florida light rail discussions carrying estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile and argued Glydways would come in far below that range. Funding could potentially come from Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. Tuesday's County Commission meeting is not expected to approve construction, but rather determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal and potentially seek competing bids.
