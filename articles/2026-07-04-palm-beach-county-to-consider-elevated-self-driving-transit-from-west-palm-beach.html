Palm Beach County to consider elevated self-driving transit from West Palm Beach to airport

Glydways, a California-based autonomous transit company, will present a proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport. The system would link the airport, convention center, Brightline and Tri-Rail stations through small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers traveling along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the concept functions like a "horizontal elevator," carrying passengers directly between destinations without traffic delays or intermediate stops. Glydways estimates travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center, potentially cutting the downtown-to-airport trip to less than 10 minutes.

The company describes the system as a modern alternative to traditional transit such as light rail, streetcars and people movers. Gettinger said the system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system while offering comparable passenger capacity, referencing previous South Florida light rail estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile. Funding could come from Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. Tuesday's meeting will determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal. If commissioners move forward, the county could seek competing bids and begin deeper discussions on routes, station locations, costs and financing.
