Autonomous elevated transit proposed to connect West Palm Beach to airport

California-based autonomous transit company Glydways is proposing an elevated network of self-driving vehicles to connect downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport, along with nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations. The company will present the proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for initial consideration. Glydways describes the system as a modern alternative to traditional transit such as light rail, streetcars and people movers, functioning like a "horizontal elevator" that carries passengers directly between destinations without traffic delays or intermediate stops.

Small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers would travel along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. Glydways estimates travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center. Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system while offering comparable passenger capacity, referencing previous South Florida light rail estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile. Potential funding sources include Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. Gettinger emphasized the service would be priced at transit levels, not private rideshare rates, and would require local employees for operations centers and maintenance facilities.
