Delray Beach Public Library offers free summer STREAM programs, daily lunches for kids and teens

The Delray Beach Public Library is brightening summer for local kids and teens by offering free STREAM programs covering Science, Technology, Reading, Art, Engineering and Math. All Florida residents can participate in activities at the library's 36,000-square-foot facility located at Atlantic Avenue and Swinton Avenue in downtown Delray Beach. According to Kae Jonsons, director of development and community relations, the library has a long history of serving the community. It was founded in 1913 when 30 women from the Ladies Improvement Society each donated a book to start the collection. The library now welcomes more than 193,000 visitors annually.

Young adult librarian Natalie Baron is leading sessions designed for teens, including ColorSpace: Artistic Excavations, where participants explore art history spanning from cave paintings to ancient pottery. Teens can also participate in lab-based activities like collaborative 3D puzzle builds and select themed book boxes. Mandy Fulop, head of the children's department, launched the STREAM Into Summer Literacy Program to help young readers set goals with librarians and track their progress. Participants take home an Unearth a Story bag containing a free STREAM book and related activities. Those interested can register at delraylibrary.org.

Working with the School District of Palm Beach County, the library provides free lunch daily from 11:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. and snacks from 2 p.m. to 4 p.m. to youth up to age 18. Family Nights on select Tuesdays invite children age 5 and older to explore STREAM topics together. The library also runs Saturday Science Discovery programs for ages 9 to 12 that feature hands-on archaeology activities including "Piece Together History," trash bag archaeology, carbon dating, and fossil hunts.

Upcoming special events include visits from Malachi Fenn of the Florida Public Archaeology Network on June 11, a Sea Turtle Adventures beach cleanup on June 25, American Shark Conservancy's SharkSmarts presentation on July 2, and FPAN's Beyond Artifacts program on July 16. The library's free, high-quality education programs help bridge summer learning gaps while keeping families engaged and promoting community connections in an accessible, safe space within walking distance for many residents. A complete program lineup and registration details for each event are available at delraylibrary.org.
