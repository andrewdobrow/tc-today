Florida attorney general investigates solar companies as Treasure Coast complaints mount

A wave of complaints from Treasure Coast homeowners has prompted the Florida attorney general's office to launch formal investigations into solar panel companies. At least six families have come forward with similar stories about aggressive door-to-door sales tactics that promised significant savings on electricity bills. The investigation uncovered court records and lawsuits showing a troubling pattern of elderly homeowners who say they were misled during the sales process. Seniors vs. Crime, a division of the attorney general's office, became involved after watching WPTV's initial investigation into the issue.

The financial impact on affected homeowners has been severe. Mike Schopp is locked into a 25-year, $60,000 loan and now pays nearly double his previous electricity costs. Jane Bibee's $40,000 solar loan has made it nearly impossible to sell her home. Paula Wass and David McCauley are still paying off $39,000 for panels that never reduced their nearly $300-monthly FPL bills. The couple discovered their system stopped working in November 2024, and an engineering report found moisture intrusion at the solar panel brackets, with unclear approval status for the installation brackets. Wass noted the problems were particularly frustrating because the installation occurred on a brand new home.

Data obtained by WPTV shows nearly 750 consumer complaints have been filed in Florida against three solar companies examined in the investigation. The attorney general has opened formal investigations into two of those companies, with active litigation filed against the third. Marty Jacobsen, deputy director with Seniors vs. Crime, explained that his office receives many complaints about fraudulent solar deals. "Oftentimes, these rogue products are obsolete. They'll go on your roof, they can't connect to the grid, but there's a 25-year warranty with a company that doesn't exist anymore, so you can't get service," Jacobsen said. The pattern of failed installations and defunct companies has spawned at least two class-action lawsuits against multiple solar companies.

Resources are available for affected homeowners. Seniors vs. Crime has helped multiple homeowners cancel contracts and recover money through mediation and negotiation at no cost. Some homeowners may be eligible for Florida's Construction Recovery Fund, a state fund financed by permit fees that compensates consumers for contractor misconduct. Attorneys have successfully recovered money for some homeowners through litigation. The attorney general's office and Seniors vs. Crime encourage anyone experiencing similar problems to file a complaint with the attorney general's office.
