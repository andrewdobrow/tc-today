TCT’s 2026 Summer Reading Picks: 12 Books to Add to Your List

Affiliate disclosureTreasure Coast Today may earn a commission from qualifying purchases made through links in this article, at no additional cost to you.

Summer reading does not have to mean one particular kind of book. Some days call for a fast-moving crime story or romance that can keep you turning pages by the pool. Others are better suited to a searching memoir, an immersive family saga or a work of nonfiction that leaves you thinking long after you close it.

TCT's 2026 summer reading picks range from newly released books by Colson Whitehead, Tayari Jones, Annie Jacobsen, Patrick Radden Keefe and other major writers to modern favorites and classics worth discovering for the first time. The list includes literary fiction, memoir, romance, true crime, historical fiction and two enduring American novels.

Memoir Pick

All That's Unseen: An Appalachian Memoir by Emilee Hackney

In this debut memoir, Emilee Hackney looks back on growing up in the coalfields of southwest Virginia, where family stories, Pentecostal faith and Appalachian identity shaped nearly every part of her world. As the boundaries around her life tighten, an unexpected path to Harvard begins a difficult reexamination of home, belief and her right to trust her own voice.

Best for: Memoir readers interested in Appalachia, faith and reclaiming one's voice

  • A personal story that treats Appalachia with both affection and clear-eyed honesty
  • A strong choice for readers drawn to memoirs about faith, family and self-reinvention

For Writers

Unsayable: A Life in Writing by Michael Cunningham

Michael Cunningham blends memoir with a meditation on the writing life, moving through childhood, first love, early adulthood, marriage and the long process of becoming an author. Rather than offering a conventional how-to manual, he explores why writers keep trying to describe experiences that language can never capture completely.

Best for: Writers, devoted readers and anyone curious about how stories take shape

  • Combines intimate life writing with thoughtful reflections on literary craft
  • Offers inspiration without pretending that creativity follows a simple formula
View on Amazon
Crime Fiction Pick

Cool Machine: A Novel (The Harlem Trilogy) by Colson Whitehead

The final volume of Colson Whitehead's Harlem Trilogy follows furniture dealer and reluctant fence Ray Carney and his formidable partner Pepper through three episodes in 1980s New York. A last heist, the downtown art scene and an old family obligation pull them back toward the criminal world as the city around them becomes wealthier, glossier and no less dangerous.

Best for: Crime-fiction fans and readers of Whitehead's Harlem Trilogy

  • A propulsive crime story set against a sharply observed New York in transition
  • The concluding chapter for readers who followed Harlem Shuffle and Crook Manifesto
View on Amazon
Literary Escape

Country People by Daniel Mason

Miles Krzelewski is a loving husband and father who is twelve years late finishing a doctorate on Russian folktales. When his wife accepts a position in Vermont, he treats the move as a chance to restart his stalled life. Instead, he falls in with an eccentric cast of neighbors and becomes increasingly fascinated by a strange local legend that may be more than a story.

Best for: Readers who like witty literary fiction with folklore and small-town eccentricity

  • Balances family life, humor, folklore and a gently mysterious premise
  • A summery change-of-scenery novel with an appealingly unusual cast
View on Amazon
Book Club Pick

Kin by Tayari Jones

Vernice and Annie grow up as motherless best friends and neighbors in Honeysuckle, Louisiana, but adulthood sends them toward very different worlds. One leaves for Spelman College and a new circle of opportunity; the other follows a restless search for the mother who left her behind. Their stories eventually converge again in a novel about friendship, chosen family and the lasting pull of home.

Best for: Book clubs and readers of multigenerational Southern literary fiction

  • An emotionally rich story of lifelong friendship and diverging paths
  • Built for discussion about family, ambition, belonging and the American South
View on Amazon
High-Stakes Nonfiction

Biological War: A Scenario by Annie Jacobsen

Journalist Annie Jacobsen examines how a laboratory accident or deliberate biological attack could develop into a global emergency. Drawing on interviews with experts in government, medicine, science and the military, she traces a possible breakdown across hours, days and weeks, focusing on the systems meant to contain a fast-moving biological threat.

Best for: Readers of investigative journalism, public health and national-security nonfiction

  • Turns a complex national-security subject into an urgent chronological narrative
  • A natural follow-up for readers of Nuclear War: A Scenario
View on Amazon
True Crime Pick

London Falling by Patrick Radden Keefe

After nineteen-year-old Zac Brettler dies in London, his grieving family begins asking questions that the official explanation does not settle. Their search reveals that Zac had constructed an elaborate second identity and become connected to figures in a dangerous criminal world. Patrick Radden Keefe follows the family through a story of grief, deception and the hidden networks beneath modern London.

Best for: Fans of narrative nonfiction, true crime and investigative mysteries

  • A deeply reported mystery driven by a family's determination to understand what happened
  • Combines intimate human stakes with an investigation into wealth, identity and crime
View on Amazon
Romance Pick

Great Big Beautiful Life by Emily Henry

Optimistic writer Alice Scott and Pulitzer-winning journalist Hayden Anderson arrive on a secluded island competing for the same assignment: the chance to write the biography of reclusive heiress Margaret Ives. Margaret gives each writer only pieces of her story and binds them with strict confidentiality, turning their professional rivalry into a literary mystery complicated by an inconvenient attraction.

Best for: Romance readers who like writer protagonists and a layered family mystery

  • Blends romance with a mystery about memory, storytelling and who controls a life story
  • A beach-friendly choice for readers who enjoy sharp banter and slow-building chemistry
View on Amazon
Friendship Story

My Friends by Fredrik Backman

Twenty-five years ago, four teenagers found refuge from difficult home lives during long summer days on an abandoned pier. Their friendship eventually inspired a remarkable painting that unexpectedly comes into the care of eighteen-year-old Louisa. Her effort to understand the artwork sends her on a journey toward the seaside town where the friends' story began.

Best for: Readers who want a moving, hopeful story about friendship and art

  • A warm, emotional novel about the ways friendship and art outlive a single moment
  • Moves between youthful connection and a later journey of discovery
View on Amazon
Historical Fiction

The Nightingale by Kristin Hannah

In German-occupied France during World War II, sisters Vianne and Isabelle face the same war with very different temperaments. Vianne tries to protect her home and child as danger closes in, while the younger and more defiant Isabelle is drawn toward active resistance. Their parallel stories explore survival, sacrifice and the often-overlooked choices women made during wartime.

Best for: Readers seeking sweeping, character-driven historical fiction

  • An accessible, emotionally intense entry point into World War II historical fiction
  • Centers women's courage, moral choices and the complicated bonds between sisters
View on Amazon
American Classic

East of Eden by John Steinbeck

Set in California's Salinas Valley, John Steinbeck's multigenerational novel follows the intertwined Trask and Hamilton families as patterns of rivalry, longing and betrayal echo across decades. Drawing on the biblical story of Cain and Abel, the novel asks whether people are trapped by inheritance and history or remain capable of choosing a different path.

Best for: Readers who want an ambitious multigenerational American classic

  • A sweeping family saga with memorable characters and enduring moral questions
  • A substantial classic for readers ready to settle into a long summer novel
View on Amazon
Florida Classic

Their Eyes Were Watching God by Zora Neale Hurston

Zora Neale Hurston's Florida-set classic follows Janie Crawford as she looks back on a life shaped by three marriages, changing ideas of love and a persistent desire to speak in her own voice. Written with lyricism, humor and vivid dialogue, Janie's journey moves through communities and relationships while tracing her growth toward independence and self-knowledge.

Best for: Readers of literary classics, Florida history and stories of self-discovery

  • An essential American novel with a strong Florida connection
  • Combines a deeply personal search for freedom with Hurston's distinctive language and sense of place

A good summer reading list should offer more than one mood. Readers looking for a recent release can start with All That's Unseen, Cool Machine, Country People, Kin, Biological War or Unsayable. Those who want an emotional novel may gravitate toward My Friends, Great Big Beautiful Life or The Nightingale, while East of Eden and Their Eyes Were Watching God provide two very different ways to spend time with an American classic.

Book prices and availability can change, so use the links above to check the current format and price before ordering. Whichever title you choose, the best summer pick is the one that makes you look forward to finding another quiet hour to read.
