Palm Beach County residents hit with thousands in retroactive sewer fees from Delray Beach

Homeowners in an unincorporated Palm Beach County neighborhood are facing unexpected bills for years of unpaid sewer fees from the city of Delray Beach, with some residents receiving notices totaling more than $4,000. Several homeowners along Franwood Drive received demands for back payments covering up to three years of charges, plus new monthly sewer fees going forward. Candace Etzler received a notice for $3,139, and at least a dozen other homeowners in the neighborhood received similar bills. Ruth Stephens said her total bill came to $4,494.92. The situation began in 1998 when nearby development disrupted the residents' septic systems, and state law required them to connect to the closest municipal sewer system. The city of Delray Beach completed an audit in April 2026, which triggered the retroactive billing.

The sudden bills caught residents off guard, and many are questioning how the charges were calculated. Stephens said the notices were shocking because they had attempted to pay previously but were told by the city they did not need to. Etzler challenged the calculation method, asking how the city arrived at the specific 36-month period at $90 per month. Both residents expressed concern about the financial burden of these unexpected expenses. Some homeowners have already paid the fees or enrolled in payment plans because they fear their sewer service could be disconnected if they do not settle the bills.

City Commissioner Juli Casale issued a statement defending the billing practices. She said when a sewer connection is identified, the city has a responsibility to determine what costs were incurred and recover the applicable fees. Casale explained that this approach ensures public utility costs are paid by those actually receiving the service rather than being shifted to taxpayers. The city maintains that the billing follows standard utility recovery procedures.

County Commissioner Marci Woodward said she has contacted the county attorney to examine the legality of the back charges and the monthly payments moving forward. The county attorney's office will investigate whether the city's retroactive billing and fee structure comply with applicable laws. For now, residents remain frustrated with the situation, hoping for answers and potential relief from the substantial bills they were not expecting to receive.
