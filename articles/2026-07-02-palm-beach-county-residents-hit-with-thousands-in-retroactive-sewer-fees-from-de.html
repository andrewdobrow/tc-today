Palm Beach County residents face thousands in retroactive sewer fees from Delray Beach

Homeowners in an unincorporated Palm Beach County neighborhood are dealing with unexpected bills for unpaid sewer fees spanning several years, with some residents facing charges exceeding $4,000. The situation has left residents concerned about potential service disconnection if they cannot pay the amounts demanded by the city of Delray Beach.

Residents along Franwood Drive received notices demanding back payments following a city audit completed in April 2026. The bills include charges for up to three years of sewer service along with new monthly fees going forward. Candace Etzler received a notice for $3,139, and she reported that at least a dozen other homeowners in the neighborhood received similar notices. Her neighbor, Ruth Stephens, was billed $4,494.92. The retroactive billing stems from 1998, when nearby development disrupted the residents' septic systems. State law required them to connect to the closest municipal sewer system as a result. Stephens said residents had previously attempted to pay for sewer service but were told they did not need to do so.

Residents are challenging how the city calculated the back charges. Etzler questioned the methodology behind the 36-month calculation period and the $90 monthly rate the city applied. Stephens expressed frustration about the total amount, saying "I understand having to pay a monthly fee, but my total was $4,494.92, and that's hard for people to just come up with." Some residents have already paid the fees or signed up for payment plans out of concern that they could lose sewer service.

City Commissioner Juli Casale defended the billing approach in a written statement, explaining that when a sewer connection is identified, the city must determine accumulated costs and recover applicable fees from those using the service. She emphasized that the city is ensuring utility costs are paid by those receiving service rather than shifting expenses to other taxpayers. Meanwhile, County Commissioner Marci Woodward indicated she has been communicating with the county attorney's office regarding the legality of both the retroactive charges and the monthly payments being demanded from residents.
