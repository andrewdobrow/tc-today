Delray Beach demands thousands in retroactive sewer fees from Palm Beach County homeowners

Homeowners in an unincorporated Palm Beach County neighborhood are facing substantial retroactive sewer bills from the city of Delray Beach following a recent municipal audit. Residents along Franwood Drive received notices demanding thousands of dollars in back payments, with some bills exceeding $4,000. The unexpected charges have raised concerns among homeowners about potential service disconnection if they fail to pay.

The billing situation stems from a 1998 development project that disrupted the septic systems in the area. State law required residents to connect to the closest municipal sewer system, which in this case was Delray Beach. Candace Etzler, one of the affected homeowners, explained that the development "shook up our drain fields for our septic and the state law mandates you to hook up to the municipality that's closest to you." Etzler received a notice demanding $3,139, while neighbor Ruth Stephens was billed $4,494.92. At least a dozen homeowners in the neighborhood received similar notices after the city completed an audit in April 2026.

Residents have questioned how Delray Beach calculated the retroactive charges. Etzler challenged the methodology, asking "How can you have this random number of 36 months and at the price of $90 a month?" Stephens acknowledged that paying a monthly fee is reasonable but expressed frustration about the lump sum total. She stated, "I understand having to pay a monthly fee, but my total was $4,494.92, and that's hard for people to just come up with." Adding to the frustration, Stephens noted that residents had previously tried to pay for these services but were told they did not have to. "We didn't make a mistake; we tried to pay, but they said we didn't have to," she said.

City Commissioner Juli Casale defended the billing approach, stating that when a sewer connection is identified, the city must determine incurred costs and recover applicable fees. "This is about ensuring that public utility costs are paid by those receiving the service rather than being shifted to the taxpayers," Casale said. Some homeowners have already paid the fees or entered payment plans out of concern about service disconnection. County Commissioner Marci Woodward said she has contacted the county attorney to investigate the legality of both the retroactive charges and future monthly payments. The outcome of that legal review could determine whether residents face continued financial obligations.
