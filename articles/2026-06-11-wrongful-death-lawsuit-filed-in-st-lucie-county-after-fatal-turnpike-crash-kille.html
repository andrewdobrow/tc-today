C.H. Robinson named in St. Lucie County wrongful death lawsuit over fatal Turnpike crash

A wrongful death lawsuit filed in St. Lucie County names C.H. Robinson Company Inc., a major transportation broker based in Eden Prairie, Minnesota, as a defendant in connection with a fatal crash on Florida's Turnpike. On August 12, 2025, at approximately 2:57 p.m., truck driver Harjinder Singh attempted an illegal U-turn through a median crossover near Mile Marker 171. The crossover was clearly marked "U-TURN OFFICIAL USE ONLY." As Singh's 53-foot trailer swung across the roadway, it completely blocked all northbound travel lanes. A minivan driven by Herby Dufresne struck the left side of the trailer in what the lawsuit describes as an underride crash. All three occupants of the minivan were killed, including Faniola Joseph, who was pronounced dead at the scene.

The lawsuit alleges that C.H. Robinson selected White Hawk Carriers Inc. despite the trucking company's documented safety problems. According to the complaint, White Hawk Carriers had a prior reportable crash in December 2024, multiple roadside inspection citations for unsafe driving violations including speeding and improper lane changes, repeated hours-of-service violations with falsified duty status records, and vehicle defects including brake problems and air-brake leaks. This safety information was publicly available through the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's Safety Measurement System. The complaint contends that C.H. Robinson markets itself as a company that properly vets carriers using federal safety data but failed to adequately do so in this case.

The lawsuit also targets Harpreet Singh, identified as a White Hawk Carriers manager and California resident, claiming he was personally responsible for hiring, training, and supervising drivers including Harjinder Singh. The complaint alleges that Harpreet Singh failed to adequately investigate Harjinder Singh's immigration status, work authorization, English proficiency, and overall fitness before hiring. The lawsuit further alleges that Harjinder Singh was present in the United States illegally without lawful immigration status and without authorization to work. Despite this alleged status, Singh obtained a Class A California commercial driver's license, which the complaint states California issued in violation of applicable federal and state regulations. Additionally, the lawsuit claims Singh lacked required English language proficiency mandated under federal regulations.

At the time of the crash, an unauthorized passenger identified as Harneet Singh was in the truck cab, representing additional violations of safety standards and federal motor carrier regulations according to the complaint. The lawsuit was filed by an attorney representing the estate of victim Faniola Joseph and names multiple defendants including the truck driver, the trucking company, C.H. Robinson, and the company manager. The case seeks damages exceeding $75,000 under Florida's Wrongful Death Act, with Joseph's daughter entitled to compensation for her mother's death.
