Wrongful death lawsuit filed in St. Lucie County after fatal Florida Turnpike crash that killed three people

A wrongful death lawsuit was filed months after a fatal crash on Florida's Turnpike killed three people when a semi-truck driver made an illegal U-turn in St. Lucie County. The lawsuit names truck driver Harjinder Singh, trucking company White Hawk Carriers Inc., freight broker C.H. Robinson, and company manager Harpreet Singh as defendants. The case represents the estate of victim Faniola Joseph, one of three people who died in the collision.

On August 12, 2025, at approximately 2:57 p.m., Singh attempted an illegal U-turn through a median crossover clearly marked "U-TURN OFFICIAL USE ONLY" on Florida's Turnpike near Mile Marker 171. The 53-foot trailer swung across all northbound travel lanes, completely blocking traffic. A minivan driven by Herby Dufresne, carrying passengers Rodrigue Dor and Faniola Joseph, struck the left side of the trailer in what the lawsuit describes as "a classic and fatal underride crash." All three occupants were killed, with Joseph pronounced dead at the scene.

The lawsuit raises serious questions about the truck driver's qualifications and authorization to operate commercial vehicles. According to the complaint, Singh was present in the United States illegally without lawful immigration status or authorization to work. Despite this alleged status, he obtained a Class A California commercial driver's license in violation of applicable federal and state regulations. The complaint further alleges Singh lacked required English language proficiency under federal regulation 49 C.F.R. 391.11(b)(2), which mandates commercial drivers read and speak English sufficiently to understand highway signs and respond to official inquiries.

The lawsuit also scrutinizes the decisions made by C.H. Robinson, one of the largest transportation brokers in the United States, in selecting White Hawk Carriers to transport the load. Court documents allege the carrier had a documented unsafe federal safety record that was publicly available through the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's Safety Measurement System. This record included a prior reportable crash in December 2024, multiple roadside inspection citations for unsafe driving violations including speeding and improper lane changes, repeated hours-of-service violations with falsified records, and vehicle defects including brake problems and air-brake leaks. The complaint alleges C.H. Robinson failed to adequately vet the carrier despite marketing itself as a company that properly evaluates carriers using federal safety data. Harpreet Singh, identified as a White Hawk Carriers manager, is named individually in the lawsuit for allegedly failing to adequately investigate the truck driver's immigration status, work authorization, and overall fitness before hiring. The lawsuit seeks damages exceeding $75,000 under Florida's Wrongful Death Act.
