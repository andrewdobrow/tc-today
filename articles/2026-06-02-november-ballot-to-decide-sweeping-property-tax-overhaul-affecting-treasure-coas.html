Jacksonville Mayor to Address Property Tax Overhaul Headed to November Ballot

Jacksonville Mayor Donna Deegan is set to discuss the local impact of a major property tax overhaul that state lawmakers passed during a special legislative session. The measure is now headed to the November ballot for Florida voters to decide.

State lawmakers recently completed a special session focused on property tax reform, approving sweeping changes that will require voter approval before taking effect. The overhaul represents a significant shift in how property taxes are calculated and collected across Florida. Mayor Deegan's discussion will focus on how the proposed changes would affect Jacksonville and other municipalities throughout the state.

The property tax legislation emerged from a special session convened by state lawmakers to address growing concerns about rising property values and tax burdens on Florida homeowners. Property taxes fund critical local services including schools, public safety, infrastructure, and municipal operations. Any changes to the tax structure could have widespread implications for local government budgets and the services they provide to residents.

Florida voters will have the final say on the property tax overhaul when they go to the polls in November. The ballot measure will determine whether the reforms become law or if the current property tax system remains in place. Local officials across the state are beginning to analyze the potential impacts on their communities and budgets. Mayor Deegan's discussion represents one of the first major public briefings on how the legislation would affect a Florida city if approved by voters.
