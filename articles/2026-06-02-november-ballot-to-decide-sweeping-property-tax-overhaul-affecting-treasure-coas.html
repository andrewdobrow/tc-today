Florida 2026-06-27

DeSantis Signs Property Tax Restrictions as Amendment 3 Fight Intensifies Statewide

Governor Ron DeSantis signed new legislation this week that restricts how cities, counties and special districts across Florida can raise property tax revenue. The laws align local governments more closely with the rollback rate, which is intended to generate approximately the same property tax revenue as the previous year, and mandate larger voter approval margins to exceed that threshold. Florida Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia emphasized that governments at all levels should begin considering spending reductions to address budgetary concerns.

Advocates of the new restrictions frame them as taxpayer protections, arguing that local governments have allowed their budgets to expand too rapidly as property values have increased across the state. However, local officials have expressed concern that these new constraints, combined with a constitutional amendment scheduled for the November ballot, could create substantial shortfalls in city and county budgets. The restrictions will affect property tax revenue collection for the upcoming fiscal year across all Florida jurisdictions.

Amendment 3, which will appear on November ballots, would gradually expand homestead exemptions on non-school property taxes in two phases: raising the exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and then to $250,000 in 2028. Proponents contend the amendment would provide significant financial relief to homeowners. Critics, however, contend that the tax savings for some property owners could result in service reductions, increased fees, or tax increases in other areas. An outside campaign has recently launched urging voters to reject the amendment, adding intensity to what is expected to be one of Florida's most prominent ballot debates this fall.

The combination of DeSantis' newly signed property tax restrictions and the November constitutional amendment vote ensures that property tax policy will remain a central issue in Florida politics through the end of the year. Local governments across the state are now developing budget plans to operate under these new constraints while simultaneously tracking the amendment campaign that could further limit their available revenue sources.
