Palm Beach County Commission District 6 race reshuffles as Mayor Sara Baxter returns after reversing congressional bid

Mayor Sara Baxter has reentered the race for Palm Beach County Commission District 6 after deciding against running for Congress. She submitted her candidacy before the noon qualifying deadline with backing from President Donald Trump, who reportedly asked her to stay focused on county-level representation. Her decision to reverse course and rejoin the district race has prompted criticism from her opponents, who argue that voters deserve candidates with undivided attention to local concerns.

The other candidates running for the seat have questioned whether Baxter's commitment to the district is genuine. Republican Elizabeth Accomondo expressed skepticism about the timing and reasoning behind Baxter's reversal, calling it nonsensical. Democratic candidate Rudolph Tinker suggested that Baxter's credibility as a Republican candidate has suffered as a result of her flip-flopping on the congressional race. Both candidates emphasized that the election should focus on substantive issues affecting District 6 rather than endorsements from national figures. Accomondo stated that taking care of the community's true needs must come first, while Tinker stressed that his candidacy centers on serving the people rather than political maneuvering.

Fellow Democratic candidates Katherine Waldron and Mario Guzman similarly voiced the importance of focused leadership on local matters. Waldron outlined her priorities as tackling traffic congestion, protecting neighborhoods, ensuring quality education, and managing responsible growth in the district. She indicated her intention to continue presenting her platform directly to voters. Guzman called for a candidate committed to public needs who would bring stability to the county commission and move away from what he characterized as a pattern of negativity in local politics. Both candidates believe voters should ultimately decide who best represents them based on their vision for the district.

Interviews with voters and conversations among all candidates reveal that development, traffic management, and the expansion of artificial intelligence data centers are the primary concerns facing District 6. These issues have emerged as central to the campaign discussion. Baxter responded to her opponents' criticism by asserting that the election should be about people rather than politics. The primary election is scheduled for August 18, with the general election following in November. Voters will have the opportunity to choose among the field of candidates competing for the commission seat during these two electoral contests.
