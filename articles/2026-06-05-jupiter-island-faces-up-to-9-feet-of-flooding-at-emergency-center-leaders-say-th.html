Jupiter Island faces up to 9 feet of flooding at emergency center, leaders say they're prepared

Jupiter Island's Emergency Operations Center could see up to 9 feet of floodwater during future storms, according to a new vulnerability assessment by the Florida Department of Environmental Protection. The report identified 103 critical town assets at risk of flooding, including 68 stormwater facilities, 17 historic sites, conservation lands, and key evacuation routes.

Commissioner Marshall Field told reporters the findings confirm what town leaders already knew when they requested the assessment. The town's Beach District Director John Duchok said the assessment will set up a roadmap for how to adapt and prioritize improvements. Field said the goal is to document the risks, build a roadmap for improvements, and compete for state and federal funding to fix vulnerabilities.

Field addressed immediate concerns about the island's vulnerability, saying evacuation orders would be issued well before floodwaters threaten roadways. The town has a backup facility on the mainland to maintain operations during emergencies. The 126-page report warns that stormwater systems face some of the greatest risks, noting they can fail during the very flooding events they are designed to manage. Field said there is a funding window that opened June 1 for applications, and the town will take advantage of it.
