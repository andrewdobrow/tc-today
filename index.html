1:35 PM ET

Residents at the Colony Condos in Juno Beach are calling for the town to reinstall a fence at the JB-7 public beach access point after trespassing complaints increased since the barrier was removed in 2023. The town took down the original fence for safety reasons, leaving only bushes to mark the boundary between the public walkway and private residential property. The missing barrier has created ongoing conflict between condo owners and beachgoers who use the public access route during peak summer months.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, described the escalating problems that occur during summer season. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Hill explained that residents previously tried to approach trespassers directly about the property boundary, but encounters became confrontational. Now residents contact police when they witness unauthorized parking and foot traffic on their land. The clear visual marker that a fence once provided helped residents and visitors understand where private property began.

Not all beachgoers acknowledge the extent of the problem. Michael House, who regularly walks to JB-7 from a nearby public parking lot, said he has not witnessed the trespassing issues residents describe. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that."

The issue reached the Juno Beach Town Council, where officials noted the town has no legal obligation to install a fence. However, Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring a more affordable solution. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. The council decided to address the fence question during budget discussions scheduled for July 15. Hill expressed hope that the town will restore the barrier, saying residents want to protect their landscaping and parking spaces from continued trespassing.

The blaze destroyed much of the residence, and preliminary findings from the sheriff's office suggest an electrical issue may have caused the fire. Authorities continue to investigate the circumstances surrounding the deadly incident.

White's fiancé Mark learned of her death and has since received support from the community. Friends launched a fundraiser to help him rebuild after losing many of his and White's belongings in the fire. This loss carries additional weight for Mark, who previously experienced the tragic death of his only child, Jacob. Nina Fenn, a friend of White's for eight years, has been working to support Mark during this difficult time. "I just am trying whatever I can to support him," Fenn said.

Fenn spoke about White's character and the kind of person she was. "Misty was a Christian woman. She loved gardening and plants. She plays the piano," Fenn said. "She was always the first to lend a hand. She loves kids; she loves her kids. She loves people." Those who knew White remembered her as someone with a good soul who was always willing to help others. Fenn said White's memory will continue to live on in the hearts of those who loved her, noting that "She'll always be in our hearts."

The timing of White's death compounds the tragedy for her loved ones. Her birthday was July 4th, meaning she passed away just days before the holiday. Fenn reflected on this heartbreaking detail, saying "Misty's birthday is the Fourth of July. So, she passed just a few days before her birthday." The combination of losing White and the proximity to what should have been a celebration makes the loss particularly difficult for those in her life. As investigators continue their work, the community has rallied around Mark to help him move forward after this devastating loss.

Riviera Beach named only Florida city to receive 2026 All-America City Award

3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned a major national distinction, becoming the only Florida community selected for the 2026 All-America City Award. The city is one of just 10 U.S. communities to receive this honor from the National Civic League. The award was announced at the National Civic League conference in Denver, where Riviera Beach leaders presented information about the city's community engagement efforts. Mayor Douglas Lawson called the recognition a proud moment for the city, describing Riviera Beach as "one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He noted that the award reflects years of dedicated work to revitalize the city and enhance quality of life for residents.

The All-America City Award recognizes communities that unite residents, government, businesses and organizations to address local problems and create meaningful change. City leaders said the recognition reflects a long-term commitment to improving civic health, building trust between residents and local government, and giving the community a direct role in shaping the city's future. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson emphasized that the award belongs to the entire community. "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents," she said. "Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

Riviera Beach's application highlighted three key programs that promote civic participation and neighborhood investment. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy. The Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education program, achieved a 98 percent completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program offers mini-grants to longtime homeowners for necessary repairs. Miller-Anderson explained that these initiatives represent a broader commitment to treating civic engagement as an ongoing partnership between government, residents, faith groups, schools and community organizations. "We're doing a lot of innovative things to make sure that our community is able to say that they are part of what's happening here in the city," she said.

The All-America City Award has been presented annually since 1949 and is considered one of the nation's most respected civic honors. City leaders say the designation celebrates Riviera Beach's progress and positions the community for future opportunities and growth. The award sends a message to the nation that Riviera Beach is a place where collaboration produces results and residents actively shape the community's future. Miller-Anderson added that winning the award reinforces positive momentum. "Winning this brings back community pride," she said.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football team is preparing for the 2026 fall season with ambitious goals after finishing last year with a 10-2 record and a district championship. Despite their successful regular season, a second-round playoff loss has motivated the program to aim higher this year, with a state title now in their sights.

Head coach Jimmy Robertson credits the team's veteran leadership as a driving force behind their championship aspirations. He noted that most of the seniors have been with the program for four years and came through the middle school ranks, giving them deep knowledge of how the program operates. According to Robertson, "Most of these seniors are four-year seniors. I mean, if not the majority of them came from the middle school as well. So they know how things are run. They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had." This continuity and experience has created high expectations for the upcoming campaign.

The Scots are taking deliberate steps to strengthen their program by scheduling more challenging opponents this fall. Senior Jack Williams explained that the team wants to compete at a higher level and attract attention by facing strong teams. "We knew that we wanted to take our program to the next level. So we really have a challenging schedule for this year because we wanna compete. That's what we wanna do and we wanna take that next level, and it's by playing against really good teams that we do that and attract new people," Williams said. This strategic approach reflects the team's commitment to building momentum for the postseason.

The Scots are measuring success by a single metric: advancing further in the playoffs. Senior Jack Whelche emphasized that regular season accomplishments matter less than postseason performance. "A better year is just gonna be going farther in the playoffs. Obviously we wanna go undefeated in the season, but that doesn't matter if we keep losing in the second round of the playoffs. We just gotta go farther than that, that's all that matters," Whelche said. To prepare for their goals, the team is grinding through summer workouts. Senior Edmyer St. Jean said the daily training builds motivation for the season ahead, noting "It just adds up every day as something like to look forward to. Work harder so we can see the results when we come off the season positive and break our record." With experienced leadership and focused preparation, the Scots believe they have the tools to achieve a championship run.

Two veteran communications staffers from Governor Ron DeSantis's administration have publicly alleged that Layla Collins, wife of Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins, operated anonymous social media accounts to promote her husband's gubernatorial campaign while attacking the governor. Christina Pushaw, a communications specialist in the Governor's Office, and Jeremy Redfern, Deputy Chief of Staff to Attorney General James Uthmeier, both presented evidence linking the accounts to the Second Lady. The accounts were deactivated shortly after the accusations became public.

Pushaw shared information from Promota CEO Robert Salvador showing that the X account @FSUdude01670 appeared connected to a phone number and email address similar to ones associated with Layla Collins. Florida Politics verified the connection through screenshots of two-factor authentication messages, which were triggered when someone attempted to access the account without the correct password. The screenshots displayed authentication messages sent to an email address with letters obscured and a phone number showing only the final digits. Pushaw identified a second account, @bobnamy1, that she said also appeared connected to Layla Collins. According to Pushaw, the account's posts consisted entirely of promoting Jay Collins and attacking individuals who refused to endorse him, including the governor.

Redfern corroborated the allegation, stating that "the FSUDude account was tied directly to her personal cell phone number." Both accounts were deactivated after the two men publicly criticized them. Pushaw noted the apparent contradiction in Layla Collins serving on a state board appointed by the same governor whose account had called a coward. She questioned how someone with such important state duties would have time to maintain and post on multiple burner accounts so frequently. In her own posts, Pushaw emphasized that beyond the communication links, the account's content pattern suggested it existed primarily to advance her husband's political ambitions. "Account content is entirely comprised of two types of posts: Promoting Jay Collins, and attacking people who didn't endorse Jay, including the governor," Pushaw stated.

DeSantis appointed Jay Collins as Lieutenant Governor last year, positioning the former state Senator to develop a statewide political profile. The governor has not endorsed a preferred candidate in the upcoming gubernatorial race and cannot seek a third term due to term limits. Redfern currently works for Attorney General James Uthmeier, who has endorsed U.S. Representative Byron Donalds for governor. Florida Politics requested comments from Jay and Layla Collins regarding the allegations.
