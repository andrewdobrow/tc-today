JetBlue is canceling its flight route between Vero Beach Regional Airport and New York's JFK Airport, with the last flights operating on Sept. 9. The airline cited low demand as the reason for ending the route, which launched last December to excitement from Treasure Coast residents hoping for convenient travel options to the Northeast. Brandon Dambeck, interim airport director, said the decision reflects how the airline industry operates based on demand. JetBlue was not seeing the ridership numbers it had hoped for on the JFK route.

Not everyone agrees the route had a fair shot at success. Charlie Ahern, who regularly uses JetBlue flights at Vero Beach Regional Airport, said he believes the route would have grown over time. "I think if they hung in there, it would have worked. I think it would have built up pretty good," Ahern said. He emphasized the convenience of flying out of Vero Beach, saying, "It's easy. It's close." Ahern's comments reflect frustration among some passengers who valued the direct connection to New York.

While the JFK route is ending, JetBlue's Boston service from Vero Beach continues to perform well. Grece Ezzi flew into Vero Beach on JetBlue from Boston on Friday for the first time. Dambeck said the Boston route is seeing great demand, suggesting it will likely continue operating. JetBlue has not announced whether it plans to add any replacement routes from Vero Beach or if it will adjust frequency on the remaining Boston service.

Dambeck said the door is not closed on the JFK route returning in the future. "They'll evaluate continuously. So just because it's gone now doesn't mean that it never will come back," Dambeck said. All affected customers will be notified directly by JetBlue and will receive full refunds. Passengers with upcoming reservations on the discontinued route are encouraged to contact JetBlue directly or check their email for rebooking options and refund information.

Republican state Rep. Fabian Basabe faces a $450,000 civil judgment after a Leon County jury found him liable for sexual harassment, battery and defamation. The case was brought by former legislative aide Nicolas Frevola, former intern Jacob Cutbirth and Frevola's mother.

Basabe represented himself despite having no legal training. The trial was repeatedly interrupted by procedural mistakes and violations of the judge's orders. Circuit Judge J. Lee Marsh told Basabe, "If an attorney had done half of what you had done, they would have been sanctioned, sir." Basabe told jurors he represented himself because he wanted to demonstrate that he had nothing to hide. After the verdict, however, he acknowledged that proceeding without an attorney was a mistake.

Basabe continues to deny the underlying allegations and has not been criminally charged. An outside ethics investigation conducted for the Florida House previously reached an inconclusive finding. His political opponents quickly called for his resignation following the verdict.

Basabe is seeking reelection this fall, and the verdict could renew questions about whether House leaders will pursue disciplinary action. The judgment comes as the lawmaker campaigns for another term representing his district in the Florida Legislature.

Almost two decades ago, photographer Joan Monfort took photos of a teenage Lionel Messi bathing a cute baby boy in a plastic bathtub as part of a charity calendar produced by local newspaper Sport and UNICEF. That infant was Lamine Yamal. Now those images of the longhaired Messi, his hands covered in soap suds, have become the most talked about in the runup to Sunday's World Cup final, when Messi's Argentina will play Yamal's Spain. Monfort, who works as a freelance photojournalist for the Associated Press from his home in Barcelona, said he has never been a believer or thought that anything was destined to occur, but he is beginning to have his doubts. Luck dictated that Yamal's mother, who appears in the calendar photo, won a raffle of families in the city of Mataró, near Barcelona, who wanted to participate in the 2007 photo shoot. A tearful Messi left Barcelona in 2021 when the club was in financial trouble. Yamal erupted at the club two years later. The 19-year-old Yamal will face a Messi who is 20 years his senior.

Monfort had no recollection of the photos until Yamal's father posted one on social media during the 2024 European Championship, when a teenage Yamal was enjoying his international breakout and led Spain to the title. Monfort, 58, said interest in his photos has skyrocketed and that this has exploded all over the world. He is considering traveling to see the final in New Jersey. Monfort said his heart is split and he does not know if he wants Messi or Yamal to win, as he has an everlasting love for the best player of all time but Yamal has broken the mold and represents a new, diverse Spain thanks to his parents from Morocco and Equatorial Guinea.

2026-07-18

Martin County is facing a $2.1 billion transportation funding shortfall over the next 20 years, a gap that could determine how residents travel for decades to come.

The Martin County Metropolitan Planning Organization estimates the county will need approximately $2.8 billion for transportation improvements through 2050, but only $701 million is currently available. That means the county can fund just about one quarter of its identified needs. Nearly all of the available funding comes from state and federal sources, with only $10 million coming from local revenue. The cost analysis was emailed to county and Stuart officials last August and outlines transportation needs across roads, transit, aviation, freight, and other infrastructure categories.

Among the most pressing unfunded needs are nine roads the county identified as requiring widening as traffic grows. The total cost to widen those roads is approximately $213 million, money that is not currently available, leaving all nine projects out of the county's draft Cost Feasible Plan. Those roads include SW Martin Downs Boulevard, SR-714/SW Martin Highway, SW 96th Street, SW Bridge Road, CR-714/SW Martin Highway, SW Allapattah Road/CR-609, SE Green River Parkway, SW Indiantown Avenue, and SE Bridge Road/CR-708. Without funding for these widening projects, the county will be unable to address growing congestion on these corridors.

State Road 710 stands out as among the county's highest priorities and has been the subject of repeated safety concerns. About $300 million in projects are planned for the corridor, reflecting the urgent need for improvements on this route. The massive funding shortfall highlights the challenge facing Martin County as it tries to balance transportation demands with limited financial resources over the coming decades.
