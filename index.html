A Vero Beach organization is collecting donations for communities devastated by a massive 7.5 magnitude earthquake in Venezuela. Latinas Nexus Network is hosting a Venezuela Earthquake Relief Drive, with founders Lourana Navarro and Juliana Marino leading the effort alongside Heidi Ramirez. Navarro, who was born in Venezuela and now lives in Vero Beach, said her own family in Caracas is safe, but surrounding communities have suffered severe losses. The organizers are working with Global Empowerment Mission to ensure that 100% of donated supplies reach verified humanitarian relief efforts in Venezuela.

The scale of the disaster is overwhelming. Navarro described seeing the magnitude of the earthquake and said the destruction will take years to address. "Seeing the devastation, I know this is going to take decades to recover. We as a country don't have the resources," she said. Hundreds of thousands of people are in urgent need of assistance. Navarro emphasized that the most critical shortages are food, shelter, and basic hygiene services. "I can tell you that the worst is the food and the place to sleep, to take a bath. They don't have that right now," she said. Marino stressed the organization's commitment to delivering help directly to those who need it most. "From here, the only thing that I can do is do our best to come together and help them and find different resources so we can get it directly to the people and bring the help that they need," Marino said.

The relief drive is welcoming donations from everyone in the community, not just the local Hispanic population. The organizers are collecting powder Gatorade, non-perishable food, baby formula and baby food, diapers of all sizes, baby wipes, feminine hygiene products, soap, shampoo, toothbrushes and toothpaste, rain boots, toilet paper, blankets, sleeping bags, flashlights and batteries, heavy duty garbage bags, first aid kits, bandages and gauze, hand sanitizer, cleaning supplies, face masks, nitrile gloves, and plastic bins with lids.

Donations can be dropped off at 684 Old Dixie Highway in Vero Beach through Saturday, June 27, 2026, between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. For more information, contact Lourana Navarro at (954) 205-0700, Juliana Marino at (772) 913-0805, or Heidi Ramirez at (772) 213-2747.

Anne Bendel first reached out to WPTV in May regarding delays with her My Safe Florida Home grant application. An issue with paperwork from her insurance company was causing the holdup, but that obstacle was eventually resolved. My Safe Florida Home then notified her in May that her status was complete and her task was finished.

Within a week, however, representatives from My Safe Florida Home requested additional documentation. They asked for a permit of completion related to a large impact window installation. Bendel provided the requested permit, but the program then raised questions about specific dates on the document. Her application went back under review as a result, leaving the status of her grant uncertain. She remains hopeful for compensation toward the $8,000 she spent out of pocket for the home improvements.

Bendel describes herself as persistent and thorough, yet the grant application process has tested her patience. She works without a computer and handles all communications on her phone without assistance from others. "It's not easy for me because I don't have a computer, I'm doing this on my phone, and I have nobody to help me. It's gut-wrenching," she said. She expressed frustration with what she sees as unfair treatment, noting that she completed all requirements the program asked of her. "I did the work, I had it completed, I got the permits, I had the insurance they asked for. Everything is done, but it seems like when everything is done they find some other little task to send you," Bendel said.

The My Safe Florida Home program has assisted thousands of homeowners across Florida by offering grants up to $10,000 to strengthen homes against storms and help property owners qualify for insurance discounts. Despite the program's track record of helping residents, Bendel's experience has shaken her confidence in its promises. WPTV contacted the Florida Department of Financial Services in Tallahassee to inquire about Bendel's case and the current status of her grant application.

This brings his total available funds to approximately $66 million, though that number will decrease following a $20 million media purchase already booked. With roughly 50 days remaining before the race to replace Ron DeSantis concludes, Donalds' fundraising dominance is reshaping the competitive landscape of the primary.

The other major candidates in the field are struggling significantly to keep pace financially. James Fishback raised only $4,825 to his campaign account and nothing to his Florida First political committee during the same period. He had roughly $117,000 total available as of June 19, though he spent more than $60,000 from his committee that week alone. Notably, $5,000 of that spending went to his running mate Sean Lozano, bringing the would-be lieutenant governor's personal fundraising total past $25,000. Jay Collins fared slightly better but still showed weak numbers, raising just over $11,000 to his campaign account and a mere $1,500 to his political committee. His total available funds reached approximately $3.5 million, with nearly $2 million from his committee already committed to an advertising buy.

Former House Speaker Paul Renner continues to struggle with fundraising momentum. For the second consecutive period, he raised less than $30,000 to his campaign account while collecting $13,000 for his political committee. As of June 19, Renner had roughly $2.5 million available to spend, significantly less than Donalds and substantially more than Fishback.

The fundraising disparity reflects donor sentiment heading into Saturday's Florida GOP Sunshine State Showdown. Despite calls from DeSantis and some candidates for a debate at the event, organizers will not hold one. Donalds and his supporters argue that his opponents lack viability, and the financial data appears to support that assessment. Major donors seem reluctant to spend significantly against what many view as an inevitable nominee, leaving Donalds' rivals with dwindling resources and limited paths to victory as the primary draws closer.

DeSantis signs new property tax restrictions as local governments warn of budget holes

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed new laws tightening how cities, counties and special districts can raise property-tax revenue, with supporters calling it taxpayer protection and local officials warning of major budget impacts.

Yesterday, 6:02 PM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed new laws this week tightening how cities, counties and special districts can raise property-tax revenue. The measures generally tie local governments closer to the rollback rate, the rate designed to bring in roughly the same property-tax revenue as the prior year, and require larger votes to go higher. Florida Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia said the state needs government at all levels to start thinking about spending cuts.

Supporters call the changes taxpayer protection and say local governments have allowed budgets to grow too quickly as property values rise. Local officials warn the new restrictions, combined with the proposed November constitutional amendment, could leave cities and counties with major budget holes. That amendment, appearing on the November ballot as Amendment 3, would expand homestead exemptions on non-school property taxes first to $150,000 in 2027, then to $250,000 in 2028. Backers say the amendment would deliver meaningful relief to homeowners. Opponents warn the savings for some property owners could lead to service cuts, higher fees or tax shifts elsewhere. A new outside campaign is now urging voters to reject the amendment, adding another front to what is expected to be one of Florida's most closely watched ballot fights this fall.

DeSantis signs stablecoin framework and crypto kiosk fraud protections into law

Gov.

11:59 AM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed three bills Friday that directly affect Florida's financial services sector and the treatment of money. CS/CS/HB 175 brings stablecoins under state regulation, in line with the federal Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act, better known as the GENIUS Act. The Office of Financial Regulation will determine whether an entity qualifies as a qualified payment stablecoin issuer, either by issuing a money services business license or granting a certificate of approval to trust companies. Under the law, stablecoins can be used for financial settlement, with their value pegged at a fixed one-to-one rate of exchange with the U.S. dollar. The bill takes effect in October.

CS/CS/SB 1568 establishes the Florida Stablecoin Pilot Program within the Department of Financial Services, allowing applicants and licensees to voluntarily pay certain licensing, registration, application and renewal fees using approved payment stablecoins. The department will determine which stablecoins qualify based on strict financial safeguards, such as being fully backed by reserve assets worth at least $1 billion. The bill also authorizes DFS to maintain a state-designated digital wallet to convert stablecoin payments into U.S. dollars and took effect immediately upon signing. HB 505 requires virtual currency kiosk operators to register with OFR under money transmitter licensing rules and display a fraud warning on initial screens. Transaction limits are capped at $2,000 daily for new users and $10,000 daily for established customers.

The agreement does not include Hezbollah and prompted one of the group's officials in Lebanon to warn of civil war. The State Department said the framework establishes a process for dismantling Hezbollah and for Lebanon to regain territory taken by Israeli forces as they battled the militant group.

The agreement was signed in front of Rubio in Washington by Yechiel Leiter, the Israeli ambassador to the United States, and Nada Hamadeh Moawad, the Lebanese ambassador to the United States. The U.S. will facilitate a newly created Military Coordination Group for Lebanon to implement the framework and committed $100 million in humanitarian assistance. The State Department said the framework provides Lebanon a genuine pathway out of a long crisis and creates a verifiable path for Israel to remove the persistent threat on its northern border. Leiter said the final destination of the framework is peace between the two countries and that success depends on Hezbollah being disarmed and dismantled, which will allow Israel to withdraw and Lebanon to regain its full sovereignty. Moawad said the framework is a first step toward restoring Lebanese sovereignty and territorial integrity, securing a permanent cessation of hostilities, and enabling all Lebanese to live in peace, security and prosperity.

The shelter's expert team recommends ensuring pets wear a collar with current identification and have an updated microchip registered with correct contact information.

Agriculture Commissioner Wilton Simpson donates $3 million to Florida Senate GOP campaigns

Simpson raised $449,400 between his campaign account and political committee in the week ending June 19, but directed $3 million to the Florida Republican Senatorial Campaign Committee while maintaining roughly $31 million total.

12:01 PM ET

Agriculture Commissioner Wilton Simpson raised $449,400 between his campaign account and the Friends of Wilton Simpson political committee during the week ending June 19, but made a more significant donation. Simpson gave $3 million to the Florida Republican Senatorial Campaign Committee to fund statewide efforts for the chamber he previously led as president from 2020 to 2022. He maintains roughly $31 million across his campaign account, Friends of Wilton Simpson, the Florida Green PAC, Future Florida and the Home Grown PAC.

Simpson, a fifth-generation Floridian who has operated a large-scale egg-laying operation in Pasco County for more than four decades, built his political operation en route to the Senate presidency. He flipped the Agriculture Commissioner's office back to Republican control in 2022 and has secured institutional support including an endorsement from President Donald Trump, backing from the Florida Farm Bureau and endorsements from 61 of the state's 67 sheriffs. His opponents significantly lag in fundraising. Primary challenger Matt Taylor raised just over $6,000 during the week and has less than $69,000 on hand. Democrats Joey Mendoza Atkins and Don Prichard did not raise money that week, with Mendoza Atkins holding roughly $7,000 and Prichard holding less than $100.

Jim Clyburn endorses Kendrick Meek Jr. in Congressional District 24 Democratic primary

The South Carolina congressman and longtime Democratic power broker backed Meek in the race to succeed U.S. Rep. Frederica Wilson, citing concerns about Black political representation in the South.

10:00 AM ET

Jim Clyburn of South Carolina endorsed Kendrick Meek Jr. in his campaign for Congressional District 24, citing Meek's work with the Human Rights Campaign, the American Civil Liberties Union, SAVE and communities across Florida. Clyburn said Republicans across the South are working to weaken Black political power and erase Black representation in Congress, including his own district. He called Meek the kind of leader House Democrats need to win back the majority, stand up to Donald Trump and MAGA Republicans. Clyburn noted that despite gerrymandering efforts, Democrats have a good chance of winning the open seat and that Republicans know how important CD 24 is to Democrats regaining the House majority.

Clyburn acknowledged Meek's family legacy, noting his grandmother Carrie Meek was among Florida's first Black members of Congress since Reconstruction and his father Kendrick Meek served the district from 2003 to 2011. However, Clyburn said the younger Meek's candidacy stands on its own merits through his work as an attorney, civil rights advocate and community leader. A University of Miami-trained lawyer who filed for the race in early June, Meek has worked as a House Judiciary Committee intern, a legal assistant with the Human Rights Campaign and served at the federal Office of Management and Budget. He now practices land use, zoning and real estate development law as an associate at LSN Law. He is one of seven Democrats running in CD 24, which covers Miami Gardens in Miami-Dade and Hollywood, Miramar and Pembroke Park in Broward County. More than 68.7% of voters there backed Kamala Harris in 2024, making the Democratic Primary the race that will effectively determine who succeeds retiring incumbent Frederica Wilson.

West Boca Raton homeowner faces continued delays with My Safe Florida Home grant

A West Boca Raton homeowner says her efforts to receive a promised grant from the state to fix up her home keep facing one hurdle after another.

Yesterday, 5:07 PM ET

Anne Bendel of West Boca Raton first contacted WPTV in May regarding delays with her My Safe Florida Home grant application. Her initial issue involved paperwork being held up by her insurance company, which was eventually cleared. In May, representatives with My Safe Florida Home told Bendel her status was complete. However, a week later, the program asked for additional documentation, including a permit of completion for a large impact window. After she provided the permit, representatives raised questions about dates. Her application is now back under review, leaving her uncertain about receiving any grant money toward the $8,000 she paid out of pocket for home improvements.

The My Safe Florida Home program offers grants of up to $10,000 to homeowners across Florida to fortify homes against storms and earn insurance discounts. The program has helped thousands of homeowners statewide. Bendel expressed frustration with the ongoing delays, saying she completed all required work, obtained permits, and provided requested insurance documentation. WPTV has contacted the Florida Department of Financial Services in Tallahassee to inquire about the status of Bendel's grant application.
