Yesterday, 7:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie City Council has approved the first reading of an ordinance to donate 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation. The land will support construction of a partial interchange on Florida's Turnpike. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the interchange and the ongoing Midway Road widening project are designed to address a significant gap in Turnpike access and accommodate the city's growth over the next 20 to 50 years. "There's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie," Samples said. City Council will vote on final approval of the land transfer at an upcoming meeting.

The Florida Department of Transportation is managing the $95 million Midway Road Widening Project to reduce congestion and improve safety on the roadway. The first phase is currently under construction, expanding the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, which will widen the road from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction expected to begin in Summer 2027, according to the FDOT website.

In addition to widening Midway Road, FDOT is improving the Turnpike bridge and designing a partial interchange for the Turnpike. The partial interchange is still in the design phase. The Midway Road Widening Project will also include bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks to provide additional transportation options for residents.

The construction work has not gone unnoticed by Port St. Lucie residents. Local resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged that the ongoing construction can be disruptive but believes it is necessary for the community's future. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," Scheumeister said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." Her perspective reflects the broader community recognition that while construction creates temporary inconveniences, the improvements will enhance traffic flow and accessibility throughout the city as it continues to grow.

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down on July 6 for a comprehensive $3 million renovation project that city officials say is necessary to improve the facility's capacity and weather resilience. The work is expected to wrap up by next Fourth of July, nearly doubling the number of boat slips from 18 to 44 for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The state grant and transportation impact fees are funding the improvements, which will include a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock, and a smaller floating platform.

More than 1,400 boaters rely on the downtown dock annually, making the closure a significant change for the waterfront community. Stuart Mayor Sean Reed emphasized that the upgrades serve a critical purpose beyond simply adding capacity. "They're doing it to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms," Reed said. "So the year of waiting will be worth it." The enhanced design is intended to help the dock withstand severe weather events better than the current structure.

The city has worked to ensure that businesses operating from the dock can continue functioning during the renovation. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates passenger tours from the facility, initially worried about maintaining operations during the closure. However, city officials found a solution that allows the company to continue boarding passengers. "So they're allowing us to build a gate right here at the end of the downtown Stuart dock. This is the non-floating section, but still we're going to be able to open this up and board people right here," said Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises.

Kuyrkendall acknowledged the dock's current limitations and expressed enthusiasm for the expansion. "On the weekend that's when it can get a little hectic at the dock and you know it's never great for us when we're squeezing in and trying to find spots," he said. He stressed the importance of the dock to Stuart's identity and economy, noting that "Stuart is not Stuart without the water." Kuyrkendall welcomed the modernization effort, saying the project will benefit both the city and local businesses. "Modernizing and expanding this back dock is really going to be great for the city but also for us as well," he said.

Anthem said it regularly reviews coverage policies to ensure they are based on medical evidence and aligned with treatment standards, adding that without FDA approval, the mesh "does not currently meet the standards required for coverage by our medical policy." Clark and Billington continue fighting the denial.
