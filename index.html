Stuart downtown dock to close July 6 for $3 million renovation, nearly doubling boat slip capacity

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down July 6 to begin a $3 million renovation project that is scheduled to be finished by next Fourth of July. The year-long improvement will significantly increase the facility's capacity, growing from 18 boat slips to 44 slips that will accommodate both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation will consist of a 7,500 square fo...

5:05 PM ET

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down July 6 to begin a $3 million renovation project that is scheduled to be finished by next Fourth of July. The year-long improvement will significantly increase the facility's capacity, growing from 18 boat slips to 44 slips that will accommodate both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation will consist of a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock, and a smaller floating platform. The project is being funded through a state grant and transportation impact fees.

Beyond simply adding more space, the upgrades are designed with storm resilience in mind. Mayor Sean Reed explained that the improvements aim to prevent damage during hurricanes and major storms. The dock currently serves more than 1,400 boaters each year, making the expansion a significant investment in the city's waterfront infrastructure. The enhanced capacity will address crowding issues that frequently occur during busy weekends when businesses and boaters struggle to find available spots at the current facility.

The city has worked proactively to minimize disruption to businesses that depend on the dock during the construction period. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates passenger boarding services from the dock, initially faced uncertainty about whether it could continue operations. However, the city found a solution by allowing the business to construct a gate at the end of the non-floating section of the dock. This arrangement will enable Tiki Taxi & Cruises to continue boarding passengers throughout the renovation period. Austin Kuyrkendall, the company's director of operations, expressed relief that the city located a workable compromise.

Local boaters and business operators have embraced the renovation plans. Boat captain Parker Fisher, based in Stuart, acknowledged that the dock needed improvements and welcomed the city's investment. Kuyrkendall emphasized the dock's central importance to Stuart's identity and economy, noting that the modernization project represents more than just a practical upgrade. He described the expansion as beneficial not only for Stuart's water-dependent businesses but also for the community's character and livelihood. The project reflects the city's commitment to enhancing infrastructure while supporting the waterfront businesses and recreational boaters that define Stuart's relationship with its waterways.

Port St. Lucie advances plan for Florida Turnpike interchange at Midway Road

Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to transfer 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation. The land will support construction of a partial interchange where Midway Road meets Florida's Turnpike. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the interchange is designed to improve a...

7:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to transfer 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation. The land will support construction of a partial interchange where Midway Road meets Florida's Turnpike. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the interchange is designed to improve accessibility and help the city manage growth expected over the next 20 to 50 years.

The interchange is part of FDOT's $95 million Midway Road Widening Project, which focuses on reducing congestion and improving safety in the area. Currently, crews are working on the first phase, which involves widening Midway Road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second widening phase, extending from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction expected to begin in summer 2027. The partial interchange itself is also still in the design phase. As part of the overall project, FDOT is also working on improvements to the Turnpike bridge.

Samples explained that the interchange addresses a notable gap in Turnpike access within Port St. Lucie. Currently, there is limited access to Turnpike entrances and exits in the city. Beyond the interchange, the project will include bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks to improve connectivity for all users. Port St. Lucie resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged the impact of the construction but views it as worthwhile. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," she said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work."

The city council will vote on final approval to transfer the land at a future meeting. Samples emphasized that the infrastructure improvements are essential both for the growth that has already transformed Port St. Lucie and for future development planned in the area. While residents like Scheumeister have noticed the ongoing construction, there is general recognition that these projects will benefit the community by easing traffic flow and improving access to major transportation routes as the region continues to grow.

Corey Simon in Senate District 2 and Ashley Gantt in Senate District 34, which opened after Democratic state Sen. Shevrin Jones decided to run for Congress. The slate of candidates reflects demographic diversity, with 59 percent women, 50 percent people of color, and 86 percent from millennial and Generation Z age groups.

Engage Y'All was founded last year by Democratic congressional candidate Gay Valimont following her special election campaign against now-U.S. Rep. Brian Nathan from Tampa winning races with PAC support. The group also assisted Democrat Eileen Higgins in winning the Miami Mayor race. Autonomous transit company proposes elevated system connecting West Palm Beach to airport

California-based Glydways will present to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday a proposal for an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport.

5:49 PM ET

Glydways, a California-based autonomous transit company, is proposing an elevated network of self-driving vehicles to connect downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport. The system would also link the convention center, Brightline and Tri-Rail stations. Small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers would travel along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the concept functions like a "horizontal elevator," carrying passengers directly between destinations without traffic or intermediate stops. Glydways estimates travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport, and one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center.

The company describes the elevated guideway as resembling a narrow pedestrian bridge rather than a traditional highway structure, with stations potentially integrated into buildings to shield riders from heat and rain. Gettinger said the system would cost a small fraction of traditional light rail systems, which have carried estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile in previous South Florida discussions. Funding could come from Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. The service would be priced like public transit rather than private rideshare. Tuesday's County Commission meeting will determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal.
