1:35 PM ET

A missing fence at the JB-7 public beach access point in Juno Beach is creating friction between condo residents and summer beachgoers, with potential repairs that could cost the town significant money. The Colony Condos residents say people accessing the public walkway are crossing through their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. With only bushes now marking the boundary between the public path and private land, residents have seen an increase in unwanted foot traffic and parking violations during peak summer months.

Lori Hill, who lives at the Colony Condos, described the recurring problem of visitors using the area. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. She explained that people push through the bushes with coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs, then stay for extended periods. The residents have had to call police multiple times to address trespassers. Hill noted that when residents previously tried to confront visitors, they often received rude responses. Before the fence was removed, the physical barrier made the property line clear and prevented these intrusions.

Not all beachgoers share the same perspective on the situation. Michael House, a visitor to the area, said he walks to JB-7 from a nearby public parking lot and has not observed the problems residents describe. He pointed out that adequate free parking exists nearby and suggested that property damage or littering, rather than simple property line crossings, should be the primary concern.

The issue reached the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where officials clarified that the town is not legally required to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern expressed interest in exploring lower-cost alternatives to a traditional fence. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. Hill said residents hope the town will act to address their concerns. The council decided to consider the fence replacement during its upcoming budget discussions scheduled for July 15, when town officials will weigh the costs and merits of reinstalling a barrier at the beach access point.

Misty Dawn White, 46, died in a house fire Monday in Hobe Sound. Authorities discovered her body after the blaze broke out inside the home she shared with her fiancé Mark. The sheriff's office is still investigating the incident, though preliminary findings suggest an electrical problem may have caused the fire.

Nina Fenn, who had known White for eight years, learned of the death when Mark called to tell her the tragic news. Fenn has since become a vocal supporter of Mark during this difficult time, speaking publicly about the loss and what White meant to those around her. Friends and community members have organized a fundraiser to help Mark recover from the disaster, as the fire destroyed much of his and White's possessions. "It's completely a state of devastation," Fenn said of the aftermath.

Fenn described White as someone whose kindness and generosity defined her character. "Misty was a Christian woman. She loved gardening and plants. She plays the piano," Fenn said. "She was always the first to lend a hand. She loves kids; she loves her kids. She loves people." White's memory will remain with those who knew her best, Fenn added, saying "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts."

The circumstances surrounding White's death carry additional weight for Mark and those close to him. White's birthday fell on July 4th, meaning she died just days before turning another year older. Beyond this immediate loss, Mark is dealing with profound grief from his past. According to Fenn, Mark previously lost his only child, Jacob, in a tragic incident. "Mark lost his one and only child, Jacob, and lost him tragically. So, I just am trying whatever I can to support him," Fenn said. The community's response through the fundraiser and continued support reflects a collective effort to help Mark navigate this compounded loss and begin rebuilding his life.

3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has been selected as the only Florida community among 10 U.S. cities to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The announcement was made during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where city leaders presented their community engagement initiatives to judges. Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the recognition, stating "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He credited the award to the city's long-term commitment to revitalization and improving residents' quality of life. The award honors communities that unite residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and create meaningful change.

The city's application showcased three signature programs that demonstrate civic participation and neighborhood investment. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy work. The Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education program, achieved a 98 percent completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program provides mini-grants to longtime homeowners for critical home repairs. City leaders described these initiatives as evidence of their commitment to treating civic engagement as an ongoing partnership between government, residents, faith groups, schools and community organizations. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson emphasized that "this award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents. Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, is considered one of the nation's most respected civic honors. The award reflects years of work focused on improving civic health, strengthening trust between residents and local government, and creating opportunities for residents to shape the city's future. Miller-Anderson noted that the recognition reinforces the city's momentum. "Winning this brings back community pride," she said. City officials believe the designation celebrates progress already made while positioning the community for future opportunities and growth.

The recognition sends a message that Riviera Beach is a place where collaboration produces results and where residents remain actively involved in shaping their community's direction. The other nine cities receiving the 2026 award are Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football team is working hard during the summer months as they prepare for a state title push in the 2026 fall season. Last year, the team finished with a 10-2 record and won the district championship, but a second-round playoff loss has motivated them to achieve even more this year. The players and coaching staff believe they can go further in the postseason than they did in their previous campaign.

Head football coach Jimmy Robertson highlighted the strength of the senior class, noting that most of them have been with the program since middle school. This experience and continuity has given the team stability and success throughout their years at Saint Andrew's. Robertson expressed optimism about the senior class's final year, saying "Most of these seniors are four-year seniors. I mean, if not the majority of them came from the middle school as well. So they know how things are run. They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had." Because of this veteran leadership, the program made the decision to schedule more difficult opponents this fall to challenge themselves and attract attention to the program.

Senior Jack Williams explained the team's reasoning for taking on tougher competition. "We knew that we wanted to take our program to the next level. So we really have a challenging schedule for this year because we wanna compete. That's what we wanna do and we wanna take that next level, and it's by playing against really good teams that we do that and attract new people." Senior Jack Whelche emphasized that advancing in the playoffs matters more than winning every regular season game. "A better year is just gonna be going farther in the playoffs. Obviously we wanna go undefeated in the season, but that doesn't matter if we keep losing in the second round of the playoffs. We just gotta go farther than that, that's all that matters."

The team is using summer workouts as preparation and motivation for the upcoming season. Senior Edmyer St. Jean reflected on how the daily training sessions provide purpose as the team works toward their goals. "It just adds up every day as something like to look forward to. Work harder so we can see the results when we come off the season positive and break our record." The Scots are committed to the demanding summer program as they aim to have their best season in program history while competing for a state championship.

12:34 PM ET

U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds strengthened his position in the Republican primary for Florida governor, announcing that his campaign and affiliated political committee have raised more than $90 million this cycle, with more than $23 million coming in the second quarter alone. The campaign recently launched a $20 million statewide advertising buy as the August 18 primary approaches. Donalds, viewed as the front-runner to succeed term-limited Gov. Ron DeSantis, secured endorsements from Attorney General James Uthmeier and Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia, both appointed by DeSantis. He also has the backing of the two incoming legislative leaders, Senate President Jim Boyd and House Speaker Sam Garrison. These endorsements carry particular weight in a primary where DeSantis himself has not endorsed a successor, though several figures from his administration have begun supporting Donalds.

The race took a contentious turn when Lt. Gov. Jay Collins filed a lawsuit seeking to remove Republican candidate James Fishback from the ballot. Collins argues that Fishback does not meet Florida's constitutional requirement that a governor live in the state for at least seven years before being elected. The legal complaint points to Fishback's ties to Washington, D.C., including allegations that he claimed a D.C. property as a primary residence and voted there in 2020. "Election integrity matters," Collins said in explaining the challenge. Fishback has denied being ineligible, arguing he was born and raised in Florida. He accused Collins of attempting to damage his campaign and suggested, without providing evidence, that Collins is working with Donalds. At a news conference, Fishback announced, "The first motion we will file is for Congressman Donalds and Jay Collins to release all communications between their two campaigns."

Gov. DeSantis moved to use a new state law allowing Florida to designate organizations as terrorist groups. The law, which took effect July 1, allows the governor and Florida Cabinet to designate organizations based on recommendations from state domestic security officials. DeSantis said Florida will seek to designate the Council on American-Islamic Relations, the Muslim Brotherhood, and Antifa, along with more than 90 federally designated foreign terrorist organizations. If approved by the Cabinet, the designations could block taxpayer funding, contracts, and public support for listed groups and create penalties for providing material support. CAIR and civil-rights groups quickly sued in federal court, calling the move unconstitutional and warning it could threaten the organizations' ability to operate in Florida.

Florida also advanced restrictions on undocumented students. The State Board of Education voted to move forward with rules barring undocumented students from Florida's 28 state colleges and adult education programs, including GED preparation courses. The state university system's Board of Governors separately advanced a plan barring undocumented students from enrolling at Florida's more competitive public universities beginning with the 2027-28 academic year, though currently enrolled students would not be affected. Supporters argue taxpayer-backed institutions should serve only lawfully present students. Immigrant advocates pushed back, with student Alexander Vallejos saying, "We're just asking for an opportunity to earn our future through education."
