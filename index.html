The group is working with Global Empowerment Mission to ensure all donated supplies reach v...

Yesterday, 9:13 AM ET

A Vero Beach organization is gathering supplies for communities devastated by a 7.5 magnitude earthquake in Venezuela. Latinas Nexus Network is hosting the Venezuela Earthquake Relief Drive, with organizers Lourana Navarro and Juliana Marino, along with Heidi Ramirez, leading the effort. The group is working with Global Empowerment Mission to ensure all donated supplies reach verified humanitarian relief efforts in Venezuela. Navarro, who was born in Venezuela and now lives in Vero Beach, said her family in Caracas is safe but surrounding communities have suffered severe losses. She described the scale of destruction as something that will take decades to recover from, noting that resources are severely limited.

The humanitarian crisis is urgent, with Marino emphasizing that hundreds of thousands of people need immediate assistance. The most critical shortages are food and shelter, along with basic hygiene facilities. Navarro stressed that people currently lack places to sleep and bathe. The organizers are calling on the entire community to contribute, not just the local Hispanic population. "We are asking everyone," the founders said. "They really need us."

The drive accepts a wide range of essential items. Needed supplies include powder Gatorade, non-perishable food, baby formula and baby food, diapers of all sizes, baby wipes, feminine hygiene products, soap, shampoo, toothbrushes and toothpaste, rain boots, toilet paper, blankets, sleeping bags with batteries, flashlights and batteries, heavy duty garbage bags, first aid kits, bandages and gauze, hand sanitizer, cleaning supplies, face masks, nitrile gloves, and plastic bins with lids. Donations can be dropped off at 684 Old Dixie Hwy, Vero Beach, FL 32962.

The collection runs through Saturday, June 27, 2026, from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. For those who want to contact the organizers directly, Lourana Navarro can be reached at (954) 205-0700, Juliana Marino at (772) 913-0805, or Heidi Ramirez at (772) 213-2747. All donations will be directed through verified humanitarian channels to ensure supplies reach those who need them most.

A museum that opened this year in Fort Pierce is part of the festivities celebrating the country's 250th birthday. The city announced on its Facebook page this week that the Florida Highwaymen Museum is a featured stop on the America 250 Road Trip.

The museum celebrates 26 Black artists who began painting in the 1950s, creating bold, colorful landscapes of Florida's untouched beauty and selling them along the roadside when segregation kept them out of traditional galleries. Located at 1234 Avenue D, the museum is the nation's only one with exhibitions that include paintings from each of the 26 renowned Highwaymen artists.

The museum operates summer hours from June 16 through July 31 on Saturday and Sunday from noon to 4 p.m. The museum will be closed in August and reopen in September, featuring a new exhibit.

Fall and spring hours of operation from Sept. 1 through June 15 will be expanded. The museum's summer schedule runs through the end of July before the temporary closure.

Byron Donalds continues to pull away from his Republican rivals in the race to replace Ron DeSantis as Florida governor. The Naples congressman raised nearly $253,000 for his campaign account and $435,000 for his political committee, Friends of Byron Donalds, during the week ending June 19. With just over 50 days until the primary election, Donalds has accumulated nearly $66 million in available funds. Though a $20 million media buy will reduce his cash on hand in the next reporting period, his financial advantage over the rest of the field remains insurmountable.

The fundraising gap between Donalds and his opponents has grown into a decisive chasm. James Fishback raised only $4,825 to his campaign account and nothing to his Florida First political committee during the same week, leaving him with roughly $117,000 to spend. Notably, Fishback spent more than $60,000 from his committee during the period, including a $5,000 payment to his running mate Sean Lozano. Jay Collins raised just over $11,000 to his campaign account and $1,500 to his political committee, maintaining roughly $3.5 million in available funds. However, nearly $2 million of his committee money went toward an ad buy in the week. Former House Speaker Paul Renner has struggled with fundraising for two consecutive periods, raising less than $30,000 to his campaign account and $13,000 to his political committee. He has roughly $2.5 million to spend.

The weakness across the broader field reflects donor sentiment about the race's direction. The Republican Party of Florida declined to hold a gubernatorial debate at Saturday's Sunshine State Showdown, a decision that drew criticism from trailing candidates seeking opportunities to directly challenge Donalds. Donalds and his allies have argued that a debate is unnecessary given the lack of viable opposition in the race. The latest fundraising reports support this position, demonstrating that donors have not responded to pleas from other candidates to fund a competitive primary.

As Florida Republicans prepare to select their nominee, the contest appears increasingly decided. Donalds' financial dominance combined with his opponents' inability to gain traction with contributors suggests the primary outcome is largely predetermined. Without a debate platform to gain exposure and facing steep funding disadvantages, the trailing candidates appear unable to mount a meaningful challenge to the frontrunner in the remaining weeks of the campaign.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio joined Israeli and Lebanese ambassadors in Washington to announce a framework agreement described as a first step toward peace following months of conflict with Hezbollah.

Yesterday, 12:45 PM ET

Secretary of State Marco Rubio joined Israel and Lebanon's ambassadors to the U.S. Friday to announce a framework agreement described as a first step toward peace following months of conflict between Israel and the Lebanese militant group Hezbollah. The agreement was signed in Washington by Yechiel Leiter, the Israeli ambassador to the United States, and Nada Hamadeh Moawad, the Lebanese ambassador to the United States. The agreement does not include Hezbollah and prompted one of the group's officials in Lebanon to warn of civil war.

The State Department said the framework establishes a process for dismantling Hezbollah and for Lebanon to regain territory taken by Israeli forces. The U.S. will facilitate a newly created Military Coordination Group for Lebanon to implement the framework while committing $100 million in humanitarian assistance. The State Department stated the framework provides "a genuine pathway out of a long crisis" for Lebanon and "creates a verifiable path to removing the persistent threat on its northern border" for Israel. Leiter said achieving peace depends on Hezbollah being disarmed and dismantled, which will allow Israel to withdraw and Lebanon to regain its full sovereignty. Moawad said the framework is "a first step on the road to restoring Lebanese sovereignty and territorial integrity, securing a permanent and final cessation of hostilities, enabling our people to go back to their land."

Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League urges pet owners to prepare hurricane disaster plans

The rescue league recommends microchipping pets, creating emergency kits with three days of supplies, and identifying pet-friendly shelters before the next storm.

Jun 24, 1:34 PM ET

With hurricane season in full swing, Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League is urging South Florida residents to create a disaster plan for their pets before the next storm hits. Dr. Alyssa Comroe, CEO of Peggy Adams, told WPTV the safest place for pets during a hurricane is right alongside their owners. The shelter's expert team recommends ensuring pets wear a collar with current identification and have an updated microchip registered with correct contact information.

Pet owners should prepare an emergency kit containing a three-day supply of food and water in airtight containers, bowls, a sturdy carrier, bedding, towels, medications for two weeks, vaccination records, first-aid supplies, litter and box for cats, leashes, harnesses, cool packs, wipes, waste bags, and multiple forms of visible ID. The rescue league also recommends introducing pets to a designated safe room ahead of time, such as a walk-in closet or downstairs bathroom, and conducting practice runs so the hurricane isn't their first time there. Pet owners should keep a printed list of local pet-friendly shelters and hotels in an accessible place and maintain recent photos of pets, ideally with their owner, to help with identification if separated. Florida's hurricane season runs June through November.

The Florida Mango Festival will feature more than 75 vendors at the Palm Beach County Convention Center on Saturday, June 27, showcasing local mango varieties, grafted trees, tropical fruits, and mango-themed products. Festival co-organizer Vince Rogers highlighted some of Florida's most distinctive mango cultivars, including Fruit Cocktail, which features a sweet multi-layered flavor profile, Orange Sherbet with creamy citrus notes, and Pineapple Pleasure offering tropical pineapple undertones. Rogers noted that Florida produces more than 350 mango varieties, far exceeding what consumers typically encounter in grocery stores. "Our backyard-grown Tommy Atkins are just much, much more superior to anything you're going to find at a store," Rogers said.

Alex Salazar of Tropical Acres Farms explained that the air-conditioned indoor venue provides an ideal setting for visitors to taste, learn, and discover Florida-grown mangoes while avoiding summer heat. Throughout the day, Salazar will lead educational demonstrations on home mango growing and grafting techniques, offering tips for both beginners and experienced growers. The festival will also feature an "ID Your Mango" booth, a scion exchange for online mango community members, and a display of Florida's diverse mango cultivars. General admission begins at 10:30 a.m., with early entry at 9 a.m. for first pick ticket holders. The convention center is located at 650 Okeechobee Boulevard in West Palm Beach. Tickets are available at floridamangofestival.com.

James Fishback raised $4,825 to his campaign account and nothing to his political committee in the week ending June 19, while spending more than $60,000 including a $5,000 payment to running mate Sean Lozano.

Yesterday, 12:15 PM ET

James Fishback raised $4,825 to his campaign account and nothing to his Florida First political committee in the week ending June 19. He had roughly $117,000 total to spend as of that date. He spent more than $60,000 from the committee alone during the week, including a $5,000 payment to running mate Sean Lozano that pushed the would-be lieutenant governor's haul past $25,000. Fishback may be the only candidate paying someone to be on the ticket in the Republican gubernatorial primary.

The other candidates face their own cash troubles as they struggle to compete with Byron Donalds, who added nearly $253,000 to his campaign account and $435,000 to his Friends of Byron Donalds political committee during the same week, bringing his total to nearly $66 million on hand. Jay Collins raised a little more than $11,000 to his campaign account and $1,500 to his political committee, with roughly $3.5 million to spend. Former House Speaker Paul Renner raised less than $30,000 to his campaign account for the second consecutive period and $13,000 to his political committee, with roughly $2.5 million to spend. No candidates will debate at Saturday's GOP Sunshine State Showdown event.

Vero Beach group collecting earthquake relief supplies for Venezuela through Saturday

Latinas Nexus Network in Vero Beach is hosting a Venezuela Earthquake Relief Drive to collect essential supplies for communities devastated by a massive earthqu.

Yesterday, 9:13 AM ET

Latinas Nexus Network in Vero Beach is hosting a Venezuela Earthquake Relief Drive to collect essential supplies for communities devastated by a 7.5 magnitude earthquake in Venezuela. The drive is being organized by founders Lourana Navarro and Juliana Marino, along with Heidi Ramirez. Donations can be dropped off at 684 Old Dixie Hwy, Vero Beach, through Saturday, June 27, 2026, from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. The organizers are working with Global Empowerment Mission to ship the collected supplies to Venezuela, and say 100 percent of donated supplies will be directed through verified humanitarian relief efforts.

Navarro, who was born in Venezuela and now lives in Vero Beach, said her family in Caracas is safe, but surrounding communities have lost everything. She said the scale of destruction will take years to address. Marino said hundreds of thousands of people are in need right now, with the worst needs being food and places to sleep and bathe. The drive is open to everyone, not just the local Hispanic community. Organizers are collecting powder Gatorade, non-perishable food, baby formula and food, diapers, baby wipes, feminine hygiene products, soap, shampoo, toothbrushes and toothpaste, rain boots, toilet paper, blankets, sleeping bags with batteries, flashlights and batteries, garbage bags, first aid kits, bandages and gauze, hand sanitizer, cleaning supplies, face masks, nitrile gloves, and plastic bins with lids. To contact organizers, call Lourana Navarro at (954) 205-0700, Juliana Marino at (772) 913-0805, or Heidi Ramirez at (772) 213-2747.

Anne Bendel says her efforts to receive a promised grant from the state to fix up her home keep facing one hurdle after another, even though the work is complete and she has paid $8,000 out of pocket.

Yesterday, 5:07 PM ET

Anne Bendel, a West Boca Raton homeowner, says her efforts to receive a My Safe Florida Home grant have faced repeated obstacles despite completing all required work. She first contacted WPTV in May regarding her grant being held up by paperwork from her insurance company, an issue that was eventually resolved. "My Safe Florida Home contacted me, set me to a task and said my status was complete in May," Bendel said. However, a week later, representatives asked for additional documentation: a permit of completion for a large impact window. "I did the work, I had it completed, I got the permits, I had the insurance they asked for. She expressed that the process has shaken her confidence in the program's promises. WPTV has contacted the Florida Department of Financial Services in Tallahassee regarding Bendel's case and the status of her grant.

Alligator Alcatraz Detainees Scattered Across U.S. After Everglades Facility Closes

With the controversial detention facility now closed, detainees have been moved to facilities in Louisiana, Texas, New Mexico and as far as Tacoma, Washington, creating challenges for families and immigration attorneys.

Yesterday, 5:10 PM ET

With the closure of the controversial Alligator Alcatraz detention facility in the Florida Everglades, immigration officials have transferred detainees who were not deported to facilities across the country. According to immigration attorney Richard Hujber, those held at the facility have been sent to Louisiana, Texas, New Mexico, and as far away as Tacoma, Washington. "I had a client moved all the way to Tacoma, Washington, like it can't get farther than that," Hujber said. The geographic dispersal of detainees poses serious legal challenges for those seeking bond hearings and to remain in the United States. Hujber explained that different federal circuit courts apply different standards when considering bond requests. "There's different laws in different circuit courts in different parts of the country," he said, noting that some circuits have ruled against bond approval more frequently than others. This inconsistency means that a detainee might qualify for release in one jurisdiction but face denial in another.

For many immigrants, the inability to secure bond forces difficult decisions about whether to continue fighting for residency or asylum or to accept self-deportation. Gov. Ron DeSantis reported that roughly 21,000 people were deported from the facility since it opened nearly a year ago. For some families, the closure brought relief. Anna Maria Hernandez's cousin was detained at Alligator Alcatraz and was released just before closure, now under an order of supervision that allows him to drive, work, and live in the U.S. The Baker County facility in North Florida remains operational, but for many former detainees and their families, uncertainty about the future persists.
