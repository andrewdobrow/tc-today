Judge orders Martin County teacher suspended over Charlie Kirk comments reinstated with back pay

A Florida judge has ruled that Matthew Theobald, a social studies teacher and union president at Martin County School District, must be reinstated to his position and receive back pay after being suspended over comments he posted on his personal Facebook page. A few minutes ago

A Florida judge has ruled that Matthew Theobald, a social studies teacher and union president at Martin County School District, must be reinstated to his position and receive back pay after being suspended over comments he posted on his personal Facebook page. Theobald, who had worked in the district for 17 years, was removed from his job in September 2025 following the posts about Charlie Kirk. Superintendent Michael Maine recommended his termination in October, and the teacher remained suspended without pay until an administrative law judge reviewed the case in April.

The Division of Administrative Hearings judge cited a recent Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal ruling in his decision. That ruling determined that disciplining a teacher for personal social media posts made off school grounds, using personal equipment, and with no student involvement violated the teacher's First Amendment rights. The judge found that Theobald's situation involved circumstances very similar to those in the appellate case, making the school district's disciplinary action unconstitutional.

The judge also determined that Martin County School District failed to follow its own progressive discipline policy. Rather than moving through standard corrective steps like counseling, reprimands, and other lesser actions, the school board went directly to suspending Theobald without pay and recommending his termination. By skipping these procedural requirements, the district compounded its legal violations. Based on these findings, the judge ordered the school district to reinstate Theobald and provide him with back pay and benefits.

This case reflects a broader trend of disciplinary actions against educators and public employees across the country following comments about Charlie Kirk's death. Similar situations have resulted in significant financial consequences for employers. In a related case last month, a Florida Fish and Wildlife Commission biologist who was fired over a personal social media post about Kirk received a settlement of $485,000. These rulings and settlements underscore growing legal recognition that public employees cannot be disciplined for lawful personal speech made outside of work on their own time, particularly when such speech does not affect their job duties or involve students.

Jupiter approves fire assessment fee for new fire rescue department starting October 1

According to the town's funding plan, property taxes will cover 84% of the department's costs, with the assessment fee covering 8% a...

Yesterday, 3:52 PM ET

Jupiter's town council has approved a fire assessment fee to fund the town's new fire rescue department, set to begin operations on October 1. The assessment will replace the current county fire fee that residents see on their tax bills each year. According to the town's funding plan, property taxes will cover 84% of the department's costs, with the assessment fee covering 8% and EMS transport fees making up the remaining 8%.

Finance Director Scott Reynolds highlighted the financial benefits of this new arrangement for property owners. "The immediate savings for property owners, we've been talking about this for three years, starting at $25 million so right out of the gate, a $10 million savings to the residents," Reynolds said. The town sent informational letters to 30,000 homes to explain the new fee structure to residents. However, the change has generated some confusion among property owners. Town of Jupiter spokesperson Mariana DiNunzio acknowledged this confusion, noting that the town has not previously implemented a non ad-valorem assessment. To help residents understand the impact, the town created an online calculator where property owners can determine their potential savings under the new system.

Despite the overall council approval, Mayor Jim Kuretski cast the sole dissenting vote against the funding structure. His concerns centered on inconsistencies in how the assessment is calculated for different property types. "Single family homes are being assessed based on total square footage including garages and porches. Townhomes and condos are not," Kuretski said. This difference in assessment methodology raises questions about fairness across the community's various residential categories.

Some residents have also raised concerns about the long-term implications of the assessment fee. While the immediate savings appear substantial, questions remain about whether the fee could increase in future years as the department's needs evolve. The town's effort to communicate the change through direct mail and an online calculator represents an attempt to address resident concerns and clarify the transition from the current county fire fee system to the new Jupiter fire rescue department funding model.

A few minutes ago

Florida TaxWatch released a new analysis showing the state's economic and population growth will decelerate over the next decade amid challenging economic headwinds. The report indicates that maintaining the strong economic expansion Florida has experienced in recent years will become increasingly difficult. The unemployment rate has climbed every month in 2026, reaching 4.8% in April according to FloridaCommerce, the state's economic development bureau. This rate exceeds the national unemployment rate of 4.3%, signaling labor market weakness ahead.

While gross domestic product growth will remain strong and outpace the rest of the nation through 2030, the labor market presents a greater concern. The report projects that GDP growth will experience some sluggish phases next year, with unemployment and income growth rates both expected to decline. The analysis acknowledged that the full economic consequences of the war with Iran remain uncertain. Despite these headwinds, Florida's economy expanded to $1.87 trillion in 2025, and projections suggest expansion will continue into coming years due to expected population growth in 2026. According to the report, if Florida were an independent nation, its economic output would rank 13th globally.

Population growth, while continuing, will occur at a slower pace than recent years. Currently, Florida experiences a net migration of 895 more people moving into the state daily than leaving. That daily net influx is projected to drop to 689 over the next decade. Florida's population, now approximately 23 million people, is expected to grow by roughly 2.3 million by 2035. Jeff Kottkamp, CEO and President of TaxWatch, stated that "Florida's economy is now set to return to pre-pandemic growth rates over the next five years, after experiencing high economic growth in the past three years."

Despite the projected slowdown, some optimistic signs emerge from the forecast. Kottkamp noted that while Florida entered 2026 with strong growth trends and ranked number one in terms of economic strength, the organization continues to question whether the state's impressive growth rate can sustain over the coming years. He added that "Florida's economic outlook, while hampered by current global events, still reflects some of the signs for optimism it gleamed at the end of 2025." The economic forecast represents part of TaxWatch's quarterly analysis of factors influencing Florida's economy, providing a comprehensive view of both challenges and opportunities ahead.

A $1 million federal grant will help fund a major sewage system upgrade in the Ixora Park community in Indian River County, Congressman Mike Haridopolos announced at a press conference Wednesday afternoon. The Member Community Project Funding will support a $20.8 million project to retrofit the sewage system affecting over 230 homes that currently rely on a failing sewer system risking contamination of environmental resources. Haridopolos said the federal support ensures health and safety to the community by rehabilitating infrastructure while protecting the Indian River Lagoon, a designated federal resource and one of the most diverse estuaries in the country.

The Ixora Park community sits adjacent to the County South Relief Canal, which discharges into the Indian River Lagoon. Keith Drewett of the Clean Water Coalition of Indian River County said the lagoon is dramatically different from twenty years ago in terms of fishing and swimming, with nobody swimming in the lagoon anymore. The Clean Water Coalition identified the upgrade as critical because the water table is high and heavy rain flows underground through the subsurface. The project will replace aging sewer infrastructure and reduce discharges of nitrogen and phosphorus into the lagoon. Haridopolos noted the benefits extend beyond environmental protection, as replacing the failing systems improves water quality and increases the taxable value of homes in the community.

The protocol combines robotics for minimally invasive surgery, targeted numbing, and a timed schedule of non-opioid medications taken before, during, and after surgery. Warren got sober at Reprieve Recovery eight years ago and has spent the last seven years working there to help others do the same. Patients interested in opioid-free recovery can ask their doctor if the ERAS protocol is an option for them.
