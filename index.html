Jun 3, 1:03 PM ET

The annual Crossing for Cystic Fibrosis brings more than 100 paddlers across 80 miles of open Atlantic Ocean this June to raise awareness and funds for families living with cystic fibrosis. Participants launch from Bimini, Bahamas, at midnight on June 13 and arrive at Lake Worth Beach after 12 to 16 hours of paddling. The endurance trek benefits the Pipers Angels Foundation, established by event founder Travis Suit in honor of his daughter Piper, who was diagnosed with cystic fibrosis at age 4.

The crossing is part of a four-day schedule beginning June 10 with arrivals and a poolside social at Bimini Cove. On Thursday, participants take part in a 4Ocean beach cleanup and community buffet dinner. Friday brings a Paddle Out Rose Ceremony to honor cystic fibrosis warriors, followed by rest and safety checks before launch night.

All paddlers must wear a U.S. Coast Guard-approved personal flotation device, waterproof whistle, and reflective gear during dark hours. GPS trackers provided by the event must be worn and active at all times, and mandatory safety meetings will be held. Paddlers must raise at least $2,000 for the cause, and crew or virtual participants have a $500 minimum with final contributions due July 31. Spectators are invited to Lake Worth Beach on June 13 for a daylong beach celebration marking the paddlers' arrival.

Carey Baker, the former Lake County Property Appraiser, has entered the race for Florida's 11th Congressional District seat. The 63-year-old Republican is competing in a primary field that includes Lake County Commissioner Anthony Sabatini, described as one of Baker's former protégés who has previously run for the seat. Baker has deep roots in the district and a lengthy record of public service spanning more than two decades.

Baker has represented much of Central Florida in the state legislature, serving in both the Florida House and Senate before being elected as the county's Property Appraiser. A lifelong Eustis resident, he has maintained prominence in Republican politics since his initial election to the Florida House in 2000. His family has a history of legislative service, with his father also having represented the area in the state House. After losing his bid for re-election as Property Appraiser in 2024, Baker pursued another leadership position within the local party structure, working alongside Sabatini in a campaign to become Lake County Republican Party Chair, though that effort proved unsuccessful.

In his 2024 Property Appraiser race, Baker ran as a write-in candidate but was defeated decisively. Republican David Jordan, who filed as an official candidate, defeated Baker by a margin of three to one in the General Election. Despite this loss, Baker demonstrated strong fundraising abilities that will serve him in the primary race. He raised $233,000 for his Property Appraiser campaign and has already begun building financial support for his Congressional bid. Florida's 11th Congressional District encompasses all of Sumter County, portions of western Orange County, and the majority of Lake County.

The district leans heavily Republican, with more than 57 percent of voters supporting President Donald Trump in the 2024 presidential election, making a GOP nominee likely to prevail in November. On the Democratic side, repeat candidate Barbie Harden Hall suspended her campaign on Saturday due to health issues. Hall had been the leading fundraiser among Democratic candidates and appeared to be the party's strongest choice for the nomination. Three other Democratic candidates remain in the race: Royal Webster, Shawn Bettis, and Dan Williams.

Fertilizer ban begins June 1 in Martin, St. Lucie and Indian River counties

Indian River, Martin and St.

Yesterday, 5:36 PM ET

Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties have implemented a ban on any fertilizer containing phosphorus or nitrogen. The ban is designed to reduce nutrient-rich runoff during the rainy season, which can cause algae blooms in local waterways including the Indian River Lagoon and St. Lucie River.

Jordan Skaggs, with Environmental Resources in St. Lucie County, explained that algae blooms can shade out beneficial plants that fish and wildlife depend on. At certain densities, harmful algae blooms can release toxins that affect human health, wildlife health and overall ecosystem health. Grantly Ricketts, director of University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences St. Lucie County Extension, said composting provides a good alternative during the ban period. According to Ricketts, a small trash can is sufficient for most home composting needs and is the easiest and most practical way to add nutrients to landscaping and gardens.

Jupiter Island could split in half as erosion threatens to breach Peck Lake

A state report warns that nearly the entire 11.5-mile coastline of Jupiter Island is critically eroded, with ocean water threatening to break through at Peck Lake and potentially split the island in two.

Yesterday, 4:42 PM ET

The 2025 Florida Department of Environmental Protection report warns that Jupiter Island's 11.5-mile shoreline has eroded so severely the ocean could break through at Peck Lake, potentially splitting the island in half. The threat stretches from Blowing Rocks Preserve to Peck Lake, where a breach could turn a thin strip of land into an open inlet. A storm breached Peck Lake in the early 1960s before the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers filled it back in with sand. According to the report, a future breach could upend ecosystems that depend on a delicate balance of fresh and saltwater, putting at risk the Joseph Reed Mound, a 3,000- to 4,000-year-old archaeological site already half lost to erosion, along with Blowing Rocks Preserve, Hobe Sound Wildlife Refuge, and St. Lucie Inlet Preserve State Park. Mark Perry with the Florida Oceanographic Society said there are over 33 threatened and endangered species in all of those areas.

Jupiter Island continues fighting erosion through constant maintenance. In April 2025, the town completed a major beach renourishment project, dumping enough sand to fill nearly 400 Olympic-sized swimming pools. Jupiter Island Commissioner Marshall Field said about $3 million in town taxpayer dollars goes toward beach renourishment every year. "We are just reapplying the Band-Aid over and over and over," Field said. He added that if funding for beach renourishment goes away, "the coastline of Florida is going to change very quickly, and not in a way that anybody's particularly happy with."

For those unable to access the county program, Green recommends calling 211, available 24/7, which connects residents with nonprofits and other assistance programs. Florida dive operators fight federal shark feeding ban off state coast

A federal proposal to ban shark feeding in waters within 200 miles of Florida has cleared the U.S. House, drawing sharp criticism from the state's shark diving industry.

Yesterday, 5:55 PM ET

The measure, H.R. 3831, also known as the Safe Florida Seas Act, cleared the U.S. House and would prohibit shark feeding in federal waters within 200 miles of Florida, with an exception for harvesting. Supporters have called it a common-sense safety measure to reduce human-shark interactions. However, Florida's shark diving industry opposes the bill, arguing it unfairly targets conservation-driven tourism businesses while leaving fishing practices untouched. Nathan Campbell, owner of Emerald Charters, said operators avoid surface chumming and instead take bait underwater during dives, a practice they argue is less likely to condition sharks to approach boats looking for food. "What we're really requesting is the chance to speak and share the science and the facts," Campbell said. "No one from Congress has yet asked us to present data or explain our practices."

The bill cites Hawaii's 2006 ban on baited shark diving in federal waters as precedent. However, Florida operators and Dr. Matt Ajemian, an associate research professor at Florida Atlantic University, contend the Hawaii experience demonstrates different results. Ajemian says shark diving in Florida has a "near perfect safety record" for customers. Industry members note that since Hawaii's ban, shark incidents have increased, fatalities have been reported and, according to the Western Pacific Regional Fishery Management Council, at least one in four fishing trips ends with a shark biting off part of the catch.

Yesterday, 1:19 PM ET

Florida voters split on property tax proposal ahead of November ballot

A ballot measure that would raise the homestead exemption and roll back property taxes is drawing mixed reactions from West Palm Beach residents. The measure needs 60% approval to pass.

Yesterday, 5:30 PM ET

Florida voters will decide this November whether to roll back property taxes by raising the homestead exemption for homeowners. The ballot measure would need 60% approval to pass. A similar proposal to raise the homestead exemption failed in 2018.

Reactions from West Palm Beach residents are mixed. Brian Edington supports the proposal because it benefits people who permanently live in Florida as homeowners. He said he is comfortable with local governments spending less and pointed to his neighborhood as an example, noting that trash services are picked up twice weekly when once a week would be adequate. Rosabella Olivares, a shopper in West Palm Beach, expressed concern about reduced funding, saying "There's going to be less money ... because everything is expensive now." While homeowners were generally supportive, renters expressed skepticism. One resident along Clematis Avenue in downtown West Palm Beach said the savings would not reach them, stating "Pretty sure they're going to raise the rent on everybody, all the renters."

Palm Beach County rental assistance swamped as 8,000 calls come in monthly

Palm Beach County's Emergency Rental Assistance Program receives about 600 applications in the first couple hours each month, far outpacing the nearly $2 million in grant funding available.

Yesterday, 6:03 PM ET

Palm Beach County's Community Services Department has nearly $2 million in combined grant funding for its Emergency Rental Assistance Program, designed to help residents who can't pay rent. Yet demand vastly outweighs available resources. Program director Dr. James Green told WPTV that the county receives nearly 8,000 calls for housing assistance monthly, combined with data from 211. Currently, about 800 applications are in the queue for rental assistance. Applications open at the beginning of each month but fill rapidly, with the program typically receiving about 600 applications within the first couple of hours. At the time of this report, the county's assistance portal was marked closed due to high demand. Green urges anyone in need to call 1-833-CSD-WILL to check for availability.

According to the Department of Housing and Urban Development, the average rent for a one-bedroom in Palm Beach County is $2,020 a month. Joanne Bromfield, who recently moved to West Palm Beach from Tampa and spent 12 years as an educator before switching to real estate, described the strain: "Almost every day, all the bills are rising except our paychecks." Green said preventing homelessness depends on community involvement and is calling on people with extra housing to partner with the county to help neighbors in need. For those unable to access the county program, 211 offers another resource available 24/7 to connect residents with nonprofits and other assistance programs in the area.

Jupiter Island could split in half as erosion threatens to breach Peck Lake

A new state report warns nearly the entire coastline of Jupiter Island is critically eroded, with erosion so severe the ocean could break through at Peck Lake and potentially split the island in half.

Yesterday, 4:42 PM ET

A 2025 Florida Department of Environmental Protection report warns that Jupiter Island's 11.5-mile shoreline has eroded so severely that the ocean could break through at Peck Lake, potentially splitting the island in half. The threat stretches from Blowing Rocks Preserve to Peck Lake, where a breach could turn a thin strip of land into an open inlet. A storm previously breached Peck Lake in the early 1960s before the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers filled it back in with sand. According to the report, sites at risk include the Joseph Reed Mound, a 3,000- to 4,000-year-old archaeological site already half lost to erosion, along with wildlife refuges including Blowing Rocks Preserve, Hobe Sound Wildlife Refuge, and St Lucie Inlet Preserve State Park.

Mark Perry with the Florida Oceanographic Society said a future breach could upend ecosystems that depend on a delicate balance of fresh and saltwater. Perry noted there are over 33 threatened and endangered species in those areas. Jupiter Island Commissioner Marshall Field said the town continues to fight erosion through constant maintenance, completing a major beach renourishment project in April 2025. Field acknowledged the town is "reapplying the Band-Aid over and over and over" and will continue as long as funding exists. About $3 million in town taxpayer dollars goes toward beach renourishment annually.

Cuba's former President Raúl Castro makes first public appearance since U.S. indictment

Raúl Castro, Cuba's low-profile former president, appeared in public for the first time since being indicted by the United States for his alleged role in the 1996 downing of two civilian aircraft.

2:25 PM ET

Cuba's former President Raúl Castro made his first public appearance since a U.S. indictment when he celebrated his 95th birthday Friday night at the Ministry of Interior in Havana. State television broadcast footage showing Castro, dressed in his olive-green military uniform, entering a packed theater to a standing ovation, accompanied by his grandson and bodyguard Raúl Guillermo Rodríguez and Cuban President Miguel Díaz-Canel. The celebration gave Cuba's socialist government an opportunity to project defiance as the Trump administration escalates pressure on the fuel-starved island.

Díaz-Canel delivered a tribute to Castro and his late brother Fidel, calling Raúl Castro a target for enemy intelligence services because of his "courage and loyalty." He repeated the slogan "Raúl is Raúl," echoing billboards across Havana and social media posts that appeared since the May 20 U.S. indictment on murder charges. The Justice Department accuses Castro of ordering the 1996 shootdown of two civilian planes flown by Miami-based exiles. The indictment represents an escalation following the Trump administration's January move to cut off Cuba's oil supplies, worsening blackouts and a public health crisis. The administration demands Cuba release political prisoners, implement economic reforms and change its governance to avoid becoming a national security threat.

Shark diving operators push back against federal feeding ban in Florida waters

A federal proposal to ban shark feeding off Florida's coast is drawing criticism from the state's shark diving industry, with operators warning it could devastate conservation-driven tourism businesses.

Yesterday, 5:55 PM ET

The Safe Florida Seas Act, also known as H.R. 3831, has cleared the U.S. House and would prohibit shark feeding in federal waters within 200 miles of Florida, with an exception for harvesting. Supporters have called it a common-sense safety measure to reduce human-shark interactions. However, shark diving operators argue the bill unfairly targets their work while leaving fishing practices untouched. Nathan Campbell, owner of Emerald Charters, said the industry is requesting the chance to speak and share the science and the facts, noting that no one from Congress has yet asked operators to present data or explain their practices. Operators say they avoid surface chumming, instead taking bait underwater during dives, which they argue is less likely to condition sharks to approach boats looking for food.

Dr. Matt Ajemian, an associate research professor at Florida Atlantic University, says shark diving in Florida has a near perfect safety record for customers. Campbell said the industry is urging senators to delay action, gather research and involve all stakeholders in shaping any new rules. The bill cites Hawaii's 2006 ban on baited shark diving in federal waters as precedent, but Florida operators note that since Hawaii's ban, shark incidents have increased and fatalities have been reported.
