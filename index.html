Yesterday, 6:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie residents will receive refunds after the city secured a $24 million settlement with former trash hauler Waste Pro. Both the city and company filed lawsuits against each other in 2021 during the pandemic. The city alleged that Waste Pro failed to meet its contractual service standards, while the company attributed the problems to labor shortages caused by the pandemic. The city council voted unanimously to return all settlement funds to residents through credits applied to property tax bills instead of issuing physical checks.

The settlement amounts vary depending on residents' circumstances. More than 5,700 households that have maintained the same owner since 2022 will receive $364 credits, as these properties experienced the most severe service disruptions during the pandemic. Thousands of other homes will receive a $64 credit to account for rate increases that occurred while Waste Pro breached its contract. These rate increases covered expenses including a new waste site construction, litigation costs, and transitioning to a new waste provider. Councilman David Pickett emphasized that residents who endured the service failures deserve to be compensated for their suffering.

The city faces challenges in distributing funds to properties that changed ownership. The city identified 24,676 properties with title changes since 2022 and 13,241 newly constructed properties. Because the city cannot determine whether ownership changes resulted from trusts, marriage, divorce, or relocation, and cannot locate former owners, these properties will receive the $64 credit. Residents who sold a home and purchased another within the city during this period can submit documentation proving continuous ownership to claim the remaining $300 credit. A 180-day claims window will allow qualified residents to receive the additional credit on their following year's property tax bill, once the claims process is established and reviewed by city staff and the legal department.

Mayor Shannon Martin stated that the funds should benefit residents who experienced the service issues in 2021 and 2022. Resident Kelly Lee expressed satisfaction with the council's decision, saying she was "pleasantly surprised with the outcome" and that "the city presented well thought out plans." Councilman Anthony Bonna indicated the settlement struck an appropriate balance, noting the city had "reached the sweet spot here." The distribution of settlement funds through property tax credits provides direct relief to Port St. Lucie residents while acknowledging the varying degrees of impact the service failures had on different households.

Sargassum seaweed presents a paradox for sea turtles along Florida's coastline. While many beachgoers view the brown seaweed as a nuisance that creates unpleasant odors and clutters shorelines, researchers at Florida Atlantic University explain that the situation is far more complex. The seaweed serves critical functions for sea turtles at different stages of their lives, even as excessive accumulation on beaches creates new hazards for hatchlings.

When sea turtle hatchlings emerge from their nests, they immediately swim toward the ocean. Once offshore, they seek out floating mats of sargassum that become their refuge during one of the most vulnerable periods of their lives. According to Emily Turla, laboratory coordinator at FAU's Marine Science Laboratory and a Ph.D. student, "Sargassum is an important habitat for young sea turtles when it's out offshore in the currents because that's where they swim out to right as they get off the beach." These floating mats provide essential shelter from predators, sources of food, and necessary habitat for young turtles navigating the open ocean.

Dr. Brian LaPointe, a research professor at FAU's Harbor Branch Oceanographic Institute, emphasizes the importance of sargassum in the broader marine ecosystem. "When sargassum is out in the open ocean, it is deemed essential fish habitat and it's also critical habitat for hatchlings leaving the beach," LaPointe said. The seaweed plays an important ecological role that extends beyond sea turtles alone.

The challenges emerge when excessive amounts of sargassum wash onto Florida's beaches. Large piles of seaweed create physical obstacles that hatchlings must navigate to reach the ocean. Turla notes that beachgoers sometimes observe hatchlings struggling within the seaweed accumulation. LaPointe explains that the extra time hatchlings spend maneuvering around these obstacles significantly extends their journey to the water, leaving them more vulnerable to predators during this critical period. As sea turtle nesting season progresses across South Florida, scientists stress that coastal communities must address an ongoing challenge: finding effective ways to manage excessive sargassum while preserving its essential ecological benefits. Understanding the dual nature of sargassum serves as an important reminder that even the most frustrating aspects of the natural environment can play vital roles in supporting local ecosystems and marine life.

The State of Florida filed notice on June 15 in Martin County's Nineteenth Judicial Circuit County Court stating that a subpoena would be issued on June 30. The subpoena seeks records connected to any treatment or hospitalization Woods may have received.

The prosecutors' request targets specific medical documentation. The subpoena will demand all reports containing statements Woods made about his use of alcohol or chemical substances, as well as any drug screening results obtained during his medical care. Additionally, the filing requests the identification of whoever conducted any testing procedures. Assistant State Attorney Nirlaine Tallandier Smartt submitted the filing on behalf of State Attorney Thomas R., moving forward with the state's case preparation.

The legal process includes a set timeline for response. Anyone wishing to object to the subpoena must submit those objections to the Assistant State Attorney within ten days of the June 15 filing date. This notice and objection period allows Woods' legal team or medical providers to challenge the request if they believe it violates privacy rights or other legal protections before the records must be surrendered.

Woods was arrested in March on Jupiter Island after authorities responded to a traffic crash. He subsequently entered a not guilty plea to the DUI charge, setting the stage for the current discovery process. The subpoena for medical records represents standard procedure as prosecutors build their case, seeking documentation that could be relevant to establishing the circumstances surrounding the arrest and any potential impairment.

Researchers say sargassum provides important habitat for young sea turtles offshore, but excessive amounts washing ashore can create obstacles for hatchlings trying to reach the ocean.

Yesterday, 5:16 PM ET

Emily Turla, laboratory coordinator at Florida Atlantic University's Marine Science Laboratory and a Ph.D. student, explained that sargassum serves as critical habitat for young sea turtles when it floats offshore in ocean currents. After hatching, sea turtles swim away from shore during their first vulnerable days, and floating mats of sargassum provide shelter from predators, food sources, and essential habitat during this critical stage of their lives.

Dr. Brian LaPointe, research professor with FAU's Harbor Branch Oceanographic Institute, noted that sargassum plays an important role in the marine ecosystem as essential fish habitat and critical habitat for hatchlings leaving the beach. However, problems arise when excessive amounts of sargassum wash ashore. Large piles of seaweed become obstacles for hatchlings attempting to reach the ocean, and the extra time spent navigating around the seaweed increases their vulnerability to predators. As sea turtle nesting season continues across South Florida, researchers say finding ways to manage excessive sargassum while preserving its ecological benefits remains an important challenge for coastal communities and a reminder that even frustrating aspects of the natural environment play crucial roles in the local ecosystem.

Sargassum Seaweed Helps Young Sea Turtles Offshore But Creates Obstacles on Beach

Florida Atlantic University researchers say sargassum provides critical habitat for hatchling sea turtles offshore but can create obstacles for turtles trying to reach the ocean when it washes ashore.

Yesterday, 5:16 PM ET

Sargassum plays a vital but complicated role for sea turtles along Florida's coast. Emily Turla, laboratory coordinator at Florida Atlantic University's Marine Science Laboratory, explains that sargassum is an important habitat for young sea turtles when it floats offshore in ocean currents. After hatching, sea turtles swim away from shore and rely on floating mats of sargassum for shelter from predators, food sources, and critical habitat during one of the most vulnerable stages of their lives. Research professor Dr. Brian LaPointe with FAU's Harbor Branch Oceanographic Institute notes that sargassum is deemed essential fish habitat in the open ocean and serves as critical habitat for hatchlings leaving the beach.

However, problems arise when excessive amounts of sargassum wash ashore. Large piles of seaweed become obstacles for hatchlings attempting to reach the ocean, sometimes trapping them in the debris. According to LaPointe, the extra time hatchlings spend navigating around these piles increases the duration before they reach the water, making them more vulnerable to predators. As sea turtle nesting season continues across South Florida, researchers say understanding the connection between turtles and sargassum reminds us that even the most frustrating parts of the natural environment play an important role in the local ecosystem.

Hobe Sound mom sets up free food pantry outside home

Jacquelyn Koester set up a Little Free Pantry outside her Hobe Sound home to help neighbors stretch their budgets as food costs rise.

Yesterday, 3:32 AM ET

Jacquelyn Koester set up a Little Free Pantry just steps from her home behind the Hobe Sound Public Library to help neighbors stretch their budgets amid rising food costs. The pantry is stocked with food and baby essentials for families struggling with higher prices. "I knew the second I posted on that group that Hobe Sound was going to show up for us because that's what we do, we are a tight knit community," Koester said. What began as a simple Facebook post to ensure local families could access basic necessities has grown into a steady source of support available to anyone in need with no questions asked.

When gas and food prices increased, the pantry emptied faster than Koester could replace items. She put out a call for help on Facebook, and the community response has been overwhelming, with donations regularly filling the shelves. "Someone posted in the Facebook group anonymously saying I drove up from Jupiter for this, this is going to help get me through my week," Koester said. Those messages motivate her to maintain the pantry. "The saying it takes a village is so true and the villages look a lot different than they used to, but people still show up for each other and people want to help," she said.

Former Senate Agriculture Chair Denise Grimsley backs Ben Butler in Congressional District 9

Former Senate Agriculture Chair Denise Grimsley is spearheading a wave of endorsements for Republican Ben Butler as he seeks to represent Florida's 9th Congressional District.

3:45 AM ET

Former Senate Agriculture Chair Denise Grimsley is leading a slate of endorsements for Republican Ben Butler in Florida's 9th Congressional District. The endorsements also include former state Rep. Cary Pigman and former Florida Forestry Association President Jimmy Beilling. Grimsley, who served in the state Senate from 2012 to 2018 after eight years in the House, chaired the Senate Agriculture Committee during her tenure. She said Butler's experience as a dairy farmer and cattle rancher gives him firsthand knowledge of the issues facing Florida families and producers. Butler, a ninth-generation Floridian, sits on the South Florida Water Management District Governing Board and has made his rural roots and agricultural experience central to his campaign message.

Pigman described Butler as a practical problem-solver with a track record of community service, noting that good leaders listen, solve problems, and put people ahead of politics. Beilling argued that the district's farming communities need a representative who understands their challenges and will fight for their interests in Washington. Florida's 9th Congressional District includes all of Osceola County and portions of Orange and Polk counties. The seat is currently held by Democrat Darren Soto, who won re-election under the previous, more Democratic-leaning district lines. The new map has shifted the political landscape: more than 58% of voters in CD 9 backed Republican Donald Trump in 2024, making the district a top target for the GOP this cycle.
