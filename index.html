Martin County saw two brush fires this week, including one sparked by a lightning strike. Both are now fully contained and being monitored by the Florida Forest Service.

David Grubitch, a mitigation specialist and spokesperson with the Florida Forest Service, said the department is in peak fire season. The summer heat is drying out fine dead fuels, making them receptive to ignition when lightning strikes occur. Rain often extinguishes these fires immediately, but sometimes they travel underground and surface days later.

The Florida Forest Service says the conditions that fuel these fires are not going away anytime soon, but residents should not be alarmed. Grubitch explained that residents should never assume someone else has already called in a brush fire.

Grubitch stressed that the public plays a crucial role in helping the Florida Forest Service do its job. If you see something, say something, he said, as it can make all the difference in how quickly crews are able to get a fire under control.

2026-06-13

Enrique Rosario Liz, a Port St. Lucie teenager named Youth of the Year for the Southeast Region of the Boys & Girls Clubs of America, was surprised Friday at the Boys & Girls Club of St. Lucie County in Fort Pierce with a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity. He will serve as one of 12 club members from across the country selected to be an official Coca-Cola flag bearer at the FIFA World Cup finals on July 19. The surprise came courtesy of the Boys & Girls Clubs of America and their partnership with the Coca-Cola Company. When the box containing the news was walked into the room surrounded by fellow club members, his reaction was immediate. "I mean, this is so insane," Rosario Liz said. Along with the trip, he also received official gear inside the box.

Rosario Liz had already been juggling a packed summer, preparing for move-in at the University of Florida and an internship, but said the World Cup had been on his mind. "My time is really being taken up by the World Cup right now, forcing my parents to watch every game," Rosario Liz said. He credited the Boys & Girls Club with shaping who he has become as a leader. "The club really helped me to find what my leadership style was going to be, and really redefined what my definition of leadership was," Rosario Liz said.

The recognition as a flag bearer comes after his selection as Youth of the Year for the Southeast Region, one of the highest honors given by the Boys & Girls Clubs of America. As one of only 12 club members chosen nationally for this role, Rosario Liz will represent not only his local club in St. Lucie County but also youth organizations across the country at the World Cup finals. "The Boys & Girls Clubs has been my everything," he said. He closed with a message for younger members at the club. "Please stay with your Boys & Girls Clubs, because one day I want to see each and every one of you in the same position that I am in right now," Rosario Liz said.

The announcement marks a significant milestone for the Port St. Lucie teen as he prepares to transition to college life at the University of Florida while taking on this prestigious international role at the World Cup finals.

Treasure Coast Traffic Report: Major Ramp and Road Closures Planned July 12-17

Motorists should prepare for overnight interstate ramp closures, extended neighborhood road closures and lane restrictions across Martin, St. Lucie and Indian River counties during the week of July 10-17 as construction and maintenance work continues on several major roadways. The Florida Department of Transportation's latest weekly traffic report identifies multiple lane restr...

2026-07-10

Motorists should prepare for overnight interstate ramp closures, extended neighborhood road closures and lane restrictions across Martin, St. Lucie and Indian River counties during the week of July 10-17 as construction and maintenance work continues on several major roadways. The Florida Department of Transportation's latest weekly traffic report identifies multiple lane restrictions, with additional emphasis on full road, ramp and bridge closures expected to have the greatest effect on drivers.

The southbound Interstate 95 exit ramp to County Road 708, known as Bridge Road, at Exit 96 will be closed from 8 p.m. Sunday, July 12, through Thursday, July 16. Drivers will be directed to exit at Kanner Highway, travel south on Pratt Whitney Road and then head east on Bridge Road for roadway construction. The northbound I-95 exit ramp to westbound State Road 68, or Orange Avenue, will also be closed from 8 p.m. Sunday through Thursday for paving and lighting installation. Motorists will be detoured using the exit to eastbound Orange Avenue, northbound Jenkins Road and westbound Loop Road before returning to westbound Orange Avenue. Weather-related contingency dates for both closures are scheduled for July 19-23.

Extended closures are planned along several side streets near the SW Port St. Lucie Boulevard widening project, beginning at 7 a.m. Monday, July 13, and continuing until 5 p.m. Portions of SW Jaslo Avenue, SW Jacoby Avenue, SW McCullough Avenue, SW McCall Road and SW McHord Avenue will close intermittently for drainage and utility installation. FDOT said adjacent roads will not be closed at the same time and access to homes and businesses will be maintained. Detours will generally use SW Paar Drive, SW McCain Street, SW McIntyre Street, SW Kabane Street and other nearby roads depending on the closure location. Additional lane closures are planned on SW Port St. Lucie Boulevard between Seafury Lane and Lennard Road during daytime hours Monday through Friday, with two lanes potentially closing overnight for drainage work, milling and repaving.

Motorists should also expect overnight lane closures on I-95 between Bridge Road and the Martin-St. Lucie county line, with up to two lanes closing in each direction for milling, resurfacing and signage work. The existing A1A North Causeway drawbridge will not open to marine traffic from 7 a.m. Thursday, July 16, through 6 p.m. Friday as crews remove portions of the former drawbridge, though vehicle traffic on the new bridge will not be affected. Construction schedules may change because of weather or other unforeseen conditions, and motorists should allow additional travel time and use caution around workers and equipment.
