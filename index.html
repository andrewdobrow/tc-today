2:21 PM ET

First responders from Jupiter, Delray Beach, and Palm Beach County fire departments have joined a FEMA Urban Search and Rescue Task Force heading to Venezuela to help with relief efforts following an earthquake. Jupiter Fire Rescue contributed two firefighters to the deployment, which totals approximately 80 personnel from the region. The team received their deployment orders with less than 24 hours notice and is expected to remain in Venezuela for at least two weeks.

The primary focus of the mission involves conducting search and rescue operations in collapsed structures of all sizes, from large commercial buildings to residential homes. To accomplish this work effectively, the rescue team packed specialized heavy-duty equipment designed for breaking through debris, cutting through materials, breaching barriers, and stabilizing unsafe structures. They also brought technical rope rescue tools to assist in extracting people from difficult-to-reach locations within damaged buildings.

Stephen Shaw, deputy fire chief of operations for Jupiter Fire Rescue, emphasized that the deployment demonstrates the department's core mission and values. "This right here represents one of these situations where, they need help people need help and for us at Jupiter Fire Rescue, if someone needs help we go," Shaw said. He noted that responding to humanitarian crises abroad reflects how the department approaches its responsibility to assist people in urgent need, regardless of location.

Shaw also pointed out that the deployment will have positive effects for Jupiter Fire Rescue long after the team returns home. The experience gained and skills practiced during search and rescue operations in Venezuela will strengthen the department's capabilities as it continues to develop and expand. "And when they come back the learning that has taken place and the skills that they have been practicing is only going to benefit us internally as we continue to grow our fire department," Shaw said. By sending personnel on international disaster response missions, the department gains practical experience and expertise that enhances training and readiness for future emergencies in their own community.

A Vero Beach-based organization is collecting donations for Venezuelan communities affected by a devastating 7.5 magnitude earthquake. Latinas Nexus Network is hosting a Venezuela Earthquake Relief Drive to gather essential supplies for those impacted by the disaster. The effort is being coordinated by founders Lourana Navarro and Juliana Marino, along with Heidi Ramirez. Donations can be dropped off at 684 Old Dixie Hwy, Vero Beach, FL 32962, through Saturday, June 27, 2026, between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m.

Navarro, who was born in Venezuela and currently lives in Vero Beach, explained that while her family in Caracas is safe, surrounding communities have experienced catastrophic losses. She emphasized that the scale of destruction will require years of recovery work. "Seeing the devastation, I know this is going to take decades to recover. We as a country don't have the resources," Navarro said. Marino highlighted the organization's commitment to directing aid efficiently. "The only thing that I can do is do our best to come together and help them and find different resources so we can get it directly to the people and bring the help that they need," she said. The organizers are partnering with Global Empowerment Mission to transport collected supplies to Venezuela, and they guarantee that 100 percent of donations will go through verified humanitarian relief channels.

The relief drive is seeking a comprehensive range of supplies to address urgent needs. Items being collected include powder Gatorade, non-perishable food items, baby formula and baby food, diapers in all sizes, baby wipes, feminine hygiene products, soap, shampoo, toothbrushes and toothpaste, rain boots, toilet paper, blankets, sleeping bags with batteries, flashlights and batteries, heavy duty garbage bags, first aid kits, bandages and gauze, hand sanitizer, cleaning supplies, face masks, nitrile gloves, and plastic bins with lids. According to Marino, hundreds of thousands of people currently need assistance. Navarro identified the most critical shortages, stating "the worst is the food and the place to sleep, to take a bath. They don't have that right now."

The organizers emphasize that the relief drive welcomes support from the entire community. "We are asking not just the Hispanic community, we are asking everyone. They really need us," the founders said. Those wishing to contribute or coordinate donations can contact Lourana Navarro at (954) 205-0700, Juliana Marino at (772) 913-0805, or Heidi Ramirez at (772) 213-2747.

3:41 PM ET

A new study by Grigor Law Injury Car Accident Lawyers reveals a disturbing trend in Florida's traffic fatalities. The analysis of data from the Fatality Analysis Reporting System operated by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration shows that fatal crashes involving road rage nearly tripled in the state between 2023 and 2024. The number of deadly anger-related driving incidents jumped from 12 to 36 in just one year, representing a 200% increase that places Florida second in the nation for this troubling statistic.

Only Wyoming experienced a higher percentage increase in fatal road rage crashes during the same period, with a 300% spike. However, Wyoming's raw numbers tell a different story. The state saw fatal incidents rise from just one to four, far below Florida's alarming total of 36 deaths. Texas ranked third nationally with an 83.33% increase in road rage fatalities, climbing from six incidents in 2023 to 11 in 2024. These three states clearly stand out as having the most significant year-over-year increases in deadly anger-related driving confrontations.

Several other states in the top 10 for road rage fatalities experienced lower percentage increases but recorded substantially higher raw numbers than Florida. Alabama ranked seventh with a 27.03% jump, but the figures climbed from 74 fatalities to 94, dwarfing Florida's numbers. Colorado came in eighth with just an 8.33% increase, yet the state reported far more total incidents than any other state, rising from 120 in 2023 to 130 in 2024. South Carolina also had significant raw numbers despite minimal percentage growth, seeing only a 1.82% increase but recording 55 crashes jumping to 56.

The study examined data from 2020 through 2024 and highlights the growing pattern of aggressive driving incidents turning deadly on roadways across the country. While Florida's percentage increase places it second nationally, the raw numbers reveal a serious public safety concern. The data suggests that anger management issues among Florida drivers are creating a dangerous environment on the state's highways and roads. The findings underscore the need for awareness about the consequences of aggressive driving behavior and the deadly toll it takes on communities.

Gov. DeSantis signs third revision of Live Local Act to expand affordable housing

Ron DeSantis signed the updated Live Local Act this month after it was approved by the House and Senate, marking the third revision since the measure was originally approved three years ago.

11:45 AM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed the updated Live Local Act this month after it was approved by the House and Senate. The Senate bill (SB 962) was sponsored by Fleming Island Republican Sen. Jennifer Bradley, and the House companion (HB 837) was sponsored by Coral Gables Republican Rep. This marks the third time the Live Local Act has been revised since it was originally approved in 2023.

The law requires cities and counties to allow apartment buildings or mixed-use housing projects in many areas currently zoned for commercial or industrial use, if at least 40% of the units are set aside as affordable housing for 30 years. That can also apply to land owned by local governments, school districts or large religious institutions. The law limits how much local governments can restrict the height or design of these developments, making it harder for cities and counties to block them through zoning rules. The latest revision would exclude farms and farm operations from the land-use categories eligible for its development preemptions. The measure will allow more dwelling unit types, limit local restrictions and protect financial sources for buying homes. The original intent of the measure was to spur construction of affordable and workforce housing across Florida through funding, tax incentives and zoning preemptions. The original iteration fell under criticism from watchdog groups such as Florida TaxWatch for falling short on "missing middle" renters, households that earn too much to qualify for affordable housing subsidies but not enough to comfortably pay market rents in their area.

Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League urges Treasure Coast pet owners to prepare for hurricane season

With hurricane season underway, Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League is calling on South Florida residents to create disaster plans for their pets before the next storm arrives.

Jun 24, 1:34 PM ET

Dr. Alyssa Comroe, CEO of Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League, told WPTV the safest place for pets during a hurricane is right alongside their owners. The shelter is urging South Florida residents to create a disaster plan for their pets before the next storm hits with several key recommendations.

Pet owners should ensure their animals wear a collar with current identification and have an updated microchip registered with correct contact information. The shelter recommends introducing pets to a designated safe room ahead of time, such as a walk-in closet or downstairs bathroom, and conducting practice runs so the hurricane is not their first time there. A pet emergency kit should include a three-day supply of food and water in airtight containers, bowls, a sturdy carrier, bedding, towels, medications for two weeks, vaccination records, first-aid supplies, litter and box for cats, leashes, harnesses, cool packs, wipes, waste bags, and multiple forms of visible ID. Pet owners should keep a printed list of local pet-friendly shelters and hotels in an accessible place and maintain recent photos of pets, ideally with their owner, to help with identification if separated. Florida's hurricane season runs June through November.

Attorney General Ashley Moody assisted in relief efforts for Venezuela on Saturday while in Doral at a staging area for the Global Empowerment Mission. Moody and her husband, Justin Duralia, helped pack containers of food and other boxes of relief goods bound for areas affected by earthquakes that struck the South American country this past week. "Florida stands with the Venezuelan people. Justin and I were inspired by the dedicated and passionate volunteers that gathered to donate and assemble aid for the Venezuelan people reeling from the devastating earthquakes," Moody said in a news release.

The Global Empowerment Mission, founded in Doral, specializes in rapid relief for disaster victims and provides long-term recovery assistance for natural disasters and other humanitarian crises including conflicts. The situation in Venezuela remains dire as tens of thousands of people remain missing following back-to-back earthquakes that struck Wednesday. More than 1,400 people have been confirmed dead. On Friday night, Venezuelan authorities announced they would block off access to La Guaira, the epicenter of destruction, requiring official permits for entry as chaos and traffic affected search efforts. Citizens digging through rubble reported seeing few state rescue teams in the hardest hit areas despite the 7.2 and 7.5 magnitude quakes.

Off-duty Martin County Fire Rescue lieutenant recovers body of missing diver found dead near Fort Pierce

A missing diver was found dead on the ocean floor approximately 6 to 7 miles north-northeast of the Fort Pierce Inlet Friday, recovered by an off-duty Martin Co.

Yesterday, 9:12 AM ET

A missing diver was found dead on the ocean floor approximately 6 to 7 miles north-northeast of the Fort Pierce Inlet Friday. The U.S. Coast Guard Station Fort Pierce received a Mayday call over VHF Channel 16 at approximately 11:30 a.m. Friday regarding the missing diver. Following the distress call, the Coast Guard notified the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission, which immediately deployed 4 vessels to assist in the search. The St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office Marine Unit, which had independently monitored the Mayday call, was already en route to assist.

Martin County Fire Rescue Lt. Daniel DeGrazia was called in at 1:30 p.m. Friday to assist with recovery efforts. DeGrazia was off-duty at the time and assisted by his own will. Responding units made contact with the vessel involved nearly due east of Avalon State Park. At approximately 4:15 p.m., DeGrazia recovered the body of the missing diver at the bottom of the ocean in approximately 55 feet of water. The diver had drowned and was pronounced deceased. The deceased was transported to Coast Guard Station Fort Pierce. The St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office Criminal Investigations Division is the lead agency on the investigation. The victim's name is expected to be released Monday.

George Snow Scholarship Fund awards record $7.5 million to 470 Palm Beach and Broward students

The Boca Raton-based George Snow Scholarship Fund reached a milestone in 2026, distributing more than $7.5 million in scholarships and support services to students across two South Florida counties.

Jun 25, 11:46 AM ET

The George Snow Scholarship Fund, headquartered in Boca Raton, awarded a record $7.5 million in scholarships and support services to 470 students across Palm Beach and Broward counties in 2026, President Tim Snow told WPTV. Founded over 40 years ago, the nonprofit has distributed more than $45 million to over 4,100 students, making it the largest scholarship provider in Palm Beach County. The program offers four-year commitments to ensure stability throughout college and is designed to bridge the gap between traditional financial aid and what students and families can afford.

GSSF's services extend beyond financial assistance, providing mentorship, professional development, and resources like the College Compass podcast, according to Vice President of Development Jay Brandt. One 2026 recipient, Zya Chow, a Wellington Community High School graduate, earned both the Royal Palm Hearing Aid Scholarship and the Davidowitz Family Caregiving Youth Scholarship and plans to attend Georgetown University. The fund also operates the Doyle-Whelchel Scholars Boutique, led by Scholar Services Coordinator Garvey Antoine, which offers professional, formal, and casual wear for interviews, internships, and campus life. The boutique accepts donations of new or gently used clothing, footwear, and accessories.

Jupiter firefighters deploy to Venezuela for earthquake rescue operations

Jupiter Fire Rescue sent two firefighters to Venezuela as part of an 80-member FEMA search and rescue team responding to earthquake damage.

2:21 PM ET

First responders from Jupiter, Delray Beach, and Palm Beach County fire departments have deployed to Venezuela as part of a FEMA Urban Search and Rescue Task Force to assist with earthquake relief efforts. Jupiter Fire Rescue deployed two firefighters as part of a crew of 80 that was sent with less than 24 hours notice and is expected to remain in Venezuela for at least two weeks.

The mission focuses on search and rescue operations in collapsed structures, ranging from large buildings to smaller homes. The team brought heavy-duty equipment for breaking, cutting, breaching, and stabilization, as well as technical rope rescue tools. Stephen Shaw, deputy fire chief of operations for Jupiter Fire Rescue, said the deployment reflects the department's commitment to helping those in need. Shaw noted that the experience will also strengthen the department when the crew returns home, as the learning and skills practiced will benefit Jupiter Fire Rescue internally as the department continues to grow.
