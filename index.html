1:04 PM ET

Florida prosecutors announced plans to obtain Tiger Woods' medical records as the golfer faces a driving under the influence charge. Woods entered a not guilty plea following his March arrest on Jupiter Island in Martin County. On June 15, the State of Florida filed a notice in the County Court of the Nineteenth Judicial Circuit in Martin County indicating that a subpoena would be issued on June 30 for records related to Woods' medical treatment and hospitalization.

The subpoena will seek specific categories of information from Woods' medical providers. Prosecutors are requesting any reports that document statements Woods made about his use of alcohol or chemical substances. The subpoena will also target all drug screening results on file and the identity of whoever conducted any testing procedures. Assistant State Attorney Nirlaine Tallandier Smartt submitted the filing on behalf of State Attorney Thomas R. The prosecution is giving anyone who wishes to object to the subpoena ten days to submit their objections to the Assistant State Attorney.

The move represents a significant step in the prosecution's case building against the prominent athlete. By obtaining Woods' medical records, prosecutors can develop evidence related to the circumstances of his arrest and any potential impairment at the time. The specific focus on statements about substance use and drug screening results suggests the state is constructing a detailed timeline and documentation of Woods' physical and chemical condition.

Woods' not guilty plea indicates he intends to contest the DUI charge in court. The subpoena for his medical records will likely play a central role in the legal proceedings ahead. As the case moves forward through the court system, both the prosecution and defense will rely on medical evidence and documentation to support their respective positions regarding whether Woods was operating a vehicle while under the influence of alcohol or drugs.

A Hobe Sound resident has created a Little Free Pantry outside her home to support neighbors struggling with elevated food and gas prices. Jacquelyn Koester established the pantry to make basic necessities and baby essentials available to anyone in the community who needs them, with no requirements or questions asked. Located just steps from her home near the Hobe Sound Public Library, the pantry represents a direct response to financial hardship facing local families.

Koester initially reached out to the community through Facebook when the pantry's supplies began disappearing faster than she could restock them. As prices for essentials climbed, demand for the free resources grew significantly. The response from Hobe Sound residents proved to be substantial and sustained. "I knew the second I posted on that group that Hobe Sound was going to show up for us because that's what we do, we are a tight knit community," Koester said. Donations have regularly filled the pantry's shelves since her appeal, allowing her to maintain consistent access to items for those in need.

The pantry has attracted support from beyond the immediate Hobe Sound area. One individual posted anonymously in the Facebook group, sharing that they had driven from Jupiter specifically to use the resource. "Someone posted in the Facebook group anonymously saying I drove up from Jupiter for this, this is going to help get me through my week," Koester said. These messages of gratitude and impact fuel her commitment to keeping the pantry well-stocked and accessible to everyone who needs it.

Koester views the pantry as evidence that communities continue to support one another despite changing circumstances. She emphasizes that while neighborhoods have evolved over time, the fundamental human desire to help others persists. "The saying it takes a village is so true and the villages look a lot different than they used to, but people still show up for each other and people want to help," she said. What began as a single mother's effort to assist her neighbors has grown into a meaningful community resource that demonstrates how collective action can ease the burden of economic hardship. Those interested in accessing the pantry or learning more about how to contribute can find information through the Facebook group that Koester created for this purpose.

Martin County could lose $67 million under proposed Florida homestead exemption expansion

A proposed expansion of Florida's homestead exemption could reduce property tax bills for homeowners, but there are concerns that renters may face higher costs if local governments increase tax rates to make up for lost revenue. According to a Florida Policy Institute analysis, Martin County could lose more than $67 million in tax revenue if voters approve the amendment in Nove...

Yesterday, 4:49 PM ET

A proposed expansion of Florida's homestead exemption could reduce property tax bills for homeowners, but there are concerns that renters may face higher costs if local governments increase tax rates to make up for lost revenue. According to a Florida Policy Institute analysis, Martin County could lose more than $67 million in tax revenue if voters approve the amendment in November. The same study projects that local governments across Florida could lose nearly $4.8 billion statewide.

Mike Readling, executive director of Habitat for Humanity of Martin County, reports a significant increase in people seeking assistance with rent and utilities, with 95 percent of those requests coming from renters. He emphasizes that the proposed tax cut would provide little benefit to the population his organization serves. "The demographic that we're working with and Habitat for Humanity tries to help, it's not going to help them at all," Readling said. Attorney John Tolley notes that local governments facing budget shortfalls would have limited options: reduce services, use reserve funds, or increase millage rates.

If millage rates increase, homeowners with the exemption would still benefit from the tax break, according to realtor Michael Caputo. However, landlords without the exemption could face higher tax bills and might pass those costs to renters. Caputo acknowledged that renters could be priced out of an already expensive rental market. A 2025 Florida Senate analysis found only 24 affordable and available rental units for every 100 extremely low-income renters in the state. The amendment also includes a provision that could offset some rental increases. It would lower the property tax cap on non-homesteaded properties, including commercial apartment complexes, from 10 percent to 5 percent. Tolley suggested this change could lead to lower rents. "The fact that you could have less taxes, you actually might see a trend where rents go down even further than they were before," Tolley said.

Caputo expressed skepticism that municipalities would raise millage rates significantly and believes the rental market may stabilize through supply and demand forces. He noted that landlords would try to pass on increased costs to tenants, but if that strategy fails, they would adjust their pricing accordingly. The outcome remains uncertain as voters prepare to make their decision on the amendment in November.

10:26 AM ET

Rapids Water Park in Riviera Beach has discontinued its interim youth supervision policy that mandated adult chaperones for guests under 18. The park noted that this policy was temporary in nature, implemented and removed periodically based on coordination with local law enforcement and monitoring of social media activity. The policy is currently no longer active at the facility.

During the time the policy was in place, visitors under age 18 had to be accompanied by a parent, legal guardian or another responsible adult at least 21 years old who arrived at the park with their group. Supervising adults were required to show a valid government-issued photo ID with their date of birth when entering the park. They also had to remain at the facility throughout the visit and could oversee no more than five minors per day. These requirements were intended to maintain order and safety across the water park's grounds.

Tina Hatcher, speaking on behalf of the park, emphasized the organization's commitment to guest safety. "At Rapids Water Park, we are committed to providing a safe and family-friendly environment for our guests at all times. We work hand in hand with local officials and monitor social media trends as we uphold strict safety standards through our code of conduct, on-site police officers and a visible security presence," Hatcher said in a statement. She explained that the temporary supervision requirement was created to support these broader safety measures and deliver a positive experience for visitors.

The park maintains several permanent safety features beyond the now-ended supervision policy. These include a established code of conduct for all visitors, uniformed police officers stationed on-site, and noticeable security personnel throughout the facility. Those planning to visit can find more information on the park's website at rapidswaterpark.com. The removal of the interim youth supervision policy reflects the park's flexible approach to safety measures, which it adjusts in response to specific conditions and input from community authorities.

2:34 PM ET

Governor Ron DeSantis signed five public safety bills Tuesday in Winter Haven, advancing Florida's tough-on-crime agenda with measures targeting various criminal activities and threats to law enforcement. The legislation strengthens the career-offender registry, increases penalties for trafficking xylazine (an animal tranquilizer known as "tranq"), and restricts nitrous oxide sales. Two additional bills update gang statutes to include social media activity and online admissions, while the Officer Jason Raynor Act increases penalties for crimes committed against police officers. The measure is named for a Daytona Beach police officer killed in the line of duty. Attorney General James Uthmeier emphasized the state's commitment to protecting law enforcement, stating "If you lay a finger on a police officer, you're going away for a long time."

DeSantis also addressed the issue of teen takeovers, large gatherings of young people that have overwhelmed public spaces in some cases. Following a recent Clearwater incident that ended in a shooting, DeSantis warned participants that such gatherings will not be tolerated. "We do not recognize any teen takeover. If you try that, you are doing that at your peril," he said. The governor further warned that if local officials fail to hold participants accountable, he will appoint someone who will.

Democrats are shifting focus to a separate property tax amendment backed by Republicans that will appear on the November ballot. The measure would expand homestead exemptions and limit certain local tax revenues, requiring 60 percent voter approval to pass. Republicans argue the proposal would provide relief to primary homeowners while protecting core services. Democrats counter that the amendment could reduce funding for cities and counties, forcing local governments to make difficult budget choices. House Democratic Leader Fentrice Driskell stated "The property tax is a major source of revenue for local communities, funding public services such as hospitals, education, public safety, garbage pickups, libraries, public parks, road maintenance, and even election resources." Senator Lori Berman warned communities could face stark choices: "We could find ourselves in a situation where our communities have to decide between funding the police department or keeping open a public school."

The amendment faces legal challenges over its ballot language, with opponents arguing the wording is misleading and resembles campaign language rather than neutral summary text. DeSantis said Tuesday he believes the state will prevail in court but emphasized the case needs to move quickly before ballots are printed later in summer. The contrast between the tough-on-crime bill signing and the property tax debate reflects competing priorities within Florida's government about how best to support public safety and local services.

3:05 PM ET

A 34-year-old property manager was arrested Tuesday on five counts of animal cruelty following the discovery of severely neglected animals at a tree farm on Southwest Connors Highway. Jose Arreaga faces first-degree misdemeanor charges after an investigation that began when a community outreach manager reported an anonymous tip with photographs showing deceased dogs on the property.

On June 4, deputies responded to the property in the 16000 block of Southwest Connors Highway and found animals in alarming condition. Inside a makeshift enclosure with a tarp roof, investigators discovered a decomposed dog with food and water bowls filled with sand. The enclosure gate was tied shut with rope. Near the residence, deputies found Gorda, a reddish-brown, white, and black female cur mix severely infested with fleas and heavily lactating. A second dog, a cream-colored male Anatolian shepherd, was found collapsed beneath a tractor. The dog was so severely emaciated he initially appeared dead and could barely move his head. No food or water was available where he lay. Both dogs tested positive for heartworm disease.

Over the following day, deputies trapped three puppies sheltering beneath the house that Arreaga said were about 4 weeks old. All three female Anatolian shepherd and cur mix puppies had severe flea infestations, open sores, and lethargy requiring multiple baths at the Humane Society. A brown domestic shorthair cat that Arreaga surrendered was underweight and possibly pregnant. The cat gave birth the night she arrived, but all four kittens were stillborn. The Humane Society's lead veterinarian determined the Anatolian shepherd's condition reflected severe medical neglect, starvation, and malnutrition. The dog had anemia consistent with iron deficiency and blood loss, plus acute and chronic injuries including possible fracture and disc injury. The three puppies were diagnosed with severe ringworm, flea bite dermatitis, fungal infection, bacterial skin conditions, hookworms, and anemia from severe flea infestation.

Deputies consulted with a doctor at the ASPCA's Veterinary Forensic Sciences Unit regarding the deceased dog. Given the advanced decomposition, the doctor advised that establishing a definitive cause of death was unlikely. Based on this assessment, felony animal cruelty charges related to the deceased dog will not be pursued. The five misdemeanor charges are based on the deprivation of adequate food, water, and nutrition for Gorda, her three puppies, and the Anatolian shepherd. Arreaga was taken to Martin County Jail on a $12,500 bond with conditions prohibiting him from owning or maintaining any animals.

Tampa General Hospital launches Florida's first newborn genome sequencing program this summer

Tampa General Hospital will serve as the flagship site for the Sunshine Genetics Newborn Screening Program, expanding testing from 60 conditions to nearly 900 genetic diseases before symptoms appear.

A few minutes ago

Florida will launch the Sunshine Genetics Newborn Screening Program this summer at Tampa General Hospital, becoming a national example of newborn care through whole genome sequencing. The program was established by House Bill 907, the Sunshine Genetics Act, filed in 2025 by Representative Anderson. Anderson was inspired to pursue the legislation after his son Andrew, known as Drew, was born healthy in 2015 but diagnosed with Tay-Sachs disease following more than a year of misdiagnoses. Drew died at age 4. The voluntary and free program expands Florida newborn screening from 60 conditions to nearly 900, identifying rare genetic diseases before symptoms develop.

Tampa General will serve as the flagship launch site this summer, with expansions planned to Orlando and Miami later this year before a statewide rollout over five years. The program was developed in partnership with genomic medicine company GeneDx. Dr. Patricia Emmanuel, chief of pediatrics at Muma Children's Hospital at Tampa General, said the program will greatly expand the ability to diagnose rare conditions and advance cures. Supporters say early diagnosis could save the state hundreds of millions of dollars by reducing the lengthy and costly diagnostic process many rare disease families face, while opening the door to gene therapies and precision treatments before symptoms appear.

Rapids Water Park in Riviera Beach ends youth supervision policy

Rapids Water Park has ended its interim policy that required guests ages 17 and younger to be accompanied by an adult chaperone.

10:26 AM ET

Rapids Water Park in Riviera Beach has ended its interim youth supervision policy, which had required guests ages 17 and younger to be accompanied by an adult chaperone. The park said the policy is intermittent and pulled in and out based on work with local authorities and social media trends. It is no longer in effect.

While in effect, the policy required minors ages 17 and under to be accompanied by a parent, legal guardian or other responsible adult who was at least 21 years old and had traveled to the park with the minor's group. Adult chaperones were required to present a valid government-issued ID showing both a photograph and date of birth upon entry, remain on-site for the duration of the visit and were limited to supervising no more than five guests ages 17 and under per day. Guests can visit Rapids Water Park's website for additional details before planning their visit.
