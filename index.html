Residents at the Colony Condos in Juno Beach are experiencing increased trespassing problems since the town removed a fence from the JB-7 public beach access point in 2023. The fence was taken down for safety reasons, but its absence has left only bushes to mark the boundary between the public walkway and private property. During peak summer months, Colony residents report that beachgoers regularly park in private parking spots and cut through landscaping to access the beach, carrying coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs across what should be private land.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, said the situation has become a frequent source of conflict. She noted that residents previously tried confronting trespassers directly but found that approach unsuccessful. "We used to confront them and tell them this is private property, and they would get pretty rude. So, now what we've just been doing is just calling the police, and they've been coming up and confronting them," Hill said. The recurring incidents have prompted residents to seek a permanent solution that would restore the clear property boundary a fence once provided.

The issue reached the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting. Town officials stated that the municipality is not obligated to install a fence on the property. However, Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern indicated openness to exploring lower-cost alternatives. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. The council decided to include the fence question in its budget discussions scheduled for July 15.

Not everyone views the situation as problematic. Michael House, a beachgoer who frequents the area, said he has not observed the trespassing issues described by residents and noted that ample free parking is available nearby. However, residents remain hopeful that the town will address their concerns. "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking," Hill said. The council's upcoming budget meeting will determine whether the town moves forward with installing new fencing or pursuing an alternative solution to resolve the ongoing dispute between residents and beach visitors.

When describing the state of the home after the fire, Fenn said it presented a completely devastating scene. She reflected on what made White special, noting that White was a Christian woman who had a passion for gardening and plants. White also played piano and was always ready to help others. Fenn emphasized that White loved children and people in general, and that she was known for being the first to offer assistance when someone needed it.

The loss carries additional weight for Mark, who has already experienced profound grief. According to Fenn, Mark lost his only child, Jacob, in a tragic circumstance. White's death compounds the heartbreak he has already endured. The timing of the tragedy makes the loss even more difficult for those close to them. White's birthday was July 4th, meaning she died just days before what would have been her birthday celebration.

Despite the devastating circumstances, those who knew White want her memory to live on. Fenn said White had always been a good soul and will remain in the hearts of those who loved her. The community's response through the fundraiser and support for Mark demonstrates how White's impact extended beyond her immediate family. As the investigation into the fire's cause continues, the focus for those close to White remains on honoring her memory and supporting Mark through this difficult period of loss and recovery.

Riviera Beach named only Florida city to win 2026 All-America City Award

3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned national recognition as one of only 10 communities across the United States selected to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city stands as the sole Florida recipient of this year's honor. The prestigious award recognizes communities that unite residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and create meaningful change. City leaders presented Riviera Beach's accomplishments at the National Civic League conference in Denver, where judges announced the city as a first-time winner. Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the recognition, stating "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." Lawson emphasized that the city earned the award through sustained efforts to revitalize the community and enhance quality of life for residents, noting that people "are seeing the changes; they're seeing the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do."

The award reflects years of work focused on improving civic health, strengthening trust between residents and government, and enabling the community to actively shape the city's future. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for the honor, saying "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents. Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another." She emphasized that the achievement belongs to the entire community and motivates continued progress. Miller-Anderson noted that winning the award reinforces the city's positive momentum and brings back community pride.

Several civic programs were highlighted in the city's award application, demonstrating the commitment to community engagement. The Riviera Beach Youth Council enables high school students to participate in civic leadership and advocacy. The Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education program, achieved a 98% completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program offers mini-grants to longtime homeowners for critical property repairs. City leaders describe these initiatives as part of a broader commitment to treating civic engagement as an ongoing partnership between government, residents, faith groups, schools and community organizations. Miller-Anderson stated the city is "doing a lot of innovative things to make sure that our community is able to say that they are part of what's happening here in the city."

The All-America City Award has been presented annually since 1949 and ranks among the nation's most respected civic honors. Officials say the designation celebrates Riviera Beach's progress while positioning the community for future opportunities and continued growth. Being named an All-America City communicates to the nation that Riviera Beach is a community where collaboration produces results and residents remain deeply engaged in shaping their city's direction.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football program is working hard this summer with the goal of winning a state championship during the 2026 fall season. After finishing last year with a 10-2 record and claiming the district title, the team fell short in the second round of the playoffs. That early exit has motivated the Scots to push further in postseason play this year.

Head coach Jimmy Robertson highlighted the strength of his senior class, noting that most of them have been with the program since middle school. "Most of these seniors are four-year seniors. I mean, if not the majority of them came from the middle school as well. So they know how things are run. They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had," Robertson said. The experience and continuity within the leadership group is helping set higher expectations for the team moving forward.

To prepare for increased competition, the Scots have intentionally scheduled more challenging opponents for the fall. Senior Jack Williams explained the reasoning behind this approach. "We knew that we wanted to take our program to the next level. So we really have a challenging schedule for this year because we wanna compete. That's what we wanna do and we wanna take that next level, and it's by playing against really good teams that we do that and attract new people," Williams said. The difficult schedule serves both as preparation for postseason play and as a way to draw attention to the program.

For the Scots, success is ultimately measured by playoff performance rather than regular season results. Senior Jack Whelche emphasized that advancing beyond the second round is the team's primary goal. "A better year is just gonna be going farther in the playoffs. Obviously we wanna go undefeated in the season, but that doesn't matter if we keep losing in the second round of the playoffs. We just gotta go farther than that, that's all that matters," Whelche said. Senior Edmyer St. Jean noted that the daily summer training sessions are building momentum and serving as motivation. "It just adds up every day as something like to look forward to. Work harder so we can see the results when we come off the season positive and break our record," St. Jean said. With experienced leadership and an intensive offseason program, the Scots are positioning themselves for a deeper run into the playoffs.

12:34 PM ET

Florida's State Board of Education voted to move forward with rules that would prevent undocumented students from enrolling in the state's 28 community colleges and adult education programs. The restrictions would also affect GED preparation courses, which many students use as a pathway to college or workforce training. This action follows a similar decision by Florida's public university system, which approved a plan to bar undocumented students starting in the 2027-28 academic year. Students already enrolled would not be affected by the university proposal.

Those backing the restrictions argue that taxpayer-funded educational institutions should prioritize students who are legally present in the United States. However, immigrant advocates and students have strongly opposed the changes. Alexander Vallejos, a student with DACA protections, argued that the rules would shut doors for young people raised in Florida who graduated from Florida schools. "We're just asking for an opportunity to earn our future through education," Vallejos said. "Because when Dreamers succeed, Florida succeeds." These new restrictions build on Florida's earlier decision to eliminate in-state tuition rates for many undocumented students, including those protected under DACA.

The education restrictions are part of a broader week of political activity in Florida under Governor Ron DeSantis. The governor moved to use a new state law, effective July 1, that allows the state to designate organizations as terrorist groups. DeSantis announced that Florida will seek to designate the Council on American-Islamic Relations, the Muslim Brotherhood, and Antifa as terrorist organizations, along with more than 90 federally designated foreign terrorist organizations including cartel-connected groups and Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. If approved by the Cabinet, these designations could cut off taxpayer funding, government contracts, and other public support to listed groups and create penalties for providing material support.

CAIR and civil rights organizations immediately filed lawsuits in federal court, arguing that the designations violate constitutional protections. Scott McCoy, deputy legal director for the Southern Poverty Law Center, stated that "Gov. DeSantis does not have the power to unilaterally brand an organization with this vilifying designation and punish those who support it without due process or judicial review." The lawsuit contends the designation process infringes on free speech and due process rights. DeSantis and supporters of the law maintain that the state is taking necessary steps to combat extremism and protect public institutions.

6:43 PM ET

Celebrating Independence Day with a cookout for 10 people will cost significantly more this year. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, the average holiday meal now costs $73.82, representing a 4% increase from last year. The price hike reflects broader inflation pressures affecting most cookout staples, from main courses to side dishes and desserts.

Several key ingredients are driving the cost increases. Ground beef prices have risen 5.5%, while hamburger buns climbed even higher at 7.7%. Strawberries have been hit particularly hard, jumping 12.4% in price due to a Florida frost that damaged crops. Ice cream prices are also up 5.3%, making frozen desserts more expensive for holiday celebrations. These items represent some of the most popular choices for Fourth of July gatherings, putting upward pressure on typical holiday budgets.

Not all cookout items are becoming more expensive, offering some relief for budget-conscious shoppers. Potato prices have declined this year, benefiting those planning to serve potato chips or potato salad. Corn prices have been more volatile, though locally grown corn from the Glades region can provide better value for area residents. Finding these savings requires attention to what is available in local markets.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommended several strategies for controlling cookout costs without sacrificing quality. "It's really important to shop savvier than ever, it is comparing the stores to see who has the best deals for the groceries on your list," Woroch said. She emphasized that families should prioritize reducing food waste, which begins with planning meals in advance. By creating a detailed meal plan before shopping, consumers can avoid purchasing unnecessary items and make more strategic purchasing decisions across different retailers. These simple planning steps can help offset the inflationary pressures affecting cookout expenses this year, allowing families to celebrate the holiday while managing their food budgets more effectively.
