DeSantis allies accuse Lieutenant Governor's wife of running anonymous accounts attacking Governor

Two prominent members of Ron DeSantis' communications team have publicly alleged that Layla Collins, wife of Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins, operated anonymous social media accounts to promote her husband's gubernatorial campaign while attacking the Governor. Christina Pushaw, a Communications Specialist in the Governor's Office, and Jeremy Redfern, Deputy Chief of Staff to At...

11:20 AM ET

Two prominent members of Ron DeSantis' communications team have publicly alleged that Layla Collins, wife of Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins, operated anonymous social media accounts to promote her husband's gubernatorial campaign while attacking the Governor. Christina Pushaw, a Communications Specialist in the Governor's Office, and Jeremy Redfern, Deputy Chief of Staff to Attorney General James Uthmeier, both presented evidence linking the accounts to the Second Lady.

Pushaw shared information from Promota CEO Robert Salvador indicating that the account @FSUdude01670 was connected to a phone number and email address matching ones known to be associated with Layla Collins. Florida Politics obtained screenshots of two-factor authentication messages sent to an email address with letters concealed and a phone number showing only the final digits. Pushaw identified a second account, @bobnamy1, which she also said appeared tied to Layla Collins. According to Pushaw's posts, the account's content focused entirely on promoting Jay Collins and attacking individuals who did not endorse him, including the Governor. Pushaw noted that someone serving on an important state board, as Layla Collins does on the Board of Education, would typically lack time to maintain burner accounts so actively.

Redfern corroborated the findings, stating that the FSUDude account was tied directly to Layla Collins' personal cell phone number. Both accounts were deactivated shortly after Pushaw and Redfern made their accusations public, suggesting the individuals behind them were aware of the exposure. The sudden removal of the accounts came after the criticism appeared online.

The controversy creates a significant challenge for the final six months of DeSantis' gubernatorial term. DeSantis appointed Jay Collins as Lieutenant Governor last year and appointed Layla Collins to the state Board of Education. Jay Collins is competing in the Republican primary for Governor against U.S. Representative Byron Donalds, who has received the endorsement of Attorney General Uthmeier. As of late June, Collins had raised less than 3.5 million dollars, considerably trailing Donalds' fundraising efforts. Both Pushaw and Redfern previously served as Press Secretary for DeSantis before their current positions. Florida Politics has requested comment from both Jay and Layla Collins.

Martin County School Board fights judge's order to reinstate teacher suspended over Charlie Kirk Facebook comments

Jul 3, 3:05 PM ET

The Martin County School Board is challenging a judge's recommendation to reinstate Matt Theobald, a social studies teacher at Spectrum Academy since 2009, who was suspended following Facebook comments about conservative activist Charlie Kirk. In a Thursday court filing, the board argued that Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone made errors in her recommendation, stating that "numerous findings ... are not based upon competent substantial evidence." The board's attorney contends that Theobald's Facebook post about Kirk, in which he labeled Kirk as racist, misogynistic and a neo-Nazi, constituted a "serious offense" that justified his removal.

According to an investigation report, Theobald acknowledged writing the comments but said they were posted on his mother's private Facebook page "within a discussion among personal acquaintances" and were not intended for public view. Judge Livingstone cited a recent Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal ruling that determined disciplining a teacher for personal Facebook posts made off school grounds, on personal equipment, with no student involvement, violated First Amendment rights. She recommended Theobald be reinstated and awarded back pay and benefits. The school board retains final authority over whether Theobald will be rehired. After the judge's recommendation, Theobald told WPTV the case raises broader questions about where the line is drawn for teachers speaking outside of school. No timeline for a final decision has been announced.

A 40-year-old man died in a head-on collision on State Road 710 in Martin County early Sunday morning, according to the Florida Highway Patrol. The crash occurred around 2 a.m. near the intersection of State Road 710, also known as Beeline Highway, and Southwest Silver Fox Lane.

The FHP investigation determined that a 2015 Kia Optima traveling north on SR-710 drifted into the southbound lane and struck a 2021 Toyota Tundra head-on. The left front of the Kia hit the left front of the Tundra. The impact caused the Kia to catch fire. The vehicle came to rest on the grass shoulder, still facing north. The Tundra ended up facing southeast in the southbound lane after the collision.

The Kia driver, a 40-year-old man, was pronounced dead at the scene. The Tundra driver, a 41-year-old man from Indiantown, was airlifted from the scene by Lifestar. The FHP continues to investigate the circumstances of the crash.

The identities of both drivers have not been released. Anyone with information about the crash is encouraged to contact the Florida Highway Patrol.

Jul 3, 10:13 AM ET

Beth Clark, 48, was diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma three months ago and underwent a full double bilateral mastectomy. Less than 24 hours before her reconstruction surgery, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield denied coverage for the procedure. According to her denial letter, the surgery involving a surgical mesh called GalaFlex is considered "investigational" and "there is no or not enough proof it improves health." While GalaFlex is FDA-approved for some uses in the body, it is not approved for breast reconstruction and remains an off-label, investigational, and experimental treatment. In a 2023 letter to health care providers, the FDA stated that the treatment's "efficacy and safety for breast cancer patients have not been determined."

Dr. Alicia Billington, Clark's plastic surgeon, uses GalaFlex mesh on most of her breast reconstruction patients and argues that insurance companies should reconsider their coverage policies. She says the product produces fewer complications than other options with proven success. Despite the denial, Clark received her reconstruction surgery with the GalaFlex mesh a little more than a week ago after Billington found a mechanism to make it happen. Anthem responded by stating it regularly reviews coverage policies to ensure they are based on medical evidence and that without FDA approval, the mesh "does not currently meet the standards required for coverage by our medical policy."

Yesterday, 2:48 PM ET

The Peacock House Art Foundation is establishing Fort Pierce as a vibrant artistic destination while maintaining the historic Crain Estate. The nonprofit preserves the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, who formerly lived on the property. Jaxson Crain worked as a tile worker, and evidence of his skilled craftsmanship appears throughout the facility in the flooring, walls, and ceilings. According to art director Krystal Masters, the estate remains 90% unchanged from the Crains' era. Guided tours of the lush gardens and distinctive architecture are available every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. Visitors can explore the smoking and bourbon room, which features oak paneling, masculine colors, and pre-stained glass that opens to a patio bar. The couple traveled globally nine times, acquiring unique items for their Florida home, including a gazebo shipped from India that features decorative peacocks.

The estate takes its name from the peacocks that have inhabited the grounds for decades. The Crains introduced seven peacocks to Fort Pierce during the 1970s, and the population has since grown to over 100 birds. Bob, believed to be 60 years old and recognizable by his distinctive limp, has become a beloved resident. He lives in a dedicated space, has his own bowls, and receives daily hand-feeding from staff. Tour guides note that visitors, particularly children, treasure the experience of feeding the peacocks directly. The foundation has made deliberate choices to preserve the estate's original character and resist commercialization.

The artist-in-residency program draws creative professionals from around the world, providing simple yet elegant accommodations on the property. The art lab welcomes both children and adults of all experience levels, with Krystal offering one-on-one instruction for complete beginners. Students explore various mediums including sculpting and watercolor painting. Current workshops include "Paint Your Pet in Watercolor and Rainbow." According to Krystal, the majority of her classes are individual sessions where people "come in with zero experience and you walk out with a piece of art."

For Krystal, art serves a therapeutic purpose beyond creative expression. "Art is a form of therapy and it destresses people," she explains. Her goal is helping students leave feeling fulfilled and disconnected from daily distractions. Seeing participants walk away satisfied drives her commitment to the foundation's mission. Through its combination of historical preservation, artist support, and accessible art education, the Peacock House Art Foundation continues transforming Fort Pierce into a meaningful cultural hub.
