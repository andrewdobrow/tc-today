St. Lucie County residents and visitors can learn life-saving skills through free rip current safety demonstrations at local public pools this summer. The St. Lucie County Parks & Recreation Department is hosting three sessions using a new rip current simulation machine, which creates controlled currents in swimming pools to teach proper escape techniques. Jennifer Gill, St. Lucie County Parks & Recreation manager, said education is one of the most effective tools to prevent water-related emergencies. She explained that the demonstrations allow participants to experience how a rip current works in a safe, controlled environment while learning skills that could save their life or the life of someone they love.

Rip currents are powerful channels of water that flow away from shore and are capable of quickly pulling swimmers away from the beach. According to the National Weather Service, there have been 23 rip current fatalities this year so far in the United States. Many swimmers make a critical mistake when caught in a rip current by trying to swim directly back to shore, which often leads to dangerous exhaustion. The proper technique is to remain calm, swim parallel to the shoreline until escaping the current, then swim back to shore at an angle.

The county has scheduled three free demonstrations this summer. The first will take place on July 19 from 1 to 4 p.m. at Lakewood Park Pool with registration required and attendance limited to 50 participants. The second demonstration will be held on July 28 from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. at Boatwright Pool as part of the Back-to-School Splash Jam event. This session requires no registration and operates on a first-come, first-served basis. The final demonstration will occur on August 16 from 1 to 4 p.m. at Ravenswood Pool with registration required and limited to 50 participants.

The hands-on training aims to build swimmer confidence and prepare participants for real ocean conditions before they head to area beaches. The rip current simulation machine provides a controlled setting where people can practice the techniques needed to escape dangerous currents without the risks associated with actual ocean conditions. For more information about the rip current safety demonstrations, residents can contact St. Lucie County Parks & Recreation.

The site where Jensen Beach Park's concession building and restrooms once stood is now an empty plot of land following a fire that destroyed the facility on New Year's Eve in December 2024. Beachgoer Gonda Ghebert reached out to WPTV's Let's Hear It inbox, writing that beachgoers are left with only one very small restroom facility to serve the entire beach. The situation has become increasingly unreasonable during weekends, holidays, and peak tourist season. Regular visitors say they miss both the restrooms and the cafe, with one beachgoer noting the location previously offered nice, clean facilities, showers, and the convenience of getting snacks and drinks right off the deck.

Kevin Abbate, Parks and Recreation director, said the permitting process takes time because the site sits on a protected dune and requires approval from the Department of Environmental Protection. That permit is currently in process. The design for the new 9,000 square foot Sea Turtle Beach Cafe is 80 percent complete. "It's about 15 feet in elevation, so there's a good seat for everyone, 100 seats and 33 at the bar," Abbate said.

The county says final funding is expected to be approved in September. They hope to start construction in early 2027 with a completion goal of late 2027, just in time for the winter season. The county told WPTV they do not have any plans for additional temporary restrooms or facilities in the meantime. Abbate said the wait will be worthwhile because doing the project correctly from the start will prevent problems later.

"We want to do it right. If we design it properly, when we get through construction, we'll have fewer issues, it'll be a much smoother process," Abbate said. Beachgoers say they are anxiously awaiting the construction. One visitor hopes the project moves very fast, while another expressed eagerness to see the bathrooms up and running again and called it nice to have another convenient spot at the beach.

Andre Leonard Barnes, an art teacher for St. Lucie County, died at a local hospital after being shot with a rifle at a residence in the 2700 block of Essex Drive. The suspect has been identified as Dale A. Mincey, who remains in custody on unrelated felony warrants from Broward County.

Deputies responded to the home around 3:30 p.m. Tuesday after receiving reports of a person with a gunshot wound. When they arrived, law enforcement learned that one man had shot another man with a rifle. Barnes was transported to a local hospital, where he died from his injuries. Authorities have not released details about what led to the shooting or the relationship between Barnes and the suspect.

Mincey is currently being held on active felony warrants out of Broward County for dealing in stolen property and false verification to a pawn broker, according to the sheriff's office. The homicide investigation remains active, and detectives continue to work with the State Attorney's Office as evidence is reviewed. Additional charges related to the investigation will be determined as the case progresses. Deputies said that based on information developed during the investigation, this remains an isolated incident with no threat to the public.

The school district released a statement following Barnes's death, acknowledging his service as an art teacher in St. Lucie County. The sheriff's office said additional information will be released as it becomes available. The investigation continues as authorities work to determine what charges will be filed in connection with Barnes's death.

The St. Lucie County Clerk and Comptroller's website was down for hours Tuesday, redirecting visitors to another site before coming back online by about 1:30 p.m. Clerk and Comptroller Michelle Miller posted a video on social media Tuesday afternoon addressing the outage, assuring the public that no personal or confidential information was compromised or at risk. The clerk's office does not store sensitive personal information on the public-facing website, Miller said in the social media post. Miller said the website faced a temporary redirection issue caused by a third-party service provider.

WPTV reporter Tyler Hatfield went to the County Clerk's office in search of answers and spoke to Jose Clavijo, the agency's IT director. Clavijo said they received a report just before 8 a.m. that something was wrong with the county clerk's homepage. He said as soon as he identified the problem, he turned off the homepage immediately and started an investigation. Clavijo said getting the site fixed was the absolute highest priority.

WPTV asked the St. Lucie County Clerk's Office if it believed there was any suspicious activity about the incident, such as a hack. The clerk's office communications team said this was not a hack because there was not any personal information compromised. The office said residents could still access information and services on the website through direct links instead of using the homepage. Clavijo said payments, property fraud services, and all other services remained available. He said the main page was the only issue.

Clavijo said the office is working to prevent similar issues from happening again. He said as soon as they identify the exact issue, there will be a tremendous remediation. The website outage lasted several hours Tuesday morning before service was restored in the early afternoon. The clerk's office emphasized that despite the redirection problem, no sensitive data was at risk and most services remained accessible throughout the incident.

The Women's Professional Baseball League on Wednesday unveiled the nicknames and logos of its four teams ahead of its inaugural season, with each team name inspired by pioneering women. Kelsie Whitmore, the star pitcher who was the first overall pick in the WPBL's inaugural draft, will play for the San Francisco Firebells. Mo'ne Davis, the history-making former Little League sensation, will play center field for the Los Angeles Queens, along with veteran Japanese pitcher Ayami Sato. The league also includes the New York Heights and the Boston Hunters.

The WPBL's debut season begins on Aug. 1 in Springfield, Illinois. Games will be played at Robin Roberts Stadium and will run through the second half of September, including a regular season and playoffs. The league hopes to create a space for women's baseball players similar to the one the All-American Girls Professional Baseball League did 70 years ago. "This is just every little girl's dream to play professional baseball," Whitmore told The Associated Press at a scrimmage in March, "and now for us to actually be with each other, there's so much freedom and peace within it."

Each team identity holds a unique meaning. The New York Heights were inspired by Dorothy Irene Height, a pioneering civil rights and women's rights leader from New York. Their logo includes graphic lines surrounding a baseball, mimicking the speed of a fastball and resembling the New York skyline. The San Francisco Firebells were inspired by Lillie Hitchcock Coit, known as "Firebelle," who was a champion of San Francisco's volunteer firefighters. Their logo fuses a bell with a phoenix. The Los Angeles Queens were created after Lizzie Murphy, a baseball pioneer known as the "Queen of the Diamond." The logo is a capital Q topped with four baseball diamonds.

The Boston Hunters are inspired by Harriot K. Hunt, a pioneering Boston physician and women's rights activist. Their logo is an osprey clasping a baseball bat in its talons. The league represents years of planning to establish professional opportunities for women in baseball, giving players a chance to compete at the highest level. With rosters finalized and team identities now revealed, the WPBL is set to make its debut next month in Illinois, marking a new chapter in women's professional sports.

Indian River County condo owners are seeing waste management fees nearly double after a new contract with Waste Management began in October 2025. Thomas Clark, a Vero Beach condo owner, brought his concerns to WPTV during a Let's Hear It community meetup, reporting his monthly waste management fees had jumped from $11 to $21.

WPTV Anchor Mike Trim brought these concerns directly to the Indian River County Department of Utility Services during a video conference meeting. The county explained the new contract raised average condo association waste management fees by roughly 93 percent. Clark told Trim he was not happy with the increase and suggested the county should help residents if the county made a mistake in the contract.

County officials said condo owners are receiving additional benefits under the new contract. Himanshu Mehta of the Indian River County Solid Waste Disposal District explained that residents can now place bulk items near condo dumpsters, a service that was not previously available. Mehta and other county officials emphasized the importance of community involvement in public hearings, noting that this contract was approved during 2024 hearings.

Mehta said his team is available to meet with homeowners associations to explain the process in more detail. He stated that if Clark or his HOA wanted to invite county officials to a meeting, they would be happy to send someone to go over the process. Residents can stay updated on Indian River County public hearings and find current recycling and garbage schedules on the county website.
