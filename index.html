Port St. Lucie ranks as the 67th most stressed city in the United States according to a WalletHub study released this month. The study compared 182 cities across 39 metrics including average weekly work hours, unemployment rate, divorce and suicide rates.

The report cited Port St. Lucie as the 42nd most financially stressed city in America. Jacksonville ranked as the most stressed city in Florida at number 37 nationally. In South Florida, Miami placed at number 50, with affordable housing ranking fourth worst in the country, while Fort Lauderdale came in at number 83.

WalletHub analyst Chip Lupo said cities with high crime rates, weak economies, less effective public health and congested transportation systems naturally lead to elevated stress levels for residents. He noted that some stress is out of people's control due to issues with family, friends or employers, but where you live can play a big role in how stressed you are. Lupo emphasized the importance of considering how a certain city may impact your mental health when moving, not just your financial opportunities. The report found that money, health and the economy all remain top stressors.

Detroit, Baltimore, Cleveland, Memphis and Shreveport, Louisiana topped the list of most stressed cities in the United States. Fremont, California, in the Bay Area ranked as the least stressed city. The notably affluent area has average home prices of about $1.5 million. South Burlington, Vermont, and Bismarck and Fargo, North Dakota followed as the next least stressed cities. Florida performed fairly well overall, with no cities in the top 35 most stressed.

The Indian River County Sheriff's Office said two bodies found near U.S. Highway 1 north of Sebastian this week appear unrelated but both deaths remain under investigation as suspicious. Sheriff Eric Flowers held a news conference Thursday morning to address the discoveries.

On Monday, a person fishing found the body of James Thompson, 44, a Brevard County resident, on the west shoreline near U.S. Highway 1 and the north county line. Thompson had been staying with his girlfriend at a hotel in Sebastian and was last seen Sunday around 1:30 p.m. walking northbound on U.S. Highway 1. Flowers said Thompson had a history of fentanyl use and mental health issues and had visited mental health facilities in Brevard County many times. The sheriff said Thompson purchased methamphetamine on July 3 and had gone into a manic state. According to the sheriff, there was only a minor contusion on his eyebrow with no signs of violence. An autopsy is pending.

On Wednesday shortly after 4 p.m., a heavily decomposed body of a 65-year-old man was found in shrubbery along the east shoreline near 14510 U.S. Highway 1, just north of Sebastian and across the street from Monday's location. The man had a temporary address with his brother in Sebastian and was out of jail on pretrial release after Plantation police arrested him on an aggravated battery with a deadly weapon charge in Broward County. Plantation police had just obtained a warrant for him for absconding on his pretrial release. Flowers said the man had a serious history of seizures and his brother took him to a hospital on Sunday. The man walked away from the hospital against medical advice at 7:30 a.m. Sunday while still wearing hospital socks, telling his brother he would not return. The sheriff's office has not released his name.

Flowers said autopsies on both bodies showed no stab wounds or evidence of violence and that nothing connects the two cases at this point. The sheriff said the deaths could be related to bad fentanyl or methamphetamine on the streets and urged drug users to seek treatment. Both cases remain suspicious until toxicology results determine the cause of death. Flowers said investigators will work to find out who sold drugs to the men if drugs are involved. Anyone with information is asked to contact the Indian River County Sheriff's Office or Treasure Coast Crime Stoppers.

Park Vista Community High School's football team is building momentum heading into the 2026 season after a strong finish to last year's campaign. The Cobras struggled to find their footing early in 2025 but turned things around in the second half of the season, winning four of their last five games. Now the team is looking to carry that success through an entire season as they set their sights on reaching the state playoffs.

Senior Harris Bender reflected on how the team felt during their late-season surge and what it means for the upcoming year. "Felt good throughout the second half of that season," Bender said. "We're just working towards being able to carry that through the whole season, like you said, and we have all the pieces in place now, it's just about executing." The Cobras' ultimate goal extends beyond just making the playoffs. The program is aiming for a state title, something Park Vista has not accomplished since 2017.

Head coach Donald Hanna emphasized the importance of staying focused on the immediate challenges ahead rather than looking too far down the road. "We got to take it one game at a time," Hanna said. "We can't look past anyone. We got to handle business weekly and if we do that, do our job, that'll get us in the playoffs." His approach reflects a measured mindset designed to keep the team grounded as they pursue their championship aspirations. The coach's philosophy centers on consistent weekly execution as the path to postseason success.

Senior Erik Lee acknowledged that while the roster has the talent necessary to achieve their goals, success will require dedication and effort from everyone involved. "Coming in, I feel like we got the guys that we need to get it done," Lee said. "But also, we got to put the work in to get there as well." Senior Justice Jones expressed even stronger confidence in the team's ability to reach its objectives. "It's going to feel amazing, because I already know it's gonna happen, definitely," Jones said. Park Vista will open its 2026 season with a road game against Atlantic in August, providing an early test for a program eager to prove its late 2025 resurgence was the beginning of something bigger.

Yesterday, 5:27 PM ET

A proposed 85-acre development off Indrio Road and Russakis Road in St. Lucie County is moving closer to reality, but not without pushback from neighbors who say it will transform the area's rural character. The project, called Indrio Town Center, would bring a mix of commercial and retail space and possibly residential units to land near Interstate 95. On June 18, county planning and zoning unanimously recommended rezoning the land from agricultural to planned retail workplace. County commissioners are expected to vote on the rezoning in August. The proposed development is 447,250 square feet of commercial, retail and possible residential space across more than 85 acres.

Susan Lewis has lived near the proposed site for 15 years, running a farm rescue on a property that has been in her family for decades. She said the development would surround her property and worsen existing conditions. "I have dogs that have 10 acres to run around. I have horses that have 10 acres to run around. It's gone," Lewis said. "We will be surrounded. I think that there's plenty of other places for them to build."

County Planning and Zoning Manager Kori Benton said the land is part of a planned design area called Town, Villages and Countryside, along with 14,000 other acres of land in St. Lucie County. Benton said this land was anticipated to transition from traditional legacy agricultural or citrus uses to development and will help avoid urban sprawl. She said the overlay district is trying to allow for tactful development and new communities that embrace the county's rural character, maintain lands for open space and agricultural activities, and considers compatibility with the county's need for infrastructure, quality of life, jobs and connectivity to the interstate and roadway system. Leah Heinzelmann from Cotleur and Hearing, who is representing the property owners and developer Glassman Holdings LLC, said the development will also include open green space and an over 26-acre park. She said the design incorporates a lot of open space while keeping a native buffer along Indrio Road.

Lewis said she's worried this development will change the rural landscape in St. Lucie County. "We moved here for a reason; this is why we do what we do. We like to be in the wilderness. This is what Florida used to look like," Lewis said. The rezoning still needs approval from county commissioners in August.

The first body was found Monday on the west shoreline near U.S. Highway 1 and the north county line by a person fishing in the area. The deceased was identified as James Thompson, 44, a Brevard County resident who had been staying with his girlfriend at a hotel in Sebastian. Thompson was last seen Sunday walking northbound on U.S. Highway 1 around 1:30 p.m. Flowers said Thompson had a history of fentanyl use and mental health issues and had made a purchase of methamphetamine on July 3 before going into a manic state. The sheriff said Thompson had been to mental health facilities in Brevard County many times. According to Flowers, there was only a minor contusion on Thompson's eyebrow with no signs of violence.

The second body was found Wednesday shortly after 4 p.m. in shrubbery along the east shoreline near 14510 US Highway 1, just north of Sebastian and across the street from Monday's location. The heavily decomposed body was that of a 65-year-old man with a temporary address at his brother's home in Sebastian. The man had been out of jail on pretrial release after being arrested by Plantation police on a charge of aggravated battery with a deadly weapon in Broward County. Flowers said Plantation police had just obtained or was in the process of obtaining a warrant for him for absconding on his pretrial release. The man had a serious history of seizures and his brother had taken him to a hospital on Sunday. The man walked away from the hospital against medical advice around 7:30 a.m. Sunday while still wearing hospital socks, with his brother last seeing him walking north. Flowers quoted the man as saying he was not going back to the hospital.

Flowers said autopsies on both bodies showed no stab wounds or signs of violence, and nothing at this point connects the two cases.
