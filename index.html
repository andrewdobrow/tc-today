Yesterday, 5:36 PM ET

Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties have a ban on any fertilizer containing phosphorus or nitrogen that takes effect June 30. Jordan Skaggs with Environmental Resources in St. Lucie County said the ban is to help reduce nutrient rich runoff during the rainy season, which can cause algae blooms. Those blooms could disrupt waterways like the Indian River lagoon and St. Lucie River.

Skaggs said harmful algae blooms can shade out beneficial plants to fish and wildlife. At certain densities, harmful algae blooms can release toxins that can affect human health, wildlife health and overall ecosystem health.

Grantly Ricketts, director of University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences St. Lucie County Extension, said a good alternative during the ban is using composting for your garden and landscaping. Ricketts said a small trash can is the easiest and most practical way to add nutrients to your landscape.

A 9-year-old Port St. Lucie resident has started a new chess club for young players in the community. Orion Jean Baptiste created the club to share his passion for the game with others in the area. The initiative has already attracted more than 36 junior members who gather every Friday at the Port St. Lucie library. The organization, called the Atlantis Chess Club, plans to expand with two additional programs launching at the Fenn Center in Fort Pierce.

Baptiste developed his interest in chess at age 5 while playing with family members. His goal now is to introduce more young people to the game and help them appreciate board games. "I just wanted kids to learn about chess," Baptiste said. He believes there is too much focus on technology among young people. "Everybody's more focused on like tech stuff," Baptiste said. "I'm hoping that more people have respect for board games."

Diana Cowan Kempton, the club's program director, sees chess as a valuable tool for developing young minds. She believes the game teaches important life skills that will benefit the next generation. "It will help to build leaders or members that are more focused, more patient, more strategic and thoughtful in their decision making," Kempton said. The club welcomes players with varying levels of experience, from beginners to more advanced players.

Parents are responding positively to the opportunity. Thomas Furlong brought his children to join the club and praised the environment it provides. "This is like the perfect chance for her to learn chess, my son to get better at chess and then them to have a little sibling rivalry competition," Furlong said. His daughter Francesca, a new member of the club, finds the social aspect most appealing. "I like playing with other kids," Francesca said. The club continues to demonstrate that traditional games like chess remain engaging and valuable for young people seeking an alternative to digital entertainment.

Jun 3, 1:03 PM ET

More than 100 paddlers will cross 80 miles of the open Atlantic Ocean this June to raise awareness and funds for families living with cystic fibrosis in the annual Crossing for Cystic Fibrosis. Participants will launch from Bimini, Bahamas, at midnight on June 13, arriving at Lake Worth Beach after 12 to 16 hours of paddling. The endurance trek benefits the Pipers Angels Foundation, which event founder Travis Suit established in honor of his daughter, Piper, diagnosed with cystic fibrosis at age 4.

The crossing is part of a four-day schedule, beginning June 10, with arrivals and a poolside social at Bimini Cove. On Thursday, participants take part in a 4Ocean beach cleanup and a community buffet dinner. Friday brings a Paddle Out Rose Ceremony to honor cystic fibrosis warriors, followed by rest and safety checks before launch night.

All paddlers are required to wear a U.S. Coast Guard-approved personal flotation device, waterproof whistle, and reflective gear during dark hours. GPS trackers provided by the event must be worn and active at all times, and mandatory safety meetings will be held. Paddlers must raise at least $2,000 for the cause, and crew or virtual participants have a $500 minimum. Final contributions are due July 31.

Spectators are invited to Lake Worth Beach on June 13 to cheer paddlers during a daylong beach celebration marking their arrival. Suit, an avid paddler, was inspired to create the event after learning about the natural respiratory benefits of ocean salt air. The foundation has since grown to provide direct support for cystic fibrosis patients and families, connecting them with resources and community. More information, registration, and donation details are available at crossingforcysticfibrosis.com and pipersangels.org.

Jupiter Island faces erosion so severe ocean could breach Peck Lake and split island in half

A state report warns that Jupiter Island's entire 11.5-mile shoreline is critically eroded, threatening a breach at Peck Lake that could split the barrier island in two.

Yesterday, 4:42 PM ET

A 2025 Florida Department of Environmental Protection report warns that Jupiter Island's 11.5-mile shoreline has eroded so severely the ocean could break through at Peck Lake, potentially splitting the island in half. The threat stretches from Blowing Rocks Preserve to Peck Lake. A storm breached Peck Lake in the early 1960s before the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers filled it back in with sand. Mark Perry with the Florida Oceanographic Society said a future breach could upend ecosystems that depend on a delicate balance of fresh and saltwater to survive and could create more vulnerable coastal marshes.

According to the report, the Joseph Reed Mound, a 3,000 to 4,000-year-old archaeological site, is already half lost to erosion, along with wildlife refuges like Blowing Rocks Preserve, Hobe Sound Wildlife Refuge, and St Lucie Inlet Preserve State Park. There are over 33 threatened and endangered species in those areas. Jupiter Island Commissioner Marshall Field said the town continues to fight erosion through constant maintenance. In April 2025, the town completed a major beach renourishment project. About $3 million in town taxpayer dollars goes toward beach renourishment every year. Field said if the island were to breach, repairs could require massive state and federal investment.

For those who cannot get into the county's program, 211 is another important option available 24/7.
