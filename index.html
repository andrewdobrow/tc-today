The Peacock House Art Foundation is reshaping Fort Pierce's cultural landscape by establishing the Peacock Arts District as a hub for artistic expression. Located within the historic Crain Estate, this nonprofit organization honors the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, the property's original owners. The estate has been preserved to maintain its historical integrity, with about 90 percent of the space remaining exactly as it was during the Crains' residence, according to art director Krystal Masters.

Jaxson Crain's profession as a tile worker left an indelible mark on the property. His craftsmanship is evident throughout the facility, visible in the flooring, walls, and ceilings. The Crains' extensive world travels, documented in nine separate journeys, resulted in a remarkable collection of global artifacts now displayed at the estate. Among their acquisitions is a gazebo imported from India, which features decorative peacock motifs. The house retains distinctive spaces from the Crains' era, including a smoking and bourbon room with wood paneling, a bathroom decorated in masculine tones, and pre-stained glass leading to a patio bar. Guided tours are offered every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, allowing visitors to explore the manicured gardens and distinctive architecture.

The foundation takes its name from the peacocks that have inhabited the property for decades. The Crains are credited with introducing peacocks to Fort Pierce during the 1970s, beginning with seven birds that have since multiplied to over one hundred. A standout resident is Bob, a peacock believed to be sixty years old and recognizable by his distinctive limp. Bob lives in the house with his own food bowls and receives hand-feeding from tour guides daily. His presence delights young visitors, who often have the opportunity to feed him directly. The Crains' collection extended beyond peacocks; Jackson reportedly traveled to South America, obtained a baby monkey, and transported it home in his pocket.

The foundation operates an artist-in-residency program that welcomes creative professionals from around the world, providing simple yet elegant housing on the grounds. The on-site art lab serves both children and adults through accessible classes and workshops. Krystal leads one-on-one instruction designed for complete beginners, enabling participants to arrive with no experience and leave with finished artwork. The lab offers diverse mediums and projects, including sculpting and watercolor painting. Krystal conducts themed workshops such as "Paint Your Pet in Watercolor and Rainbow." She views art as a therapeutic practice that helps people decompress and disconnect from digital distractions. For Krystal, the most rewarding aspect of her work is watching students leave feeling fulfilled and happy.

This Fourth of July weekend is shaping up to be the busiest travel period on record, and South Florida drivers are navigating the challenge amid wet weather conditions. According to AAA, 72.2 million Americans are expected to travel at least 50 miles from home between June 27 and July 5, with 61.4 million of those trips being made by car. The combination of record travel numbers and rainy conditions has created difficult driving situations, particularly on the Florida Turnpike south of Orlando on Friday. Driver Ed Budd described how quickly conditions deteriorated when the rain arrived, noting that poor weather seems to affect driver behavior on the roads. Despite the challenging circumstances, some motorists like Jim Macfawn chose to continue their journeys, relying on their experience to handle the unpredictable conditions.

Orlando and Miami have emerged as top five travel destinations for the holiday weekend, making South Florida a particularly congested area. The experiences of travelers have varied significantly depending on timing and route. Carlos Borras and his wife completed a round trip between Orlando and Miami on Friday without encountering major obstacles, reporting no traffic, accidents, or significant delays. However, other drivers have had markedly different experiences. Fernando Amado found the roads considerably busier, attributing the congestion partly to weather conditions and noting that many people appear to be traveling northward on this particular day.

AAA has provided specific recommendations for travelers hoping to avoid the worst congestion. Those still planning to hit the road should depart after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to minimize delays. The busiest periods are expected to occur between 10 a.m. and noon on Saturday, and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday. AAA emphasizes that departure time, destination, and origin point all significantly influence the travel experience during this busy weekend.

The scale of this year's travel numbers has not gone unnoticed by drivers. Macfawn expressed surprise at the volume of people traveling, particularly given current fuel prices. The record-breaking number of Americans on the roads this weekend reflects strong consumer demand for holiday travel, though it also means increased competition for road space and heightened risks during adverse weather. Drivers planning to travel should remain cautious, monitor weather updates, and allow extra time for their journeys given the unprecedented traffic levels expected throughout the weekend.

The Martin County School Board is challenging a judge's recommendation to reinstate Matt Theobald, a social studies teacher at Spectrum Academy since 2009, who was suspended following controversial Facebook comments about Charlie Kirk. In a Thursday court filing, the school board argued that Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone erred in her recommendation, claiming "numerous findings ... are not based upon competent substantial evidence." The board's attorney contends that Theobald's Facebook post about Kirk, in which he labeled the activist as racist, misogynistic and a neo-Nazi, constituted a "serious offense" warranting his removal.

In her order issued last month, Livingstone cited a recent Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal ruling that determined disciplining a teacher for personal Facebook posts made off school grounds on personal equipment with no student involvement violated First Amendment rights. The judge recommended Theobald be reinstated and awarded back pay and benefits. According to an investigation report obtained by WPTV, Theobald admitted writing the comments but said they appeared on his mother's private Facebook page "within a discussion among personal acquaintances" and were not intended for public view. The school board retains final authority over Theobald's employment status, and no timeline has been announced for a final decision.

The cost of celebrating Independence Day with a cookout has increased significantly this year. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, hosting a holiday meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, representing a 4% increase from the previous year. The rise reflects broader inflationary pressures affecting grocery prices across multiple food categories that are staples of Fourth of July celebrations.

Inflation is the primary driver behind the higher cookout costs. Ground beef, a centerpiece of many holiday grills, has increased by 5.5%. Hot dog and hamburger buns have risen even more steeply at 7.7%. Strawberries have experienced the sharpest price jump at 12.4%, largely due to damage from a Florida frost that impacted the crop. Ice cream, another popular dessert choice for summer cookouts, has gone up 5.3%. These increases across multiple items have combined to push overall holiday meal expenses higher than they were a year ago.

However, not all food prices have climbed. Shoppers looking to keep their budgets in check will find some relief in the produce aisle. Potato prices have actually decreased this year, offering savings for those planning to serve potato chips or potato salad at their gatherings. Corn prices have been more volatile, though locally grown corn from the Glades region can provide better pricing options for budget-conscious consumers. These lower-priced alternatives can help offset some of the increases seen in other categories.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends several strategies for managing cookout expenses during inflationary times. She emphasizes the importance of shopping strategically by comparing prices across different stores to identify the best deals for items on your shopping list. Beyond comparing stores, Woroch highlights that reducing food waste is crucial for families trying to control costs, and this process should begin with creating a detailed meal plan before shopping. By planning meals in advance and being intentional about purchases, families can celebrate the Fourth of July without breaking the bank despite the year-over-year price increases.

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. According to the center, these programs are designed to help visitors learn about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection announced it is waiving admission fees at Florida State Parks from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. All residents and visitors can explore the state's award-winning parks at no charge for day use. Florida's state park system offers hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling and horseback riding, as well as historic sites including forts, battlefields, archaeological sites and other landmarks. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends and their family to the state parks to enjoy the outdoors, make new memories and support treasured public lands. The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award.

Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks saw more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated $3.6 billion in statewide economic impact. Three parks are excluded from the free admission: Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. Free admission applies to day use only. All other fees, including overnight accommodations, special events, concessions and rentals, remain in effect.
