Activists Rally to Save Rare Deep-Sea Coral Reef Off Fort Pierce as Federal Decision Looms

Dozens of community members gathered at the Blake Library in Martin County to rally for the protection of the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef located just off the coast of Fort Pierce. Yesterday, 1:28 AM ET

Dozens of community members gathered at the Blake Library in Martin County to rally for the protection of the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef located just off the coast of Fort Pierce. The reef is home to more than 2,000 marine species and is found nowhere else on Earth. Unlike most coral that needs shallow sunlight to survive, the Oculina Bank thrives in deep, dark waters hundreds of feet below the surface.

The gathering followed a vote by a federally appointed council to roll back longstanding protections for the reef. Two proposals would reopen parts of the reef to shrimp trawling, even though scientists say trawling destroyed as much as 90 percent of the reef before federal protections were put in place decades ago. Dr. Grant Gilmore, a scientist who previously dived the rare reef, told the crowd he is very concerned from an ecosystem point of view. He explained that fish need habitat and food, and the coral reef provides both. Removing that habitat means losing the fish.

Bob Zales, executive director of the Southeastern Fishing Association and a generational shrimper, believes reopening shrimping access to the Oculina Bank is essential for the struggling rock shrimp industry. Council data shows the rock shrimp industry has plummeted from over 700 trips per year in the mid-1990s to under 100 since 2010. The number of active rock shrimpers dropped from 144 in 1996 to less than 30, with just 12 active boats in 2018. Zales attributes the decline to high costs, competition from imported shrimp, and closures of previously open fishing areas.

The proposals followed a Trump executive order issued in April of 2025 that tasked eight regional councils nationwide to find ways to reinvigorate the fishing industries. Mark Perry, executive director of the Florida Oceanographic Society, joined Gilmore at the gathering to urge the public to help save the reef. Scientists warned reopening the reef could irreparably damage what little remains of a coral system that grows very slowly and has been there for a long time. Corals are in trouble worldwide, and Gilmore said we should not be destroying any more of them.

More than 100 paddlers will cross 80 miles of open Atlantic Ocean this June in the annual Crossing for Cystic Fibrosis, launching from Bimini, Bahamas, at midnight on June 13 and arriving at Lake Worth Beach after 12 to 16 hours of paddling. The endurance event benefits the Pipers Angels Foundation, established by event founder Travis Suit in honor of his daughter Piper, who was diagnosed with cystic fibrosis at age 4. Spectators are invited to Lake Worth Beach on June 13 to cheer paddlers during a daylong beach celebration marking their arrival.

The crossing is part of a four-day schedule beginning June 10 with arrivals and a poolside social at Bimini Cove. Thursday brings a 4Ocean beach cleanup and community buffet dinner, followed Friday by a Paddle Out Rose Ceremony to honor cystic fibrosis warriors. Safety requirements are strict. All paddlers must wear U.S. Coast Guard-approved personal flotation devices, waterproof whistles, and reflective gear during dark hours. GPS trackers provided by the event must be worn and active at all times, and mandatory safety meetings will be held.

Participants from around the world are expected to join the fundraiser. Paddlers must raise at least $2,000 for the cause, while crew or virtual participants have a $500 minimum. Final contributions are due July 31. Suit, an avid paddler, created the event after learning about the natural respiratory benefits of ocean salt air for cystic fibrosis patients. The foundation provides direct support for patients and families, connecting them with resources and community.

Cystic fibrosis is a life-shortening genetic condition that affects the lungs and digestive system. High-profile community events like this one raise funds and spotlight ongoing needs for research, treatment, and family support. More information, registration, and donation details are available at crossingforcysticfibrosis.com and pipersangels.org.

Port St. Lucie woman sentenced to 3 years in prison for running illegal med spa out of backyard shed

Rosa Mena, 50, of Port St.

22 hours ago

Rosa Mena, 50, of Port St. Lucie has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation after operating an unlicensed medical spa out of a shed in her backyard. The sentencing follows a multi-agency investigation that began in May 2025 when a victim reported partial facial paralysis after receiving Botox injections at the makeshift facility called Miracle Hand and Spa.

The victim, who had been referred to Mena by coworkers, received 62 units of Botox injections during a session at the shed located at a residence in the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive. When the victim reported the paralysis, Mena refunded the $325 fee and asked her to return for another injection of vitamins to reverse the issues. After the paralysis continued, the victim asked to see Mena's medical license. Mena provided an altered certificate of phlebotomy that had expired in February 2024 and claimed to be a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

A search warrant executed by Port St. Lucie Police, PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax, and the Florida Department of Health uncovered extensive evidence of illegal medical operations. Detectives found medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines, and injection devices for fillers and Botox inside the shed.

Mena was charged with three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, one count of fraud to obtain property valued at $300 or more, one count of fraud to obtain property under $20,000, and one count of using a two-way communication device to facilitate a felony. The case involved multiple victims who had received services at the illegal operation.

Jupiter Island leaders say town is prepared despite report warning of 9 feet of flooding at emergency operations center

Jupiter Island officials say they are prepared for future storms following a new vulnerability assessment that identified 103 critical town assets at risk of flooding, including the town's Emergency Operations Center which could see up to 9 feet of floodwater.

29 minutes ago

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection report identified 68 stormwater facilities, 17 historic sites, conservation lands, and key evacuation routes at risk. Jupiter Island Commissioner Marshall Field told Investigative Reporter Kate Hussey that the findings confirm what town leaders already knew, which is why they requested the assessment in the first place. The town's Beach District Director John Duchok said the vulnerability assessment will set up a roadmap for how to adapt and prioritize improvements.

Field said the goal is to document the risks, build a roadmap for improvements, and compete for state and federal funding to fix vulnerabilities. A window for funding applications opened June 1, and Field said the town will take advantage of that opportunity. The findings come after a separate state study warned that severe erosion could one day break through part of the island.

Field addressed immediate concerns about the island's vulnerability, saying evacuation orders would be issued well before floodwaters threaten roadways and that the town has a backup facility on the mainland to maintain operations during emergencies. The 126-page report also warns that stormwater systems face some of the greatest risks, noting they can fail during the very flooding events they are designed to manage. Field noted that there is no conventional way to deal with stormwater runoff on a skinny island as you would on the mainland.
