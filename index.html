Indian River Sheriff says two deaths near Sebastian River Bridge not connected

The Indian River County Sheriff's Office says there is no connection between two deaths that occurred near the Sebastian River bridge within a two-day period, though both remain under investigation as suspicious. Yesterday, 2:00 PM ET

The Indian River County Sheriff's Office says there is no connection between two deaths that occurred near the Sebastian River bridge within a two-day period, though both remain under investigation as suspicious.

Authorities discovered two bodies in the area near the Sebastian River bridge over the course of two days. The sheriff's office examined both incidents to determine whether they might be related but found no evidence linking the deaths. Despite ruling out a connection between the cases, investigators continue to treat both deaths as suspicious while they work to determine what happened in each situation.

The bodies were found in close proximity to each other near the Sebastian River bridge, raising initial questions about whether the deaths might be connected. The brief time frame between the discoveries prompted law enforcement to carefully review both cases for any potential links. After conducting their investigation, sheriff's officials stated they have not found evidence suggesting the two deaths are related to one another.

Both investigations remain active as authorities work to gather more information about each case. The sheriff's office has not released additional details about the circumstances surrounding either death or the identities of the individuals found near the bridge. Investigators continue working to piece together what led to each death in the Sebastian area.

New Aldi opens in Hobe Sound as Martin County shoppers seek relief from rising grocery costs

Yesterday, 5:23 PM ET

A new Aldi grocery store opened Thursday at 8867 Southeast Bridge Road in Hobe Sound, offering Martin County shoppers an affordable option as food prices continue to strain family budgets. Customers Cheryl and Tony Cooperman said they are excited to have a store with reasonable prices close to their home. Cheryl Cooperman said grocery prices are getting expensive and starting to change the way she shops. "We're the type of people who never used to look at prices," she said. "I will go with what's on sale instead of just with what we want, and we never used to do that."

The opening comes as the USDA expects grocery prices to rise 2.8% this year. Fruits and vegetables are seeing an increase of less than 1%. Egg prices are dropping, and beef prices fell 1.3% from April to May, though wholesale beef prices remain at all-time highs for this time of year. Pork rose 1% from April to May, and chicken prices rose 1.3% over the same period.

Shoppers James and Harry said Thursday they do not expect relief at the register anytime soon. "Prices are gonna stay inflated; they're not gonna go down. It's just I'd be willing to bet on that," they said. Still, they said stores like Aldi, which often have lower prices than other stores, can make a difference for everyday shoppers.

The shoppers said having a store like Aldi for the average person is extremely beneficial, because there is nobody who is not feeling the price increase. The new Hobe Sound location gives Martin County residents another option to help cut grocery costs as food prices continue to rise. Cheryl Cooperman said she and her husband are thrilled to have the new store nearby. "Now we have something with reasonable prices that's close by," she said.

Yesterday, 4:35 PM ET

Port St. Lucie was ranked the 67th most stressed city in the United States according to a study released this month by WalletHub. The study compared 182 cities across 39 metrics including average weekly work hours, unemployment rate, divorce and suicide rates. The report cited Port St. Lucie as the 42nd most financially stressed city in America.

Jacksonville is the most stressed city in Florida, topping out at number 37 in the United States. Compared to other states, Florida performed fairly well, with no cities in the top 35 most stressed. In South Florida, Miami was on the list at number 50 with affordable housing being fourth worst in the country, and Fort Lauderdale at number 83.

WalletHub analyst Chip Lupo said cities with high crime rates, weak economies, less effective public health and congested transportation systems naturally lead to elevated stress levels for residents. "Some stress is out of our control, due to issues with family, friends or employers. However, where you live can play a big role in how stressed you are," Lupo said. "When moving, it's important to consider how a certain city may impact your mental health – not just your financial opportunities." The report found that money, health and the economy all remain top stressors.

Detroit, Baltimore, Cleveland, Memphis and Shreveport, Louisiana topped the list of most stressed cities in the United States. Fremont, California in the Bay Area topped the list of least stressed cities, followed by South Burlington, Vermont, and Bismarck and Fargo, North Dakota. Fremont is a notably affluent area with average home prices of about $1.5 million.

Yesterday, 10:59 AM ET

Park Vista Community High School's football team is building on late-season momentum as the Cobras set their sights on the state playoffs in 2026. The Cobras struggled to find their footing early in 2025, but finished strong, winning four of their last five games.

"Felt good throughout the second half of that season," senior Harris Bender said. "We're just working towards being able to carry that through the whole season, like you said, and we have all the pieces in place now, it's just about executing." The team believes it has assembled the right roster to make a deep postseason run, though players acknowledge the work required to reach their goals. "Coming in, I feel like we got the guys that we need to get it done," senior Erik Lee said. "But also, we got to put the work in to get there as well."

The ultimate goal is the state playoffs and eventually a state title, something the program has not achieved since 2017. Park Vista head football coach Donald Hanna emphasized the importance of maintaining focus throughout the season rather than looking too far ahead. "We got to take it one game at a time," Hanna said. "We can't look past anyone. We got to handle business weekly and if we do that, do our job, that'll get us in the playoffs." The approach reflects lessons learned from their 2025 campaign, when the Cobras needed time to develop consistency before finding their rhythm late in the year.

Senior Justice Jones expressed confidence that the team will achieve its championship aspirations. "It's going to feel amazing, because I already know it's gonna happen, definitely," Jones said. Park Vista opens the 2026 season on the road against Atlantic in August. The Cobras will look to avoid the slow start that plagued them last season and instead carry their late 2025 momentum into the opening weeks of the new campaign.
