1 hour ago

Rosa Mena, 50, of Port St. Lucie has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation after operating an illegal medical spa from a shed in her backyard on Northwest Coosa Drive. The sentencing follows a multi-agency investigation that began in May 2025 when a victim reported partial facial paralysis after receiving Botox injections at Mena's makeshift business, Miracle Hand and Spa.

The victim received 62 units of Botox injections from Mena and later experienced partial facial paralysis. When confronted, Mena refunded the $325 payment and asked the victim to return for another injection of vitamins to reverse the issues. When the paralysis persisted and the victim requested to see Mena's medical license, Mena provided an altered phlebotomy certificate that investigators discovered had expired in February 2024. Mena also falsely claimed to be a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

Port St. Lucie Police, working with PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax, and the Florida Department of Health, executed a search warrant at Mena's residence in the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive. Detectives found medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines, and injection devices for fillers and Botox.

Mena was convicted on multiple charges including three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, one count of fraud for obtaining property valued at $300 or more, one count of fraud for obtaining property under $20,000, and one count of using a two-way communication device to facilitate a felony. The victim had been referred to the illegal business by coworkers who had previously received services from Mena.

A federally appointed council voted to roll back longstanding protections for the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef found only off the coast of Fort Pierce. The reef sits hundreds of feet below the surface and is home to more than 2,000 marine species. Unlike most coral that requires shallow sunlight, the Oculina Bank thrives in deep, dark waters.

Dozens of community members gathered at the Blake Library in Martin County to rally support for preserving the reef. Dr. Grant Gilmore, a scientist who has dived the rare reef, warned that the ecosystem depends on the coral's habitat. "Very concerned about an ecosystem point of view, where your fish don't exist in a vacuum, they need a habitat, they need food," Gilmore said. "The coral reef was giving them habitat and food. You take that away, you don't have the fish."

Two proposals would reopen parts of the reef to shrimp trawling, even though scientists say trawling destroyed as much as 90% of the reef before federal protections were established decades ago. The proposals followed a Trump Executive order issued in April 2025 that directed eight regional councils nationwide to find ways to reinvigorate fishing industries. Bob Zales, executive director of the Southeastern Fishing Association and a generational shrimper, believes reopening shrimping access is essential for the struggling rock shrimp industry. "The shrimping industry is teetering on the brink of extinction," Zales said.

Council data shows the rock shrimp industry has plummeted from over 700 trips per year in the mid-1990s to under 100 since 2010. The number of active rock shrimpers dropped from 144 in 1996 to less than 30, with just 12 active boats in 2018. Mark Perry, Executive Director of the Florida Oceanographic Society, and other scientists urged the public to help save the reef. "Corals are in trouble worldwide. We shouldn't be destroying anymore," Gilmore said. The reef's corals grow very slowly and have existed for a long time, making recovery from damage extremely difficult.

A federal court in Illinois has granted a preliminary injunction blocking Florida from pursuing an enforcement lawsuit against the American Academy of Pediatrics, dealing a significant blow to the state's efforts to penalize the medical organization for its support of gender-affirming care for transgender youth. The injunction halts Attorney General James Uthmeier's lawsuit, which accused the nonprofit pediatricians organization of deceptive trade practices, racketeering, and antitrust violations based on its medical guidance and policy advocacy.

The federal court found the academy plausibly demonstrated that Florida filed the lawsuit to punish and suppress its protected speech. In granting the injunction, the court determined that the Florida complaint contains significant misrepresentations of the academy's policy statements and rests on a weak legal theory that its medical guidance constituted commercial speech. The court concluded the state appears to have brought the case in bad faith without a reasonable expectation of success on the merits.

The ruling represents a major development in the ongoing legal and political battle over transgender healthcare in Florida. The American Academy of Pediatrics, a national organization representing pediatricians, has publicly supported gender-affirming care for transgender youth as medically appropriate treatment. Florida officials have taken an aggressive stance against such care, and the enforcement lawsuit represented an attempt to use consumer protection and antitrust laws to challenge medical organizations that advocate for treatment policies at odds with state positions.

The preliminary injunction means Florida cannot proceed with its enforcement action while the underlying case continues in federal court. The decision raises questions about whether other states might face similar legal obstacles if they attempt to use enforcement powers against medical organizations based on their advocacy and clinical guidance. The case highlights the intersection of free speech protections, medical professional standards, and state regulatory authority. The American Academy of Pediatrics can continue its advocacy and policy work without facing immediate legal consequences from Florida during the litigation.

Yesterday, 2:59 PM ET

The Martin County Sheriff's Office announced arrests in a coordinated retail theft ring that targeted hardware stores across the Treasure Coast and beyond. Major Ruben Romero said Johnny Batista, 19, Roberto Hernandez-Castro, 39, and Roberto Aldana-Ferrera, 31 were involved in a series of burglaries at hardware stores across Martin, Palm Beach and St. Lucie counties from December 2025 to April 2026. The operation, dubbed Operation Hammer Time, saw at least 40 stores burglarized across 13 counties.

According to the sheriff's office, the thieves used sledgehammers or other blunt objects to smash glass doors and windows at hardware stores in Stuart, Lake Worth, Delray Beach, Boynton Beach, West Palm Beach, Fort Pierce and Jupiter, as well as locations in Broward County. The crews completed thefts within minutes, often before police arrived or alarms were triggered. Investigators say the stolen merchandise was transported back to Miami-Dade County, where the suspects lived, to be sold. Roughly $500,000 worth of property was stolen, in addition to damage caused to the stores.

Batista, connected to the first known theft at Sewell Hardware in Stuart in December 2025, was arrested in February for a vehicle theft and remains in Martin County Jail. Aldana-Ferrera is in custody in Georgia, while Hernandez-Castro has a warrant out for his arrest. A fourth suspect, Maikell Fleitas, is deceased. Romero said there are burglaries that happened before December, and investigators are still looking for one other suspect.

The suspects face charges of racketeering, burglary over $1,000 in damage while masked, burglary of an occupied structure and burglary of an unoccupied structure. Romero highlighted the broader community impact, noting that retail theft contributes to higher prices. "There's already inflation that we notice severely in our state, and the more they steal, the more damage they cause, the more expensive things are," Romero said. "People can't go to work because they can't afford the tools."

Martin County residents rally at Blake Library to save rare deep-sea coral reef off Fort Pierce

Dozens of community members gathered at the Blake Library in Martin County after a federally appointed council voted to roll back longstanding protections for the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef found nowhere else on Earth.

23 hours ago

Dozens of community members gathered at the Blake Library in Martin County after a federally appointed council voted to roll back longstanding protections for the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef found nowhere else on Earth. Located just off the coast of Fort Pierce, the reef is hidden hundreds of feet below the surface and thrives in deep, dark waters unlike most coral that needs shallow sunlight to survive. The reef is home to more than 2,000 marine species.

Scientists Dr. Grant Gilmore and Mark Perry, Executive Director of the Florida Oceanographic Society, urged the public to help save the reef at the gathering. Two proposals would reopen parts of the reef to shrimp trawling even though scientists say trawling destroyed as much as 90% of the reef before federal protections were put in place decades ago. "Very concerned about an ecosystem point of view, where your fish don't exist in a vacuum, they need a habitat, they need food," Gilmore said. "The coral reef was giving them habitat and food. You take that away, you don't have the fish."

The proposals followed an executive order issued in April 2025 that tasked eight regional councils nationwide to find ways to reinvigorate the fishing industries. Bob Zales, executive director of the Southeastern Fishing Association and a generational shrimper, believes reopening shrimping access to the Oculina Bank is essential for the struggling rock shrimp industry. Council data shows the rock shrimp industry has plummeted from over 700 trips per year in the mid-1990s to under 100 since 2010. The number of active rock shrimpers dropped from 144 in 1996 to less than 30, with just 12 active boats in 2018.
