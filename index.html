2026-07-17

JetBlue is ending its direct flights between New York's JFK Airport and Vero Beach Regional Airport, citing low passenger demand for the route that launched in December. The airline announced that service between the two airports will end Sept. 9, just nine months after the route began operating with much fanfare and optimistic passengers. JetBlue said in a statement that demand for this new route has not materialized as the airline had hoped.

JetBlue will continue serving Vero Beach through its Boston route and has not announced whether it plans to add any replacement routes from Vero Beach or if it will adjust frequency on the remaining Boston service. All affected customers will be notified directly by JetBlue and will receive full refunds. Passengers with upcoming reservations on the discontinued route are encouraged to contact JetBlue directly or check their email for rebooking options and refund information.

The JFK-Vero Beach route was seen as a major win for the Treasure Coast when it launched in December 2025, providing direct access to one of the nation's busiest airports and potentially boosting tourism to the area. The inaugural flight brought enthusiastic passengers who called the service a game changer for connecting family and friends between New York and the Treasure Coast. The route's cancellation comes as a disappointment to local officials and residents who had hoped the direct connection would strengthen ties between the two regions.

The discontinuation of the JFK route after such a short period highlights the challenges smaller regional airports face in sustaining air service to major hubs. While JetBlue's Boston service will continue to provide some connectivity for Vero Beach travelers, the loss of direct access to JFK eliminates what many saw as a crucial link to the New York metropolitan area. The airline has not indicated whether it might reconsider the route in the future or explore other destinations from Vero Beach Regional Airport.

Demolition has begun at Murray Middle School in Martin County, marking the start of a major reconstruction effort following tornado damage caused by Hurricane Milton. The project includes reconstruction of buildings destroyed in the tornadoes, along with a new administration building, media center, art lab, and a full overhaul of the parking lot. The demolition phase alone costs $3.3 million, though the total project cost has not been finalized. The project is being funded by local capital improvement, sales tax and Public Education Capital Outlay.

Students who were displaced from the affected classrooms will continue learning in portable classrooms until construction is complete. Barriers have already been placed around the work zone to keep students out. The school district said parents can be assured their students will be safe during construction.

"Murray Middle School was already slated to get all of this construction work with a new admin area, a new media center and a brand new art lab," said Kirstie Gernaine, coordinator of public information and community relations for the Martin County School District. "With Hurricane Milton and those tornadoes destroying the buildings in the back buildings 18, 10, and nine we were able to add these on."

The district hopes to complete the renovations by December 2027. The tornado damage from Hurricane Milton affected buildings 18, 10, and nine at the school, which allowed the district to incorporate rebuilding those structures into the already planned construction work. The comprehensive project will transform the campus with updated facilities while replacing the buildings lost to the storm.

The St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office is intensifying traffic enforcement this summer, deploying additional deputies and launching a new targeting initiative aimed at reducing speeding and reckless driving across the county. Sheriff Richard Del Toro said traffic is the top concern among residents and described the crackdown as a quality of life issue. Del Toro is using extra deputies from the sheriff's school resource division to boost enforcement while the school year is on break and placing top-performing deputies in areas of concern.

Deputies Christopher Rodrigues and Cheyenne Benning are among those targeting high-problem corridors, including Midway Road, U.S. Highway 1 and Indian River Drive. Benning said the county sees a lot of excessive speeding and that someone recently passed her on I-95 doing 105 mph. Rodrigues said he stopped a driver doing 72 mph on Midway Road and another doing 98 mph on U.S. Highway 1. On Indian River Drive, where the speed limit is 25 mph, Rodrigues said his top speed recorded there was 73 mph.

In the past four weeks, deputies conducted 1,162 traffic stops, wrote 470 citations and issued 1,728 written warnings. The enforcement push is part of a broader strategy Del Toro put in place when he took office. He eliminated the agency's dedicated traffic unit and made traffic enforcement the responsibility of every deputy. Since 2025, the sheriff's office has issued more than 26,000 citations, a 155% increase.

Residents say the problem is hard to miss. Ashley, a St. Lucie County resident, described what she sees on local roads as insane and said nobody slows down. Jessica, another local driver, said drivers are careless and cut people off. Del Toro said he has a family that drives on these roadways and cares about his loved ones and everyone else's loved ones. He said he wants to make the community safer.

Yesterday, 5:00 PM ET

Republican U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds and Democrat David Jolly have emerged as frontrunners in Florida's gubernatorial primary race as the August 18 primary approaches. Donalds holds a commanding lead in Republican polling and fundraising, while Jolly has consolidated Democratic support, though both face scrutiny in the closing weeks.

Lt. Gov. Jay Collins used a Thursday campaign call to position himself as the strongest Republican alternative to Donalds, despite trailing significantly in all metrics. Collins defended his support for Immigration and Customs Enforcement and law enforcement, rejected claims of tension between his campaign and staff inside the governor's office, and urged former Florida House Speaker Paul Renner to leave the race. Collins also argued Donalds could struggle in a general election. "This is a two-person race in the primary," Collins said. "It is between Byron Donalds and myself, and we absolutely have a clear path to do this." A Tyson Group survey released this week placed Donalds at 48 percent, followed by Collins at 9 percent, businessman James Fishback at 8 percent and Renner at 4 percent. About 26 percent of respondents remained undecided. Donalds has raised more than $90 million across his campaign operation and holds President Donald Trump's endorsement. Gov. Ron DeSantis, who appointed Collins lieutenant governor in August 2025, has remained publicly neutral in the primary.

Fishback faces dual challenges as his campaign comes under fire. The Front Line, a Republican group aligned with U.S. Sen. Ted Cruz, announced a $400,000 campaign targeting Fishback over statements the group characterizes as antisemitic. The artificial intelligence-generated ad depicts Fishback as a talking fish making racist comments. Fishback denies being antisemitic and says the spending shows Republican establishment figures are concerned about his campaign. Collins is also challenging Fishback's candidacy in court, alleging Fishback lived and voted in Washington, D.C., and does not meet Florida's seven-year residency requirement for governors. A two-day hearing is scheduled to begin July 21 in Tallahassaba. Fishback says he was born and raised in Florida, has lived in the state throughout his life and purchased a Washington condo only to visit his parents. Because ballots have already been printed, the judge said a ruling against Fishback would likely involve directing election officials not to count votes cast for him rather than removing his name from ballots.

Jolly moved this week to address his past business and political relationship with Clearwater financier Leo Govoni. Federal prosecutors allege Govoni and accountant John Witeck stole more than $100 million from a nonprofit that managed trust funds for people with disabilities. The indictment alleges the scheme operated from 2009 through 2025. Jolly is not a defendant in the case. Jolly previously worked as a vice president at Boston Finance Group, a Govoni-controlled company, before entering Congress. Govoni also supported Jolly's past political campaigns and served as a finance co-chairman during Jolly's unsuccessful 2016 U.S. Senate campaign. In a campaign video, Jolly said he had no knowledge of the alleged theft and welcomed scrutiny of his record. "Unlike too many politicians today who hide behind lawyers or refuse to answer questions, I'm an open book," Jolly said. "Because when you've done nothing wrong, you have nothing to hide."

Yesterday, 6:41 PM ET

Demolition has begun at Murray Middle School in Martin County as part of a major reconstruction effort following tornado damage from Hurricane Milton. The project will rebuild buildings destroyed by the tornadoes and add a new administration building, media center, art lab, and a complete parking lot overhaul. The demolition phase alone costs $3.3 million, with the total project cost not yet finalized. Funding comes from local capital improvement, sales tax, and Public Education Capital Outlay.

Kirstie Gernaine, coordinator of public information and community relations for the Martin County School District, explained the school was already scheduled for significant construction work when the hurricane struck. "Murray Middle School was already slated to get all of this construction work with a new admin area, a new media center and a brand new art lab," said Gernaine. "With Hurricane Milton and those tornadoes destroying the buildings in the back buildings 18, 10, and nine we were able to add these on." The tornado damage led to the destruction of three buildings that have now been incorporated into the planned renovation project.

Students displaced from affected classrooms will continue learning in portable classrooms until construction is complete. The school district has implemented safety measures to protect students during the ongoing construction work. Barriers have been placed around the work zone to keep students away from construction areas. School officials have assured parents that students will remain safe throughout the renovation period despite the active construction on campus.

The Martin County School District hopes to complete the renovations by December 2027. The project represents a significant investment in rebuilding and modernizing the middle school campus following the hurricane damage. The reconstruction effort combines previously scheduled improvements with necessary repairs from the tornado destruction, allowing the district to comprehensively update the campus facilities in one extended construction project.

Mon, 13 Jul 2026 18:36:08 -0400

Registration is now open for Port St. Lucie City University, a free nine-session program designed to help residents better understand how their local government operates.

Classes begin Wednesday, Aug. 12, and will be held from 5:30 to 8:30 p.m. in the Council Chambers at Port St. Lucie City Hall, located at 121 SW Port St. Lucie Blvd.

Participants will learn about City departments, municipal services and the decision-making processes that shape the community. The program also gives residents opportunities to meet City staff, ask questions and gain a closer look at the work involved in operating one of Florida's largest cities.

Residents who complete the course will receive a certificate of completion and may also choose to become a City Ambassador, helping share information about City programs and services throughout the community.

City University is intended to encourage civic engagement while strengthening connections between residents and local government. Since the program began in 2016, more than 600 residents have participated.

Enrollment is limited to 160 participants.

Residents who are unable to attend the in-person classes can also access City U on Demand, which offers a more flexible way to learn about Port St. Lucie government and municipal operations.
