Jupiter weekend beach dance parties to continue near sea turtle nests with new lighting rules

The Town of Jupiter held a meeting Wednesday at the police department to discuss ongoing concerns about weekend beach dance parties in an area where sea turtles nest. The gathering brought together representatives from local, county, and state agencies along with staff from Loggerhead Marinelife Center. The meeting ran approximately ninety minutes, and organizers of the dance e...

A few minutes ago

The Town of Jupiter held a meeting Wednesday at the police department to discuss ongoing concerns about weekend beach dance parties in an area where sea turtles nest. The gathering brought together representatives from local, county, and state agencies along with staff from Loggerhead Marinelife Center. The meeting ran approximately ninety minutes, and organizers of the dance events reported afterward that the discussion was constructive. Following the session, Foreli of Happy Beach Dancing confirmed the weekly gatherings will proceed with modifications to their current setup.

The primary adjustment involves limiting the lighting at the events to minimize disruption to nesting sea turtles. Some community members had advocated for relocating the dance parties away from the nesting area during sea turtle breeding season, but organizers opted instead to implement the lighting changes. The Happy Beach Dancing group has operated at Beach Access 55 for the past three years, regularly attracting dozens to hundreds of attendees each week. The gatherings appeal to diverse groups, including families, children, and people bringing dogs to the beach. Organizers announced they plan to hold their next event at Beach Access 55 this Saturday.

Jupiter's beaches represent one of the world's most densely populated loggerhead sea turtle nesting sites. Joanna Walczak, Vice President of Conservation at Loggerhead Marinelife Center, explained that the area contains approximately 150 nests. Walczak noted that the presence of large crowds on the beach itself, regardless of the specific activity, can discourage nesting mother turtles from coming ashore to lay their eggs. This concern highlighted why the meeting became necessary and why the lighting modifications were considered important by conservation officials.

The Town of Jupiter issued a statement regarding the outcome of the meeting but the specific content was not detailed in available reporting. The decision to continue the beach dancing with adjusted lighting represents a compromise between allowing the popular community gatherings to persist and protecting the sensitive nesting habitat. The lighting restrictions aim to balance both concerns going forward. The group's commitment to returning this weekend indicates they view the new agreement as workable for their operations while addressing environmental protection goals.

Yesterday, 6:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie residents will receive money back after the city secured a $24 million settlement from Waste Pro, their former trash hauler. Both the city and company filed lawsuits against each other in 2021 during the pandemic. The city alleged that Waste Pro failed to meet its contractual service standards, while the company attributed the problems to labor shortages caused by the pandemic. Rather than receiving checks, residents will see credits applied directly to their property tax bills.

The settlement amounts vary depending on property ownership history. More than 57,000 properties with the same owner since 2022 will receive $364 credits each. The city determined that these residents experienced the most significant problems during Waste Pro's service failures in 2021 and 2022, including service interruptions. The remaining credits address rate increases that occurred while Waste Pro breached its contract, plus costs associated with litigation and transitioning to a new provider.

A second group of properties will receive $64 credits. This includes 24,676 properties that experienced title changes since 2022. Because the city cannot determine whether these changes resulted from trusts, marriage, divorce, or relocation, and cannot reach former owners, all properties in this category receive the smaller amount. Additionally, 13,241 new properties will also get $64 credits since these households have paid pandemic-related rate increases.

The city has created an opportunity for property owners who sold their homes and purchased another within the city during the same period. These residents may submit documentation proving continuous ownership to receive the remaining $300 credit on their next year's tax bill. A 180-day claims window will allow eligible households to apply. The city will establish the claims process after review by city staff and the legal department. Mayor Shannon Martin stated that from the beginning, he maintained the funds should benefit residents who experienced the problems in 2021 and 2022. Councilman Anthony Bonna said the city had reached the right balance, while Councilman David Pickett noted that residents suffered through the disputes and litigation. One resident, Kelly Lee, expressed that she was pleasantly surprised by the outcome and appreciated the city's thoughtful approach to the settlement distribution.

Palm Beach police arrested a 19-year-old West Palm Beach resident accused of impersonating a parking official and defrauding visitors on Worth Avenue. Ti'Miyah De'Asia Allen allegedly posed as an employee of ParkMobile and the Palm Beach Parking Authority while running a scheme that targeted unsuspecting drivers. The investigation began after victims reported fraudulent charges to police, revealing a calculated effort to steal credit card information from people trying to pay for parking in the upscale shopping district.

According to an arrest affidavit, Allen would approach drivers either before they paid for parking or while they were in the process of paying. She allegedly offered to let them pay approximately $1 for parking instead of the legitimate rate of $5 per hour plus a 40 cent transaction fee. To make the scheme more convincing, Allen carried what appeared to be a legitimate ParkMobile payment device and displayed a fake badge, police say. The convincing appearance of official credentials helped her gain the trust of her targets, many of whom were over 65 years old.

The consequences for victims have been substantial. In one documented case from June 5, a person approached by Allen reported discovering a fraudulent $2,500 charge on their credit card from a company called Health Aid. Police determined that Allen's illegal operation resulted in the town losing parking fee revenue that should have been legitimately collected. The affidavit explicitly noted this financial impact on municipal finances in addition to the direct harm to individual victims.

This is not Allen's first brush with law enforcement involving financial fraud. Police say she was involved in a similar scheme the previous year while working at a Wawa location on Belvedere Road. During that incident, she allegedly voided customer transactions for profit. The pattern of fraudulent behavior led authorities to bring multiple charges against Allen in connection with the Worth Avenue scheme. She faces charges including falsely impersonating an officer, organized scheme to defraud, and unlawful use of a badge or other indicia of authority. These charges reflect the premeditated nature of the operation and her deliberate use of false credentials to carry out the scam.

Women in Palm Beach County will have two opportunities next month to receive free mammogram screenings through Promise Fund, a South Florida nonprofit dedicated to increasing breast and cervical cancer survivorship. The organization is hosting free Mammography Screening Days on July 11 in Jupiter and July 12 in West Palm Beach. These events are made possible through partnerships with Florida Mobile Mammography, HealthyMe, the Health Care District of Palm Beach County, Origin Church, the Florida Department of Health and Monarch Health Services.

She added that early detection can significantly impact treatment outcomes and quality of life. "Treatment is often easier to manage, and people are able to focus more on their health, their families, and their everyday lives," she explained.

Promise Fund serves uninsured, underinsured and underserved women throughout Palm Beach, Broward and Martin counties. The nonprofit has made substantial progress in expanding access to care across South Florida. Through education and advocacy efforts, the organization has reached more than 145,000 women. Additionally, more than 27,000 women have received access to screenings, diagnostics and care through Promise Fund programs. The organization has also assisted 286 women who were diagnosed with cancer in navigating their treatment options.

Currently, Promise Fund operates four co-located screening programs across Palm Beach and Broward counties. The organization is actively seeking funding to expand this model to Martin County. These free screening events represent an important opportunity for women who lack adequate health insurance or have limited access to preventive care services. Early detection through mammography screening remains a critical tool for improving health outcomes and survival rates for breast cancer patients.

3:40 PM ET

Hillsborough County Commissioners voted unanimously to direct the County Attorney's Office to draft ordinance changes targeting 7-hydroxymitragynine, a potent kratom extract that federal and state officials say poses abuse risks. The move comes as a statewide emergency rule restricting the compound approaches its June 30 expiration date. Commissioner Joshua Wostal, who introduced the measure, emphasized the proposal would focus exclusively on concentrated 7-OH products and not affect traditional kratom leaf sold at kava bars, coffee shops, or other local businesses.

The concentrated extract has drawn scrutiny from regulators due to its ability to bind to opioid receptors and produce stronger effects than natural kratom. FDA Commissioner Marty Makary has stated that 7-OH can be up to 13 times more powerful than morphine. The FDA has expressed particular concern about concentrated 7-OH being manufactured into fruit-flavored gummies, drink mixes, and other products potentially appealing to children, with such items sold online, in gas stations, convenience stores, and vape shops. Attorney General James Uthmeier issued the emergency rule last year, classifying isolated or concentrated 7-OH as a Schedule I controlled substance in Florida, making it illegal to sell, possess, or distribute. Wostal said he heard Uthmeier indicate plans to renew the rule at a Tiger Bay forum, but argued the county should not wait for state action to protect residents.

Florida lawmakers considered making the restriction permanent during the 2026 Legislative Session through SB 436, sponsored by Jacksonville Senator Clay Yarborough, but that language was removed before passage. This left the emergency rule as the primary statewide safeguard. Commissioner Donna Cameron Cepeda characterized synthetic opioids as a crisis demanding local attention, saying the county bears responsibility for preventing resident harm. Commissioner Chris Boles noted that designer drugs often do not respond to lifesaving overdose interventions like NARCAN, making it important for the county to stay ahead of emerging synthetic compounds that continue to evolve.

The commissioners' vote does not immediately establish a ban but initiates the county's process for reviewing its synthetic drug ordinance and determining whether local 7-OH restrictions are necessary. Wostal amended his motion to specifically target 7-OH sales while excluding kratom generally, clarifying he did not want to surprise local businesses during the drafting process. He expressed concern about the consequences if the state emergency rule expired without local protections in place.

A few minutes ago

Jupiter's town council has approved a fire assessment fee to help fund the town's new fire rescue department, which begins service Oct. 1. The per-property charge will cover about 8% of the total cost of the department, with property taxes funding 84% and EMS transport fees covering the remaining 8%. The assessment will replace the current county fire fee on residents' tax bills. Finance Director Scott Reynolds highlighted the financial benefit to property owners, stating "The immediate savings for property owners, we've been talking about this for three years, starting at $25 million so right out of the gate, a $10 million savings to the residents." Some residents raised concerns about potential increases in the fee over time.

Mayor Jim Kuretski cast the lone no vote on the funding structure, citing concerns about how the assessment is calculated across different property types. Kuretski noted that single family homes are assessed based on total square footage including garages and porches, while townhomes and condos are not. The town mailed letters explaining the fee to 30,000 homes. Town of Jupiter spokesperson Mariana DiNunzio acknowledged confusion among residents, attributing it to the new non ad-valorem assessment. The town has established an online calculator to help residents see their potential savings.

The project would construct a new at-grade railroad crossing between Tamarind Avenue and Australian Avenue, creating a direct connection between Fern Street and Clearwater Drive. The commission approved two resolutions: Resolution 119-26, a $3 million state-funded grant agreement through the Florida Department of Transportation, and Resolution 120-26F, covering associated funding appropriations. The total estimated cost for the roadway portion is $14.8 million, with remaining funding coming from existing city budget allocations and future sources including HUD grants, mobility fees, and CRA funding. The city is currently in the preliminary design phase, with the project expected to go out to bid as a design-build contract in fall 2026. Construction is slated to begin in early 2027 with a target completion date of early 2028.

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium invites visitors to explore Florida's coastal habitats through fully live ecosystem displays. Since 1980, the facility has operated under the Smithsonian with a philosophy that distinguishes it from most public aquariums. Manager Bill Hoffman told WPTV the aquarium houses more species than many larger facilities and called it "the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast." The centerpiece is the Atlantic coral reef tank, which recreates one of Florida's most significant coastal ecosystems. Visitors also encounter live models of seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities that demonstrate intricate relationships between organisms, including a four-inch barracuda.

The exhibit's touch tank allows guests to handle unusual marine species under staff supervision. According to exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran, visitors often encounter animals they have never seen before, making it a crowd favorite. The facility operates in partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station and aims to raise public awareness about ocean conservation threats. Hoffman emphasized that stable ecosystems depend on numerous small organisms. Upcoming programming will tie into the facility's 25th anniversary celebration later this year. The aquarium is located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce and is open year-round. More information is available at sms.si.edu and Smithsonian Marine Station social media channels.

Researchers explain how sargassum helps and hurts Florida's sea turtles

Sargassum plays a complicated role for sea turtles, providing critical habitat offshore but creating obstacles for hatchlings when it washes ashore in large amounts.

Yesterday, 5:16 PM ET

Sargassum presents a complicated challenge for Florida's sea turtles, despite being viewed as a nuisance by many beachgoers. When the brown seaweed floats offshore in ocean currents, it serves as critical habitat for hatchlings leaving the beach. Emily Turla, laboratory coordinator at Florida Atlantic University's Marine Science Laboratory and a Ph.D. student, explained that after hatching, young sea turtles spend their first days swimming away from shore, where floating mats of sargassum provide shelter from predators, food sources, and protection during one of the most vulnerable stages of their lives.

The problem emerges when excessive amounts of sargassum wash ashore. Large piles of seaweed become obstacles for hatchlings trying to reach the ocean, sometimes leaving them stuck in the debris. Research professor Dr. Brian LaPointe with FAU's Harbor Branch Oceanographic Institute notes that the extra time hatchlings spend navigating around accumulated seaweed increases their vulnerability to predators. As sea turtle nesting season continues across South Florida, researchers say finding ways to manage excessive sargassum while preserving its ecological benefits will remain an important challenge for coastal communities.

Delray Beach considers new e-bike rules after dangerous encounters on city streets

Dangerous encounters with e-bikes and dirt bikes on Delray Beach streets have city leaders considering new rules. City commissioner Juli Casale is pushing for a new ordinance to crack down on speeding and sidewalk riding.

Yesterday, 10:35 PM ET

City Commissioner Juli Casale is pushing for a new ordinance to address dangerous e-bike and dirt bike behavior on Delray Beach streets. Casale has witnessed riders moving in and out of traffic, sometimes riding on sidewalks and wearing masks. The proposed ordinance would crack down on speeding and sidewalk riding while requiring helmets and identification for riders under 16. "The kids should not be riding over 20mph, they should be wearing helmets, they should carry identification and they should be very careful," Casale said.

Residents have reported safety concerns about the e-bike activity. Fred Levy, from Highland Beach, who rides along A1A regularly, said the scenic route comes with risks. "It's really scary for us bikers, really scary. I say every day I have like an incident where my sort of life passes in front of my eyes," Levy said. Frequent runner Kayla Genest said she has seen younger kids on e-bikes around the city doing wheelies and other tricks. City staff will draft the ordinance and present it at a later meeting. Casale said the goal is to give officers the tools they need to enforce the rules, with safety as the top priority in Delray Beach.

Promise Fund to offer free mammogram screenings in Jupiter next month

Women in Jupiter will have access to free mammogram screenings on July 11 through Promise Fund, a South Florida nonprofit focused on breast and cervical cancer survivorship.

12:00 PM ET

Promise Fund will hold free Mammography Screening Days on July 11 in Jupiter and July 12 in West Palm Beach. The Jupiter event is scheduled for 8 a.m. on Indiantown Road, while the West Palm Beach screening will take place July 12 from 9 a.m. The screenings are being offered in partnership with Florida Mobile Mammography, HealthyMe, the Health Care District of Palm Beach County, Origin Church, the Florida Department of Health and Monarch Health Services. Appointments are required for both events. Those interested can register by calling 877-427-7664.

Promise Fund serves women throughout Palm Beach, Broward and Martin counties who are uninsured, underinsured or have limited access to health care. Lissette Lahoz, Promise Fund's Palm Beach County navigator, said early detection can make a real difference, noting that treatment is often easier to manage when cancer is caught early. Promise Fund has reached more than 145,000 women across South Florida through education and advocacy efforts. More than 27,000 women have received access to screenings, diagnostics and care through its programs, and 286 women diagnosed with cancer have received assistance navigating treatment. The nonprofit currently operates 4 co-located screening programs in Palm Beach and Broward counties.

Lake Worth Beach approves 10-year lease extension for Benny's on the Beach through February 2043

The Lake Worth Beach City Commission unanimously approved a 10-year lease extension for Benny's on the Beach, pushing the pier restaurant's lease from January 2033 to February 2043 with a 5% rent increase every two years.

12:47 PM ET

Dylan Lipton, Benny's owner, defended the extension by pointing to the restaurant's decades-long presence in the community. For four decades, Lipton said, Benny's has been far more than a restaurant and has become part of the identity of Lake Worth Beach. Lipton said the restaurant employs more than 100 local residents, invests $1 million annually in maintenance, repairs, utilities and improvements, and serves as a major tourism driver. The restaurant operates as a year-round anchor, helping support surrounding businesses.

The decision generated public debate over fairness and timing. Resident Kim Stokes raised concerns about unequal treatment, questioning why Benny's wasn't receiving the same conditions as the Beach Club. Resident Jenny Powell questioned the timing of the extension, which comes seven years before the current lease expires in January 2033. Commissioner Christopher Serafinchan led the extension's advocacy, calling Benny's the city's highest paying tenant by a mile. Commissioner Sarah Malega conducted an affordability check before voting, ordering the sunrise for $16. Mayor Betty Resch said Benny's deserves the stability of continuing there. The commission approved the 10-year extension unanimously, extending the lease through February 2043 with a 5 percent rent increase every two years.

Gas prices are starting to fall just in time as a new survey shows two out of three Floridians say higher prices could tank their summer road trips this year. Florida drivers polled say if gas prices go up another $1.01, their summer road trip travel is off.

11:06 AM ET

A new survey by RV Windshield Replacement shows that two out of three Floridians say higher gas prices could cancel their summer road trips this year. The survey found that if gas prices rise another $1.01, Florida drivers say their summer travel plans are off the table. According to the poll, 68% of Florida drivers agree that shorter state trips might be the most doable option, though some say even that could be difficult to manage.

WPTV Anchor Hollani Davis spoke with drivers at a gas station on 45th Street in West Palm Beach about whether they could afford higher prices. One driver responded, "No. There is no way." Another driver added, "I mean, no one can unless you're rich." A WPTV viewer shared how rising costs have already changed their travel habits, saying they now go to Orlando once a month instead of every two weeks, and sometimes skip months entirely because of gas prices. Gas prices are starting to fall, which could provide relief for drivers planning summer travel.
