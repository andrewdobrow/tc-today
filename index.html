Martin County Commissioner seeks to move Brightline station away from downtown Stuart

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi has asked county staff to begin discussions with Brightline about relocating the planned train station from its current proposed location in downtown Stuart. The station was previously agreed to be built on a parking lot across from the courthouse in the downtown area. Ciampi suggested the fairgrounds as an alternative site, citing parking c...

Yesterday, 6:50 PM ET

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi has asked county staff to begin discussions with Brightline about relocating the planned train station from its current proposed location in downtown Stuart. The station was previously agreed to be built on a parking lot across from the courthouse in the downtown area. Ciampi suggested the fairgrounds as an alternative site, citing parking congestion and other issues at the downtown location as reasons to reconsider.

The commissioner's request comes in response to concerns raised by the FEC, which sent a letter to then-Stuart Mayor Christopher Collins stating it does not support a Brightline station in Stuart. According to the FEC, the proposed downtown station would violate U.S. Coast Guard rules because it would require the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge to remain lowered while the train stops at the station. Ciampi acknowledged that if the Army Corps of Engineers or U.S. Coast Guard determine the downtown location is too close to the bridge, the issue becomes moot for him. He argued that moving the station further south to the fairgrounds would eliminate the bridge proximity problem entirely.

The suggestion drew mixed responses from the community. Paul Gedwed, a resident, supported the idea, calling it a well-thought-out opinion given the congestion at the proposed downtown site. However, Steven Vitale, owner of the Colorado Inn, voiced concerns about moving the station away from downtown. He questioned why the county would build a train station that people cannot easily walk to, noting that most people would want to access downtown Stuart. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, expressed a different perspective, saying her downtown store would benefit either way the county decides, but urged officials to make a decision and proceed with the project regardless of location, as long as it remains in Martin County.

County officials clarified that Ciampi's comments do not constitute official direction for staff to implement changes. The board would need to formally discuss the relocation proposal and vote on it before staff could begin exploring alternative sites. Meanwhile, Martin County continues waiting for approval on its grant application to fund the Brightline station project. Until the funding is secured, the project cannot move forward with either location.

Riviera Beach's paid parking program at Ocean Walk and Singer Island is temporarily paused after City Council Member Dr. Glen Spiritis was ticketed without parking. Mayor Douglas Lawson explained that Spiritis was charged for driving into the parking lot and immediately leaving. The pause was announced during last Wednesday's city council meeting after the city received an influx of concerns since the program launched in October of last year. Lawson noted that paid parking represents a significant change for the community and that officials are "learning through the bumps and bruises trying to make sure that we get it right."

The program requires visitors to scan a QR code to pay for parking and has drawn complaints from business owners, customers and residents since its launch. The pause provides relief for Kerry Lutz, who filed a lawsuit challenging the program, claiming it violates federal standards. Mayor Lawson said the city will address concerns regarding handicap parking and timing, and will issue refunds to anyone wrongfully charged. Parking officials will prepare a full report on the program's fees and system. Lawson emphasized the pause is not permanent, stating the city will continue the system "to make sure that it's done in the most effective, efficient way" while generating additional revenue for the city.

Yesterday, 5:31 PM ET

Nick Greene, author of "How to Watch Soccer Like a Genius," believes Americans are embracing soccer far more than many people recognize. During a recent interview with WPTV's Jon Shainman, Greene shared his passion for the sport and discussed his book's unique approach to understanding the game. The book examines soccer through the perspectives of agronomists, architects, computer scientists, and other experts, offering readers fresh insights into why the sport captivates billions of fans worldwide. Greene emphasized soccer's fundamental appeal, saying "you have this game that is the simplest game, you just need a ball and you don't even need grass."

Several key moments in the 1990s transformed America's relationship with soccer. The 1994 World Cup, which took place in the United States, marked a turning point for the sport domestically. The launch of Major League Soccer that same decade provided American fans with a professional league to follow regularly. The 1999 U.S. Women's World Cup victory further energized public interest and demonstrated the sport's potential to capture American attention. These three milestones collectively contributed to soccer's sustained growth in the country over the past several decades.

The sport has undergone a dramatic evolution since its early days. Greene explained that soccer's transformation reflects a shift from individual-focused play to sophisticated team strategies. The first modern players, who emerged in England during the 1860s and 1870s, were aristocratic gentlemen who approached the game differently than today's professionals. One early player famously characterized the sport as something played "purely for my own pleasure," highlighting how individualistic the game once was. Modern soccer, by contrast, emphasizes complex tactical coordination and collective team objectives.

Looking toward the future, Greene contemplates soccer's next chapter in America. He questions whether the sport will maintain its current trajectory of steady growth or whether it will ascend to become a truly dominant spectacle in American culture, reaching what he calls "the stratosphere." Greene's expert-driven approach in his book aims to deepen readers' appreciation for soccer's tactical nuances and strategic elements. As the sport continues gaining momentum across the country, understanding these complexities becomes increasingly valuable for American audiences seeking to engage more fully with the game.

Yesterday, 1:45 PM ET

The Delray Beach Public Library is brightening summer for local kids and teens by offering free STREAM programs covering Science, Technology, Reading, Art, Engineering and Math. All Florida residents can participate in activities at the library's 36,000-square-foot facility located at Atlantic Avenue and Swinton Avenue in downtown Delray Beach. According to Kae Jonsons, director of development and community relations, the library has a long history of serving the community. It was founded in 1913 when 30 women from the Ladies Improvement Society each donated a book to start the collection. The library now welcomes more than 193,000 visitors annually.

Young adult librarian Natalie Baron is leading sessions designed for teens, including ColorSpace: Artistic Excavations, where participants explore art history spanning from cave paintings to ancient pottery. Teens can also participate in lab-based activities like collaborative 3D puzzle builds and select themed book boxes. Mandy Fulop, head of the children's department, launched the STREAM Into Summer Literacy Program to help young readers set goals with librarians and track their progress. Participants take home an Unearth a Story bag containing a free STREAM book and related activities. Those interested can register at delraylibrary.org.

Working with the School District of Palm Beach County, the library provides free lunch daily from 11:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. and snacks from 2 p.m. to 4 p.m. to youth up to age 18. Family Nights on select Tuesdays invite children age 5 and older to explore STREAM topics together. The library also runs Saturday Science Discovery programs for ages 9 to 12 that feature hands-on archaeology activities including "Piece Together History," trash bag archaeology, carbon dating, and fossil hunts.

Upcoming special events include visits from Malachi Fenn of the Florida Public Archaeology Network on June 11, a Sea Turtle Adventures beach cleanup on June 25, American Shark Conservancy's SharkSmarts presentation on July 2, and FPAN's Beyond Artifacts program on July 16. The library's free, high-quality education programs help bridge summer learning gaps while keeping families engaged and promoting community connections in an accessible, safe space within walking distance for many residents. A complete program lineup and registration details for each event are available at delraylibrary.org.

Congressman Byron Donalds is dominating the Republican primary race for Florida governor, according to a new poll from the Associated Industries of Florida. The survey shows Donalds capturing 54 percent support among Republican primary voters, giving him a 46-point advantage over his nearest competitor. Conducted June 3-7 among 386 Republican primary voters, the poll found James Fishback at 8 percent, Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins at 5 percent, and former House Speaker Paul Renner at 2 percent. Nearly a third of respondents, 31 percent, remain undecided about the race.

Donalds demonstrates strong support across Florida's major population centers and demographic groups. In Fort Myers, he leads with 71 percent support, followed by 69 percent in West Palm Beach, 54 percent in Orlando, 53 percent in the Panhandle, and 51 percent in Tampa. Among white Republicans, Donalds draws 55 percent support, while earning 54 percent among Hispanic Republicans. Senior voters, who typically show the highest participation rates in primary elections, back Donalds at 60 percent. These results align with previous polling data showing his consistent strength. An Emerson College survey in April found Donalds at 46 percent in the primary, with Collins and Fishback each at 4 percent.

Donalds enters the primary race with significant financial and political advantages. He has raised more than 81 million dollars through his campaign and the Friends of Byron Donalds PAC from over 35,000 donors. He also carries the complete endorsement of President Donald Trump. The campaign has constructed an extensive field apparatus throughout Florida to prepare for early voting, which begins in two months. An April poll by Echelon Insights showed Donalds leading Democrat David Jolly 49 percent to 43 percent in a potential general election matchup.

Jeremy Sheftel, AIF vice president of Political Operations, said the polling demonstrates Donalds' commanding position heading into the primary. Ryan Smith, the campaign's chief strategist, emphasized that Florida Republicans are uniting behind Donalds and his message about making the state more affordable. Smith characterized Donalds as a common sense conservative who will defeat liberal candidates. The primary election is scheduled for August 18, with the Republican and Democratic nominees advancing to the November 3 general election. The Associated Industries of Florida will present the full poll findings at its Summer Political and Policy Retreat in Sarasota.

The initiative is part of ongoing efforts by police to address criminal activity in the region during the summer months. One recent case illustrates the types of incidents the operation targets.

A Port St. Lucie man was arrested following a road rage incident involving a pregnant driver. Robert Kniceley, 78, was driving a yellow Chevrolet pickup truck east on St. Lucie West Boulevard on a Tuesday afternoon when he began honking and driving aggressively. At the intersection of SW Peacock Boulevard and SW St. Lucie West Boulevard, he pulled alongside the victim's vehicle. When she rolled down her window, he allegedly pointed a handgun at her. The woman, who is in late pregnancy, became frightened for her safety and called 911.

Police responded and obtained video evidence the victim recorded of Kniceley leaving the area. Officers traced his license plate to his residence, where they recovered a loaded Smith Wesson .22 caliber handgun with a round in the chamber. During the investigation, Kniceley told authorities that the victim had cut him off on the roadway, causing him to become angry. He was charged with aggravated assault with a deadly weapon and transported to St. Lucie County Jail. A risk protection order seizure process was also initiated as part of the investigation.

Officer Brittany Holly with the Port St. Lucie Police Department emphasized the importance of avoiding escalation during traffic conflicts. "It's not worth the argument, it's not worth the fight," Holly said. "Disengage from that, allow room for that individual who clearly is upset or angry at whatever reason to get away, and you can safely get to a police station, a fire station." The message reflects law enforcement's broader approach to reducing violence during summer months when communities see increases in activity and tensions on roadways.
