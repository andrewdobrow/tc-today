A dispute over a missing fence at a Juno Beach public beach access point is now a budget question for the town, with potential costs running into thousands of dollars. The fence at the JB-7 public walkway was removed in 2023 for safety reasons, but residents at the adjacent Colony Condos say the removal has created significant problems. Without the barrier, only bushes now separate the public access path from private property, leading to what residents describe as regular trespassing during the summer months.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, explained that the lack of a clear boundary has resulted in beachgoers parking in condo spots and cutting through landscaping to access the beach with coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs. Hill said the problem intensifies during summer season and has led to confrontations between residents and trespassers that sometimes turn hostile. Rather than continuing to engage in these disputes, residents have begun calling police to handle the situation. Hill expressed hope that the town would reinstall the fence to restore the clear property line and protect the condo's private parking and landscaping.

Not all beachgoers agree there is a major problem. Michael House, who regularly walks to JB-7 from a nearby public parking lot, said he has never witnessed the trespassing issues residents describe. House noted there is plenty of free parking available in the area and suggested that property damage or littering would be more significant concerns than the current access situation.

When the matter came before the Juno Beach Town Council, officials noted the town has no obligation to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested the town might explore lower-cost alternatives rather than a full fence replacement. Halpern proposed a combination approach using split rail fencing and shrubs to help residents while controlling expenses, acknowledging that traditional fencing carries a significant price tag. The council agreed to address the fence question as part of its budget discussions scheduled for July 15. The decision on whether to reinstall a fence and which solution to pursue will ultimately depend on available funds and council priorities during the budget deliberations.

Misty Dawn White, 46, was found dead Monday after a fire broke out inside her home in Hobe Sound. Preliminary findings from the sheriff's office indicate an electrical issue may have caused the blaze. The fire remains under investigation as authorities work to determine the exact cause of the fatal incident.

White's fiancé Mark is now rebuilding his life after losing both his partner and many of his belongings in the flames. Friends learned of White's death when Mark called to inform them of the tragedy. In response to the loss, people who knew the couple have launched a fundraiser to help Mark recover from the financial devastation caused by the fire. "It's completely a state of devastation," said Nina Fenn, who had been White's friend for eight years and spoke about the impact of losing her longtime companion.

Fenn described White as a devoted Christian woman with a generous spirit who found joy in gardening, caring for plants, and playing piano. Those who knew her remembered her as someone who was always willing to help others and showed genuine love for the people around her, particularly children. The pain of White's death is intensified by the timing, as her birthday falls on the Fourth of July, meaning she passed away just days before turning another year older.

The tragedy is particularly difficult for Mark, who is grieving not only the loss of his fiancée but also carries the weight of previous heartbreak. Mark previously lost his only child, Jacob, in a tragic incident. Fenn emphasized her commitment to supporting Mark through this difficult period, acknowledging the compounding nature of his grief. "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts," Fenn said, reflecting on how White's memory will remain with those who loved her.

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned one of the nation's most prestigious civic honors, becoming the only Florida community selected as a 2026 All-America City Award winner. The National Civic League selected Riviera Beach as one of just 10 U.S. communities receiving the award this year. The recognition was announced at the National Civic League conference in Denver, where city leaders presented their community engagement initiatives to judges. Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the achievement, stating that "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." The award recognizes communities that effectively bring together residents, government agencies, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and create meaningful progress.

Lawson attributed the award to the city's long-term commitment to revitalization and improving quality of life for residents. "They're seeing the changes; they're seeing the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do," he said. City leaders emphasized that the recognition reflects extensive efforts to strengthen civic health, build trust between residents and government, and empower community members to shape the city's future. The achievement comes after years of focused work on these priorities.

Riviera Beach's application highlighted three key programs that demonstrate its commitment to civic participation and neighborhood investment. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy. The Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education initiative, achieved a 98% completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program provides mini-grants to longtime homeowners for critical repairs. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson emphasized that these efforts represent a broader partnership approach involving government, residents, faith organizations, schools and community groups. She stated that "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents. Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

Miller-Anderson noted that the award reinforces positive momentum and community pride in Riviera Beach's progress. City officials view the All-America City designation, presented annually since 1949, as validation that collaboration and resident involvement drive results in the community. The award signals to the nation that Riviera Beach is positioned for future growth and opportunities. Along with Riviera Beach, the 2026 honorees include Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football program is focused on winning a state championship as they prepare for the upcoming 2026 fall season. Last year, the team finished with a 10-2 record and won their district title, but their playoff run ended in disappointment when they lost in the second round. That early exit has motivated the program to aim higher this year.

Head coach Jimmy Robertson emphasized that the team has a strong foundation built on continuity. Most of the current seniors have been with the program for four years, and many came up through the middle school program. This veteran leadership has brought success throughout their high school careers, and Robertson believes this senior class has the potential to deliver the best season in program history. The coaching staff is confident that experienced players who understand how the program operates can push the team to new heights.

To prepare for deeper playoff runs, the Scots have intentionally scheduled more challenging opponents this fall. Senior Jack Williams explained that the team wanted to elevate their program by competing against top-tier teams. Playing stronger competition serves two purposes: it tests their abilities and helps attract talented players to the program. Senior Jack Whelche stressed that success this season will not be measured by regular season performance alone. He noted that an undefeated regular season means nothing if the team continues to lose in the second round of playoffs. The goal is simply to advance further than they did last year and make a real playoff push.

The players are using their summer training sessions as fuel for the upcoming season. Senior Edmyer St. Jean said the daily grind of workouts provides motivation and something to work toward. Each training session adds up as the team builds toward their goals. The Scots are using their preparation and hard work as a foundation to break their previous records and finish the season with positive results. With their experienced senior class, more demanding schedule, and clear focus on playoff success, Saint Andrew's believes they have the pieces in place to make a state title run this fall.

Secretary of State Cord Byrd Seeks to Block Paula Stark From House District 47 Ballot After Filing Failure

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking a Leon Circuit Court to keep St. Cloud Republican Paula Stark off the ballot after she failed to submit required paperwork before the June 12 qualifying deadline. Yesterday, 5:56 PM ET

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking a Leon Circuit Court to keep St. Cloud Republican Paula Stark off the ballot after she failed to submit required paperwork before the June 12 qualifying deadline. Stark filed her qualifying materials less than 30 minutes before the noon deadline, which state officials say prevented her from discovering a critical missing document. Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests required for all candidates, was never submitted by her treasurer and partner, Joel Davis.

Five state employees provided sworn written declarations stating that Davis never submitted Form 6 and was not accidentally handed it back when he submitted other paperwork during the final hour of the qualifying window. Election Records Division Bureau Chief Erica Ikay Reeve said in a court filing that three weeks after the deadline, Stark still has not submitted Form 6. According to Reeve, Stark was one of 12 candidates who failed to properly turn in the form, though half of them corrected the problems and qualified. Byrd argued that Stark and Davis knew Form 6 was required and could have discovered the missing document if they had not waited until the final hour to file their materials.

Reeve also documented a pattern of filing problems with Stark's campaign. The campaign has filed finance reports more than a month late, reported excessive contributions, and maintained an incorrect address on file. Audits in 2022 and 2024 found inconsistent transfers between campaign and office accounts. Additionally, a late report fine upheld by the Florida Elections Commission was referred to debt collection for nonpayment in November 2025. The state revoked Stark's Friends of Paula political committee in November due to over 16,000 dollars in unpaid fines from missed campaign finance deadlines.

If Stark remains off the ballot, the August Primary for House District 47 would be open to all voters to decide between Democrats Jorge Figueroa and Anthony Nieves. No other Republicans filed to run in the traditionally blue House District representing Osceola and Orange counties. Byrd emphasized the time constraints involved in the decision, noting that ballots must be mailed to overseas military and their families by July 4, with vote-by-mail ballots due out between July 9 and 16. The Secretary of State argued that reversing course would be costly and likely risk confusion in the results of this race and others on the ballot.
