Yesterday, 9:34 PM ET

Beachgoers at Jensen Beach Park are waiting for the rebuild of the park's restrooms and concession building, which was destroyed in a fire on New Year's Eve in December 2024. The site is currently an empty plot of land, leaving visitors with limited facilities during what is typically peak season for the popular beach destination.

Beachgoer Gonda Ghebert reached out to WPTV expressing frustration that visitors are left with only one very small restroom facility to serve the entire beach, which has become increasingly unreasonable during weekends, holidays, and peak tourist season. Regular visitors say they also miss the cafe that once provided convenient access to food and drinks. Julie Goings, who frequents the beach, said the previous facility offered nice, clean restrooms and showers, and it was convenient being able to get a snack or drink right off the deck. Sam Elliott noted that the only thing really missing is concessions and food availability.

Kevin Abbate, the Parks and Recreation director, says the permitting process takes time because the project sits on a protected dune and must go through the Department of Environmental Protection. That permit is currently in process. The design for the new 9,000 square foot Sea Turtle Beach Cafe is 80 percent complete. The building will be about 15 feet in elevation with 100 seats and 33 at the bar. Final funding is expected to be approved in September.

The county hopes to start construction in early 2027 with a completion goal of late 2027, just in time for the winter season. The county told WPTV they do not have any plans for additional temporary restrooms or facilities in the meantime. Abbate says the cafe will be worth the wait, explaining that the county wants to do it right and that designing it properly will result in fewer issues and a much smoother construction process. Beachgoers say they are anxiously awaiting construction, hoping it moves quickly and looking forward to having the bathrooms up and running along with another convenient spot for refreshments.

Fort Pierce overhauls development process after multiple waterfront lawsuits

Fort Pierce city commissioners voted unanimously Monday night to reject a settlement offer from Kevin Keene, who is suing the city over its handling of Little Jim's Bait & Tackle restaurant. Yesterday, 5:34 PM ET

Fort Pierce city commissioners voted unanimously Monday night to reject a settlement offer from Kevin Keene, who is suing the city over its handling of Little Jim's Bait & Tackle restaurant. The rejection means more taxpayer dollars will likely go toward defending the case. Keene told WPTV he will continue the litigation and fight. City Commissioner Michael Broderick said the settlement was not practical, particularly a provision requiring taxpayer votes on future waterfront land decisions. Broderick also said he feels the lawsuit does not have merit, though the city has an obligation to defend it.

The Little Jim's case is at least the fourth waterfront property dispute reported by WPTV and the second to result in a lawsuit. In 2023, King's Landing developer Dale Matteson threatened legal action after claiming the city sold him buildable land filled with old power plant concrete. In 2024, Fisherman's Wharf developer Chris Shelli filed suit after state records showed the city promised property it did not own. In 2025, the city abruptly canceled a bid process for Little Jim's after public outcry, prompting Keene's lawsuit and leaving developers Luigi and Drita Lucaj frustrated. This year, the city became responsible for nearly $200,000 in unpaid rent tied to Crabby's Waterfront Restaurant.

Broderick acknowledged controversies mentioned in the lawsuit, which allege a broader pattern of defective administration of city waterfront properties. When asked about allegations of mismanaging waterfront properties, Broderick said he would not say the properties are managed poorly but rather that they are not being managed at all, calling it an issue he takes very seriously. Broderick said fixing the problems will require a top down, bottom up approach to examine the entire portfolio and fix assets one at a time.

The city is creating a new real estate management position to track leases, rent payments, and waterfront properties with the goal of preventing future problems. Broderick said he will not rest until he gets to the bottom of the issue and categorizes all properties in spreadsheets. Both sides remain open to negotiating, with Keene saying he wants transparency rather than money and is willing to revise his offer. Broderick said he will consider any feasible settlement that keeps the case out of court.

Trump called FIFA president to get Balogun red card suspension lifted before U.S. World Cup match

Jul 5, 4:49 PM ET

President Donald Trump intervened on behalf of American star forward Folarin Balogun, whose red-card suspension was lifted in a decision that allows him to play in a World Cup match against Belgium on Monday. Balogun received a red card for stepping awkwardly on the right ankle of Tarik Muharemović of Bosnia-Herzegovina in a 2-0 round of 32 win on Wednesday, triggering an automatic one-game suspension. FIFA announced Sunday that the suspension had been lifted for the round of 16 match, an extraordinary move that appeared to be the first time since 1962 that a red card during a World Cup did not result in a suspension. Trump called FIFA president Gianni Infantino after the game asking FIFA review the red card, according to a person familiar with the call who spoke on condition of anonymity because the person was not authorized to speak publicly about the matter.

The Royal Belgian Football Association said it was astonished by the decision and is investigating all potential options to challenge it. Belgium coach Rudi Garcia mocked FIFA's action, saying he didn't know that in FIFA's offices the fifth of July was the first of April in Europe, making an April Fools' Day comparison. U.S. coach Mauricio Pochettino defended the decision, saying the team was punished enough against Bosnia-Herzegovina to play with 10 men for 30 minutes in a decision that was completely unfair. Balogun's three goals matched Landon Donovan in 2010 for the second-most goals by an American in a World Cup, behind only Bert Patenaude's four in 1930.

Leon County judge sets July hearing on lawsuit challenging Fishback's eligibility in Florida governor's race

Yesterday, 4:40 PM ET

A Leon County judge set aside two days later this month to decide whether Republican gubernatorial candidate James Fishback meets Florida's seven-year residency requirement to run for governor. Judge David Frank scheduled the matter for July 21 and 22 during a Tuesday scheduling hearing, warning attorneys there is an extreme shortage of time with the Aug. 18 primary approaching. The lawsuit was filed by Lt. Jay Collins, one of Fishback's rivals in the GOP primary, who argues Fishback does not meet the state's residency requirement. Collins' complaint points to records tied to Fishback's voter registration and residency in Washington, D.C.

Frank noted it is likely already too late to stop ballots from being printed. He explained that if Fishback is disqualified, there would be an injunction to the Department of State directing supervisors of elections not to count votes for Fishback and to post a notice. Fishback's attorney, former state representative Anthony Sabatini, told the judge the case turns on more than paperwork, arguing Fishback's intent will be central to determining residency. Sabatini said the defense wants time to present witnesses and documents. Witness and exhibit lists are due five days before the July 21 hearing.

The courtroom fight comes just days after the issue spilled onto the debate stage. During last week's GOP primary debate, Fishback, Collins and former House Speaker Paul Renner appeared together. Frontrunner Byron Donalds skipped the event but still loomed large over the debate, with his rivals repeatedly invoking him and his position in the race. Fishback pressed Collins directly over the lawsuit, getting the lieutenant governor to note his Florida roots. Fishback said during the debate, "Case closed. Drop the lawsuit." He later accused Collins of trying to steal the election with Byron Donalds. Collins pushed back, saying the challenge belongs in court. "That is a matter for the court," Collins said. "I actually respect the judicial system."

For now, Fishback remains on the ballot. The case adds uncertainty to the primary calendar at a time when election officials are already preparing ballots and candidates are running out of time to shift the race. Even if Fishback is removed, the broader GOP contest may still revolve around Donalds, who has maintained a commanding position in the primary through polling, fundraising and endorsements. Axios reported Tuesday that Donalds continues to tower over the field as the Aug. 18 primary approaches.

Andre Leonard Barnes, 66, of Fort Pierce died Tuesday after being shot at a home in the 2700 block of Essex Drive, according to the St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office. Deputies responded around 3:30 p.m. after receiving reports of a person with a gunshot wound.

When deputies arrived, they learned an adult man had shot Barnes with a rifle. Deputies took the suspect into custody and began providing emergency medical care to Barnes until St. Lucie County Fire District personnel arrived. Barnes was then taken to a local hospital, where he died.

Detectives with the sheriff's office's Criminal Investigations Division are continuing to process evidence and conduct interviews to determine the circumstances surrounding the incident. The suspect remains in custody.

Investigators say there is no threat to the public. The case remains under investigation.
