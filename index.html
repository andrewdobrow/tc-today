5:36 AM ET

A WPTV story about Westlake's Housing Assistance Purchase Program Yearly sparked more than 200 inquiries after airing in April, according to Taylor Garcia, the city's housing assistance program manager. Of those inquiries, 40 people submitted applications and 22 families were awarded assistance. The surge in interest prompted city leaders to expand the program to help even more families achieve homeownership.

On June 2, Westlake City Council approved major updates to the program. Buyers can now purchase a home up to $679,324, an increase from the previous limit of $568,577, to keep up with today's market. Instead of offering fixed amounts, the city now evaluates each family's financial situation to provide exactly what they need. The funds come as a 0% interest, forgivable loan that can be used for down payments, closing costs or buying down interest rates.

The program targets first-time homebuyers or those who haven't owned a home in three years. Applicants must be U.S. citizens, meet income limits, and buy a newly built home in Westlake under $679,324. Essential workers like police, firefighters, teachers and nurses can earn up to $300,000 and still qualify. Current Westlake residents and past program participants receive special consideration, and families can combine HAPPY money with other assistance programs to maximize their help.

The city is developing an online portal to streamline the application process. "A lot of families think homeownership is out of reach, but this program makes it possible," Garcia said. The expanded program reflects Westlake's commitment to helping families navigate the challenging housing market and establish roots in the community.

Yesterday, 2:48 PM ET

The Peacock House Art Foundation is transforming Fort Pierce into an artistic destination, putting the Peacock Arts District on the map and connecting local and international artists. Operating from the historic Crain Estate, the non-profit organization preserves the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, who once called the property home. Jaxson Crain was a tile worker by trade, and his craftsmanship is visible throughout the entire facility. "Jackson is the one who did all the tiling throughout the facility, and you'll see it from the ground to the walls to the ceiling," says Krystal Masters, the art director. The estate remains largely unchanged, staying 90% the way it was when the Crains lived there.

Visitors can take guided tours of the lush gardens and vibrant architecture every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. Many parts of the house look the same as when the Crains lived there, including the smoking and bourbon room with man paneling, manly colored bathroom, and stained glass that leads out to the bar on the front patio. The couple traveled the world nine times, collecting one-of-a-kind items for their Florida estate, including a gazebo shipped from India that features tiny peacocks. The foundation has made a conscious effort to preserve the estate's original character without commercializing it.

The Peacock House gets its name from the birds that have roamed the property for decades. The Crains are believed to have introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, originally bringing in seven birds that have since grown to over 100. Among the flock is Bob, believed to be 60 years old and recognizable because he walks with a limp. "Bob usually typically lives in here. He is a very spoiled peacock as you can see he has his own bowls we hand feed him everyday," says Masters. Tour guides say peacocks feeding from children's hands create unforgettable moments. The Crains also once kept monkeys on the property after Jaxson Crain traveled to South America and returned with a baby monkey in his pocket.

The foundation's artist-in-residency program attracts artists from around the world, offering simple but elegant accommodations on the property. The art lab welcomes visitors of all skill levels with classes for children and adults. "The majority of my classes are one on one classes you come in with zero experience and you walk out with a piece of art and it makes you feel good," Krystal said. The lab features various mediums and projects, including sculpting and watercolor workshops like paint your pet in watercolor and rainbow. "Art is a form of therapy and it distresses people………leave happy and haven't been on their phone scrolling," Krystal said.
