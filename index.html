Mayor Sara Baxter has reentered the race for Palm Beach County Commission District 6 after deciding against running for Congress. She submitted her candidacy before the noon qualifying deadline with backing from President Donald Trump, who reportedly asked her to stay focused on county-level representation. Her decision to reverse course and rejoin the district race has prompted criticism from her opponents, who argue that voters deserve candidates with undivided attention to local concerns.

The other candidates running for the seat have questioned whether Baxter's commitment to the district is genuine. Republican Elizabeth Accomondo expressed skepticism about the timing and reasoning behind Baxter's reversal, calling it nonsensical. Democratic candidate Rudolph Tinker suggested that Baxter's credibility as a Republican candidate has suffered as a result of her flip-flopping on the congressional race. Both candidates emphasized that the election should focus on substantive issues affecting District 6 rather than endorsements from national figures. Accomondo stated that taking care of the community's true needs must come first, while Tinker stressed that his candidacy centers on serving the people rather than political maneuvering.

Fellow Democratic candidates Katherine Waldron and Mario Guzman similarly voiced the importance of focused leadership on local matters. Waldron outlined her priorities as tackling traffic congestion, protecting neighborhoods, ensuring quality education, and managing responsible growth in the district. She indicated her intention to continue presenting her platform directly to voters. Guzman called for a candidate committed to public needs who would bring stability to the county commission and move away from what he characterized as a pattern of negativity in local politics. Both candidates believe voters should ultimately decide who best represents them based on their vision for the district.

Interviews with voters and conversations among all candidates reveal that development, traffic management, and the expansion of artificial intelligence data centers are the primary concerns facing District 6. These issues have emerged as central to the campaign discussion. Baxter responded to her opponents' criticism by asserting that the election should be about people rather than politics. The primary election is scheduled for August 18, with the general election following in November. Voters will have the opportunity to choose among the field of candidates competing for the commission seat during these two electoral contests.

Yesterday, 5:18 PM ET

On August 12, 2025, three people traveling for work in St. Lucie County died when their minivan became wedged beneath a semi-truck. The crash occurred when the truck driver allegedly made an illegal U-turn in a clearly marked no U-turn zone. The victims were Herby Dufresne, 30, Rodrigue Dor, 54, and Faniola Joseph, 37. Joseph had been living in Indiana and traveled south to purchase goods for her shop when the tragedy struck.

Angeline Dowdian, Joseph's 22-year-old daughter, has filed a wrongful death lawsuit against truck driver Harjinder Singh, White Hawk Carriers, and freight broker C.H. Robinson. The lawsuit alleges negligence and unsafe practices led to the crash. At the time of the accident, Angeline was living abroad and had not seen her mother in person for six years, though they spoke daily through video calls. She missed a call from her mother that day, and the last message she received was a voice recording asking where she was. Angeline says she listens to that message every morning. She described her mother as both parents to her and expressed the emotional weight of losing her only family support system. Visa restrictions have prevented her from visiting her mother's grave.

Angeline is represented by attorney Dax Bello of Stuart Tillman, Fox, Bianchi & Kane, along with co-counsel Mark Brumer. Bello argues that the crash was not accidental but a predictable outcome of negligent business practices. The lawsuit contends that C.H. Robinson, a multibillion-dollar freight broker, continued giving business to White Hawk Carriers despite the company's serious safety violations. A transportation expert described White Hawk's violations as "very egregious." The case is strengthened by a May 14 U.S. Supreme Court ruling in Montgomery v. Caribbean Transport, which determined that federal law does not protect freight brokers from state negligence claims when they place unsafe drivers on the road. Justice Brett Kavanaugh's concurring opinion stated that while not all truck accidents can be prevented, some carriers are known to be less safe, and brokers have responsibility to vet driver records before hiring.

White Hawk Carriers has shut down, though Bello warns the company may reopen under a different name, a common practice in the industry. Singh faces criminal traffic homicide charges in Florida. Bello, a former homicide prosecutor, is helping Angeline navigate both the civil and criminal proceedings under Marsy's Law, which provides victims' families with rights to be heard and informed in criminal cases. Though no legal victory can ease her pain, Angeline hopes the lawsuit prevents other families from experiencing similar loss. She expressed that her mother worked her entire life to provide Angeline with better opportunities and died in a tragic manner before witnessing her daughter's future or meeting her grandchildren.

Yesterday, 1:07 PM ET

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium is inviting visitors to explore Florida's coastal habitats, offering rare close-up encounters with marine life in Fort Pierce's waterfront district. Manager Bill Hoffman says the facility's philosophy of displaying fully live, complex models of ecosystems dates back to 1980 under the Smithsonian, making it unlike most public aquariums. "We feel like we're the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast, and we don't want to be," Hoffman said, noting that more species are housed here than in many larger exhibits.

The highlight is the Atlantic coral reef exhibit, a sprawling tank that recreates one of Florida's most significant coastal ecosystems. Visitors will also see live models of seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities, each demonstrating the intricate relationships between organisms. The exhibit's touch tank allows guests to handle unusual marine species under staff guidance, according to Chelsie Corcoran, exhibit educator. "You'll likely touch animals you've never seen before," she said.

The facility operates in partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station, aiming to raise public awareness about threats facing oceans while inspiring conservation. Hoffman emphasized that stable ecosystems depend on myriad small organisms. The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit is located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce and is open to the public year-round.

U.S. Representative Byron Donalds introduced the Tax Dollar Accountability Act in Congress this week to increase federal oversight of how state and local governments spend federal funds. The legislation would require subsidiary governments to grant the Comptroller of the Currency complete access to their financial records. State and local agencies receiving federal money would need to provide detailed budgets, expenditure reports, vendor contracts, and grant distributions annually to demonstrate they are using appropriated funds in compliance with federal requirements.

Donalds brought forward the bill in response to documented cases of misallocated Medicaid and COVID-19 funding across multiple states. During a Fox News appearance on Saturday, he noted that $200 billion in misallocated payments have already been identified. He argued that any local or state agency receiving federal dollars should make those financial documents available for federal audit at any time. According to Donalds, "I believe local jurisdictions have a responsibility that if you're going to get taxpayer dollars, then your books need to be open and subject to audit by the federal government."

Florida is independently working to combat fraud in its programs. Governor Ron DeSantis announced a Medicaid integrity initiative on Friday designed to strengthen provider screening, use technology to identify fraud, control enrollment for high-risk provider categories, and revalidate existing Medicaid providers. Donalds pledged his support for continuing DeSantis's approach if elected to higher office.

The Naples Congressman vowed to expand these accountability efforts statewide. He stated that his administration would conduct thorough audits of all local governments and state agencies without exception. "We will DOGE all local governments and all state agencies. There will be no sacred cows because the taxpayers deserve to make sure that their dollars are being spent efficiently and transparently," Donalds said. He committed to maintaining these standards of financial oversight and transparency across Florida if he advances in political office.

Florida homestead exemption increase could strip $11.86 billion annually from local governments, state economists warn

State economists revealed Friday that a constitutional amendment increasing homestead exemptions could cost Florida's 67 counties $11.86 billion per year if vot.

2:10 PM ET

State economists revealed Friday that a constitutional amendment increasing homestead exemptions could cost Florida's 67 counties $11.86 billion per year once fully implemented. The Revenue Estimating Conference analysis of HJR 1-F showed the fiscal impact would escalate over time, with local governments losing nearly $5 billion in revenue during fiscal year 2027-28, increasing to $8.775 billion in 2028-29, $9.7 billion in 2029-30, and $10.751 billion in 2030-31. The amendment would raise homestead exemptions to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, with further increases tied to the Consumer Price Index. Under the measure, local governments would be restricted to funding public safety, infrastructure, schools, debt service, pensions, and constitutional officers including Elections Supervisors, Clerks of Court, Property Appraisers, and County and City Commissions.

Commuter counties face the steepest proportional losses. St. Lucie County could lose 35 percent of its revenue, while Clay, Baker, Citrus, and Hernando Counties could face losses exceeding 30 percent. Large urban counties would sustain the biggest dollar losses: Miami-Dade would face a $445 million revenue decrease by fiscal year 2028-29, Hillsborough could lose $353 million, Broward roughly $326 million, Duval $277 million, and Orange $253 million. Governor Ron DeSantis argued local governments can absorb the impact, noting local property tax revenue nearly doubled from roughly $32 billion in 2019 to about $60 billion today. School taxes would not be subject to the increased exemption limit and would remain at $25,000.

Indian River County faces $50 million budget loss from property tax amendment on November ballot

Indian River County could lose around $50 million from its $597 million budget if voters approve a property tax amendment on the November ballot, county leaders.

Yesterday, 4:40 PM ET

Indian River County leaders gathered Friday at the Heritage Center in Vero Beach to warn the public about a property tax amendment on the November ballot. The amendment would raise the homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. According to the Tax Payers Association, the county's $597 million budget could lose around $50 million in funding by 2028. Laura Moss, vice chair of the Indian River County Commission, said the state is dictating how local governments fund operations. Lance Lunceford, president of the Indian River County Tax Payers Association, said there is no way to fill a $50 million hole without reducing law enforcement and first responder services.

Christen Brewer, president of Indian River Firefighters, said fire rescue could lose around $15 to $20 million of their $70 million budget, threatening three projected fire stations. About 79.1% of fire rescue funding comes from ad valorem taxes. Lunceford described the amendment as a tax shift rather than a tax cut, saying the county would likely raise taxes on non-homesteaded and commercial properties to compensate. He warned that increasing taxes on businesses increases costs for everyone through higher prices for goods and services.
