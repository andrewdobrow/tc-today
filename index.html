Yesterday, 5:56 PM ET

For the first time in its history, Boys & Girls Clubs of St. Lucie County has produced a Southeast Region Youth of the Year. Enrique Rosario Liz, the valedictorian of Treasure Coast High School, earned this prestigious recognition and will now compete for the national title in New York City on October 1. The Southeast Region Youth of the Year award honors standout teenagers across the Boys & Girls Clubs community who demonstrate dedication to academics, strong personal values, and leadership through service to others.

Enrique's path to this achievement began eight years ago when he arrived in Port St. Lucie from the Dominican Republic without English language skills. With support from his school and the local Boys & Girls Club, he took advantage of every opportunity to learn and develop. "I could never have imagined this moment when I was 8 years old," Enrique said after receiving the award. "It's a privilege to share my journey, and I hope it inspires belief in every young person who needs a helping hand and an opportunity." His determination and growth have been remarkable. He has earned the titles of county Youth of the Year and Florida Youth of the Year. This fall, he will begin his studies at the University of Florida.

Will Armstead, CEO of Boys & Girls Clubs of St. Lucie County, highlighted what makes Enrique exceptional. "Enrique's achievement is historic, but his character impresses me most. He's turned challenges into opportunities and advocated for others, all with humility and determination," Armstead said. Travis Leonard, owner of A&G Concrete Pools and a longtime Youth of the Year judge, observed that Enrique distinguished himself from his peers throughout the competition process. "From the start, Enrique stood out. Thanks to his hard work and all those who support him, we finally have a Southeast Region Youth of the Year. I believe he's ready for the national competition, and I'm proud of him and our whole community," Leonard said.

Enrique's parents expressed their pride in their son's accomplishments and their gratitude for the support he has received. "We're grateful to God for every opportunity and to Boys & Girls Clubs of St. Lucie County for helping Enrique grow as a leader and a person," they said. The Youth of the Year program represents Boys & Girls Clubs of America's premier recognition for teenagers who function as role models, leaders, and change-makers within their communities.

Yesterday, 5:18 PM ET

On August 12, 2025, three workers traveling in St. Lucie County were killed when their minivan became wedged beneath a semi-truck. Herby Dufresne, 30, Rodrigue Dor, 54, and Faniola Joseph, 37, died in the crash after the truck driver allegedly made an illegal U-turn in a clearly marked no U-turn zone. The truck driver, Harjinder Singh, worked for White Hawk Carriers, a company with a documented history of safety violations. Joseph's daughter, Angeline Dowdian, 22, has filed a wrongful death lawsuit against Singh, White Hawk Carriers, and freight broker C.H. Robinson, claiming negligence and unsafe practices caused the tragedy.

Angeline was living in another country at the time of the crash and had not seen her mother in person for six years, though they communicated daily through video calls. She was represented by attorney Dax Bello of Stuart Tillman, Fox, Bianchi & Kane and co-counsel Mark Brumer of Brumer & Brumer. Bello argues that this crash was not an accident but a predictable outcome of negligent hiring practices. He contends that C.H. Robinson, a multibillion-dollar freight broker, knowingly continued business with White Hawk Carriers despite its serious safety violations. Transportation expert James Lewis has described White Hawk's violations as very egregious. Bello points to a May 14 Supreme Court ruling in Montgomery v. Caribbean Transport, which clarified that federal law does not protect freight brokers from state negligence claims when they place unsafe drivers on roadways. That ruling involved the same broker named in Dowdian's case.

Bello emphasized that brokers and carriers have a responsibility to verify safety records before assigning drivers to routes, noting that such information is publicly available online. He stressed that industry incentives should naturally favor safe operations. White Hawk Carriers has since shut down, though Bello warned the company may reopen under a different name, a common practice in the trucking industry. In addition to the civil case, Singh faces criminal traffic homicide charges in Florida. As a former homicide prosecutor, Bello is helping Angeline navigate both the civil and criminal proceedings under Marsy's Law, which grants victims' families rights to be informed and heard in criminal matters.

Angeline acknowledged that no legal outcome will restore what she lost. Her mother, who had hoped Angeline would become a nurse, was her primary support system after her father rejected her at birth. Visa restrictions have prevented Angeline from visiting her mother's grave. She expressed hope that the lawsuit will prevent other families from experiencing similar loss, saying she does not want others to suffer as she has. Joseph would have turned 38 on May 26, 2026.

Yesterday, 1:07 PM ET

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium in Fort Pierce welcomes visitors to discover Florida's coastal habitats through immersive encounters with marine life. Located at 420 Seaway Drive, the facility operates year-round and has maintained a distinctive approach to ocean education since 1980. Manager Bill Hoffman describes the aquarium's commitment to displaying fully functional, complex ecosystem models. "We feel like we're the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast, and we don't want to be," Hoffman said. The facility houses more species than many larger aquariums, offering visitors a unique educational experience.

The centerpiece of the exhibit is the Atlantic coral reef tank, a large display that recreates one of Florida's most important coastal ecosystems. Beyond the coral reef, visitors encounter live exhibits representing seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities. These displays demonstrate how different marine organisms interact and depend on one another. The exhibits even include smaller creatures, such as a four-inch barracuda, highlighting the diversity found within these ecosystems. According to Hoffman, understanding the importance of small organisms is essential to appreciating how stable ecosystems function.

A popular attraction within the exhibit is the touch tank, where guests can handle unusual marine species under the supervision of trained staff. Exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran notes that visitors often encounter animals they have never encountered before in this hands-on environment. This interactive component allows guests of all ages to engage directly with marine life and develop a deeper connection to ocean conservation.

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit operates in partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station to educate the public about ocean threats while promoting conservation efforts. As concerns about ocean health grow, exhibits like this one serve an important role in connecting visitors to the fragile ecosystems of Florida's coastal waters. The facility is celebrating its 25th anniversary later this year with special programming. For more information about the aquarium and its exhibits, visitors can check sms.si.edu and the Smithsonian Marine Station's social media channels.

6:00 AM ET

Florida will gain approximately 2.3 million residents between 2026 and 2035 while continuing to outpace national economic growth, according to a new economic outlook from Florida TaxWatch. The state's population is expected to grow from 23.8 million in 2026 to just over 26 million by 2035. Despite these gains, daily net migration will gradually decline from about 895 people per day to 689. Florida's appeal remains strong thanks to its business climate, absence of a personal income tax, and favorable weather, allowing the state to attract new residents even as economic headwinds persist.

The latest TaxWatch report reflects significant downgrades compared to previous forecasts. The organization cited global instability, rising energy costs, and uncertainty regarding the Strait of Hormuz as primary concerns. The statewide unemployment rate is projected to peak at 4.8% in 2027 before gradually settling near 4.2% in the early 2030s. Per-capita personal income is still expected to increase from roughly $88,000 in 2026 to more than $126,000 by 2035, but overall income projections have declined by an average of 10.3% from the prior forecast. Total employment will grow from 10.1 million workers to 11.3 million over the decade.

Tourism experienced the steepest downgrade of any sector, with visitor growth expectations falling by an average of 45.3% since the previous report. Visitor numbers are still projected to increase annually, but growth rates will slow dramatically, reaching just 0.8% growth next year. This represents the most significant impact from current economic uncertainties on Florida's major industries.

Florida's broader economy is expected to weather these challenges without major disruption. Real GDP growth will decelerate from 2.5% in 2026 to 1.9% in 2027, then rebound above 3% for much of the following decade, which actually represents an improvement compared to TaxWatch's previous forecast. According to the report, the single biggest variable shaping the outlook is uncertainty surrounding the Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately a quarter of the world's seaborne oil passes. Should energy markets stabilize, many of the more pessimistic assumptions in the forecast could improve. If not, Florida residents may face a more challenging economic path ahead.

Indian River County faces $50 million budget loss from property tax amendment on November ballot

Indian River County could lose around $50 million from its $597 million budget if voters approve a property tax amendment on the November ballot, according to p.

Yesterday, 4:40 PM ET

Indian River County leaders gathered Friday morning at the Heritage Center in Vero Beach to discuss the potential financial impact of a property tax amendment on this November's ballot. The amendment would raise the homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. According to the Indian River County Tax Payers Association, the county's approximately $597 million budget could lose around $50 million by 2028. Vice Chair Laura Moss criticized the measure as unsound public policy, saying "They've declared war on local government." Three projected fire stations may not be built if the budget shortfall occurs. Lunceford characterized the amendment as a tax shift rather than a tax cut, potentially requiring the county to increase impact fees, utility fees, and taxes on non-homesteaded and commercial properties to compensate for lost revenue.

Governor DeSantis announces Medicaid fraud crackdown in West Palm Beach targeting fake providers

More than a dozen entities presenting themselves as Medicaid providers in West Palm Beach were actually staged operations created solely to steal from the state program, officials revealed.

Yesterday, 9:42 AM ET

Governor Ron DeSantis announced sweeping Medicaid fraud prevention measures Friday afternoon in West Palm Beach, targeting sophisticated criminal operations that have cost Florida taxpayers millions of dollars. Speaking at the Health Care District of Palm Beach County, DeSantis said the state is partnering with President Donald Trump's administration and federal agencies to combat Medicaid fraud, which he identified as particularly concentrated in southeast Florida, including Miami-Dade and Broward counties.

Agency for Healthcare Administration Secretary Shevaun L. Harris revealed that more than a dozen entities presenting themselves as Medicaid providers in West Palm Beach were staged operations created solely to steal from the state program. Investigators discovered the fake operations through shared addresses across multiple unrelated entities, suspicious ownership structures, and no evidence of actual patient care. The state announced three major initiatives: a pilot program with identity fraud detection company Ventolin, enrollment moratoriums on high-risk provider categories including durable medical equipment suppliers and adult day care providers, and comprehensive revalidation requiring all active Florida Medicaid providers to verify credentials and identity. Harris said Florida's program integrity efforts have already saved $136.45 million in recovery and prevention benefits, and over the past two years AHCA has terminated or denied enrollment to more than 3,200 providers. Florida's Medicaid program covers nearly 4 million recipients statewide.

A Palm Beach County deputy pulled over Katie Thomas of Lake Worth for allegedly holding her phone in her right hand while driving. The traffic stop went viral on social media, generating 110 million views. Body camera footage shows the deputy telling Thomas what he observed, saying "You drove past me, holding the phone with your right hand, manipulating that phone." Thomas responded by raising her arm to show her stump. She was born without her right hand and has spent her life as an adaptive athlete, raising awareness for the limb-difference community. "Literally being accused of something that I did not have a limb to be accused. The citation was dropped for lack of evidence. The Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office said an agency review of the case is underway. Thomas said she harbors no ill will against the sheriff's office and would be open to a conversation about using the moment to spread awareness about the limb difference community. She started selling "Hand to God" merchandise on her Instagram, with proceeds benefiting Shriners Children's Hospital. Thomas said she has no plans to sue and hopes the attention helps people better understand the limb difference community.
