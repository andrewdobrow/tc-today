The elevated system would connect President Donald J. Trump International Airport to the convention center and nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations using small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers alo...

Jul 2, 5:49 PM ET

Glydways, a California-based autonomous transit company, will present its proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for initial consideration. The elevated system would connect President Donald J. Trump International Airport to the convention center and nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations using small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. The system would bypass traffic congestion, potentially cutting the trip between downtown and the airport to less than 10 minutes, with estimated travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center.

Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system while offering comparable passenger capacity, referencing previous South Florida light rail estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile. The company described it as functioning like a "horizontal elevator," carrying passengers directly between destinations without intermediate stops. Potential funding sources include Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. Tuesday's County Commission meeting will determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal. If commissioners move forward, the county could seek competing bids and begin discussions on routes, station locations, costs and financing, with a planning and design contract potentially awarded later this year.

Riviera Beach has been named one of just 10 communities in the U.S. to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the only Florida city selected for the honor this year. The National Civic League announced the award during its conference in Denver, where Riviera Beach leaders presented the city's efforts to strengthen community engagement. Mayor Douglas Lawson called the recognition a proud moment for the city, saying residents are seeing "the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do."

Riviera Beach's application highlighted several programs designed to increase civic participation and neighborhood investment. These include the Riviera Beach Youth Council, which helps high school students become involved in civic leadership and advocacy; the Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education initiative with a 98% completion rate; and the Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program, which provides mini-grants to help longtime homeowners make critical repairs. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for the honor, saying the award is a tribute to the heart and determination of the community. City leaders said the designation positions the community for future opportunities and sends a message to the nation that Riviera Beach is a place where collaboration drives results.

Yesterday, 3:05 PM ET

The Martin County School Board is resisting a judge's recommendation to reinstate a teacher who was suspended over social media comments about conservative activist Charlie Kirk. Matt Theobald, a social studies teacher at Spectrum Academy since 2009, faced discipline after posting remarks on Facebook that characterized Kirk as racist, misogynistic, and a neo-Nazi.

In court filings submitted Thursday, the school board's legal team argued that Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone made errors in recommending Theobald's reinstatement. The board contended that numerous conclusions in the judge's decision lack adequate factual support. The school board's attorney maintains that the Facebook post about Kirk constituted a serious violation that justified removing the teacher from his position. The board emphasized that the offense warranted the disciplinary action taken against Theobald.

The judge's recommendation relied on a recent ruling from the Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal establishing that disciplining teachers for personal Facebook posts created away from school, using personal devices, and without student involvement violates First Amendment protections. In her order from last month, Livingstone recommended not only that Theobald be rehired but also that he receive back pay and benefits for the period of his suspension.

According to an investigation report reviewed by WPTV, Theobald acknowledged writing the comments about Kirk but explained the posts appeared on his mother's private Facebook page as part of a conversation among people he knew personally. He stated the comments were not intended to reach a wider public audience. Following the judge's recommendation, Theobald raised broader concerns about where schools should establish boundaries regarding teachers' speech outside the workplace. The school board retains ultimate authority to decide whether to reinstate Theobald, and no timeline has been announced for when the board will make its final determination. The case highlights ongoing tensions between protecting educators' personal freedoms and maintaining school district standards for employee conduct.

Serena Williams withdrew from her doubles match with sister Venus at Wimbledon on Saturday due to a right knee injury sustained during her singles return earlier in the week. The 44-year-old lost her singles match 6-3, 6-7 (6), 6-3 to 20-year-old Maya Joint of Australia on Tuesday. In an Instagram post, Serena said she was heartbroken to withdraw from doubles, stating that the opportunity to play alongside Venus meant the world to her. "I did everything I could but unfortunately my knee just isn't ready to compete," she wrote.

Serena posted images of four syringes showing the fluid drained from her knee after her singles match, along with a video of her walking with strapping on her right leg and what appeared to be one of her daughters holding a cane for her. The Williams sisters were scheduled to play Camila Osorio and Solana Sierra in a first-round doubles match that had been pushed back to give Serena more recovery time. Despite the withdrawal, Serena hinted at future competition, saying "stay tuned to a city near you" with the U.S. Open approaching. Venus, 46, played in mixed doubles on Friday with Kevin Krawietz but lost in straight sets. The Williams sisters are six-time women's doubles champions at Wimbledon.

Jun 30, 11:20 AM ET

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. According to the center, these programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection is waiving admission fees at Florida State Parks from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. All residents and visitors can explore the parks for free during this weekend, with opportunities for hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling and horseback riding. The parks also feature historic sites including forts, battlefields, archaeological sites and other historic landmarks.

Three parks are excluded from the free admission promotion: Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. Free admission applies to day use only, and all other fees remain in effect, including overnight accommodations, special events, concessions and rentals. The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the National Recreation and Park Association's National Gold Medal Award. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks saw more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated 3.6 billion dollars in statewide economic impact. For park locations and details, visit FloridaStateParks.org or America250FL.com.

The Peacock House Art Foundation is establishing Fort Pierce as an artistic destination by maintaining the historic Crain Estate and supporting the Peacock Arts District. The nonprofit preserves the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, who originally owned the property. Jaxson Crain worked as a tile worker, and his skilled craftsmanship remains visible throughout the facility in the flooring, walls, and ceilings. Guided tours of the gardens and distinctive architecture are available on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays. The foundation has deliberately kept the estate largely unchanged from the Crains' era, maintaining approximately 90 percent of the property in its original condition. This commitment reflects a conscious decision to preserve the estate's authentic character without pursuing commercialization. The smoking and bourbon room exemplifies this preservation approach, featuring distinctive man paneling, masculine color schemes, and stained glass windows that open onto a front patio bar.

The Crains were world travelers who journeyed globally nine times, acquiring unique items for their Florida home. Among their international purchases was a gazebo shipped from India, which features decorative peacocks in its design. The foundation is currently restoring the gazebo's base to match its original painted pattern. The estate's name derives from the peacocks inhabiting the property for decades. The Crains introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce during the 1970s, beginning with seven birds that have since multiplied to over 100. One particularly notable peacock named Bob, believed to be 60 years old, is recognizable by his distinctive limp. Bob resides at the facility in his own designated space with personal bowls and receives hand-feeding from tour guides daily. Staff members note that peacock interactions create memorable experiences, particularly for young visitors who feed the birds from their hands. The estate also has historical connections to monkeys, as one story recounts that Jaxson Crain brought a baby monkey back from South America in his pocket.

The artist-in-residency program draws creative professionals from around the world to work at the foundation. Participating artists receive simple yet elegant accommodations on the property that provide everything necessary for their stay. The foundation's art lab offers classes for both children and adults regardless of experience level. Art director Krystal Masters conducts one-on-one instruction designed for complete beginners, allowing students to develop skills in various mediums including sculpting and watercolor. Classes range from specialized workshops such as "Paint Your Pet in Watercolor and Rainbow" to personalized projects matched to individual interests.

Masters emphasizes that art serves therapeutic purposes beyond creative expression. She describes art as a stress-relieving activity that leaves participants feeling fulfilled and engaged. Students complete classes with finished artwork that generates genuine satisfaction and connection. This philosophy guides the foundation's educational mission, ensuring that visitors to the Peacock House leave having experienced both artistic growth and personal renewal.
