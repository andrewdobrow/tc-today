Residents at the Colony Condos are seeking town help to reinstall a fence at the JB-7 public beach access point, saying beachgoers have been trespassing on their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. The barrier that once clearly marked the boundary between public walkway and private land has been replaced only by shrubs, leaving condo owners frustrated with increased foot traffic and vehicle parking on their property during the busy summer months.

Lori Hill, a resident at the condominiums, described how the absence of the fence has led to regular incidents of people cutting through private land with coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs to access the beach. Hill said these trespassers also park in spots reserved for residents. The situation has escalated to the point where residents no longer confront people directly about being on private property, instead calling police to handle the encounters. Hill said the problem is particularly severe during summer season when beach traffic increases significantly.

Not all observers see the fence removal as problematic. Michael House, a beachgoer who walks to JB-7 from a public parking lot nearby, said he has not witnessed the trespassing and vehicle issues residents describe. He noted that ample free parking is available in the area and questioned whether property damage or littering represents the real concern rather than people crossing the boundary.

When the matter came before the Juno Beach Town Council, officials clarified that the town has no legal obligation to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring more affordable alternatives, proposing that the town might help fund a lower-cost fence option or combine split rail fencing with additional shrubs to create a clearer boundary. The council decided to address the fence question during its budget discussions scheduled for July 15. Hill expressed hope that town officials will take action to restore the fence, allowing residents to reclaim their landscaping areas and parking spaces.

White's fiancé, Mark, survived the incident, but the flames destroyed much of their home and belongings.

Nina Fenn, who had known White for eight years, spoke about the loss and visited the scene in the days following the fire to process her grief. The timing of White's death compounds the tragedy for those close to her. Her birthday was July 4th, meaning she passed away just days before turning a year older. The loss is especially difficult for Mark, who has already endured significant personal tragedy. "Mark lost his one and only child, Jacob, and lost him tragically," Fenn said. "So, I just am trying whatever I can to support him." As the community supports Mark through this devastating time, White's loved ones remember her for the kindness and joy she brought to their lives.

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned a major national recognition as one of only 10 communities selected nationwide for the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city stands as the sole Florida recipient of this honor. The award was presented during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where city leaders showcased their efforts to build stronger community engagement. Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the achievement, stating that "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." Lawson attributed the award to years of work aimed at revitalizing the city and improving residents' quality of life.

The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, recognizes communities that unite residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address challenges and create meaningful change. City leaders emphasized that the recognition reflects a long-term commitment to improving civic health, rebuilding trust between residents and government, and enabling the community to directly shape the city's future. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for the accomplishment, saying "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents. Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

Riviera Beach's application highlighted three key programs that demonstrate civic participation and community investment. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy work. The Citizens Leadership Academy provides civic education with a 98% completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program offers mini-grants to longtime homeowners for necessary repairs. Miller-Anderson noted that these initiatives represent the city's commitment to treating civic engagement as a partnership involving government, residents, faith groups, schools and community organizations. She emphasized that the award reinforces the city's positive momentum and brings back community pride.

City officials view the All-America City designation as a significant statement to the nation about Riviera Beach's character and future potential. The honor positions the community for continued growth and new opportunities while demonstrating that collaboration produces results. The recognition validates what residents have observed during years of change and revitalization efforts throughout the city. The other nine communities receiving the 2026 award are Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

Secretary of State seeks to block St. Cloud Republican from ballot after last-minute filing error

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking courts to keep Paula Stark off the ballot for House District 47 after the St. Cloud Republican failed to properly submit required paperwork. In response to Stark's lawsuit seeking ballot access, Byrd's office argues that Stark's treasurer and partner, Joel Davis, never submitted Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests, to state...

Yesterday, 5:56 PM ET

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking courts to keep Paula Stark off the ballot for House District 47 after the St. Cloud Republican failed to properly submit required paperwork. In response to Stark's lawsuit seeking ballot access, Byrd's office argues that Stark's treasurer and partner, Joel Davis, never submitted Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests, to state employees. Nor was the form accidentally handed back when Davis submitted other paperwork in the final hour of the qualifying window on June 12. Stark's incomplete materials were timestamped less than 30 minutes before the noon deadline. Five state employees provided sworn written court declarations supporting the state's position that the form was never received.

Byrd said in his court response that Stark and Davis could have avoided this situation through basic care and caution. Had they questioned why the Form 6 was allegedly handed back, or had Davis not waited until the last minute to submit the documents, the missing form could have been caught and corrected before the deadline. Election Records Division Bureau Chief Erica Ikay Reeve noted that Stark was one of 12 candidates who did not properly submit Form 6, but half of them corrected the problems and still qualified. Three weeks after the qualifying deadline, Stark still has not submitted the form. Without seeing it, Reeve said, the state cannot review whether Stark would have met qualification requirements.

Reeve's court filing also detailed a pattern of problems with Stark's campaign paperwork over multiple election cycles. Davis has filed campaign finance reports more than a month late, reported excessive contributions, and has an incorrect address on file. In 2022 and 2024, audits found inconsistent fund transfers and balance issues. The state revoked Stark's Friends of Paula political committee in November due to more than 16,000 dollars in unpaid fines from missed campaign finance deadlines. A late report fine that was upheld by the Florida Elections Commission went unpaid and was referred to debt collection in November 2025.

The stakes are significant. If Stark remains off the ballot, the August Primary for House District 47 becomes open to all voters to decide between Democrats Jorge Figueroa and Anthony Nieves. No other Republicans filed to run in the traditionally blue House District representing Osceola and Orange counties. Byrd emphasized the tight timeline, noting that ballots must be mailed to overseas military and their families by July 4 and vote-by-mail ballots are scheduled to go out July 9-16. The Universal Primary Contest for House District 47 is already underway, and reversing course would be costly and likely create confusion, according to Byrd's filing.

Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

Residents at Colony Condos in Juno Beach are concerned about trespassers crossing their private property since the town removed a boundary fence in 2023 for safety reasons. With only bushes now separating the public beach walkway from private land, condo owners say beachgoers are increasingly cutting through their property during peak summer months. The situation has escalated to the point where residents are regularly contacting police to address the problem.

Colony resident Lori Hill described the challenges her community faces. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Hill noted that when the fence was in place, property lines were clearly marked and confrontations were rare. Since its removal, residents have experienced repeated incidents of trespassing. "We used to confront them and tell them this is private property, and they would get pretty rude," Hill said. "So, now what we've just been doing is just calling the police, and they've been coming up and confronting them."

The matter was brought to the Juno Beach Town Council, where officials clarified that the town has no obligation to install a fence. However, Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring more affordable alternatives. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. Not all beachgoers perceive the situation as problematic. Michael House, a visitor who regularly accesses JB-7 from a nearby public parking lot, said he has not witnessed the trespassing issues described by residents. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that."

Hill expressed hope that the town will help resolve the dispute. "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking," Hill said. The town council indicated it will address the fence question during budget discussions scheduled for July 15. A potential fence replacement could cost the town thousands of dollars, making cost considerations central to the council's decision.
