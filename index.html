9:09 AM ET

A record-breaking number of Americans are hitting the roads this Fourth of July weekend, with travel challenges compounded by heavy rain across South Florida. The AAA estimates that 72.2 million people will travel at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, marking an all-time high for the holiday. Of those travelers, 61.4 million are making the journey by car, also an all-time record. The sheer volume of vehicles has created congestion on major routes, particularly the Florida Turnpike heading south of Orlando, where Friday's wet weather made conditions especially difficult.

Rain significantly impacted driving conditions as the holiday weekend began. Driver Ed Budd described the situation on rain-soaked roads, saying "Really beautiful until it starts raining and then everyone forgets how to drive." Despite the challenging weather, most drivers continued their holiday trips. Jim Macfawn, who has extensive driving experience, took the conditions in stride. "I have a lot of experience driving, so I just roll with the punches," he said. Macfawn also noted his surprise at the scale of this year's travel, commenting on the high gas prices that would accompany such widespread journeys.

Orlando and Miami rank among the top five travel destinations for Independence Day travelers, according to AAA. Experiences varied significantly depending on when drivers hit the road. Carlos Borras and his wife completed a round trip between Orlando and Miami on Friday without encountering problems. "I don't think I saw any traffic, no accidents or anything, so it's been great," Borras said. In contrast, driver Fernando Amado reported busy conditions on the same route, particularly affected by the weather. Amado noted that Friday appeared to be the day when most travelers were heading north, suggesting that the heaviest volume had not yet passed through the area.

For those still planning to travel, the AAA offers specific recommendations to avoid peak congestion. Drivers should hit the road after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to experience the lightest traffic conditions. The heaviest congestion is expected between 10 a.m. on Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday. These recommendations highlight the importance of timing when planning holiday travel, as departure time can significantly impact the overall driving experience on crowded roads during one of the busiest travel weekends of the year.

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has been named one of just 10 communities in the United States to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the only Florida city selected for the honor this year. The award was announced during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where Riviera Beach leaders presented the city's efforts to strengthen community engagement. Mayor Douglas Lawson called the recognition a proud moment for the city, saying "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." Lawson said the city earned the award through a long-term effort to revitalize Riviera Beach and improve quality of life for residents.

The prestigious award recognizes communities that bring residents, local government, businesses and organizations together to solve local challenges and create lasting change. Riviera Beach's application highlighted three key programs: the Riviera Beach Youth Council, which helps high school students become involved in civic leadership; the Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education initiative with a 98 percent completion rate; and the Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program, which provides mini-grants to help longtime homeowners make critical repairs. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for the honor, saying the award reflects the heart and determination of the community and represents years of work focused on improving civic health and strengthening trust between residents and local government.

Yesterday, 3:05 PM ET

The Martin County School Board is opposing a judge's recommendation to reinstate teacher Matt Theobald, who was suspended following social media comments about Charlie Kirk. Theobald, a social studies teacher at Spectrum Academy since 2009, faced discipline after posting comments that labeled Kirk as racist, misogynistic, and a neo-Nazi. In a court filing submitted Thursday, the school board argued that Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone's recommendation lacked sufficient evidence, claiming Theobald's Facebook post constituted a "serious offense" that warranted his removal.

Livingstone's order cited a Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal ruling protecting teachers' personal speech rights, specifically stating that disciplining teachers for personal Facebook posts made off school grounds using personal equipment with no student involvement violated First Amendment protections. According to an investigation report, Theobald acknowledged writing the comments but said they were posted on his mother's private Facebook page within a discussion among personal acquaintances and were not intended for public view. The judge recommended Theobald receive his job back along with back pay and benefits. The school board retains final authority over whether Theobald returns to his position, with no timeline established for when that decision will be announced.

Yesterday, 6:43 PM ET

The tradition of Fourth of July cookouts is becoming more expensive for Florida families. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, hosting a holiday meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, representing a 4% increase from the previous year. The rise reflects broader inflationary pressures affecting grocery prices across multiple food categories.

The survey data shows significant price increases for several cookout staples. Ground beef prices have climbed 5.5% compared to last year, while hamburger buns are up 7.7%. Strawberries have experienced the steepest jump at 12.4%, a consequence of frost damage affecting Florida crops. Ice cream prices have also risen, increasing 5.3% year over year. These increases combine to noticeably impact the overall cost of planning a traditional holiday meal.

Not all grocery items have become more expensive, offering some relief for budget-conscious shoppers. Potato prices have declined this year, making potato chips and potato salad more affordable options for cookout sides. Corn prices have shown more variability, though locally grown corn from the Glades region can provide good value for those willing to seek out regional producers. This variation in pricing suggests that strategic shopping decisions can help offset some of the broader inflation trends.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends several practical strategies for families looking to manage cookout expenses. Comparing prices across different grocery stores is essential to finding the best deals on specific items on your shopping list. Woroch also emphasizes the importance of creating a meal plan before shopping, as this approach helps families cut down on food waste, which represents a significant portion of household food spending. By combining store comparisons with careful meal planning, families can keep their Fourth of July celebration costs manageable despite the overall upward pressure on grocery prices.

Hundreds of boaters are hitting Florida's waterways to celebrate the Fourth of July weekend. Brendan and Ashley Shannon were among those launching off the ramps Friday at Sandsprit Park in Martin County with their young daughter and puppy. The Shannons prioritized safety as they prepared to hit the water.

Lt. Brett Harry with Martin County Maritime Response Unit said his team is expecting hundreds of boaters on the water this weekend. Two boats with medics will be on standby, positioned in the north and south of Martin County. Harry said the team will be available to help anyone in need, from minor injuries like a stubbed toe to serious medical emergencies including cardiac events or spinal injuries. Harry urged boaters to keep their eyes on the water, designate a driver, and bring life vests, whistles and flotation devices. "Just have fun and be safe," Harry said.

Jun 30, 11:20 AM ET

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn more about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

Florida waives state park admission fees through Sunday for America's 250th anniversary

The waiver applies to admission fees at Florida State Parks and includes opportunities for hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling, and horse...

6:00 AM ET

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection announced that all residents and visitors can explore state parks for free from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. The waiver applies to admission fees at Florida State Parks and includes opportunities for hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling, and horseback riding. The parks also feature historic sites including forts, battlefields, archaeological sites, and other landmarks. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends, and family to enjoy the outdoors and support the state's public lands during this significant Independence Day.

Three parks are excluded from the free admission offer: Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park, and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. The waiver applies to day use only, and all other fees including overnight accommodations, special events, concessions, and rentals remain in effect. The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks attracted more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated $3.6 billion in statewide economic impact.

2:48 PM ET

The Peacock House Art Foundation is transforming Fort Pierce into an artistic destination through its preservation of the historic Crain Estate and artist-in-residency program. The non-profit organization operates from the former private residence of Jaxson and Mary Crain. Mr. Crain was a tile worker, and his craftsmanship is visible throughout the facility from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. The couple traveled the world nine times, collecting one-of-a-kind items for their Florida estate, including a gazebo shipped from India. The estate remains 90 percent the way it was when the Crains lived there, including the smoking and bourbon room with man paneling, manly bathroom colors, and stained glass leading to a bar on the front patio. Guided tours of the lush gardens and vibrant architecture are available every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. The foundation has made a conscious effort to keep the property original and not commercialized.

The Peacock House gets its name from birds the Crains are believed to have introduced to Fort Pierce in the 1970s. From an original seven peacocks, the property now has over 100. Among the flock is a bird named Bob, believed to be 60 years old and recognizable by his limp. Bob has his own bowls and is hand-fed daily. The foundation's artist-in-residency program offers simple but elegant accommodations for artists from around the world. The art lab offers classes for children and adults of all skill levels, with art director Krystal Masters leading one-on-one classes for complete beginners. Masters teaches mediums including sculpting and watercolor, with workshops like paint your pet in watercolor and rainbow.
