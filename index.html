Yesterday, 1:28 AM ET

A federally appointed council voted to roll back longstanding protections for the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef located just off the coast of Fort Pierce. Dozens of community members gathered at the Blake Library in Martin County to rally for the reef, which scientists say is found nowhere else on Earth and supports more than 2,000 marine species.

The reef sits hundreds of feet below the surface and thrives in deep, dark waters without the sunlight most corals require. Scientists warn that reopening parts of the reef to shrimp trawling could irreparably damage what little remains after trawling destroyed as much as 90% of the reef before federal protections were put in place decades ago. Dr. Grant Gilmore, a scientist who has dived the reef, said the coral provides critical habitat and food for fish in the ecosystem.

The proposals to reopen the reef followed a Trump executive order issued in April 2025 that directed eight regional councils nationwide to reinvigorate the fishing industry. Bob Zales, executive director of the Southeastern Fishing Association and a generational shrimper, argued that reopening access is essential for the struggling rock shrimp industry. Council data shows the rock shrimp industry has plummeted from over 700 trips per year in the mid-1990s to under 100 since 2010, with just 12 active boats in 2018.

Mark Perry, executive director of the Florida Oceanographic Society, joined Gilmore in urging the public to help save the reef at the gathering. The federal agency is preparing a final ruling on the protections. Scientists emphasized that corals grow very slowly and that worldwide coral populations are already in trouble, making further destruction particularly concerning for this ecosystem found only in waters off Fort Pierce.

Rosa Mena, 50, has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation after operating an unlicensed medical spa out of a shed behind her home on the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive in Port St. Lucie. Mena ran the business under the name Miracle Hand and Spa, offering Botox injections and other cosmetic procedures without proper medical credentials.

The case came to light in May 2025 when a victim reported experiencing partial facial paralysis after receiving 62 units of Botox injections from Mena. The victim, who had been referred to the business by coworkers, paid $325 for the services. When the victim reported the paralysis, Mena refunded the money but asked her to return for another injection of vitamins to reverse the effects. After the paralysis continued, the victim requested proof of Mena's medical license, and Mena provided an altered phlebotomy certificate that had actually expired in February 2024. Mena also falsely claimed to be a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

A multi-agency investigation involving Port St. Lucie Police, PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax and the Florida Department of Health led to a search warrant at Mena's residence. Detectives discovered medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines and injection devices for fillers and Botox inside the backyard shed.

Mena was charged with three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, one count of fraud-swindle obtain property $300 or more, one count of fraud-swindle obtain property under $20,000 and one count of use of two-way communication device to facilitate a felony. The sentence reflects the serious nature of performing medical procedures without proper licensing or training.

More than 100 paddlers will cross 80 miles of the open Atlantic Ocean this June to raise awareness and funds for families living with cystic fibrosis in the annual Crossing for Cystic Fibrosis. Participants will launch from Bimini, Bahamas, at midnight on June 13, arriving at Lake Worth Beach after 12 to 16 hours of paddling. The endurance trek benefits the Pipers Angels Foundation, which event founder Travis Suit established in honor of his daughter, Piper, diagnosed with cystic fibrosis at age 4.

The crossing is part of a four-day schedule, beginning June 10, with arrivals and a poolside social at Bimini Cove. On Thursday, participants take part in a 4Ocean beach cleanup and a community buffet dinner. Friday brings a Paddle Out Rose Ceremony to honor cystic fibrosis warriors, followed by rest and safety checks before launch night. The fundraiser expects participants from around the world. Paddlers must raise at least $2,000 for the cause, and crew or virtual participants have a $500 minimum. Final contributions are due July 31.

Safety is paramount. All paddlers are required to wear a U.S. Coast Guard-approved personal flotation device, waterproof whistle, and reflective gear during dark hours, Suit said. GPS trackers provided by the event must be worn and active at all times, and mandatory safety meetings will be held. Suit, an avid paddler, was inspired to create the event after learning about the natural respiratory benefits of ocean salt air. The foundation has since grown to provide direct support for cystic fibrosis patients and families, connecting them with resources and community.

Spectators are invited to Lake Worth Beach on June 13 to cheer paddlers during a daylong beach celebration marking their arrival. Cystic fibrosis is a life-shortening genetic condition that affects the lungs and digestive system. High-profile community events like this one not only raise funds but also spotlight ongoing needs for research, treatment, and family support. More information, registration, and donation details are available at crossingforcysticfibrosis.com and pipersangels.org.
