Yesterday, 6:40 PM ET

A Vero Beach nonprofit organization has collected more than 100 boxes of supplies intended for Venezuela earthquake relief efforts and is now requesting community assistance to transport them. Latinas Nexus Network is partnering with Global Empowerment Mission in Miami to deliver the supplies to Venezuela, which is recovering from a 7.5-magnitude earthquake. The collected boxes contain essential items ranging from diapers to medical supplies.

Lourana Navarro, founder of Latinas Nexus Network, said the community's response to their relief efforts has surpassed what they anticipated. The organization began collecting donations on Friday afternoon and has been overwhelmed by the outpouring of support. Co-founder Juliana Marino expressed the impact of the community's generosity, saying "The community has come together. They all came forward and brought something." However, the volume of donations has created a logistical challenge that the organization cannot solve alone. As Marino explained, the initial plan to transport supplies in their personal vehicles is no longer feasible. "We thought we were just going to put everything in our cars and we were going to drive to Miami. And there's no more room," she said.

To move forward with their mission, Latinas Nexus Network is urgently asking the community to donate the use of a box truck measuring between 12 and 16 feet. They need the truck by Thursday so they can load it and deliver the supplies to Global Empowerment Mission in Miami on Friday. The effort holds deep personal significance for Navarro, who was born in Venezuela and has family members living near the earthquake's epicenter. "It really kills me to see thousands, hundreds of thousands of people in the streets," she said, explaining her commitment to the relief effort.

Despite their determination to help, Navarro and Marino recognize they cannot complete this mission without community support. "We put it together, now we need to deliver," Marino said. Anyone who can help by donating a box truck or providing other assistance should contact Navarro at (954) 205-0700 or Marino at (772) 913-0805. The organization remains focused on ensuring these relief supplies reach the people in Venezuela who need them most.

1:05 PM ET

Bainbridge Companies has submitted a proposal to transform the 18 acres currently occupied by the empty Nordstrom store at the Mall at Wellington Green into a 620-unit apartment complex. The developer plans to demolish the existing building if the project receives approval from the village. According to Village Manager Jim Barnes, the new development would feature a combination of one-, two-, and three-bedroom units arranged in three towers, along with a dedicated parking structure.

Village officials view the project as part of a solution to address housing shortages affecting not only Wellington and the county but the broader state and region. Barnes emphasized that the area faces a significant need for additional residential units. However, the proposal has sparked opposition from some residents. Richard Meyers, a Wellington resident for over a decade, expressed skepticism about the project, saying the area already has sufficient apartment complexes. Gaby Martinez shared another common concern, warning that the increased density could create traffic problems on local roads. She noted that even routine commuter traffic to the area could cause bottlenecks on surrounding streets.

Village leadership has acknowledged ongoing community discussions about the future of the mall and its relevance in the changing retail landscape. Rather than viewing the apartment complex as a threat to existing retail, Barnes suggested that new residential development nearby could actually benefit the anchor stores and other retailers currently operating at the mall. He pointed out that Macy's, Dillard's, and JCPenney continue to perform well despite broader challenges facing traditional shopping malls. Barnes highlighted that the Macy's location at Wellington Green is the highest-performing store in the state, suggesting the mall maintains commercial viability.

At this stage, the project remains in the proposal phase with no approval granted. The village has not announced a timeline for the project's development or provided a cost estimate for the conversion. The plans are still under review and will require village approval before moving forward.

Supreme Court blocks Trump effort to end birthright citizenship in 6-3 ruling

The Supreme Court ruled that constitutional protection remains in place for children born in the United States, regardless of their parents' immigration status.

Yesterday, 6:10 PM ET

The Supreme Court on Tuesday blocked President Donald Trump's effort to end birthright citizenship, ruling 6-3 that the constitutional protection remains in place for children born in the United States, regardless of their parents' immigration status. Trump signed an executive order targeting birthright citizenship on his first day back in office, arguing the move would help repair the nation's immigration system. The order sought to deny automatic citizenship to children born in the U.S. to parents who are in the country illegally. Opponents challenged the policy in federal court, arguing it violated the 14th Amendment, which was ratified in 1868 and guarantees citizenship to nearly anyone born on U.S. soil.

Immigration advocates celebrated the ruling outside the Supreme Court. Ana Marie Hernandez with the Florida Immigrant Coalition said the executive order created fear and confusion for many immigrant families. Among those following the case closely was 18-year-old Sindia, a recent high school graduate and full-time student whose parents immigrated from Guatemala in the early 2000s. Sindia said she panicked when Trump announced plans to end birthright citizenship, noting it was written in the Constitution that people born on U.S. soil are automatically granted citizenship. Supporters of Trump's proposal argued the 14th Amendment was never intended to apply to children born to parents without lasting ties to the United States. Unless Congress passes a constitutional amendment, birthright citizenship is expected to remain protected.
