A few minutes ago

The restroom and concession building at Jensen Beach Park remains an empty plot of land more than a year after a fire destroyed the facility on New Year's Eve in December 2024. Beachgoer Gonda Ghebert reached out to WPTV's Let's Hear It inbox to report that beachgoers are left with only one very small restroom facility to serve the entire beach and feels it has become increasingly unreasonable during weekends, holidays, and peak tourist season. Regular visitors say they also miss the cafe itself. Julie Goings said she went to the park because they had a nice, clean facility with showers and it was convenient to get a snack or drink coming off the deck. Sam Elliott said the only thing really missing is concessions and food availability.

Kevin Abbate, the Parks and Recreation director, says the permitting process takes time because the project must go through the Department of Environmental Protection since the site sits on a protected dune. That permit is currently in process. The design for the new 9,000 square foot Sea Turtle Beach Cafe is 80% complete. Abbate says the facility will be about 15 feet in elevation with 100 seats and 33 at the bar, providing a good seat for everyone.

The county says final funding is expected to be approved in September. They hope to start construction in early 2027 with a completion goal of late 2027, just in time for the winter season. The county also told WPTV they do not have any plans for additional temporary restrooms or facilities in the meantime. Abbate says the cafe will be worth the wait, explaining that they want to do it right and design it properly so when they get through construction, they will have fewer issues and a much smoother process.

Those who use the beach say they are anxiously awaiting construction to begin. Elliott said he is hoping it moves real fast. Goings said she wants the bathrooms up and running and it would be nice to have another little convenient spot at the beach.

Fort Pierce to overhaul waterfront development process after multiple lawsuits, controversies

Fort Pierce city commissioners voted unanimously Monday night to reject a settlement offer from plaintiff Kevin Keene, who is suing the city over its handling of the historic Little Jim's Bait & Tackle restaurant. 5:34 PM ET

Fort Pierce city commissioners voted unanimously Monday night to reject a settlement offer from plaintiff Kevin Keene, who is suing the city over its handling of the historic Little Jim's Bait & Tackle restaurant. The rejection means more taxpayer dollars will likely go toward defending the case. City Commissioner Michael Broderick said the city has an obligation to defend itself but also believes the lawsuit lacks merit. He said the settlement was not practical, particularly a provision that would have required taxpayers to vote on future waterfront land decisions. "You can't wait for an election or fund a special election every time you want to do something on city-owned property that's waterfront," Broderick said.

The Little Jim Bait & Tackle case is at least the fourth waterfront property dispute and the second to result in a lawsuit. In 2023, the developer of King's Landing threatened legal action after claiming the city sold him buildable land filled with old power plant concrete. In 2024, developer Chris Shelli of Fisherman's Wharf filed suit after state records showed the city promised property it did not own. In 2025, the city abruptly canceled the Little Jim's lease bidding process after public outcry, prompting Keene's lawsuit and leaving at least two developers frustrated. This year, the city became responsible for nearly $200,000 in unpaid rent tied to Crabby's Waterfront Restaurant.

Broderick acknowledged the controversies alleged in the lawsuit, which claim a broader pattern of defective administration of city waterfront properties. When asked how the city responds to allegations of mismanaging waterfront properties, Broderick said he would not call it poor management. "I'm not going to say it's managed poorly. I'm going to say it's not being managed, and that's an issue I take very seriously," Broderick said. He told reporters the city plans a top down, bottom up approach to examine the entire portfolio and fix issues one asset at a time.

The city is creating a new real estate management position to track leases, rent payments, and waterfront properties with the goal of preventing these problems from happening again. "I will not rest until I get to the bottom of this issue, it's going to be categorized, it's going to be spreadsheeted," Broderick said. Both sides say they are open to negotiating. Keene says he wants transparency, not money, and is willing to revise his offer. Broderick says he will consider any feasible settlement that keeps the case out of court.

3:10 PM ET

Lionel Messi scored his eighth goal of the tournament and led Argentina to a 3-2 comeback victory over Egypt on Tuesday despite trailing by two goals with only 11 minutes of regulation time remaining. The defending champions will face either Switzerland or Colombia in the quarterfinals on Saturday in Kansas City, Missouri.

Messi scored his record-extending 21st World Cup goal in the 83rd minute to level the score at 2-2. Enzo Fernandez scored the winning goal in stoppage time to complete one of the great World Cup comebacks after Cristian Romero started the rally in the 79th minute. Egypt had led 2-0 on goals from Yasser Ibrahim and Mostafa Zico. Fernandez said after the match that his team never gives up and that four years have passed since Qatar. He added that Argentina came to enjoy another World Cup and wants to win it again. Argentina coach Lionel Scaloni praised his players afterward, saying he was so emotional about what a group of players they are.

Egypt took a surprising lead in the 15th minute when Ibrahim got ahead of Lisandro Martinez to meet Marwan Attia's cross and head the ball into the bottom corner. Argentina was quickly given the chance to level when Haissem Hassan tripped Nicolas Tagliafico in the box moments later. Referee François Letexier pointed to the penalty spot and Messi stepped up, but Egypt goalkeeper Mostafa Shobeir dove to his left to block the shot. This was Messi's second penalty miss of the tournament after also failing from the spot against Austria in the group stage. Despite being the all-time leading scorer at World Cups, Messi has now missed four of eight penalty kicks at the tournament. After Messi hit the post later in the half, Shobeir pulled off another great save to stop Julian Alvarez from close range.

Argentina is bidding to become the first team to win back-to-back titles since Brazil did so in 1958 and 1962. Messi, 39, was in tears after the final whistle in what might be the last of his six World Cups. For much of the game, it looked like it would be a painful exit for the Argentina captain. Egypt thought it had doubled its lead in the second half when Zico finished off a sweeping attack, but the goal was disallowed after video review confirmed a foul earlier in the move. The second goal for Egypt came in the 67th minute from a similar break, and this time Zico's effort counted, but it was not enough.

12:36 PM ET

The Food and Drug Administration has issued a recall for frozen Publix GreenWise Organic Blueberries sold across eight states due to possible E. coli contamination. The affected product, GreenWise Organic IQF Blueberries in 10-ounce packages, was distributed to Publix retail stores throughout Florida, Alabama, Georgia, Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and Virginia. Consumers who purchased these blueberries are urged not to eat them and should either throw them away or return them to their place of purchase for a full refund.

The specific product under recall is frozen GreenWise Organic IQF Blueberries in 10-ounce packages with lot code 60401 and a best by date of February 9, 2028. The FDA has reported 12 confirmed cases of consumers experiencing stomach illness between May 11, 2026 and June 5, 2026 that have been linked to E. coli infections. Only products matching this specific lot code and best by date are affected by the recall.

Those who consume food contaminated with E. coli can experience severe stomach cramps, diarrhea that may be bloody, and vomiting. While most healthy individuals can recover within a week, some infections can result in serious health complications. Consumers should check their freezers for the affected product and take immediate action if they have purchased the recalled blueberries.

Consumers with questions or concerns about the recall can contact the company by email at Info.foodsafety@comfrut.com or by phone at 336-899-5612. The recall affects only the specific lot of frozen organic blueberries sold at Publix stores in the eight southeastern states. Customers are reminded to check both the lot code and best by date on their packages to determine if their product is included in this recall.

St. Lucie pancreatic cancer patient Russell Reed approved for experimental treatment

9:55 AM ET

Russell Reed, 64, of St. Lucie spent 62 days waiting to learn whether he would be approved to receive daraxonrasib, an experimental drug his oncologist believed could help after traditional treatments were no longer effective. Reed's oncologist submitted a request through the FDA's Expanded Access Program, which can allow patients with serious or life-threatening illnesses to receive treatments outside of clinical trials when other options are limited. The pancreatic cancer patient said chemotherapy was no longer helping him at that point in his treatment.

Daraxonrasib, also known as RMC-6236, is an investigational once-daily oral pill developed by Revolution Medicines. The drug targets mutated KRAS proteins, which drive more than 90% of pancreatic cancers. While the treatment is not yet FDA-approved, early clinical trial results have generated optimism among some patients and physicians. Reed said research and clinical trial results gave him hope that daraxonrasib could help slow the progression of his disease. "There's a lot of anecdotal evidence and a lot of evidence from the clinical trials that show that this is the miracle drug for pancreatic cancer," Reed said. "It is not a cure, but it will give me time."

While waiting for an answer during the two-month period, Reed and his wife, Marnie, focused on making the most of their time together. For Marnie, the fight was never just about accessing a drug but about more family trips, more memories and more moments together. "I need someone's attention," Marnie Reed said. "The idea of sitting here and waiting for the phone to ring is not ok with me." She added that she did not want to miss a minute with her husband. "You're still here, and if you don't pay attention, you're going to miss it," she said.

Reed received the call about his approval just two days after sharing his story with WPTV. The drug is now in his hands, and his family says he is already feeling better and optimistic about what comes next. While daraxonrasib is not considered a cure for pancreatic cancer, the Reeds say the approval represents something they have been fighting for all along: more time together.
