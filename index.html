Riviera Beach named only Florida city to receive 2026 All-America City Award

Riviera Beach has been selected as one of only 10 communities in the United States to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the sole Florida city honored this year. The prestigious award recognizes communities that successfully unite residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and create meani...

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has been selected as one of only 10 communities in the United States to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the sole Florida city honored this year. The prestigious award recognizes communities that successfully unite residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and create meaningful change. City leaders presented Riviera Beach's accomplishments during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where judges announced the city as a first-time winner.

Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the recognition, stating "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." Lawson credited the award to the city's long-term commitment to revitalization and improving quality of life. He noted that residents are witnessing the changes and reimagining of Riviera Beach that leaders have worked toward for years. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson highlighted the community's role in the achievement, saying "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents. Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

The city's application emphasized three key civic engagement programs. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy work. The Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education program, achieved a 98% completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program provides mini-grants to longtime homeowners for critical home repairs. These initiatives reflect the city's broader approach to treating civic engagement as a partnership between government, residents, faith groups, schools and community organizations. Miller-Anderson explained that the city is "doing a lot of innovative things to make sure that our community is able to say that they are part of what's happening here in the city."

The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, is considered one of the nation's most respected civic honors. City officials say the designation celebrates Riviera Beach's progress while positioning the community for future opportunities and growth. The award sends a message to the nation that Riviera Beach is a place where collaboration produces results and residents play an active role in shaping their community's future. The nine other cities receiving the 2026 award are Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

A missing fence at a Juno Beach public beach access point is creating conflict between condo residents and summer beachgoers, with potential expenses in the thousands to fix the problem. Residents at the Colony Condos contend that people accessing the JB-7 public walkway have been cutting through their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. With only bushes now marking the boundary between the public path and private land, trespassing has become increasingly common during peak summer months.

Lori Hill, who lives at the Colony Condos, described how the issue intensifies when warm weather arrives. She explained that beachgoers park in private spots and push back bushes to access the sand with coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs. Hill noted that residents once confronted trespassers directly but have shifted to calling police to handle the problem. She said the fence previously made the property line obvious to visitors. However, not all beachgoers acknowledge the issue. Michael House, who walks to JB-7 from a public parking lot blocks away, said he has not observed the trespassing problems residents describe. "There's plenty of free parking," House said. "If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue."

The matter reached the Juno Beach Town Council during a recent meeting. A town official stated that the municipality is not legally required to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring less expensive options than a full fence replacement. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. The council decided to address the fence question during its budget discussions scheduled for July 15.

Hill expressed optimism that the town will take action on the issue. "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking," she said. The outcome will likely depend on how town officials prioritize the expense during their upcoming budget deliberations and whether they determine a lower-cost alternative could adequately address the problem.

Hill explained that residents appreciated how the original fence made property boundaries obvious and prevented the current wave of unwanted visitors. Not all beachgoers agree that a serious problem exists. Michael House, a regular visitor to JB-7, said he walks to the access point from a nearby public parking lot and has not observed the trespassing issues that residents describe. The town council voted to include the fence question in its upcoming budget discussions scheduled for July 15. Hill and other Colony residents remain hopeful that the town will take action to restore both their landscaping and exclusive use of their private parking areas. Jun 30, 11:20 AM ET

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The center designed these programs to help visitors learn about sea turtle conservation at one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data on nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection announced that all residents and visitors can explore state parks free of charge from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, in honor of America's 250th anniversary. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends and family to the state parks to enjoy the outdoors, make new memories and support treasured public lands. Florida's state parks offer outdoor recreation opportunities including hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling and horseback riding. The parks also feature historic sites such as forts, battlefields, archaeological sites and other landmarks.

The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks saw more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated 3.6 billion in statewide economic impact. Free admission applies to day use only and excludes Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. All other fees, including overnight accommodations, special events, concessions and rentals, remain in effect.

A record number of Americans are traveling this Fourth of July weekend, with heavy rain compounding congestion on South Florida roads. Friday's wet weather created difficult conditions on the Florida Turnpike heading south of Orlando. AAA estimates 72.2 million Americans will have traveled at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, marking an all-time high for the holiday. Of that number, 61.4 million are making the trip by car. Orlando and Miami rank among the top five travel destinations this Independence Day, according to AAA.

Drivers experienced mixed conditions on the roads. Ed Budd said conditions were "really beautiful until it starts raining and then everyone forgets how to drive." Carlos Borras reported a smooth round trip between Orlando and Miami on Friday, seeing no traffic or accidents. However, Fernando Amado described heavy traffic, particularly with the weather, noting Saturday was the day people were heading north. AAA recommends hitting the road after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to avoid the worst congestion. The heaviest traffic is expected between 10 a.m. on Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday.

Yesterday, 6:43 PM ET

Floridians planning Fourth of July cookouts will need to dig deeper into their wallets this year. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, hosting a holiday meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, representing a 4% increase compared to last year. The rise in prices reflects broader inflation affecting grocery stores across the state and the nation.

Several key ingredients are driving up cookout expenses. Ground beef prices have climbed 5.5%, while hamburger buns have seen a particularly steep increase of 7.7%. Strawberries have been especially hard hit, rising 12.4% due to damage from a Florida frost. Ice cream, a summer staple, is up 5.3%. These increases show how inflation is touching nearly every aspect of the traditional Fourth of July meal, from the main course to dessert and side dishes.

Not all grocery prices are climbing, however. Potato prices have declined this year, offering relief for shoppers planning to serve potato salads or chips at their celebrations. Corn prices have been more volatile, but locally grown corn from the Glades provides an affordable option for those seeking the best deals. These price drops offer some opportunities for budget-conscious consumers to offset increases in other categories.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends several strategies to keep cookout costs manageable. She suggests shopping strategically by comparing prices across different stores to find the best deals for items on your shopping list. Perhaps most importantly, families should focus on reducing food waste, which starts with creating a meal plan before heading to the grocery store. By being intentional about what they purchase and preparing only what they need, Floridians can enjoy their Fourth of July traditions while keeping expenses under control despite this year's inflation-driven price increases.
