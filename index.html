Port St. Lucie is moving forward with a partial interchange project on Florida's Turnpike at Midway Road to improve accessibility and support the city's continued growth. Last month, the Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance authorizing the transfer of 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to facilitate the interchange cons...

Yesterday, 7:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie is moving forward with a partial interchange project on Florida's Turnpike at Midway Road to improve accessibility and support the city's continued growth. Last month, the Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance authorizing the transfer of 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to facilitate the interchange construction. The city council will hold a final vote on the land donation at a future meeting.

The new interchange is intended to address a significant gap in Turnpike access currently affecting Port St. Lucie residents. Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for the city, explained that the project serves a dual purpose: accommodating growth that has already happened while preparing for anticipated development over the next two to five decades. The partial interchange represents part of a larger effort by FDOT to improve regional transportation, including the $95 million Midway Road Widening Project designed to reduce congestion and enhance safety in the area.

Work is underway on the first phase of the Midway Road widening initiative, which will expand the roadway from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, stretching from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design stage with construction expected to commence in Summer 2027. The partial Turnpike interchange is also still in design phase. Along with the road expansion, FDOT is planning to construct bike and pedestrian paths plus sidewalks throughout the project corridor.

Local residents acknowledge both the challenges and benefits of the ongoing construction. Port St. Lucie resident Erin Scheumeister noted that while the work can be disruptive, she recognizes its necessity for the community. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," Scheumeister said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." As the project progresses through its various phases over the coming years, residents can expect continued improvements to regional connectivity and safety along this key transportation corridor.

The renovation is being funded through a state grant and transportation impact fe...

Yesterday, 5:05 PM ET

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down on July 6 for a major renovation expected to wrap up by next Fourth of July. The $3 million project will significantly expand the facility's capacity, increasing available boat slips from 18 to 44 to accommodate both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation is being funded through a state grant and transportation impact fees. The work will include construction of a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock, and a smaller floating platform. With over 1,400 boaters relying on the dock annually, city officials say the improvements are essential for the community's future.

Stuart Mayor Sean Reed emphasized that the upgrades serve multiple purposes beyond simply adding more spaces. "They're doing it to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms," Reed said. "So the year of waiting will be worth it." The enhanced design and structure should better protect the facility and vessels during severe weather events, a critical consideration for a waterfront community.

Businesses that operate from the dock have worked with the city to develop solutions for continuing their operations during the closure. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which depends on the dock for passenger boarding and drop-off, expressed initial concerns about the yearlong shutdown. However, city officials found a compromise that allows the business to remain functional. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, explained the arrangement: "So they're allowing us to build a gate right here at the end of the downtown Stuart dock. This is the non-floating section, but still we're going to be able to open this up and board people right here." Kuyrkendall stressed the importance of the dock to Stuart's identity and economy, stating "Stuart is not Stuart without the water."

Local boat captain Parker Fisher, who operates from Stuart, supports the project. "It's definitely needed some work over the years, so I'm glad they're putting money into it," Fisher said. Kuyrkendall acknowledged the dock's current crowding issues, particularly during weekends when available spots become scarce. He sees the modernization as beneficial for both the city and the businesses that depend on the facility. "Modernizing and expanding this back dock is really going to be great for the city but also for us as well," Kuyrkendall said.

Moody endorsed Donalds, saying "He is a proven conservative leader who will fight to make Florida safer, healthier, and more affordable as Governor." The campaigns plan to emphasize public safety, education, economic growth, and senior issues while contrasting themselves with the Democratic field.

Donalds, who represents Naples in Congress, entered the gubernatorial race in early 2025 with backing from former President Donald Trump. He has qualified for the ballot and secured endorsements from the Florida Farm Bureau's FarmPAC and legislative leaders. A former state Representative with a finance background, Donalds would become Florida's first Black Governor if elected. He faces primary opposition from Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins, former House Speaker Paul Renner, and businessman James Fishback, though Trump's endorsement and fundraising strength have positioned him as the frontrunner.

Moody, a fifth-generation Floridian and former federal prosecutor and Hillsborough Circuit Judge, served six years as Attorney General before Ron DeSantis appointed her to the U.S. Senate seat vacated by Marco Rubio, who became Secretary of State. During her time as Attorney General, she helped secure billions in opioid settlements for Florida. She was sworn in on January 21, 2025, has received Trump's endorsement, and has qualified for the Special Election to complete the remaining two years of Rubio's term.

On the Democratic side, former Republican Congressman David Jolly leads the gubernatorial field with former U.S. Representative Gwen Graham as his running mate. The Democratic Senate race remains uncertain after teacher Josh Weil withdrew due to a medical issue. The Primary Election is scheduled for Tuesday, August 18, with voter registration closing July 20. The General Election will follow on Tuesday, November 3, with registration closing October 5.

Glydways will present a proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport.

Yesterday, 5:49 PM ET

California-based autonomous transit company Glydways is proposing an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport. The system would also connect the convention center, nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations. Small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers would travel along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads, potentially cutting the trip between downtown and the airport to less than 10 minutes. Glydways estimates travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center.

Glydways will present the proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for initial consideration. Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system while offering comparable passenger capacity, referencing previous South Florida light rail estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile. Funding could potentially come from Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. The company also said the system would be priced like public transit rather than private rideshare and would require local employees for operations centers and maintenance facilities. Tuesday's meeting is expected to determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal rather than approve construction.

While municipal fireworks displays will light up the sky across Florida, the FWC warns that personal fireworks can startle and disrupt wildlife. FWC officials stress the importance of cleaning up after a day at the beach by removing all trash, filling in holes dug in the sand, and taking home toys or beach furniture before nightfall. Obstacles on the beach can prevent sea turtles from nesting and prevent hatchlings from reaching the water once they emerge from their nests, while also entangling shorebirds, turtles and other wildlife. For those living near the beach, officials recommend turning off or dimming lights at night, as artificial lighting along the shoreline is a major hazard for nesting sea turtles. Lights on or near the beach can misdirect turtles and hatchlings away from the ocean toward potential danger.

After completing most of the work, the company left two holes unfinished on the back side of his home and stopped responding to his calls, texts and emails starting in January. McCoy said he visited the company's building two or three times and received only one response, on February 5, when a worker promised the owner would call him, but never did.

The situation worsened when letters from Gulfeagle Supply arrived warning that a nearly $7,000 lien had been filed on his home. The lien stated that Gulfeagle Supply had furnished materials to Seabreeze Roofing for McCoy's property but was not paid. WPTV investigative reporter Kate Hussey spoke with the owner of Seabreeze Roofing, who declined to comment on camera citing legal reasons. Within an hour of that conversation, Hussey returned to McCoy's home with a final waiver of lien signed by Gulfeagle Supply and notarized. McCoy also learned the contractor would return to finish the roof repairs, and about an hour after receiving the waiver, crews were back on his roof completing the work.

Delray Beach golf training center delayed three months, costs $90,000 after contractor problems

Yesterday, 4:44 PM ET

Tom Molen's dream of opening Tee Box, a virtual golf training and performance center near Delray Beach along Jog Road south of Linton Boulevard, became costly when contractor problems delayed the project by three months. Instead of opening in April as scheduled, Molen said the licensed contractor's work had to be redone, costing approximately $90,000. The delays also triggered what Molen called a "double pay" situation on multiple services. "It was double pay on a lot of things from electricians to HVAC to drywall," he said. That estimate does not account for lost revenue during the delays or the expense of hiring another contractor to fix the problems.

Code Pro of the Palm Beaches, the organization Molen worked with, is warning property and business owners to thoroughly vet contractors before hiring them. Founder David Allan estimates that 25% to 35% of homeowners and property owners in Palm Beach County face major issues with unvetted contractors. Allan recommends using Palm Beach County's online portal to verify whether contractors have proper licenses and insurance before work begins. The most common contractor complaints in the county involve roofing, windows and air conditioning work. Despite the setbacks, Tee Box is preparing for its grand opening on July 17.
