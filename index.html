Riviera Beach named only Florida city to receive 2026 All-America City Award

Riviera Beach has earned one of the nation's most prestigious civic honors, becoming the only Florida community selected for the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned one of the nation's most prestigious civic honors, becoming the only Florida community selected for the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city was one of just 10 U.S. communities to receive this distinction. The award recognizes municipalities that bring together residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address community challenges and create meaningful, lasting change. City leaders announced the achievement during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where Riviera Beach presented its efforts to strengthen community engagement and civic participation.

Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the recognition, stating that "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He credited years of work to revitalize the city and improve the quality of life for residents. Lawson said the community is witnessing the changes and reimagining efforts the city set out to accomplish long ago. City leaders emphasized that the award reflects their commitment to improving civic health, building trust between residents and local government, and enabling residents to shape the city's future directly.

The city highlighted several key programs in its award application that demonstrate its investment in civic participation. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy. The Citizens Leadership Academy provides civic education with a 98 percent completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program awards mini-grants to longtime homeowners for necessary repairs. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson noted the city is implementing innovative approaches to ensure residents actively participate in community development. She stated that the award "brings back community pride" and reinforces positive momentum throughout Riviera Beach.

The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, stands as one of the nation's most respected honors for community achievement. City officials believe the designation positions Riviera Beach for future opportunities and continued growth while signaling to the nation that collaboration and resident involvement drive results. The nine other communities receiving the award are Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

Residents at the Colony Condos in Juno Beach are facing an ongoing problem with trespassers since the town removed a protective fence in 2023 for safety reasons. The old barrier once clearly marked the boundary between the public JB-7 walkway and private property, but now only bushes separate the two areas. Without the fence, residents say beachgoers regularly cut through their land, parking in private spots and pushing through vegetation with coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs. Lori Hill, a Colony resident, explained that the issue becomes especially severe during summer months when visitors arrive unexpectedly and occupy private parking spaces for extended periods.

The situation has created tension between residents and beach visitors. Hill said that residents once confronted trespassers directly to explain the property was private, but these interactions frequently turned hostile. Colony residents now call police to handle the problem instead. Not all beachgoers acknowledge the conflict, however. Michael House, who regularly walks to JB-7 from a nearby public parking lot, said he has never witnessed the trespassing issues residents describe and noted that ample free parking exists in the area. He suggested that property damage or littering would be more significant concerns than boundary violations.

The fence dispute recently came before the Juno Beach Town Council, where officials clarified that the town holds no legal obligation to install a fence on the property. However, Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern expressed openness to exploring more affordable solutions. He suggested the town might consider helping residents with a lower-cost fence option or a combination of split rail fencing and shrubs to mark the boundary more clearly while reducing expenses.

The council has scheduled the fence question for discussion during its budget talks on July 15. Hill said residents remain hopeful that town officials will reinstall a barrier so that Colony Condos can reclaim their landscaping and private parking areas. The cost of replacing the fence could amount to six figures, making the budget decision a significant financial consideration for the town. Residents are waiting to see whether the council will prioritize the boundary solution or pursue alternative approaches during the upcoming budget meeting.

Yesterday, 9:38 AM ET

Misty Dawn White, 46, died in a house fire that broke out in her Hobe Sound home on Monday. She was discovered inside after emergency responders arrived at the scene. The sheriff's office has identified a possible electrical issue as the cause, though investigators continue to examine what happened. The fire destroyed much of the home and the possessions belonging to White and her fiancé Mark.

Word of White's death reached her close friend Nina Fenn through a phone call from Mark. Fenn, who had known White for eight years, has since become a key voice in sharing details about the tragedy and supporting those affected. "It's just a terrible shame and awful loss of life," Fenn said. Following the fire, friends and community members organized a fundraiser to help Mark rebuild his life after losing his home and belongings in the flames. Fenn described the situation as "completely a state of devastation" and has been working to support Mark however she can.

Fenn remembered White as a devoted Christian who had a passion for gardening and plants and played the piano. She noted that White was generous with others and always willing to help. "She was always the first to lend a hand," Fenn said. "She loves kids; she loves her kids. She loves people." Those who knew her say her kindness and generous spirit made a lasting impression on everyone around her. Fenn emphasized that White's memory will endure among those who loved her. "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts," Fenn said.

The tragedy compounds an existing grief that Mark has already experienced. Fenn explained that Mark previously lost his only child, Jacob, in a tragic circumstance. The timing of White's death adds additional sorrow for those close to her. White's birthday fell on the Fourth of July, meaning she passed away just days before what would have been her birthday celebration. For Mark and White's loved ones, this convergence of loss underscores the depth of heartbreak the community is processing together.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football team is working through the summer with a clear goal in mind: winning a state championship. After finishing the previous season with a 10-2 record and capturing the district title, the team felt they left opportunity on the table when they exited the playoffs in the second round. Now, with experienced leadership, they are determined to make a deeper postseason run.

Head coach Jimmy Robertson credits the team's veteran makeup as a major advantage heading into the 2026 fall season. The majority of the senior class has been with the program since middle school, giving them a deep understanding of the program's culture and standards. Robertson believes this continuity gives the Scots their best chance to achieve their goals. "Most of these seniors are four-year seniors, if not the majority of them came from the middle school as well. So they know how things are run. They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had," Robertson said.

To prepare for a stronger playoff push, Saint Andrew's has intentionally scheduled more difficult opponents for the upcoming season. Senior Jack Williams explained the rationale behind this decision, saying the team wants to test itself against quality competition to elevate its level of play and attract attention to the program. Senior Jack Whelche emphasized that regular season success, while important, is secondary to playoff advancement. "A better year is just gonna be going farther in the playoffs. Obviously we wanna go undefeated in the season, but that doesn't matter if we keep losing in the second round of the playoffs," Whelche said.

The team is using summer workouts as a foundation for their ambitions. Senior Edmyer St. Jean highlighted how daily training sessions provide motivation and accountability as the players prepare for what they hope will be a historic season. The Scots are focused on breaking their previous records while leveraging their experienced senior class to make a state title run. With their experienced roster, challenging schedule, and clear objectives, Saint Andrew's believes they have what it takes to advance further in the postseason and compete for a state championship this fall.

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. According to the center, the programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn more about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

Yesterday, 5:56 PM ET

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking courts to keep St. Cloud Republican Paula Stark off the ballot after she failed to submit required paperwork before the June 12 qualifying deadline. Stark submitted her materials just 30 minutes before the noon deadline, but state employees say she never filed Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests. Stark has since sued to get on the ballot, claiming her treasurer Joel Davis accidentally received the form back during the submission process.

In response to her lawsuit, Byrd cast doubt on Stark's account of events. Five state employees submitted sworn declarations stating that Davis never submitted Form 6 and was not accidentally handed it back when he turned in other paperwork. Election Records Division Bureau Chief Erica Ikay Reeve noted in court filings that Stark still has not submitted Form 6 three weeks after the qualifying deadline. "Without seeing the Form 6 that was allegedly submitted, we cannot review it for facial sufficiency in order to determine if Candidate Stark would have qualified," Reeve stated. Byrd argued that Stark and Davis should have exercised greater care by questioning why the form was allegedly returned or by submitting documents earlier rather than waiting until the final moments before the deadline.

Reeve documented a troubling pattern of paperwork violations in Stark's campaign history. Davis has filed campaign finance reports more than a month late, reported excessive contributions, and maintained an incorrect address on file. Audits in the 2022 and 2024 election cycles found inconsistent amounts transferred between accounts and accounting discrepancies. The state revoked Stark's Friends of Paula political committee in November over 16,000 dollars in unpaid fines from missed campaign finance deadlines. In the 2024 cycle, a failure to pay a late report fine upheld by the Florida Elections Commission was referred to debt collection in November 2025. Of the 12 candidates who failed to properly submit Form 6, half of them corrected the problems and still qualified.

Byrd emphasized the time constraints in his court response, noting that ballots must be mailed to overseas military and their families by July 4, with vote-by-mail ballots due July 9 through 16. If Stark remains off the ballot, the August primary for House District 47 in Osceola and Orange counties becomes open to all voters choosing between Democrats Jorge Figueroa and Anthony Nieves. No other Republicans filed to run in the traditionally Democratic district, making Stark's exclusion a significant win for Florida Democrats.

Yesterday, 7:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to transfer 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation for a partial interchange at the Florida Turnpike. The city will vote on final approval at an upcoming meeting. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the interchange addresses a significant gap in Turnpike access points. "This is a way for us to try to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years," Samples said.

FDOT has been constructing the $95 million Midway Road Widening Project to address congestion and safety concerns. The first phase widens the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027. FDOT is also improving the Turnpike bridge and designing the partial interchange, which is still in development. The project will add bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks. Resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged the construction is disruptive but necessary. "We definitely needed it to be expanded. It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work," she said.

The American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey shows that a Fourth of July meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, up 4 percent from last year. Inflation is driving the price increases across key cookout items. Ground beef prices rose 5.5 percent, while buns increased 7.7 percent. Strawberries experienced the steepest jump at 12.4 percent after a Florida frost damaged crops. Ice cream prices climbed 5.3 percent. Potato prices declined this year, offering relief for shoppers buying potato chips and salad ingredients. Corn prices have fluctuated, but locally grown corn from the Glades can offer better deals.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommended comparison shopping between stores and cutting down food waste through meal planning. Shoppers interviewed Friday described their holiday meal plans, which included hamburgers, steak, coleslaw, chips, burnt ends, mashed potatoes, baked potatoes and fresh broccoli. Some noted challenges like preparing meals in limited spaces, such as condos without grills.
