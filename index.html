Yesterday, 10:19 PM ET

Diana Chaparro Contreras, 33, and Sandra Peralta Aguilar, 50, face multiple charges including human trafficking and profiting from arranging prostitution. Both women are currently being held in the Palm Beach County Jail following an investigation into illegal operations at a Lake Worth Beach residence.

The case originated in January when a California woman responded to a housekeeping job posting on Indeed. She was offered $35 per hour and accepted an offer to relocate to Florida with airfare provided. Upon arriving at the home on S. M Street in Lake Worth Beach, her identification documents were confiscated. The victim reported that her passport, Social Security card, and work permit were taken and kept from her. She told investigators she was threatened with deportation and coerced into sexual activity with men who visited the property. The victim was confined to a room with boarded windows and claimed she was forced to have sexual contact with seven men before escaping. She used a lamp to break through plywood covering the window to get out. The victim also reported seeing what may have been a 15-year-old girl present at the location.

During the extended investigation, law enforcement observed numerous men entering and leaving the residence. In April, investigators discovered the operation had relocated to another site less than a mile away. Financial records showed that Chaparro was involved in handling more than $177,000 in cash and bank transactions related to the operation.

Laura Cusack, an advocate with the Human Trafficking Coalition of the Palm Beaches, emphasized that trafficking operations can exist anywhere in the community. "It doesn't just happen in a vacuum or in the underground world. It could happen in the house next door," Cusack said. She stressed that community awareness is vital for identifying these operations. Cusack noted that suspicious activity such as unusual high traffic patterns can be warning signs. She called on residents to report suspected trafficking to authorities, saying that one report can make a significant difference. "These predators are in our community. They can target our children so it just takes one person to make the call, report what they see and that can make all the difference," Cusack said.

Anyone who suspects human trafficking activity should contact the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office. For additional resources and information, the Human Trafficking Coalition of the Palm Beaches can be reached at 561-267-5783 or htcoalitionpb@gmail.com.

Yesterday, 6:52 PM ET

Tri-Rail secured $60 million in Florida's 2026-2027 state budget, ensuring the commuter rail system can continue operating after facing a potential shutdown in July without the funding. Florida State Senator Mack Bernard emphasized the importance of the allocation for residents traveling between Palm Beach County, Broward, and Miami-Dade counties. "Transportation is the key, because I know people are trying to get to work, schools and Tri-Rail plays that major role, between the residents of Palm Beach County, Broward and Dade," Bernard said. Regular rider Stanley Lucien, who commutes from Boca Raton to Miami for business, praised the service's affordability. "It's cheaper it does save you a lot of money," Lucien said. "If I could fund it, I'd fund it myself."

Palm Beach County also achieved significant infrastructure funding through the $117 billion state budget. The county secured $1.5 million for the new Riviera Beach water treatment plant and $2 million for drainage repairs to Australian Avenue from 45th Street to Palm Beach Lakes, which is part of a larger $65 million project. The state approved an additional $3 million for traffic safety improvements on Okeechobee Boulevard. Bernard noted that Palm Beach County received over $30 million in approved funding, representing 75 percent of the total amount requested in the budget. "In all this was a good year for Palm Beach County," Bernard said.

Other local organizations benefited from the budget approval. The Arc of the Palm Beaches in West Palm Beach received $1.5 million for a special needs shelter, while the Boys & Girls Club secured $1.5 million for its Teen Readiness Center. These investments support vulnerable populations and youth development in the region.

However, Governor Ron DeSantis vetoed nearly $1.6 billion in proposed spending, including several Palm Beach County priorities. The governor struck $2 million for water treatment plant expansions, $900,000 for road improvements and beautification in South Bay, $125,000 for Ocean Inlet beach access improvements, and $200,000 for repairs and operations at Delray Beach's historic American Legion building. State Representative Rob Long, who advocated for the American Legion funding, expressed determination to continue pushing for the project during the next budget cycle. The historic building holds significant cultural importance to the community as a hub for Black residents in the area.

Singer Island 25-story development proposal raises concerns over world-renowned Blue Heron Bridge reef

A New York-based developer, The Continuum Company, has proposed Singer Island Gateway, an approximately $14 million mixed-use development at 2525 Lake Drive in Palm Beach County. The project would include hundreds of residential units, commercial and public space, and a marina, with the main structure rising 25 stories at the entrance to Singer Island. The location places the d...

Yesterday, 6:08 PM ET

A New York-based developer, The Continuum Company, has proposed Singer Island Gateway, an approximately $14 million mixed-use development at 2525 Lake Drive in Palm Beach County. The project would include hundreds of residential units, commercial and public space, and a marina, with the main structure rising 25 stories at the entrance to Singer Island. The location places the development directly above the underwater reef at Phil Foster Park near Blue Heron Bridge, a destination that attracts divers and snorkelers from around the world.

The reef draws visitors for its remarkable marine ecosystem. People who visit the area describe seeing stingrays, rainbow-colored fish, blue and yellow fish, pink coral, yellow coral, and striped fish in the shallow waters. Louis Espinosa, a diver who frequents the location, described it simply as "beautiful, amazing" and called it one of the best spots he has visited. The combination of accessible shallow water and diverse sea life has made this area renowned among diving enthusiasts globally.

However, longtime resident Gary Heiser, who grew up in the area, has expressed serious reservations about the project's scale and placement. He believes the 25-story tower is inappropriate for the location, noting that while such a structure might work in cities like Chicago, it does not fit here. Beyond aesthetic concerns, Heiser worries that construction activities, the shade cast by the tall building, and the marina could harm the marine life below. "I don't think it's a good idea that this treasure gets impacted to such a great extent," he said. An environmental study posted on the developer's website presents a contrasting view. A geoscientist concluded that the development would have no impact on marine life in the Lake Worth Lagoon, with the ecosystem remaining unchanged. Heiser remains skeptical of these findings, stating he would like to see additional research before accepting such conclusions.

Opinions among reef visitors diverge on the matter. While Heiser opposes the project based on potential environmental damage, Espinosa indicated his position depends on whether there is genuine evidence of harm to the reef. "If it doesn't disturb the reef, I don't see a problem with it," Espinosa said. The proposal has not yet been approved. The developer has been contacted for comment, with a response still pending. Meanwhile, residents have launched a petition opposing the Singer Island Gateway project.

The vetoes, detailed in a 39-page line-item letter sent to Secretary of State Cord Byrd, are part of $1.7 billion in total state vetoes DeSantis announced when he signed the budget. In Broward County, both of the two largest vetoes were $2 million each: a stormwater mitigation project in Hallandale Beach and a bridge replacement in Lighthouse Point. Palm Beach County's biggest veto was $4.8 million for a remodel of Palm Beach State College's Boca Raton administration building, and DeSantis also struck $3 million for a helicopter purchase for the Health Care District of Palm Beach County's emergency operations.

More than a hundred new state laws take effect July 1, bringing court challenges against several measures that civil liberties experts say threaten constitutional freedoms.

4:30 AM ET

Civil liberties groups are mounting legal challenges against several Florida laws set to take effect July 1. HB 1471 concentrates power in the hands of a few government officials to label organizations as "domestic terrorist organizations" without adequate due process, transparency, or safeguards against political abuse. Civil liberties experts contend the law violates the First Amendment and threatens to chill protected speech and association. HB 991, taking effect in 2027, makes voter registration and casting ballots harder for Floridians who lack easy access to a new, more restrictive list of citizenship documents. The law affects older voters, low-income voters, naturalized citizens, citizens born at home, and women whose names changed after marriage or divorce. Legal analysts say HB 991 is likely unconstitutional and violates the 14th Amendment's Equal Protection Clause.

The property tax amendment that Gov. Ron DeSantis pushed through in a Special Session also faces legal challenges from local officials, who claim the ballot language is confusing and deceitful. Local officials argue the amendment will result in higher rents, cuts to critical local services, and a shift toward increased taxes and fees. A provision requiring new Florida residents to wait five years to receive the tax break could be deemed unconstitutional. DeSantis' new congressional maps are also in legal limbo, with critics calling them an unconstitutional partisan power grab that violates the Fair Districts Amendments Florida voters passed in 2010.
