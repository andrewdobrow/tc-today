Eridjon Dacruz of West Palm Beach is traveling to Miami this month to watch Cape Verde compete in the World Cup, marking a historic moment for one of the world's smallest nations to ever qualify for the tournament. Dacruz grew up in Cape Verde, an archipelago consisting of 10 islands located off the African coast, and later relocated to the United States. The island nation, which gained independence from Portugal during the 1970s, has a population of approximately half a million people. As Dacruz prepared for the big game, he looked through his collection of soccer jerseys, pulling out his favorite national team gear from his home country.

Cape Verdeans have expressed tremendous excitement about their national team, called the Tubarão Azul or Blue Sharks, qualifying for the World Cup for the first time in history. Dacruz, who still has many family members living in Cape Verde, noted that the qualification has been a major topic of conversation throughout the islands. He expressed amazement at the achievement, saying he never thought it would happen during his lifetime given the country's small size. When reflecting on his past gaming experiences, Dacruz recalled searching for Cape Verde players in FIFA video games and finding only about two options available. He is confident in his team's potential, believing they can advance if they win a couple of matches.

Dacruz's trip comes courtesy of his girlfriend, who gave him World Cup tickets as an anniversary gift. During their anniversary dinner, she presented him with a handwritten note inside a small box that read "I'm going to the World Cup," surprising him completely. He will attend Cape Verde's match against Uruguay in Miami. The team opens the tournament against Spain, one of the tournament favorites. While waiting for his new Cape Verde national jersey to arrive in time for the game, Dacruz anticipates showing his support in person.

Cape Verde's World Cup qualification will provide significant financial benefits to the nation's soccer development. The soccer federation will receive more than 10 million dollars to help build the next generation of players. Beyond sports, Cape Verde remains frequently mentioned during hurricane season, when tropical systems form near the islands before tracking across the Atlantic toward the United States, according to meteorologist Sami Squires. These weather systems originating from the African coast occasionally develop into storms that move westward across the ocean, though forecasters hope major storms will stay away this year.

Yesterday, 5:58 PM ET

St. Lucie County established new anchoring restrictions in two waterway areas beginning June 1. The anchor limitation zones are located in the Indian River Lagoon and Fort Pierce Harbor near the South Causeway Bridge. Under the new rules, boaters cannot anchor a vessel for more than 45 consecutive days within any six-month period in these designated areas. If a boater wants to anchor again after reaching the 45-day limit, they must leave all anchor limitation areas for at least 24 hours before returning.

County officials implemented the restrictions in response to a growing problem with abandoned vessels in these waterways. According to Erick Gill with St. Lucie County, the Fish and Wildlife Commission collected approximately two dozen derelict vessels last year. Gill explained that the new rules will help improve navigation in the area. "We need to make sure that highway for vessels is clear and free for other vessels to come and go," he said. The restrictions aim to prevent vessels from remaining stationary in these waterways for extended periods.

Environmental protection is another key concern driving the new policy. When abandoned vessels remain in the water too long, they can threaten water quality through leaks from diesel tanks, oil tanks, and holding tanks. James Beams with St. Lucie County Port, Inlet and Beaches noted in December that these environmental hazards posed significant risks to the ecosystem. Gill said the anchoring restrictions will help prevent pollutants from entering the water and damaging the lagoon. The Indian River Lagoon supports over 4,000 species of animals, plants, and birds, making it an environmentally sensitive area that requires protection.

The county created these anchor limitation areas to balance the needs of boaters with environmental and navigational concerns. Officials want to ensure the waterways remain accessible for vessels while also protecting the lagoon from the damage caused by long-term vessel abandonment. A site map showing the specific anchor limitation areas is available from the county for boaters who need to understand the exact boundaries of the restricted zones.

12:25 AM ET

Hector Mujica is preparing to withdraw from the Democratic primary in Florida's 28th Congressional District following a party registration problem that makes him ineligible to run. The former Google executive wants federal prosecutor Robin Peguero, currently running in the neighboring 27th Congressional District, to switch races and take his place, according to multiple sources.

The eligibility issue stems from a party affiliation change in Mujica's voter registration records. Broward County records show that Mujica switched from Democrat to no party affiliation on June 4, 2025, then returned to Democrat on July 26, 2025. Florida law requires candidates to maintain continuous party membership for 365 consecutive days to run for a party's nomination. Democratic lawmakers unanimously passed this requirement earlier this year, and Governor Ron DeSantis signed it into law in April.

Mujica initially claimed the registration change was a clerical error and stated he was unaware of the switch until his opponent's campaign discovered it in late July. However, the Broward Supervisor of Elections rejected this explanation, saying their records review found no evidence of any administrative mistake. Mujica's inability to account for the change is notable given that he had recently run for U.S. Senate, raising over $717,000 before withdrawing about two weeks before that race's qualifying period. Retired Air Force Commander Phil Ehr, Mujica's primary opponent in the district, filed a complaint with the Florida Elections Commission and threatened to file a lawsuit on Friday, the final day of qualifying, if Mujica remains on the ballot.

Ehr's campaign manager Vanessa Brito confirmed that Mujica is attempting to recruit Peguero to replace him in the race. Peguero has raised $800,000 through March 31 and is currently in a Democratic primary against Eliott Rodriguez for the 27th District seat, which is being defended by Republican Maria Elvira Salazar. Brito expressed disapproval of the recruitment effort, saying Mujica should withdraw and wait until he becomes eligible to run. The 28th Congressional District covers portions of southwestern Miami-Dade County, including Florida City, Homestead, South Miami, and Sweetwater, along with the Florida Keys in Monroe County. The 27th District includes Miami, Coral Gables, and several other communities. Federal candidates must reside in Florida but not necessarily in their specific district.
