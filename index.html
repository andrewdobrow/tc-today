Yesterday, 6:43 PM ET

Americans planning Fourth of July cookouts this year will face noticeably higher grocery bills. The American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey shows that preparing a holiday meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, representing a 4% increase from the previous year. The primary culprit behind these rising costs is inflation, which has affected nearly every item on a typical cookout shopping list.

The price increases are significant across multiple categories. Ground beef, a staple for holiday burgers, has jumped 5.5% in cost. Buns to serve those burgers have become even more expensive, rising 7.7%. Strawberries have experienced the steepest increase at 12.4%, largely due to damage from a Florida frost that impacted the harvest. Ice cream, another popular summer treat, has climbed 5.3% in price. However, not all items have become more costly. Potato prices have actually decreased, offering some relief for families planning to serve potato chips or potato salad. Locally grown corn from the Glades is another option where shoppers can find competitive prices.

Andrea Woroch, a budgeting expert, offers practical advice for families looking to manage cookout expenses without cutting back on their celebrations. She recommends shopping more strategically by comparing prices across different stores to identify the best deals for items on your grocery list. More importantly, Woroch emphasizes that families should focus on reducing food waste as part of their cost-saving efforts. She stresses that this process should begin with planning out your meals in advance, which helps ensure you purchase only what you need.

Local shoppers preparing for their Fourth of July celebrations shared their menu plans, which reflect traditional cookout fare. One shopper described plans for hamburgers with coleslaw, chips and beer for lunch, followed by burnt ends, mashed potatoes and fresh broccoli for dinner, though they noted they would be unable to grill due to living in a condo. Another shopper indicated they prepare hamburgers, beef, steak and baked potatoes for the holiday. These varied menus show how families are adapting their celebrations while working within their budgets and living situations, making smart choices about which items to prioritize for their Fourth of July gatherings.

Yesterday, 6:17 PM ET

The School District of Palm Beach County has achieved an "A" rating from the Florida Department of Education for the third consecutive year. The district's sustained success reflects strong student achievement across all grade levels, according to school leaders. This year, 143 district-operated schools, representing 84 percent of the district, earned either an "A" or "B" rating, an increase from 120 schools in the previous year. The district also set a new graduation rate record of 93.5 percent.

Student participation in advanced academic programs has been a significant driver of the district's success. Eighty-six percent of high school seniors took at least one college-level course, including Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, Cambridge Advanced International Certificate of Education, or Dual Enrollment classes. Additionally, 144 recent graduates earned an associate degree while completing their high school diploma. Superintendent Mike Burke stated that the rating demonstrates the district's commitment to supporting student success and credited the achievement to the combined efforts of students, teachers, school leaders, support staff, families, and community partners.

One of the district's most impressive turnarounds occurred at S.D. Spady Elementary School in Delray Beach, which jumped from a "C" rating to an "A" rating this year. Principal Rona Tata, who has led the school for 15 years after five years as assistant principal, attributed the improvement to curriculum changes implemented three years ago. The school modified its early childhood program, which serves children starting at age 3 and connects with kindergarten instruction. Students who participated in this improved program are now in third grade and demonstrating higher performance levels. Tata noted the school had earned an "A" rating two years prior and had narrowly missed earning a "B" the previous year. Beyond curriculum changes, the school refined its overall academic approach and challenged both students and staff to raise expectations. Last year's school theme, "Be the reason you succeed," reinforced the school's focus on personal responsibility and achievement.

Spady Elementary was one of four district schools to significantly improve this year. The others earning the jump from "C" to "A" ratings included Dwight D. Eisenhower K-8, Glade View Elementary, and West Gate Elementary. School grades in Florida are determined by multiple factors, including student performance on state tests, graduation rates, and advanced coursework participation. Community members have expressed enthusiasm about Spady's achievement, noting that the improvement strengthens confidence in the school and the broader Delray Beach community. The School District of Palm Beach County's sustained high performance demonstrates ongoing progress in preparing students for college and career success.

Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

Residents at the Colony Condos say people using the JB-7 public walkway are cutting through their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. Without it, only bushes separate the public access path from private land. Lori Hill, a Colony resident, said the problem peaks in summer, with cars parking in private spots and beachgoers pushing through bushes with coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs. Hill said residents remember when the fence made the boundary clear and that confrontations with trespassers have become recurring. She said residents used to confront people about trespassing, but they would become rude, so now residents call police to handle the situations.

The dispute came before the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where a town official noted the town is not obligated to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern said a lower-cost solution may be worth exploring, suggesting the town could help with a lower-cost fence or a combination of split rail and shrubs. The council agreed to take up the fence question as part of its upcoming budget discussions on July 15. Hill said residents are hopeful the town will reinstall the fence so they can have their landscaping and private parking restored.

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. According to the center, the programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn more about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

6:00 AM ET

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection announced it would waive admission fees from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, at Florida State Parks to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends and family to enjoy the outdoors and make new memories while supporting public lands. The free admission applies to day use only, with overnight accommodations, special events, concessions and rentals remaining at regular price.

Florida's state parks offer hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling and horseback riding across diverse landscapes including caverns, wetlands, beaches and springs. The park system also maintains historic sites such as forts, battlefields and archaeological sites. Three parks are excluded from the free admission offer: Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award, recognizing the state's investment in protecting natural resources while maintaining public access and environmental stewardship. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks saw more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated $3.6 billion in statewide economic impact. Visitors can plan trips at FloridaStateParks.org and learn more about Florida's semiquincentennial celebrations at America250FL.com.

A record-breaking 72.2 million Americans traveled at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, according to AAA estimates. This marks an all-time high for Fourth of July holiday travel. Of those travelers, 61.4 million chose to drive, making cars the dominant mode of transportation for the extended weekend getaway.

Friday's rainy conditions created hazardous driving situations on the Florida Turnpike south of Orlando as holiday travelers hit the roads. The wet weather caught drivers off guard and led to unsafe driving practices. Driver Ed Budd commented on the sudden shift in conditions, saying "Really beautiful until it starts raining and then everyone forgets how to drive." Despite the challenging weather, many drivers continued their journeys. Some, like Jim Macfawn, remained calm and composed. "I have a lot of experience driving, so I just roll with the punches," Macfawn said about navigating the wet roads.

Orlando and Miami rank among the top five travel destinations for this Independence Day weekend. Travel experiences varied significantly depending on route and timing. Carlos Borras and his wife made the round trip between Orlando and Miami on Friday and encountered favorable conditions. "I don't think I saw any traffic, no accidents or anything, so it's been great," Borras reported. However, other drivers faced very different circumstances. Fernando Amado described busy conditions compounded by the weather, noting that "It's been pretty busy, especially with the weather. I thought people were leaving yesterday, but today is the day when people are really heading up North." The sheer volume of travelers surprised some drivers. Jim Macfawn reflected on the scale of this year's numbers, saying "It sounds like a lot. I'm surprised people can afford all the gas."

For travelers still planning trips, AAA recommends strategic timing to avoid the worst traffic. Those heading out should aim to depart after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday. The heaviest congestion is expected between 10 a.m. on Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday. AAA notes that departure location, destination, and travel time all significantly impact the driving experience on holiday weekends. With record numbers of Americans on the roads and unpredictable weather adding to the challenge, careful planning and patience remain essential for a safe holiday trip.
