A record number of Americans are traveling this Fourth of July weekend, with AAA estimating 72.2 million people will have journeyed at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, marking an all-time high for the holiday. Of that total, 61.4 million are making the trip by car. Orlando and Miami rank among the top five travel destinations this Independence Day.

Friday's wet weather created difficult driving conditions on the Florida Turnpike heading south of Orlando. Driver Ed Budd said the conditions were "really beautiful until it starts raining and then everyone forgets how to drive." Despite the challenging weather, drivers continued their holiday trips. For those still planning to travel, AAA recommends hitting the road after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to avoid the worst congestion. The heaviest traffic is expected between 10 a.m. on Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday.

S.D. Spady Elementary in Delray Beach Jumps From C to A Rating as Palm Beach County Schools Earn Third Consecutive A

The School District of Palm Beach County has earned an A rating from the Florida Department of Education for the third consecutive year, marking continued academic excellence across the district. This year, 143 district-operated schools received either an A or B rating, an increase from 120 schools the previous year. The district also achieved a record graduation rate of 93.5%. School grades in Florida are determined by multiple factors, including student performance on state tests, graduation rates, and performance in advanced coursework. Additionally, 86% of seniors participated in college-level coursework through Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, Cambridge Advanced International Certificate of Education, or Dual Enrollment courses. A total of 144 recent graduates earned associate degrees alongside their high school diplomas.

One of the most notable success stories involves S.D. Spady Elementary School in Delray Beach, which made a remarkable leap from a C rating to an A rating this year. Principal Rona Tata, who has led the school as principal for 15 years and previously served as assistant principal for five years, attributed the improvement to strategic changes implemented three years ago. The school modified its early childhood curriculum program, which serves students starting at age 3 and integrates with kindergarten. Many of those students are now in third grade and demonstrating higher academic performance levels. Tata explained that the school also refined its overall academic approach, focusing on challenging both students and staff to raise expectations across the campus. She noted the school had previously earned an A rating two years ago and narrowly missed achieving a B rating last year by just a few points.

Three other district schools also improved from C to A ratings this year: Dwight D. Eisenhower K-8, Glade View Elementary, and West Gate Elementary. Superintendent Mike Burke praised the district's accomplishments, stating that the A rating "reflects our ongoing commitment to sparking academic success and helping every student ignite their future."

Community member Chris Ceasar, who attended Spady as a child and has family members currently enrolled there, expressed enthusiasm about the school's transformation. "For them to be an A school from a C school, that's a huge jump," Ceasar said. "It's a big thing for the community because education is first in Delray Beach." He added that the improved rating would attract more talent to the school. Tata emphasized the collaborative effort behind the achievement, saying "There is a lot of work that went on behind the scenes" and that students and staff "brought everything to the table and did everything that they were supposed to do."

The Martin County School Board filed a court document Thursday challenging an administrative law judge's recommendation to reinstate Matt Theobald, a social studies teacher at Spectrum Academy who was suspended last year following social media comments about Charlie Kirk. Theobald's Facebook posts labeled Kirk as racist, misogynistic and a neo-Nazi. The school board's attorney argued that Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone made "numerous findings" that "are not based upon competent substantial evidence" and contended that Theobald's Facebook post constituted a "serious offense" warranting his removal.

In an order issued last month, Livingstone cited a recent Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal ruling determining that disciplining a teacher for personal Facebook posts made off school grounds on personal equipment with no student involvement violated First Amendment rights. The judge recommended Theobald receive back pay and benefits. According to an investigation report, Theobald admitted writing the comments but said they were posted on his mother's private Facebook page within a discussion among personal acquaintances and were not intended for public view. The school board retains final authority on whether to reinstate Theobald. Theobald told WPTV the case raises questions about where the line is drawn for teachers speaking outside school. No timeline has been announced for a final decision.

Yesterday, 6:43 PM ET

Americans planning Fourth of July cookouts this year will need to dig deeper into their wallets. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, a holiday meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, representing a 4% increase from last year. The price hike reflects broader economic pressures affecting groceries across the board, with some items seeing significantly steeper increases than others.

Inflation is the primary driver behind the rising cookout costs. Ground beef prices have climbed 5.5% compared to a year ago, while hamburger buns have become 7.7% more expensive. Strawberries have experienced the most dramatic price jump at 12.4%, a spike largely attributed to frost damage in Florida's growing regions. Ice cream, another cookout staple, is up 5.3%. These increases compound to make traditional summer meals noticeably pricier for households across the country.

However, not all cookout ingredients are following the upward price trend. Potato prices have actually declined this year, offering relief to consumers who enjoy potato chips and potato salad at their gatherings. Corn prices have been more volatile, though shoppers can find good deals on locally grown corn from the Glades region. These price decreases provide some opportunity for families to offset costs in other areas of their menus.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends several strategies to manage Fourth of July expenses. The first step is to compare prices across different grocery stores to identify which retailers offer the best deals on items on your shopping list. Woroch emphasizes that the most impactful way families can control costs is by reducing food waste, which starts with creating a detailed meal plan before shopping. By planning meals in advance, households can purchase only what they need and avoid the common problem of throwing away unused groceries. These practical approaches can help families enjoy their Independence Day celebrations without breaking the budget.

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering a variety of educational programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. According to the center, these programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn more about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

6:00 AM ET

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection announced that all residents and visitors can explore state parks for free from July 3 through July 5, 2026, to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends and their family to the state parks to enjoy the outdoors, make new memories and support treasured public lands. The free admission offer applies to day use only, with all other fees, including overnight accommodations, special events, concessions and rentals, remaining in effect.

The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award, showcasing state investment in protecting natural resources while maintaining public access and environmental stewardship. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks saw more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated $3.6 billion in statewide economic impact. Parks offer hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling and horseback riding, along with historic sites including forts, battlefields, archaeological sites and other historic landmarks. Three parks are excluded from the free admission offer: Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. Visitors can find the nearest park at FloridaStateParks.org.
