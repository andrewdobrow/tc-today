The ninth-ranked Muchova defeated Gauff 6-2, 1-6, 7-6 (10) Thursday in a match that ended with a drama-filled tiebreaker. She became the fourth Czech woman in the last six years to reach the final of the grass-court Grand Slam. It will be Muchova's second Grand Slam final after losing to Iga Swiatek in the 2023 French Open championship match. Muchova could face another Czech player in Saturday's final, with Linda Noskova to play Marta Kostyuk of Ukraine in the other semifinal next on Centre Court. The other Czech women in the Wimbledon final recently were Karolina Pliskova, runner-up in 2021, Marketa Vondrousova, champion in 2023, and Barbora Krejcikova, champion in 2024.

For Gauff, it was still her most successful Wimbledon. Previously, the seventh-ranked American had gone only as far as the fourth round three times, including during her breakthrough run as a 15-year-old in 2019. The men's semifinals on Friday feature top-ranked and defending champion Jannik Sinner against seven-time Wimbledon winner Novak Djokovic and French Open champion Alexander Zverev against British wild card Arthur Fery.

Florida families can save on back-to-school shopping during the state's 32-day sales tax holiday running from July 20 through August 20, 2026. The tax-free shopping period offers significant savings on essential items families need for the new school year, with no sales tax due on qualifying purchases. Qualifying clothing items include shirts, pants, dresses, shoes, uniforms and athletic wear. Tax-free bags include backpacks, handbags, diaper bags and fanny packs, while accessories like wallets, belts, ties and graduation caps and gowns also qualify. Notable exclusions include briefcases, suitcases, watches, jewelry, skis, swim fins, roller blades and any clothing item over $100.

Tax-free school supplies include pens, pencils, erasers, crayons, notebooks, binders, folders, construction paper, poster board, markers, scissors, glue, tape, staplers, lunch boxes and composition books. The holiday also covers computer disks with blank CDs only. Learning materials such as flashcards, learning cards, jigsaw puzzles, puzzle books, interactive books, educational toys, memory and matching games, stacking blocks and educational sets qualify for the exemption. Qualifying computers include laptops, desktops, tablets, electronic book readers and calculators. Tax-free accessories include keyboards, mice, monitors without TV tuner, printers, ink cartridges, modems, routers, cables, educational and productivity software, storage devices and memory cards. Computers must be for personal, non-commercial use, as business purchases do not qualify.

Price thresholds apply to different categories. Clothing and bags qualify at $100 per item, school supplies at $50 per item, learning aids and puzzles at $30 per item, and computers and accessories at $1,500 per item. Items normally sold together must be purchased as a set and cannot be split up to meet price thresholds. A family spending $500 on qualifying items could save $35 in sales tax. The tax exemption is not available at theme parks and entertainment complexes, hotels and public lodging establishments, or airports.

Items ordered online during the holiday period qualify for the exemption, even if delivered after August 20. If shoppers buy an eligible item during the holiday and later exchange it for the same item in a different size or color, no tax applies to the exchange. Items purchased with rain checks issued during the holiday period remain tax-free. Using gift cards to buy eligible items during the holiday qualifies for the exemption, regardless of when the gift card was purchased. Items qualify if shoppers take delivery during the holiday period or put eligible items on layaway during the holiday, even if final payment comes later. Florida retailers have no option to opt out of the sales tax holiday and must process all qualifying purchases tax-free during the designated period.

2026-06-16

A new Aldi grocery store opened Thursday at 8867 Southeast Bridge Road in Hobe Sound, bringing more affordable choices to customers already facing tight budgets. The opening comes as the USDA expects grocery prices to rise 2.8% this year. For shoppers looking to stretch their dollars, the discount grocer offers an alternative to traditional supermarkets at a time when food costs continue to climb.

Customers Cheryl and Tony Cooperman said they're excited an affordable option like the new Aldi store is now open close to their home. Cheryl said grocery prices are getting expensive and are starting to change the way she shops. "They're the type of people who never used to look at prices, but now she will go with what's on sale instead of just with what they want, and they never used to do that," she said. The changing shopping habits reflect a broader trend as more households adjust their purchasing decisions based on price rather than preference.

Price movements in key grocery categories show a mixed picture for consumers. Egg prices are dropping, and beef prices fell 1.3% from April to May, though wholesale beef prices remain at all-time highs for this time of year. Pork rose 1% from April to May, and chicken prices rose 1.3% over the same period. Fruits and vegetables are seeing an increase of less than 1%. Despite some declining categories, the overall trajectory points to continued upward pressure on grocery bills.

Shoppers James and Harry said Thursday they do not expect relief at the register anytime soon. "Prices are gonna stay inflated and are not gonna go down," they said, adding they'd be willing to bet on that. Still, they said stores like Aldi, which often have lower prices than other stores, can make a difference for everyday shoppers. "Having stuff like this for the average person is extremely beneficial because there's nobody who isn't feeling the price increase," they said. The new location gives Treasure Coast residents another option for managing household budgets as inflation continues to impact the cost of food.
