11:43 AM ET

JetBlue is ending its direct flights between New York's JFK Airport and Vero Beach Regional Airport, citing low passenger demand for the route that launched in December. The airline announced that service between the two airports will end Sept. 9, just nine months after the route began operating with much fanfare and optimistic passengers. JetBlue said in a statement to WPTV that demand for the new route has not materialized as the airline had hoped. The airline will continue serving Vero Beach through its Boston route. All affected customers will be notified directly by JetBlue and will receive full refunds.

The JFK-Vero Beach route was seen as a major win for the Treasure Coast when it launched in December 2025, providing direct access to one of the nation's busiest airports and potentially boosting tourism to the area. The inaugural flight brought enthusiastic passengers who called the service a game changer for connecting family and friends between New York and the Treasure Coast. Despite the initial excitement surrounding the route's debut, passenger numbers apparently fell short of what JetBlue needed to sustain the service.

Vero Beach Regional Airport has worked to expand its commercial service in recent years, with JetBlue's Boston route continuing to serve travelers seeking alternatives to larger South Florida airports. The continuation of the Boston service provides some consolation for the regional airport, which has sought to increase flight options for Treasure Coast residents and visitors. The loss of the JFK route represents a setback for those expansion efforts, though the airport still maintains commercial service through the remaining JetBlue connection.

JetBlue has not announced whether it plans to add any replacement routes from Vero Beach or if it will adjust frequency on the remaining Boston service. Passengers with upcoming reservations on the discontinued route are encouraged to contact JetBlue directly or check their email for rebooking options and refund information. The airline's decision to cut the route after such a short period highlights the challenges smaller regional airports face in sustaining service to major metropolitan hubs, even when initial demand appears promising.

Three young gymnasts from Flip City Gym and Cheer secured a top 15 finish at AAU Nationals in Knoxville, Tennessee last month, qualifying them for the Junior Olympics in Iowa next month. Skylar Rose-Daris, Victoria Supan, and Charli Rine each came to gymnastics through different paths but now compete together as one unit on the national stage.

The three gymnasts found their way to the sport for varied reasons. Rose-Daris said it started with a simple skill. "It wasn't really gymnastics, it was the fact that I knew how to do a cartwheel," she said. Supan drew her inspiration from watching elite athletes online. "All the gymnasts that I saw on YouTube, and how amazing they are, and how fun it looks," she said. Rine expressed a love for the learning aspect of the sport. "I love it because I learn new things," she said.

Head Coach Candice Lazuka described the team's path to nationals as part of a strong season. "We had a really good season. We competed locally, we traveled, and we went to states and regionals," Lazuka said. At AAU Nationals last month, the gymnasts came remarkably close to achieving a perfect score. "It's hard to get a perfect 10, but we got a 9.9 and were very close to a 10," Lazuka said. Their top 15 finish at the competition earned the team their qualification for the Junior Olympics.

The three gymnasts expressed excitement about their upcoming trip to Iowa for the national competition. Rose-Daris looked forward to the travel experience itself. "It's going to be really fun that I'm going to an entire different part of the world," she said. Supan described her feelings about the opportunity by saying, "It's happy and exciting at the same time." Rine has already set her sights on a specific goal for the Junior Olympics. When asked what she is focused on heading into the competition, she said, "Getting first place on floors."

Security First Insurance Cuts Florida Homeowners Rates by 5.6%, Launches Disappearing Deductible Program

1:35 PM ET

Security First Insurance is lowering homeowners insurance rates by an average of 5.6%, marking the latest in a series of rate decreases the Ormond Beach-based company has implemented over the past two years. The company holds approximately 155,000 policies across Florida.

Along with the rate reduction, Security First is launching a disappearing deductible program for Florida homeowners. Company President Melissa Burt DeVriese explained how the program works. "What this is, over the course of five years, every year your deductible goes down 20 percent as long as you don't have a loss; once you have a loss, the discounted deductible applies and then it resets back to the full deductible amount so it rewards consumers who don't have losses," Burt DeVriese said. The program provides incentives for policyholders who remain claim-free over extended periods.

Burt DeVriese said the move signals improvement in Florida's insurance landscape, pointing to increased competition from more companies entering the market. The rate cut and new program by Security First represent positive developments in a state where homeowners insurance has become increasingly challenging to secure and afford. The changes affect the approximately 155,000 policyholders the company serves throughout Florida.

Despite these positive developments at Security First, many Florida homeowners continue to struggle with insurance costs. According to Insurify, the average homeowners insurance premium in Florida currently sits around $8,200 per year. The high average premium reflects the ongoing difficulties Florida property owners face in securing affordable coverage in a state vulnerable to hurricanes and severe weather events.

A Palm Beach County man led Martin County deputies on a high-speed chase through Indiantown after driving more than 100 mph on State Road 710, one of the area's most dangerous roads. Deputies spotted 33-year-old Markell Tillman driving aggressively at speeds exceeding 100 mph, forcing other vehicles off the roadway. When deputies attempted to stop him, Tillman fled, weaving through traffic in an attempt to lose law enforcement before turning onto SW Martin Highway toward Palm City, where he continued to drive recklessly.

Deputies located Tillman again traveling eastbound on Martin Highway near Allapattah Road. Law enforcement deployed stop sticks to disable his vehicle near Interstate 95, bringing the dangerous pursuit to an end. However, Tillman refused to surrender peacefully after his vehicle was disabled. After his car was stopped, Tillman bailed out of the vehicle and attempted to flee on foot. Deputies caught up with him and took him into custody without further incident.

The arrest ended a chase that put numerous motorists at risk as Tillman traveled at excessive speeds and forced vehicles off the road during his flight from law enforcement. Tillman was arrested on multiple charges related to the chase and his driving behavior. He faces charges including fleeing and eluding, aggravated fleeing with injury or damage, operating a vehicle exceeding the speed limit by 50 or more mph, driving while license suspended or revoked, obstruction without violence, and operating a vehicle at 100 or more mph.

Tillman also had an out-of-county warrant at the time of his arrest. He was booked into the Martin County Jail. The incident highlights ongoing safety concerns on State Road 710, which has a reputation as one of the most dangerous roads in the area.

Yesterday, 5:39 PM ET

Villa Del Sol residents in St. Lucie County are returning home after nearly two years of displacement from their condos. Residents were notified last month they could return to their condos after an inspection in August 2024 found structural damage in buildings 3, 4, and 5, which were deemed unsafe. WPTV received emails sent to residents from the Board of Directors telling them they could come back, and obtained a letter from engineers sent to St. Lucie County's Building Department requesting they lift occupancy restrictions following their special inspection.

Katie Johnson was the first to share with WPTV that residents had been told they could come back. She said she almost didn't believe it and it was like too good to be true. She described work still underway at the property, noting they're still moving dirt back under the buildings. She also reflected on the effort required to bring residents home, saying it's hard work. Robert Segedin took WPTV inside his family's home, excited to return but thinking about those who cannot. He said their hearts were broken as they wanted to spend the rest of their lives there.

Eric Johnson is among those not coming back. He was one of the first residents WPTV met at Villa Del Sol and is now living in Georgia. He said it's been a terrible two years and the financial toll has been severe. He said they pretty much had to drain out all their savings accounts and all their 401ks just trying to make ends meet while this was going on. He is filing for bankruptcy and filing his condo for foreclosure. He said it's been a real struggle and that he'd like to say they are finally starting to climb out of it, but it still feels like they're kind of still falling and waiting to start climbing back up.

Katie Johnson said she is thinking of neighbors like Eric Johnson who cannot return. She said she feels so bad that some were hit harder than others with the situation. The displacement has left lasting impacts on residents, with some able to return to their homes while others face continued financial hardship that prevents their return to the community where they had planned to spend the rest of their lives.
