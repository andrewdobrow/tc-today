Florida attorney general investigates solar companies as Treasure Coast complaints mount

Yesterday, 5:19 PM ET

A wave of complaints from Treasure Coast homeowners has prompted the Florida attorney general's office to launch formal investigations into solar panel companies. At least six families have come forward with similar stories about aggressive door-to-door sales tactics that promised significant savings on electricity bills. The investigation uncovered court records and lawsuits showing a troubling pattern of elderly homeowners who say they were misled during the sales process. Seniors vs. Crime, a division of the attorney general's office, became involved after watching WPTV's initial investigation into the issue.

The financial impact on affected homeowners has been severe. Mike Schopp is locked into a 25-year, $60,000 loan and now pays nearly double his previous electricity costs. Jane Bibee's $40,000 solar loan has made it nearly impossible to sell her home. Paula Wass and David McCauley are still paying off $39,000 for panels that never reduced their nearly $300-monthly FPL bills. The couple discovered their system stopped working in November 2024, and an engineering report found moisture intrusion at the solar panel brackets, with unclear approval status for the installation brackets. Wass noted the problems were particularly frustrating because the installation occurred on a brand new home.

Data obtained by WPTV shows nearly 750 consumer complaints have been filed in Florida against three solar companies examined in the investigation. The attorney general has opened formal investigations into two of those companies, with active litigation filed against the third. Marty Jacobsen, deputy director with Seniors vs. Crime, explained that his office receives many complaints about fraudulent solar deals. "Oftentimes, these rogue products are obsolete. They'll go on your roof, they can't connect to the grid, but there's a 25-year warranty with a company that doesn't exist anymore, so you can't get service," Jacobsen said. The pattern of failed installations and defunct companies has spawned at least two class-action lawsuits against multiple solar companies.

Resources are available for affected homeowners. Seniors vs. Crime has helped multiple homeowners cancel contracts and recover money through mediation and negotiation at no cost. Some homeowners may be eligible for Florida's Construction Recovery Fund, a state fund financed by permit fees that compensates consumers for contractor misconduct. Attorneys have successfully recovered money for some homeowners through litigation. The attorney general's office and Seniors vs. Crime encourage anyone experiencing similar problems to file a complaint with the attorney general's office.

Latinas Nexus Network in Vero Beach has collected more than 100 boxes of supplies to help Venezuela recover from a devastating 7.5-magnitude earthquake. The organization is partnering with Global Empowerment Mission in Miami to transport and deliver the donations, but they need community assistance to complete their mission. Leaders say they must secure a box truck by Thursday to load the supplies and transport them to Miami on Friday.

Founder Lourana Navarro and co-founder Juliana Marino launched their collection effort Friday afternoon and were amazed by the community's response. The boxes contain essential items including diapers and medical supplies needed by people affected by the earthquake. "The community has come together. They all came forward and brought something," Marino said. The overwhelming generosity created an unexpected problem: the organization ran out of space to transport everything using personal vehicles alone.

What began as a plan to move donations in their own cars quickly became impossible as boxes continued arriving from the community. "We thought we were just going to put everything in our cars and we were going to drive to Miami. And there's no more room," Marino explained. The organization is now making an urgent appeal for someone to donate the use of a 12 to 16-foot box truck to transport all the collected supplies. For Navarro, the cause holds deep personal significance. She was born in Venezuela and has family living near the earthquake's epicenter. "It really kills me to see thousands, hundreds of thousands of people in the streets," she said, emphasizing why this relief effort matters so much to her.

Navarro and Marino say they cannot complete this relief mission without the community's continued support. "We put it together, now we need to deliver," Marino said. Anyone with access to an appropriate box truck or who wants to help in other ways is encouraged to contact Navarro at 954-205-0700 or Marino at 772-913-0805. The tight timeline means organizers need commitments quickly, as they plan to load the truck Thursday and transport the supplies to Global Empowerment Mission in Miami the following day.

The Florida Board of Governors voted 15-1 Wednesday to approve Stuart Bell as the next president of the University of Florida, concluding a search that has lasted two years. Bell, who previously served as president of the University of Alabama, expressed his commitment to the university during the meeting. "I will serve with integrity. I will serve with humility. I will serve with accountability for this group and to my Board of Trustees," he told the board. He outlined his vision to make UF the best public school in the country, saying "I sense there's a hunger for leadership. There's a hunger for this direction."

Governor Aubrey Edge, who leads First Coast Energy, cast the sole dissenting vote and expressed concerns about Bell's support for diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives. Bell directly addressed these concerns, stating "I want to be very clear and maybe we can set this aside: I'm not coming to Florida to bring DEI or woke back to the state of Florida." Bell holds a background in mechanical engineering and has held several academic leadership positions throughout his career, including serving as provost at Louisiana State University and dean of engineering at the University of Kansas.

The university has been without a permanent president since Ben Sasse resigned in 2024. The board previously attempted to hire University of Michigan President Santa Ono in 2025, but the Board of Governors opposed the decision after elected officials criticized Ono for being too liberal and supporting diversity initiatives. Bell's appointment also faced unexpected resistance from U.S. Senator Rick Scott, who criticized the university for making Bell the sole finalist in what he described as a secretive hiring process. Scott also objected to the decision to overlook interim President Donald Landry, who would receive a 2 million dollar severance package if not selected as permanent president. Board of Governors Chair Alan Levine had raised questions about whether University of Florida Chair Mori Hosseini had exceeded his authority during the hiring process.

Bell's contract will run through June 30, 2031, with an average annual salary of 2.4 million dollars, according to the Gainesville Sun. Hosseini defended the search process at Wednesday's meeting, emphasizing that the board sought more than an administrator. "From the very beginning, our board of trustees and our search committee understood that we were not simply looking for an administrator. We were looking for a leader, someone who could build on this foundation," Hosseini said.

Property tax amendment threatens $8 billion revenue cut to Florida local governments

A constitutional amendment pushed by Governor Ron DeSantis onto the November ballot could reduce local government revenues by more than $8 billion in fiscal year 2027-28 and more than $14 billion the following year, according to House staff estimates.

A few minutes ago

The property tax amendment on Florida's November ballot could reduce local government revenues by more than $8 billion in Fiscal Year 2027-28 and more than $14 billion the following year, according to House staff estimates. Miami-Dade County faces a projected $445 million revenue hit by Fiscal Year 2028-29, while Broward County faces a $326 million loss. Local governments will still need to fund fire rescue, police, drainage, roads, parks, libraries, emergency response and basic infrastructure. These costs will likely be covered through fire fees, stormwater fees, garbage assessments, utility taxes, parking fees, permit fees, special assessments, local sales tax increases, delayed maintenance and reduced services.

A coalition of organized labor, local governments, first responders, housing advocates, the Florida Education Association and the Florida Chamber of Commerce has opposed the amendment. The Chamber warned the Governor in writing that without firm guardrails, the amendment would shift the tax burden onto local businesses and renters. A formal opposition campaign called Stop Unfair Tax Shifts, chaired by former Leon County Commissioner Bryan Desloge, has launched. In 2024, North Dakota voters rejected a nearly identical property tax elimination proposal by a two-to-one margin, opposing it in all 53 counties. A lawsuit filed in Leon County Circuit Court argues the ballot summary promotes rather than explains the amendment, using campaign language like "ensuring funding for core services" and "protecting small businesses."

Consumer confidence in Florida drops to lowest level in two years

Consumer sentiment in Florida fell for the fourth consecutive month in June, dropping 1.8 points to 71.1 and reaching its lowest level in two years since May 2024, according to a University of Florida survey.

10:25 AM ET

Consumer confidence in Florida continues to decline, according to a University of Florida survey. The consumer sentiment index fell 1.8 points from May to June, dropping from 72.9 to 71.1. Over the past four months, consumer sentiment has fallen 8 points, reaching its lowest level in two years since May 2024, according to Hector H. Sandoval, Director of the Economic Analysis Program at the UF Bureau of Economic and Business Research. Sandoval attributed the pessimism to the reemergence of inflation, which continues to strain household budgets, particularly in everyday categories such as gasoline. Consumers also grew less optimistic when comparing their current finances to a year ago, with that metric dropping 2.1 points from 66.9 in May to 64.8 in June. Floridians' future economic outlook over the next year also weakened, declining from 85.2 in May to 83.9 in June.

Sandoval noted that the economic impact from the war with Iran is providing nagging concerns among consumers. Although gasoline prices have eased from their spring peak as tensions in the Middle East have moderated, some uncertainty surrounding the conflict remains. Florida's unemployment rate was 4.8 percent in May, according to FloridaCommerce, holding steady from April but notably higher than the national figure of 4.3 percent. Sandoval stated that while the national labor market has remained resilient, Florida's labor market has softened, meaning Florida households remain more sensitive to inflation and broader economic uncertainty. The UF survey was conducted between May 1 and June 22 and contacted 394 Floridians across the state by cellphone.
