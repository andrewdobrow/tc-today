Community members rally to save rare deep-sea coral reef off Fort Pierce coast

Yesterday, 1:28 AM ET

Dozens of community members gathered at the Blake Library in Martin County to protest a federally appointed council vote that rolled back longstanding protections for the Oculina Bank, a rare deep-water coral reef located just off the coast of Fort Pierce. The reef, found hundreds of feet below the surface and home to more than 2,000 marine species, exists nowhere else on Earth and thrives in deep, dark waters unlike most coral that needs shallow sunlight to survive.

The proposals to reopen parts of the reef to shrimp trawling follow a Trump Executive order issued in April 2025 that directed eight regional councils nationwide to find ways to reinvigorate fishing industries. Scientists warn that trawling destroyed as much as 90 percent of the reef before federal protections were put in place decades ago. Dr. Grant Gilmore, a scientist who previously dived the reef, told the gathering that fish depend on the coral for habitat and food. Mark Perry, Executive Director of the Florida Oceanographic Society, joined Gilmore in urging the public to help save the reef.

Bob Zales, executive director of the Southeastern Fishing Association and a generational shrimper, believes reopening shrimping access to the Oculina Bank is essential for the struggling rock shrimp industry, which he says is teetering on the brink of extinction. Council data shows the rock shrimp industry has plummeted from over 700 trips per year in the mid-1990s to under 100 since 2010. The number of active rock shrimpers dropped from 144 in 1996 to less than 30, with just 12 active boats in 2018. Zales attributes the decline to high costs, competition from imported shrimp, and closures of previously open fishing areas.

Scientists warned that reopening the reef could irreparably damage what little remains of the coral. The Oculina Bank coral grows very slowly and has been there for a long time. Perry and Gilmore emphasized that corals are in trouble worldwide and no more should be destroyed. A federal agency is now preparing a final ruling on the reef's protections, with the outcome affecting both the marine ecosystem and the struggling local shrimping industry.

Rosa Mena, 50, of Port St. Lucie has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation after operating an unlicensed medical spa from a shed behind her home in the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive. The case came to light in May 2025 when a victim reported experiencing partial facial paralysis after receiving 62 units of Botox injections at the makeshift facility called Miracle Hand and Spa. The victim had been referred to the business by coworkers who had previously used Mena's services.

After the victim developed paralysis, Mena refunded the $325 payment and asked the victim to return for a vitamin injection to reverse the issues. When the paralysis continued, the victim requested to see Mena's medical license. Mena provided an altered phlebotomy certificate that had actually expired in February 2024. She also falsely claimed to be a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

Port St. Lucie Police, along with PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax, and the Florida Department of Health, executed a search warrant at Mena's residence. Detectives discovered medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines, and injection devices for fillers and Botox.

Mena was convicted on multiple charges including three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, fraud charges for obtaining property through swindles, and use of a two-way communication device to facilitate a felony. The case was reported by Emma Romano and highlights the dangers of unlicensed medical procedures being performed in residential settings.

Florida property tax cut proposal heads to voters in November

Florida lawmakers passed a property tax cut proposal that would increase the homestead exemption from $50,000 to $250,000 over two years. The measure requires approval from 60 percent of voters in November.

18 hours ago

Florida lawmakers on Tuesday passed a property tax cut proposal that would increase the homestead exemption from $50,000 to $250,000 over two years, with inflation adjustments beginning in 2029. The bill would still need approval from 60 percent of voters in November. The measure would also lower assessment caps on non-homesteaded properties from 10 percent to 5 percent.

Experts are divided on what the ripple effects could be if the measure passes. Real estate attorney Steve Wallace, based in Boynton Beach, raised the possibility of new fees tied to local services such as the sheriff's office and fire rescue, as counties and local governments would need to find alternative ways to derive revenue. CPA Paul Miller suggested businesses could end up carrying more of the financial burden to offset lost revenue through increased annual fees.

Municipal attorney Raul Gastesi offered a different perspective, suggesting the measure could actually push home prices higher because home buyers would have more money at their disposal. Realtor Danielle Blake was skeptical of that theory, pointing to interest rates as a more significant barrier to sales than property taxes alone. All four experts acknowledged there are significant unknowns about how counties, municipalities, and markets might respond to decreasing property tax revenue. If the ballot measure passes, lawmakers would need to return to Tallahassee to work out the specifics in a bill to implement the changes.
