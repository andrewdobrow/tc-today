6:41 PM ET

Demolition began this week at Murray Middle School in Martin County, marking the start of a major reconstruction effort following tornado damage caused by Hurricane Milton. The project includes reconstruction of buildings destroyed in the tornadoes, along with a new administration building, media center, art lab, and a full overhaul of the parking lot. The demolition phase alone costs $3.3 million, though the total project cost has not been finalized. The project is being funded by local capital improvement, sales tax and Public Education Capital Outlay.

Students who were displaced from the affected classrooms will continue learning in portable classrooms until construction is complete. Kirstie Gernaine, coordinator of public information and community relations for the Martin County School District, explained that the school was already scheduled for significant improvements before the hurricane struck. "Murray Middle School was already slated to get all of this construction work with a new admin area, a new media center and a brand new art lab," said Gernaine. "With Hurricane Milton and those tornadoes destroying the buildings in the back buildings 18, 10, and nine we were able to add these on."

The tornadoes destroyed buildings 18, 10, and nine located in the back of the campus. The district was able to incorporate reconstruction of those buildings into the planned improvements that were already scheduled. This allowed the school to address both the planned upgrades and the tornado damage in a single comprehensive project. The Martin County School District hopes to complete the renovations by December 2027.

The school district said parents can be assured their students will be safe during construction. Barriers have already been placed around the work zone to keep students out of the construction area. The safety measures are designed to separate the active construction site from areas where students attend classes and move around campus. While the demolition and reconstruction work continues, school operations will proceed with displaced students using portable classrooms as temporary learning spaces until the new permanent structures are completed.

6:22 PM ET

The St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office is intensifying traffic enforcement this summer, deploying additional deputies and launching a new targeting initiative aimed at reducing speeding and reckless driving across the county. Sheriff Richard Del Toro said traffic is the top concern among residents and described the crackdown as a quality of life issue. Del Toro is using extra deputies from the sheriff's school resource division to boost enforcement while the school year is on break and is placing top-performing deputies in areas of concern.

Deputies Christopher Rodrigues and Cheyenne Benning are among those targeting high-problem corridors, including Midway Road, U.S. Highway 1 and Indian River Drive. Benning said the county sees a lot of excessive speeding and that most recently she had someone pass her on I-95 doing 105 mph. Rodrigues said he stopped a driver doing 72 mph on Midway Road and another doing 98 mph on U.S. 1. On Indian River Drive, where the speed limit is 25 mph, Rodrigues said his top recorded speed was 73 mph.

In the past four weeks, deputies conducted 1,162 traffic stops, wrote 470 citations and issued 1,728 written warnings. The enforcement push is part of a broader strategy Del Toro put in place when he took office. He eliminated the agency's dedicated traffic unit and made traffic enforcement the responsibility of every deputy. Since 2025, the sheriff's office has issued more than 26,000 citations, a 155% increase.

Local drivers described what they see on county roads as serious problems. Ashley, a St. Lucie County resident, said the situation is insane and that nobody slows down. Jessica, another local driver, said drivers are careless and cut others off. Del Toro said he has a family that drives on these roadways and cares about his loved ones and everyone else's loved ones. He said he wants to make this a safer community.

JetBlue cancels Vero Beach to New York JFK flights after nine months, citing low demand

JetBlue is ending its direct flights between New York's JFK Airport and Vero Beach Regional Airport, citing low passenger demand for the route that launched in December. The airline announced that service between the two airports will end Sept. 9, just nine months after the route began operating with much fanfare and optimistic passengers. JetBlue will continue serving Vero Bea...

11:43 AM ET

JetBlue is ending its direct flights between New York's JFK Airport and Vero Beach Regional Airport, citing low passenger demand for the route that launched in December. The airline announced that service between the two airports will end Sept. 9, just nine months after the route began operating with much fanfare and optimistic passengers. JetBlue will continue serving Vero Beach through its Boston route, and all affected customers will be notified directly by the airline and will receive full refunds.

"Demand for this new route has not materialized as we had hoped," JetBlue said in a statement to WPTV. The decision marks a setback for Vero Beach Regional Airport's efforts to expand commercial service in recent years. The JFK route was seen as a major win for the Treasure Coast when it launched in December 2025, providing direct access to one of the nation's busiest airports and potentially boosting tourism to the area.

The inaugural flight brought enthusiastic passengers who called the service a game changer for connecting family and friends between New York and the Treasure Coast. However, those hopes did not translate into sustained passenger numbers sufficient to keep the route viable. While JetBlue's Boston route continues to serve travelers seeking alternatives to larger South Florida airports, the loss of the JFK connection removes what had been viewed as a significant transportation option for the region.

JetBlue has not announced whether it plans to add any replacement routes from Vero Beach or if it will adjust frequency on the remaining Boston service. Passengers with upcoming reservations on the discontinued route are encouraged to contact JetBlue directly or check their email for rebooking options and refund information. The cancellation serves as a reminder that new airline routes, even those launched with considerable excitement and perceived demand, require sustained passenger support to remain economically viable for carriers.

Three gymnasts from Flip City Gym and Cheer earned a top 15 finish at AAU Nationals in Knoxville, Tennessee last month and qualified for the Junior Olympics. Skylar Rose-Daris, Victoria Supan, and Charli Rine competed as a team after advancing through local competitions, states, and regionals during what Head Coach Candice Lazuka described as a strong season.

The team came close to a perfect score at the national competition. Lazuka said the gymnasts scored a 9.9, approaching the rare perfect 10. The finish qualified the team for the Junior Olympics in Iowa next month. Lazuka noted the trio competed locally and traveled before advancing to states and regionals during what she called a strong season for the program.

Each gymnast found her way to the sport through a different path. Rose-Daris said it started with a simple skill. "It wasn't really gymnastics, it was the fact that I knew how to do a cartwheel," Rose-Daris said. Supan drew inspiration from watching elite athletes perform. "All the gymnasts that I saw on YouTube, and how amazing they are, and how fun it looks," Supan said. Rine said the appeal lies in continuous growth. "I love it because I learn new things," Rine said.

The gymnasts expressed excitement about competing at the Junior Olympics next month. Rine has a specific target heading into the competition. "Getting first place on floors," Rine said. Rose-Daris emphasized the travel opportunity the competition presents. "It's going to be really fun that I'm going to an entire different part of the world," Rose-Daris said. Supan described her feelings about the upcoming event simply. "It's happy and exciting at the same time," Supan said. The team will represent Flip City Gym and Cheer in Iowa as they compete against other top qualifiers from across the country.

The Palm Beach County Food Bank is located at 701 Boutwell Road, Suite A-1, in Lake Worth Beach.

1:35 PM ET

Security First Insurance is lowering homeowners insurance rates by an average of 5.6 percent, the latest in a series of rate decreases the Florida-based company has made over the last two years. Insurance companies have been reducing rates or holding them stable as more companies cite a reduction in lawsuits from state reforms. Melissa Burt DeVriese, president of Security First, said the company is required to pass savings along to customers. "All those savings we're required to pass along to customers, and we have been," Burt DeVriese said.

Security First also announced new discounts and a disappearing deductible that rewards claims-free customers by reducing their deductible over time. The program lowers the deductible by 20 percent each year for policyholders who remain claims-free, continuing over a five-year period. "Over the course of five years, every year your deductible goes down 20 percent as long as you don't have a loss; once you have a loss, the discounted deductible applies, and then it resets back to the full deductible amount, so it rewards consumers who don't have losses," Burt DeVriese said. Once a customer files a claim, the reduced deductible applies to that claim, and then the deductible resets to its original full amount.

Based in Ormond Beach, Security First has about 155,000 policies in the state. The company is working to expand its customer base in parts of the state where insurance costs remain elevated. "Security First is committed to writing more policies in southeast Florida," Burt DeVriese said. The company's customers have been experiencing the benefits of the rate reductions the insurer has implemented. "Our customers are seeing the impact of our rate decreases," Burt DeVriese said.

Premiums in South Florida generally run high, and online firm Insurify reports that in 2026 Florida homeowners pay the highest average premium in the country at $8,292. State insurance regulators said that with more companies and more competition, homeowners should seek better premiums by shopping around. The recent trend of rate decreases and rate stability among Florida insurance companies reflects the effects of state reforms that have reduced lawsuit activity in the insurance sector.

A Palm City WPTV Weather Spotter with a lifelong passion for meteorology spoke with WPTV First Alert Weather Meteorologist James Wieland about how weather forecasting has evolved over the decades. Brian Botley served in the Navy, where mapping the weather was part of his duties. His interest in weather began as a child in Oakland Park, where he moved in 1951. Botley said his father was always plotting the hurricanes, which sparked his interest. He went into the Navy and was selected to go to the Aerographer's Mate School in Lakehurst. His first job was plotting weather maps. Botley said there were some satellites starting in 1961 or 1962, but it wasn't the coverage they have now.

Wieland showed Botley the technology used in today's weather coverage, which is a far cry from the tools available during his Navy days, and walked him through how computer models and modern radar systems have transformed forecasting. Botley said joining WPTV's Weather Spotter program felt like a natural extension of something he was already doing on his own. He said he does this all the time just on his own and thought it may be fun because there are other people there.

Registration is now open for Port St. Lucie City University, a free nine-session program designed to help residents better understand how their local government operates.

Classes begin Wednesday, Aug. 12, and will be held from 5:30 to 8:30 p.m. in the Council Chambers at Port St. Lucie City Hall, located at 121 SW Port St. Lucie Blvd.

Participants will learn about City departments, municipal services and the decision-making processes that shape the community. The program also gives residents opportunities to meet City staff, ask questions and gain a closer look at the work involved in operating one of Florida's largest cities.

Residents who complete the course will receive a certificate of completion and may also choose to become a City Ambassador, helping share information about City programs and services throughout the community.

City University is intended to encourage civic engagement while strengthening connections between residents and local government. Since the program began in 2016, more than 600 residents have participated.

Enrollment is limited to 160 participants.

Residents who are unable to attend the in-person classes can also access City U on Demand, which offers a more flexible way to learn about Port St. Lucie government and municipal operations.
