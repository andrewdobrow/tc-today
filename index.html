Yesterday, 5:40 PM ET

Indian River County residents face a Fair Market Rent of $1,222 for a one-bedroom apartment, according to 2026 data released by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development. This figure positions Indian River County in the mid-range of South Florida rental markets, higher than more affordable rural areas like Okeechobee County at $1,032 but substantially lower than premium markets in the region.

HUD's Fair Market Rent data shows significant price variations across South Florida counties. Belle Glade in Palm Beach County offers the lowest rates at $890 for a one-bedroom, while Wellington in Palm Beach County has the highest at $2,850. The data reflects current market conditions based on surveys, rental listings and market adjustments. These figures serve as benchmarks for housing assistance programs but do not impose rent control limits on landlords. St. Lucie and Martin areas fall between Indian River and Wellington, with rates of $1,467 for one-bedroom apartments.

Real estate professionals point to several factors driving these disparities. Talbot Sutter, president of Sutter and Nugent Real Estate, attributes Wellington's premium pricing to quality amenities and location advantages. "A lot of it has to do with school districts, especially Wellington. They have like top A-rated schools, lifestyle, they have so many aspects from equestrian to outdoor activities," Sutter said. He noted that demand for properties with more acreage and land has increased even in the rental market, particularly in wealthy enclaves like Wellington, western Lake Worth, Delray Beach and Boca Raton. Sutter's clients often include executives from New York City seeking luxury rentals before purchasing homes.

For renters navigating South Florida's challenging market, Sutter recommends conducting thorough research before committing to a lease. "Know the market, do your comps. You have to really, really know the area if you don't want to get ripped off paying rent," he said. Residents throughout the region report difficulty affording housing as rental prices continue climbing faster than income growth. The rental disparities highlight broader housing affordability challenges affecting South Florida communities across various income levels.

Yesterday, 1:31 PM ET

Port St. Lucie's Drone as First Responder program achieved immediate success during its first day of testing and evaluation on Wednesday, locating a missing autistic woman near a canal before officers arrived on scene. Police received a report of an autistic female walking through a wooded area adjacent to a canal bank. A DFR drone was remotely deployed from the police department and reached the scene faster than responding officers, quickly identifying both the missing woman and the person who reported her. Drone operators kept visual contact with both individuals and relayed their exact location to responding officers, enabling a safe reunion with her family. Police Chief Leo Niemczyk emphasized the program's immediate impact, stating "This program is about saving lives, and on day one, that's exactly what it helped us do." He added that "the successful location of a vulnerable autistic female near a canal before officers arrived demonstrates the tremendous value this technology brings to our community."

The agency completed 17 missions during the first day of operations. Beyond the missing person case, drones responded to several other emergency situations. They provided aerial surveillance during a burglary in progress, monitoring the area while officers approached and cleared the residence. During a domestic violence investigation, a drone arrived before patrol officers and spotted a suspect hiding in a backyard, providing real-time video feed to responding officers and supervisors. The drones also responded to multiple disturbance calls involving groups of minors, helping officers assess the situations and determine appropriate responses.

The DFR program integrates directly with the police department's computer-aided dispatch system. When emergency calls come in, trained personnel remotely launch drones from the station, placing surveillance on scene before officers arrive. In most cases, drones reach locations within seconds, with the longest travel time in the coverage area being approximately four minutes. Each drone operates for an average of 25 to 35 minutes per flight, depending on weather conditions. Currently, three drone docks are active as part of the testing phase, with plans to expand to six docks positioned throughout the city once the program becomes fully operational.

The DFR team consists of 12 specially trained officers. Police say drones may be deployed for active threats, burglaries, robberies, shootings, kidnappings, barricaded subjects, school emergencies, felony traffic stops, fleeing suspects, locating missing people, vehicle crashes, hazardous materials incidents, fires, and traffic monitoring. Chief Niemczyk noted that "technology will never replace our officers. But when technology allows us to locate a vulnerable person faster, improve officer safety, and provide better service to our residents, it's an investment worth making."

Senior Manager Kelly Ellis emphasized that every purchase made at either location directly supports the animals in the organization's care. The stores welcome donations of gently used items from the community, giving shoppers a way to contribute to both the animal rescue mission and the renovation efforts simultaneously. This approach has created an unexpected benefit by bringing adoptable pets into spaces where community members shop regularly.

Beyond the cats staying at the thrift store locations, the Humane Society is actively recruiting temporary foster homes for other animals affected by the construction. This multi-pronged approach allows the organization to continue its full range of services even as the facility undergoes necessary improvements. Animal shelters across the country face similar challenges when buildings require upgrades, but the Treasure Coast shelter's strategy demonstrates how creative problem-solving can maintain community engagement while addressing infrastructure needs.

The two thrift stores are located at 3302 SE Federal Highway in Stuart and 1099 NW 21st St, also in Stuart. Anyone interested in shopping, donating items, or learning about foster opportunities can visit hstc1.org for store hours and donation guidelines. Before C.H. Robinson selected White Hawk Carriers to transport the load, the trucking company had already accumulated a documented unsafe federal safety record, according to the complaint. The amendment will appear on the November 2026 General Election Ballot and requires approval from at least 60 percent of voters to become law.

Baxter confirmed the decision exclusively to WPTV reporter Michael Hoffman, saying her initial run for Congress was motivated by her desire to help more people in her community. In a statement Thursday night, Baxter said she had the honor of speaking with President Donald Trump on the phone and is grateful for his tremendous leadership.

Trump, who had endorsed Baxter's campaign to stay on the county commission, posted on Truth Social Thursday night expressing his preference that she remain on the commission rather than run for Congress. "Sara Baxter is a fantastic person, and highly qualified for many top political jobs but, on a somewhat selfish basis, I would rather have her stay on the Palm Beach County Commission (where so many important things are happening!), than run for Congress," Trump said. Political analyst Brian Crowley said that without Trump's endorsement for Baxter's run for Congress, the race would be an uphill battle. Crowley noted that if Trump stays quiet or endorses somebody else, it may make the hill she has to climb even higher.

The team, known as the "Blue Wave," drew enthusiastic supporters eager to see their favorite players before the tournament debut. Marionella Barzeycoggins, originally from Curaçao, brought her whole family after learning about the training event in Boca Raton. Curaçao faces Germany this Sunday in Houston, Texas.
