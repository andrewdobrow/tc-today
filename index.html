Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

A dispute over a missing fence at a public beach access point in Juno Beach is creating friction between condo residents and summer visitors. The town removed the fence at the JB-7 walkway in 2023 for safety reasons, but residents at the Colony Condos now say the removal has created problems. With only bushes marking the boundary between public access and private property, residents report that beachgoers are regularly cutting through their land.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, described the impact during peak summer months. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Hill said residents used to directly confront trespassers about being on private property, but those interactions often turned confrontational. Now, residents call police to handle the situation instead. The repeated trespassing has made the boundary between public and private land unclear to beachgoers.

Not all beach users agree there is a serious problem. Michael House, a beachgoer who walks to JB-7 from a public parking lot blocks away, said he has not witnessed the problems residents describe. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that."

The issue was brought before the Juno Beach Town Council, where a town official noted that the town is not legally required to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring lower-cost alternatives. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. Residents hope the town will reinstall the fence to restore clear property lines and protect their landscaping and parking areas. The town council agreed to address the question during its budget discussions scheduled for July 15. A full replacement fence could cost the town six figures, making the decision a significant budget consideration.

Misty Dawn White, 46, died in a house fire at her Hobe Sound home on Monday. Investigators from the sheriff's office have preliminarily identified an electrical issue as a possible cause, though the investigation remains ongoing. White's fiancé Mark learned of her death and has since received support from their community as he faces the aftermath of losing both his home and his future wife.

Nina Fenn, who had been close friends with White for eight years, learned about the tragedy through a phone call from Mark. Fenn later returned to the scene to process her grief and has been vocal about supporting Mark during this difficult time. "It's just a terrible shame and awful loss of life," Fenn said. Friends have organized a fundraiser to help Mark rebuild his life after the fire destroyed many of his and White's belongings. According to Fenn, "It's completely a state of devastation."

Fenn shared memories of White that paint a picture of a generous and caring person. White was a Christian woman who had many passions, including gardening, plants, and playing the piano. Fenn recalled that White was always willing to help others. "She was always the first to lend a hand. She loves kids; she loves her kids. She loves people," Fenn said. Those who knew White hope to keep her memory alive through the impact she made on their lives. "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts," Fenn said.

The timing of White's death has compounded the tragedy. Her birthday fell on the Fourth of July, meaning she passed away just days before celebrating another year. The loss is made more difficult by Mark's previous hardship. Fenn explained that Mark had already endured significant grief when he lost his only child, Jacob, in a previous tragedy. "Mark lost his one and only child, Jacob, and lost him tragically. So, I just am trying whatever I can to support him," Fenn said. As the community continues to rally around Mark, those who knew and loved White are finding solace in remembering her spirit and generosity.

Beth Clark, 48, received a diagnosis of invasive ductal carcinoma, the most common type of breast cancer, three months ago. She decided to undergo a full double bilateral mastectomy to eliminate any possibility of cancer returning. However, less than 24 hours before her scheduled reconstruction surgery, her insurance company, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, denied coverage for the procedure. According to the denial letter, the surgery involving a surgical mesh called GalaFlex was considered investigational with insufficient proof that it improves patient health outcomes.

GalaFlex is a synthetic surgical mesh that has become contentious in breast reconstruction treatment. Although plastic surgeons favor it and the FDA has approved it for certain uses elsewhere in the body, it lacks FDA approval specifically for breast reconstruction and remains classified as an off-label, investigational treatment. In a 2023 letter to health care providers, the FDA maintained that the efficacy and safety of GalaFlex for breast cancer patients have not been determined. Anthem stated in response to Clark's case that the mesh does not currently meet the standards required for coverage under its medical policy and that patients retain the right to appeal such decisions.

Dr. Alicia Billington, Clark's plastic surgeon, uses GalaFlex mesh on most of her breast reconstruction patients. She contends that while no treatment is risk-free, the product produces fewer complications compared to other established options. Billington has become vocal about criticizing insurance denials, joining other doctors who are taking their frustrations public and calling out specific insurers. She emphasizes that existing published literature and data from plastic surgeons support GalaFlex as the gold standard in breast reconstruction with implants and is requesting that insurers review this evidence. Last year, after investigative reporting highlighted breast cancer patients' denial stories as part of a "Patient No More" series, Cigna became the first and only major insurer to begin approving GalaFlex for breast reconstruction.

Despite Anthem's denial, Clark proceeded with her reconstruction surgery using GalaFlex mesh a little more than a week after the initial rejection. Billington found an alternative mechanism to make the procedure possible. Clark emphasized that her decision to move forward was based on trust in her medical care team, who she said were seeing excellent results and outcomes in their patients. She and Billington continue challenging the denial. Additionally, a new bill has been introduced to modernize the Women's Health and Cancer Rights Act, the federal law designed to protect breast cancer patients from treatment denials during their cancer care journeys.

They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had." The demanding schedule is designed both to test the team and to draw attention to the program.

With a determined veteran class leading the way, the Scots believe this is their year to finally break through and compete for a state title.

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. The center said guests can participate in seasonal experiences including guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. According to the center, the programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn more about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The center's Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations during the programs may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. Loggerhead Marinelife Center said its education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

Yesterday, 2:38 PM ET

St. Petersburg has chosen The Burg Bid, a proposal led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, as its preferred path forward for transforming Tropicana Field and the surrounding Historic Gas Plant District. The $8.1 billion plan would convert stadium parking and public land into a mixed-use neighborhood featuring housing, hotels, offices, retail, cultural institutions, workforce training, public art and entertainment venues. The development team proposes purchasing 58 acres of city-owned land for $275 million, valuing the full land package at $405 million when including $130 million in land the city would retain. They plan to deposit $20 million when a purchase and sale agreement is finalized.

The proposal centers on Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre central green space designed to serve as a civic and cultural hub rather than organizing around a new stadium as previous plans had done. The park would anchor 18.5 acres of total public park space and house The Woodson African American Museum of Florida, a planned Museum of Public Art, a Discovery Center, event lawns, a playground, a splash pad, cafe areas, public art and a concert lawn. The Woodson would serve as "an anchor and a civic landmark for the Gas Plant District," positioned within Samuel Davis Park along a diagonal pedestrian corridor called the Legacy Link. The proposal heavily emphasizes the history of the former Gas Plant neighborhood, a predominantly Black community demolished decades ago to build the stadium. Plans include preserving the original gas tank footprint, restoring Booker Creek from its current state as a drainage ditch, and constructing nine bridges as a reference to the nine bridges that once connected the historic area. A reimagined Pinellas Trail would integrate with wide pedestrian paths and a community park along the creek.

The development calls for 3,600 income-restricted housing units built over 15 years, with 1,800 units in the Historic Gas Plant District and another 1,800 on complementary sites elsewhere in St. Petersburg. The plan also includes homeownership opportunities at discounts to market value through partner Blue Sky Communities, a Florida-based affordable housing developer. The district would be constructed in three phases lasting approximately five years each, with early development potentially beginning outside the Tropicana Field footprint before stadium demolition. The Rays' lease ends after the 2028 season.

Phase 1 would establish cultural and workforce anchors including the Woodson and the Collaboratorium, a workforce training and small-business development center operating as "St. Petersburg's Capital Factory." Phase 2 would develop new mixed-income neighborhoods and strengthen connections with adjacent districts. Phase 3 would complete the neighborhood with a 4,000 to 5,000-seat Resilience Event Center featuring 100,000 square feet of flexible space for concerts, performances and events, plus a Discovery Center and St. Pete Surf Wave attraction.

Yesterday, 6:16 PM ET

Costco is moving forward with plans to build its first store on the Treasure Coast in Port St. Lucie. The retailer has announced the development of a new warehouse location in the city, marking the first Costco warehouse to serve Martin, St. Lucie, and Indian River counties. The membership warehouse shopping model will arrive in the Treasure Coast region for the first time with this new location.

The timing of Costco's expansion to Port St. Lucie aligns with significant infrastructure development underway in the city. Port St. Lucie is working to expand Midway Road and add a partial interchange to Florida's Turnpike to accommodate growth and improve accessibility throughout the region. The Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to give 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to assist with the interchange project. Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, noted that "there's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie." He explained that the interchange is designed to address accessibility needs and support the city's growth trajectory. "This is a way for us to try to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years," Samples said.

The Florida Department of Transportation is managing a $95 million Midway Road Widening Project to help reduce congestion and improve safety. Crews are currently working on the first phase, widening the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second widening phase, which will extend from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, is still in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027. Additionally, FDOT is improving the Turnpike bridge and working on a partial interchange that remains in the design phase.

While construction activity has been noticeable to residents, many recognize its necessity. Port St. Lucie resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged the disruption but saw the value in the improvements. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," she said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." Beyond road widening, the Midway Road project will also add bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks to improve multimodal transportation options. The city council is scheduled to vote on final approval to transfer the land at an upcoming meeting. These infrastructure improvements, combined with Costco's arrival, signal significant development momentum for Port St. Lucie.

Fourth of July Cookout Costs Rise 4 Percent Across Florida

The rise reflects broader inflationary pressures affecting grocery prices across the state and nation. Several key items driving the increase stand out...

Yesterday, 6:43 PM ET

The cost of hosting a Fourth of July cookout for 10 people has climbed to $73.82 this year, representing a 4 percent increase from the previous year, according to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey. The rise reflects broader inflationary pressures affecting grocery prices across the state and nation. Several key items driving the increase stand out as particularly expensive compared to last year's prices.

Ground beef prices have jumped 5.5 percent, while hamburger buns rose even more sharply at 7.7 percent. Strawberries have been hit especially hard, with prices climbing 12.4 percent due to damage from a Florida frost that affected the state's crop. Ice cream, a popular dessert at summer gatherings, has also become more expensive with a 5.3 percent price increase. These rising costs are forcing many families to reconsider their holiday meal planning and budgets.

Not all food prices have increased, however. Potato prices are down this year, offering relief to shoppers looking for affordable sides like potato chips and salads. Corn prices have shown more volatility throughout the season, though locally grown corn from the Glades region can provide better pricing options for budget-conscious shoppers. Finding these deals requires some strategy and attention to what's available in local markets.

According to budgeting expert Andrea Woroch, families can take several steps to manage cookout expenses without sacrificing quality or variety. She recommends shopping strategically by comparing prices across different stores to identify the best deals for items on your grocery list. Creating a meal plan before shopping is another effective approach, as it helps families avoid impulse purchases and unnecessary spending. Woroch also emphasizes that cutting down on food waste is crucial for keeping overall costs in check. By planning meals carefully and using ingredients efficiently, families can offset some of the price increases and still enjoy a festive Fourth of July celebration without breaking the bank.
