Riviera Beach named only Florida city to receive 2026 All-America City Award

Riviera Beach has earned a prestigious national recognition as one of just 10 U.S. communities selected for the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city stands alone as Florida's sole recipient of this honor. The award celebrates communities where residents, local government, businesses and organizations unite to address local problems and produce me...

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned a prestigious national recognition as one of just 10 U.S. communities selected for the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city stands alone as Florida's sole recipient of this honor. The award celebrates communities where residents, local government, businesses and organizations unite to address local problems and produce meaningful change. City leaders announced the achievement at the National Civic League conference in Denver, where they presented Riviera Beach's approach to building community engagement.

Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the honor, calling Riviera Beach "one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He credited the award to the city's long-term revitalization efforts aimed at improving residents' quality of life. "They're seeing the changes; they're seeing the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do," Lawson said. City leaders emphasize that the recognition reflects sustained work to strengthen civic health, build trust between residents and government, and give community members a genuine voice in shaping the city's future.

Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson attributed the award to residents themselves. "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents," Miller-Anderson said. "Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another." She noted that the designation reinforces the city's positive momentum and community pride. According to city officials, the award demonstrates that collaboration drives results and shows the nation that residents remain deeply involved in directing their community's future.

The application showcased several civic programs, including the Riviera Beach Youth Council, which engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy, the Citizens Leadership Academy with a 98 percent completion rate, and the Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program, which offers mini-grants for critical home repairs to longtime homeowners. Miller-Anderson emphasized the broader commitment behind these initiatives. "We're doing a lot of innovative things to make sure that our community is able to say that they are part of what's happening here in the city," she said. The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, ranks among the nation's most respected civic honors. City leaders say the designation celebrates progress and positions Riviera Beach for future opportunities and continued development. Other 2026 award winners include communities in Massachusetts, Tennessee, Texas, Alabama, North Carolina, Virginia, South Carolina, and Oregon.

Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

A dispute over a missing fence at a public beach access point in Juno Beach is creating friction between condo residents and summer beachgoers, with replacement costs potentially reaching six figures for the town. Residents at the Colony Condos say that since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons, people using the JB-7 public walkway have been cutting through their private property to reach the beach. Currently, only bushes separate the public access path from the private land, making it easy for visitors to cross over.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, explained that the problem intensifies during summer months. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Residents initially tried confronting trespassers to inform them about the private property boundary, but Hill said those interactions often turned hostile. Now residents call police to handle the situation when unauthorized parking and trespassing occur.

Not all community members agree on the severity of the issue. Michael House, a beachgoer who walks to JB-7 from a public parking lot nearby, disputed the residents' characterization of the problem. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that." The matter reached the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where officials clarified that the town is not obligated to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring more affordable options rather than a full replacement. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said.

Hill expressed hope that town leadership would reconsider the fence removal. "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking," Hill said. The council agreed to include the fence question in budget discussions scheduled for July 15, when officials will determine whether to allocate funds for a replacement or pursue alternative solutions to address the boundary issue.

Misty Dawn White, 46, died Monday after a fire broke out inside her Hobe Sound home where she lived with her fiancé Mark. Authorities discovered her body at the scene. The sheriff's office is investigating the blaze, with preliminary findings suggesting an electrical issue may have caused the fire. The investigation remains ongoing as officials work to determine the exact cause.

Mark learned of the tragedy and reached out to those closest to him to share the devastating news. Nina Fenn, who had been friends with White for eight years, received a phone call from Mark informing her that White had died in the fire. The loss struck the community hard, prompting friends to launch a fundraiser to help Mark rebuild his life. "It's completely a state of devastation," Fenn said. The flames consumed many of the couple's belongings, leaving them with significant material losses alongside their emotional pain.

Fenn spoke about who White was to those who knew her. "Misty was a Christian woman. She loved gardening and plants. She plays the piano," Fenn said. "She was always the first to lend a hand. She loves kids; she loves her kids. She loves people." White's generosity and warmth defined her relationships, and Fenn reflected on how she wants her friend to be remembered. "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts," Fenn said. The depth of White's character became even more apparent as friends gathered to support Mark during this crisis.

The timing of White's death added profound sadness for everyone who cared about her. White's birthday was the Fourth of July, meaning she died just days before what should have been a celebration of her life. The tragedy compounded grief Mark was already carrying. "Mark lost his one and only child, Jacob, and lost him tragically," Fenn explained. "So, I just am trying whatever I can to support him." Fenn's commitment to standing by Mark reflected the broader community response to his loss, as friends worked to help him navigate the aftermath of losing White while continuing to process the earlier loss of his son.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football program is working hard this summer with a clear goal: winning a state championship during the 2026 fall season. Last year, the team finished with a 10-2 record and claimed a district title, but a second-round playoff loss has motivated the players and coaching staff to push further this year. Head football coach Jimmy Robertson believes the senior class has the experience and drive to make this the best season in program history.

Robertson noted that most of the current seniors have been with the program for four years, with many joining through the middle school feeder program. Because they have experienced success throughout their careers, Robertson said "hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had." With this veteran group leading the way, the program is confident it can take the next step toward a state title.

To prepare for a deeper playoff run, Saint Andrew's has intentionally scheduled tougher opponents this fall. Senior Jack Williams explained the reasoning behind this decision, saying the team wanted to compete against strong programs while also attracting new talent to the Scots. Senior Jack Whelche emphasized that the team's focus is purely on advancing further in the playoffs, noting that an undefeated regular season does not guarantee success if the team continues to exit in the second round. For Whelche, true improvement this season means going beyond last year's playoff performance.

The players are using summer workouts as daily motivation to build toward their ambitious goals. Senior Edmyer St. Jean said the training sessions give the team something to look forward to each day and help maintain focus on breaking their current record. As the Scots prepare for the 2026 season, the combination of experienced leadership, a challenging schedule, and dedicated summer preparation has the program positioned to make a serious run at the state title they are targeting.

Jun 30, 11:20 AM ET

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The center's Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. Loggerhead Marinelife Center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

St. Petersburg selects $8.1 billion vision to redevelop Tropicana Field site

St. Petersburg Mayor Ken Welch has chosen The Burg Bid as the city's preferred proposal for transforming the Tropicana Field site and surrounding Historic Gas Plant District. Yesterday, 2:38 PM ET

St. Petersburg Mayor Ken Welch has chosen The Burg Bid as the city's preferred proposal for transforming the Tropicana Field site and surrounding Historic Gas Plant District. The plan, led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, proposes an $8.1 billion mixed-use development that would replace the previous stadium-centered approach with a district organized around Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre civic and cultural hub.

The development team would purchase 58 acres of city-owned land for $275 million, with the full land package valued at $405 million when including $130 million in land the city would retain. A $20 million deposit would be required when a purchase and sale agreement is finalized. Unlike earlier redevelopment plans centered on constructing a new baseball stadium, The Burg Bid proposal emphasizes cultural institutions and public space. Samuel Davis Park would anchor the district and include The Woodson African American Museum of Florida, a planned Museum of Public Art, a Discovery Center, event lawns, a playground, a splash pad, and a concert lawn. The park represents part of 18.5 acres of total public park space planned for the site. Blake Investment Partners CEO Thompson Whitney Blake described the project as resting "on respect for the community that once existed here, informing and educating the future generations."

The proposal emphasizes reconnecting with the Historic Gas Plant neighborhood, a predominantly Black community demolished decades ago for the stadium. The plan would preserve the original gas tank footprint within Samuel Davis Park and create a pedestrian corridor called the Legacy Link connecting City Hall with the Manhattan Casino and the Deuces District. Nine planned bridges over Booker Creek reference nine bridges that historically crossed the Gas Plant area, while restoration efforts would transform the creek from a drainage ditch back into a central natural feature with walking paths and community parks.

The development would deliver 3,600 income-restricted housing units over 15 years, with 1,800 units within the Historic Gas Plant District and 1,800 on complementary sites elsewhere in St. Petersburg. The plan includes homeownership opportunities at discounts to market value through partnership with affordable housing developer Blue Sky Communities. The project would be completed in three phases of approximately five years each, with early development beginning outside the stadium footprint before demolition. Phase 1 would establish cultural anchors and the Collaboratorium, a workforce training and small-business center. Phase 2 would develop mixed-income neighborhoods and strengthen connections to surrounding districts. Phase 3 would complete the project with a 4,000 to 5,000-seat Resilience Event Center, a Discovery Center, and a surf wave attraction. The team's lease with the city ends after the 2028 season.

Yesterday, 6:16 PM ET

Costco is planning to open its first Treasure Coast store in Port St. Lucie. The warehouse retailer has announced plans for the new location, marking a significant retail expansion for the region that includes Martin, St. Lucie, and Indian River counties. Port St. Lucie residents have not previously had access to a Costco warehouse in the immediate area, making this development an important addition to local shopping options.

The timing of this announcement comes as Port St. Lucie undergoes major infrastructure improvements to support continued growth. The city is actively working on a $95 million Midway Road Widening Project designed to address congestion and safety concerns. The first phase of construction is currently underway, expanding the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, which will widen the road from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027.

In addition to the Midway Road improvements, Port St. Lucie is working to enhance accessibility in the region. The city council approved the first reading of an ordinance to donate 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to support a partial interchange on Florida's Turnpike. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the interchange addresses a critical gap in current access points. "There's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie," Samples said. He emphasized that the project aims to accommodate growth that has already occurred and growth expected over the next 20 to 50 years. The partial Turnpike interchange remains in the design phase. The Midway Road Widening Project will also include bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks.

While construction activity has been noticeable for residents, local support for the improvements appears strong. Port St. Lucie resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged the inconvenience of ongoing work but recognized its necessity. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," Scheumeister said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." The city council is expected to vote on final approval to donate the land at an upcoming meeting. These infrastructure investments position Port St. Lucie to better serve both current residents and future population growth while supporting new commercial development like the planned Costco location.

Indian River State College operates a Promise Program designed to help students achieve debt-free college degrees. The program removes financial barriers that prevent residents from pursuing higher education by providing support to cover the costs associated with attending the college. Through this initiative, students can complete their education and earn credentials without taking on student loan debt that typically accompanies a college degree.

The Promise Program represents a significant effort to expand access to higher education across the Treasure Coast region. Indian River State College serves students throughout multiple counties in the area, offering associate degrees and certificate programs to local residents seeking career training and academic advancement. By addressing the financial obstacles that can prevent students from enrolling or completing their studies, the program aims to make college more accessible to a broader population.

The college's commitment to debt-free education aligns with broader trends in Florida's focus on educational access and affordability. Many institutions across the state are exploring ways to support student success through innovative financing and support programs. Indian River State College's Promise Program reflects this commitment by helping students achieve their educational goals while minimizing financial burden.

Students who participate in the Promise Program graduate with the knowledge and credentials needed for career advancement in their chosen fields, but without the weight of substantial student debt. This approach recognizes that financial barriers often prevent capable students from completing their degrees and entering the workforce. By removing this obstacle, the college helps more students from the community realize their educational aspirations and contribute to the region's economic development.
