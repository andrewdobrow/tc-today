Port St. Lucie's 34987 zip code has earned recognition from RentCafe as one of the fastest-growing zip codes in the country, largely due to the explosive growth of the Southern Grove neighborhood. The area has undergone a remarkable transformation in just ten years, evolving from near nonexistence into a thriving residential and commercial center. Housing inventory in the neighborhood has surged by 177 percent, representing one of the largest growth spurts recorded across Florida.

The neighborhood's rapid expansion has been fueled by its emergence as a significant jobs hub. Major employers including FedEx and Amazon have established operations in Southern Grove, with Amazon demonstrating particular commitment to the area by planning to close its current facility later this year in order to construct a much larger replacement. Beyond job creation, the neighborhood has attracted residents based on other positive attributes. Southern Grove residents are notably well-educated, and the area boasts strong homeownership rates that helped it earn high marks in RentCafe's evaluation.

The growth in Southern Grove was not spontaneous. The neighborhood was intentionally designed as one of four distinct neighborhoods created to appeal to potential residents and encourage them to establish roots in Port St. Lucie. According to Alexandra Both, a writer for RentCafe, Florida's substantial number of emerging communities reflects several competitive advantages. She noted that the state's favorable tax environment and relatively lower housing costs compared to major markets like New York and California make it attractive to new residents. Additionally, ongoing infrastructure investment throughout the state has supported increased construction activity in developing suburbs across Florida.

Port St. Lucie was not alone among Florida communities recognized in RentCafe's report. Ponte Vedra Beach topped the organization's "America's 50 Newest Neighborhoods" list, earning praise for offering a balanced work-play atmosphere that appeals to families, retirees, and young professionals. Both Daytona Beach and the St. Augustine area also made the top 50, gaining recognition for their excellent schools and robust housing demand. These results underscore Florida's status as a state experiencing significant residential growth and development across multiple regions.

Eridjon Dacruz of West Palm Beach is heading to Miami this month to witness a historic moment for his home country. Cape Verde, a small archipelago of 10 islands located off the African coast, has qualified for the World Cup for the first time in its history. The island nation, which has a population of only half a million people, will compete under the team name Tubarão Azul, or Blue Sharks.

Dacruz grew up in Cape Verde before relocating to the United States. He still has many family members living on the islands and says the World Cup qualification has been the dominant topic of conversation throughout the nation. Cape Verde gained independence from Portugal in the 1970s and is known for its beautiful beaches. When Dacruz was growing up, he played FIFA video games and recalls searching for Cape Verde players in the game, finding only about two listed. Making the World Cup will provide Cape Verde's soccer federation with more than $10 million to invest in developing future generations of players. Dacruz expressed his amazement at the achievement, saying "I didn't think that would happen in my lifetime because we're so small."

Dacruz will attend Cape Verde's match against Uruguay in Miami, having received tickets as an anniversary gift from his girlfriend. He described the gift as a special surprise, recalling that she presented him with a box containing a handwritten note saying "I'm going to the World Cup." Although Cape Verde opens the tournament against Spain, one of the tournament favorites, Dacruz remains optimistic about his team's chances. He stated confidently that "We're there you know. All we have to do is win a couple of games and we'll get far."

The timing of Cape Verde's World Cup appearance also connects the island nation to American weather discussions. During hurricane season, meteorologist Sami Squires explains that Cape Verde is frequently mentioned in weather coverage. Tropical systems regularly form near the islands before tracking westward across the Atlantic toward the United States. According to Squires, "We get these waves that form across the coast of Africa and eventually track across the Atlantic." Dacruz is currently waiting to receive his newest Cape Verde national jersey to wear to the game this month, adding another layer of excitement to this historic moment for both the nation and its diaspora community.

Former Florida state Senator Annette Taddeo announced Monday she is running to unseat Republican Blaise Ingoglia as Chief Financial Officer, hoping to become the first Democrat elected to the position in two decades. Taddeo made her announcement at Second Baptist Church in Richmond Heights, the same location where she launched her successful 2017 Senate campaign that made her the first Latina Democrat elected to the Florida Senate. She released a campaign video alongside the announcement and spoke to a small group that included many members of the press.

At the church event, Taddeo criticized Ingoglia, whom Governor Ron DeSantis appointed CFO in July 2025, for failing to serve as a watchdog for Florida taxpayers. She pointed to the Everglades-based immigration detention center known as Alligator Alcatraz, which costs Florida taxpayers upwards of 1 billion dollars in service contracts, several awarded to DeSantis donors. She also highlighted insurance rates rising in Florida at roughly double the national rate and referenced the Hope Florida scandal, in which the DeSantis administration directed millions in Medicaid settlement dollars to the Governor's wife's nonprofit, which later rerouted the money to political committees aimed at defeating ballot measures supporting abortion and recreational adult-use cannabis. Taddeo characterized Ingoglia as a lapdog for the Governor rather than a watchdog for Floridians and criticized him for not auditing millions of dollars in no-bid contracts awarded to donors, which she said is required by Florida law.

Ingoglia has spent months touring the state highlighting what he describes as wasteful local government spending. His efforts have been used to justify recently passed legislation to slash homestead property taxes, which will require 60 percent approval on the November ballot and would increase homestead property tax exemptions for residents to 250,000 dollars by 2028. Taddeo framed this initiative as an attempt by Republican policymakers to convince voters that the party has addressed cost-of-living issues after controlling Florida's government for three decades.

Taddeo, born in Colombia, fled to the United States after terrorists kidnapped her father. She won a special election for Florida Senate in September 2017 and retained her seat in the 2018 general election. She ran unsuccessfully for Miami-Dade Clerk and Comptroller in 2024, losing to Republican incumbent Juan Fernandez-Barquin. However, Taddeo must first win a Democratic primary against Earle Ford, a U.S. Army veteran, before facing Ingoglia in the general election. A late-April poll commissioned by Ruth's List Florida found she would lead Ingoglia in a head-to-head matchup for CFO.
