Stuart downtown dock to close Monday for $3 million renovation, doubling boat capacity

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down July 6 to undergo a $3 million renovation expected to take one year, with work finishing by next Fourth of July. The project represents a significant expansion of the facility, nearly doubling its capacity from 18 to 44 slips that will accommodate both motorized and non-motorized vessels. More than 1,400 boaters rely on the dock annu...

5:05 PM ET

Stuart's downtown public dock will shut down July 6 to undergo a $3 million renovation expected to take one year, with work finishing by next Fourth of July. The project represents a significant expansion of the facility, nearly doubling its capacity from 18 to 44 slips that will accommodate both motorized and non-motorized vessels. More than 1,400 boaters rely on the dock annually, making the upgrade an important investment for the community's waterfront infrastructure.

The renovation will feature a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock, and a smaller floating platform. Stuart Mayor Sean Reed emphasized that the improvements serve a dual purpose beyond simply adding capacity. "They're doing it to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms," Reed said. The project receives funding through a state grant and transportation impact fees, spreading the financial burden across multiple sources.

The city has worked proactively to ensure that businesses depending on the dock can continue operating during the closure. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which runs passenger boarding operations from the facility, initially worried about its ability to function during construction. However, Austin Kuyrkendall, the company's director of operations, confirmed that officials found a workable solution. "So they're allowing us to build a gate right here at the end of the downtown Stuart dock. This is the non-floating section, but still we're going to be able to open this up and board people right here," Kuyrkendall said.

Kuyrkendall highlighted the dock's importance to Stuart's character and economic vitality. "Stuart is not Stuart without the water," he noted. He acknowledged the challenges the current facility faces, particularly when demand is high. "On the weekend that's when it can get a little hectic at the dock and you know it's never great for us when we're squeezing in and trying to find spots," Kuyrkendall said. Boat captain Parker Fisher, based in Stuart, supports the improvements. "It's definitely needed some work over the years, so I'm glad they're putting money into it," Fisher said. The modernization and expansion of the dock should benefit not only businesses but the broader community by creating more opportunities for boaters and strengthening Stuart's waterfront infrastructure for years to come.

The council is scheduled to vote on final approval at a future meeting. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager fo...

7:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie City Council has approved the first reading of an ordinance to transfer 7 acres of city-owned land to the Florida Department of Transportation to support construction of a partial interchange on Florida's Turnpike at Midway Road. The council is scheduled to vote on final approval at a future meeting. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the new interchange will close a substantial gap in Turnpike access points currently available to city residents and commuters. The project is designed to support both the growth that has already taken place in Port St. Lucie and anticipated expansion over the next 20 to 50 years.

The partial interchange is part of a larger effort to improve transportation infrastructure in the area. FDOT is managing the $95 million Midway Road Widening Project, which aims to reduce congestion and improve safety. The first phase of construction is currently underway, widening Midway Road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, which will extend from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in summer 2027. Beyond road widening, the project will incorporate bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks.

The partial interchange remains in the design phase as FDOT works on multiple elements of the transportation improvement. The department is simultaneously working on improving the Turnpike bridge itself. Samples emphasized that the interchange represents a focus on accessibility and accommodating the city's ongoing growth. "This is a way for us to try to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years," Samples said.

The construction activity has been visible to Port St. Lucie residents. One area resident, Erin Scheumeister, acknowledged that while the ongoing work can be disruptive, she recognizes its necessity. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," Scheumeister said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." As construction continues on the Midway Road project and planning advances for the Turnpike interchange, city officials expect the improvements will enhance transportation efficiency for the expanding community.

Stephen McCoy, a disabled and retired veteran from Lantana, hired Seabreeze Roofing and Sheet Metal in Boynton Beach to replace his aging roof before hurricane season. McCoy made six payments totaling more than $17,000 to complete the project. While he was generally satisfied with most of the work, the company left two holes unfinished on the back side of his home. When McCoy tried to contact the contractor about these repairs through calls, texts, and emails between January and February, the company stopped responding to his attempts.

The situation worsened when McCoy received letters from Gulfeagle Supply warning that a nearly $7,000 lien had been filed against his home with the county. According to the lien document, Gulfeagle Supply had provided materials to Seabreeze Roofing for McCoy's property but had not been paid for those supplies. McCoy said he felt sick about the situation and worried that he would need to hire an attorney and go to court to resolve the matter, which he knew would cost thousands of dollars.

After WPTV investigative reporter Kate Hussey contacted the owner of Seabreeze Roofing directly to ask about the unpaid materials and the lien, the situation changed dramatically. Within an hour, Hussey returned to McCoy's home with a final waiver of lien that had been signed by Gulfeagle Supply and notarized. McCoy was shocked by the rapid resolution. "Oh my God, how did you do that? Are you serious?" he asked. He also learned that the contractor would return to fix the two unfinished areas on his roof.

About an hour after receiving the lien waiver, McCoy sent WPTV a photo showing crews back on his roof completing the work. McCoy expressed his overwhelming relief after months of frustration and worry. "I've been going through hell," he said. "I have not gotten a response, not even a phone call from these people in months." He added that the lien removal felt like a tremendous burden had been lifted from his shoulders. "I felt like crying," McCoy said. "You just don't know how much of a burden this was off of me."

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering a variety of educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season this summer. The center said guests can participate in seasonal experiences through September, including guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. According to Loggerhead Marinelife Center, the programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn more about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The center's Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations during the programs may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. Loggerhead Marinelife Center also said its education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success.

Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

Democratic congressional candidate Bernard Taylor, an EMT/paramedic, reported raising just under $563,000 during the second quarter of 2025 for his campaign to challenge Republican U.S. Rep. Brian Mast in Florida's 21st Congressional District. The fundraising haul came from more than 25,000 individual donations that averaged $22 each between April 1 and June 30. Taylor's campaign emphasized that it declined all corporate PAC money and special-interest donations, with more than 93 percent of contributions coming through mobile devices and approximately 41 percent processed through the digital fundraising platform ActBlue.

Taylor, a fourth-generation Floridian, said the fundraising results demonstrate support from working families, retirees, veterans, teachers, and first responders rather than wealthy interests. "I'm humbled by the incredible support we've received from thousands of people who believe it's time for new leadership in Washington," Taylor stated. "These contributions are coming from working families, retirees, veterans, teachers, first responders, and neighbors who want a representative who understands their challenges and will fight for them every day." His campaign platform prioritizes affordable healthcare access, educational investment, job creation, housing affordability, environmental protection, and representing constituents over political insiders. Taylor said his background in emergency medical services has shown him the real-world consequences of rising healthcare costs and limited options facing families across the district.

The coastal district stretches from Royal Palm Lake in Palm Beach County north through Martin and St. Lucie counties. About 57.5 percent of voters in the district voted for President Donald Trump in 2024 under new congressional map boundaries that Gov. Ron DeSantis and the Republican-dominated Legislature approved in April. Taylor faces a primary opponent in U.S. Coast Guard veteran James Martin, who entered the second quarter with nearly $307,000 raised. Taylor had raised only $32,500 before this quarter after filing for the race in April 2025, according to Federal Election Commission records. "We are grateful for what we accomplished in Q2, and we are looking forward to an even stronger Q3," Taylor said.

Republican incumbent Mast entered April with more than $2.5 million in cash on hand. Other candidates running in the race include independent Alexander Cooke and write-in candidate David Fabrikant.

Florida records lowest residential arson rate in nation during July, FBI data shows

Florida has the lowest rate of residential arson in the country during July, according to a new analysis of FBI crime data.

6:15 PM ET

Florida has the nation's lowest rate of residential arson during July, according to a new analysis of FBI crime data. The state averages just 17.8 residential arson incidents each year over the last five Julys, roughly one incident for every 586,000 homes. A study conducted by Besroi Roofing and Siding using FBI Crime Data Explorer records from 2021 through 2025 ranked all 50 states by July residential arson rates. Florida edged out New Jersey for the top spot and dramatically outperformed neighboring states, where residential arson rates were significantly higher. Georgia's residential arson rate was roughly four times higher than Florida's, while Alabama's was roughly 10 times higher. Florida's July residential arson rate is approximately one-sixth the national average, despite the state having the country's third-largest housing market.

The timing of the report comes as millions of Floridians prepare to celebrate Independence Day with backyard barbecues and fireworks. Agriculture Commissioner Wilton Simpson and the Florida Forest Service spent the lead-up to the Fourth of July reminding residents that even though drought conditions are easing, wildfire season remains active and fireworks should be used responsibly. While intentional residential fires are rare in Florida, the Forest Service and firefighters across the state emphasize that accidental fires remain a concern during holiday weekends when firework use peaks.

Glydways proposes elevated autonomous transit connecting West Palm Beach downtown to airport

California-based Glydways will present a proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport. The system could cut travel time to under 10 minutes.

5:49 PM ET

Glydways, a California-based autonomous transit company, will present a proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport, the convention center, and nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations. Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the system would function like a "horizontal elevator," carrying passengers directly between destinations without traffic delays or multiple intermediate stops. Small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers would travel along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. Glydways estimates travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center.

Gettinger said the system would cost "a small fraction" of a traditional light rail system, referencing previous South Florida light rail discussions that carried estimates of $400 million to $500 million per mile. Potential funding sources include Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. Tuesday's County Commission meeting will determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal. If commissioners move forward, the county could seek competing bids and begin discussions on routes, station locations, costs and financing, with a planning and design contract potentially awarded later this year.

Lantana veteran's roof completed and lien removed after contractor dispute

A Lantana homeowner had a nearly $7,000 lien removed from his home and unfinished roof repairs completed after months of frustration with a roofing contractor.

4:12 PM ET

Stephen McCoy, a disabled and retired veteran in Lantana, turned to WPTV for help after months of frustration with Seabreeze Roofing and Sheet Metal in Boynton Beach. McCoy paid the company more than $17,000 in six payments to replace his nearly 20-year-old roof ahead of hurricane season but said the contractor left two holes unfinished on the back of his home and stopped responding to his calls, texts and emails starting in January. A worker responded to one email on Feb. 5 saying the owner would call, but McCoy said that call never came.

The situation worsened when Gulfeagle Supply, which furnished materials to Seabreeze Roofing, filed a nearly $7,000 lien against McCoy's home. McCoy worried he would need to hire an attorney or go to court. After WPTV investigative reporter Kate Hussey spoke directly with Seabreeze Roofing's owner, a final waiver of lien signed by Gulfeagle Supply and notarized was delivered within an hour, lifting the lien from McCoy's property. The contractor also sent crews back to fix the two problem areas on his roof, completing the work about an hour after McCoy received the waiver.
