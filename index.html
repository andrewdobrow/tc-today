4:16 AM ET

A brush fire that began Friday in Indiantown escalated over the weekend before being brought under near-total control, according to Martin County Fire Rescue and the Florida Forest Service. The fire now covers approximately 125 acres and is 99% contained as of Sunday morning.

Martin County Fire Rescue firefighters first responded to the rapidly developing brush fire in the 2600 block of Southwest Warfield Boulevard just before 3:30 p.m. on Friday, June 5. Crews quickly contained the blaze at approximately 37.8 acres, with no reported injuries and no structures threatened at the time. The Florida Forest Service assisted by establishing plow lines to stop the spread of flames, while MCFR's drone team identified remaining hotspots before crews cleared the area. Officials said the fire's cause was unknown, though early indications suggested it may have been a holdover fire, meaning a lightning strike could have smoldered underground for days before igniting visible flames.

By Saturday, June 6, the situation escalated significantly when crews returned to the same area to battle a much larger 120-acre brush fire. Officials believe the fire rekindled from the previous day's incident. Shifting winds and dense vegetation contributed to rapid fire growth, prompting a large-scale response from Martin County Fire Rescue, the Florida Forest Service, and the Martin County Sheriff's Office. Firefighters worked to protect nearby structures as smoke spread across the area, though officials confirmed there was no broader threat to the public. Residents were advised to expect smoky conditions throughout the weekend as suppression efforts continued.

The Florida Forest Service remains in the area monitoring conditions to ensure no further flare-ups occur. Authorities say the investigation into the fire's origin remains ongoing.

Orion Jean Baptiste, a 9-year-old Port St. Lucie resident, has created a new junior chess club designed to introduce young people to the strategic board game. Baptiste developed his own passion for chess starting at age 5 while playing with family members, and he now wants to share that enthusiasm with others in his community. The Atlantis Chess Club meets every Friday at the Port St. Lucie library and has already enrolled more than 36 junior members.

Baptiste's motivation for starting the club stems from a desire to shift young people's attention away from technology. "Everybody's more focused on like tech stuff," Baptiste said. "I'm hoping that more people have respect for board games." Diana Cowan Kempton, the club's program director, believes chess offers valuable developmental benefits for the next generation. She explained that the game can help build young leaders who are more focused, patient, strategic, and thoughtful when making decisions. The club welcomes players at all skill levels, making it accessible to beginners and experienced players alike.

Families have embraced the club as a place where children can learn together and develop healthy competition. Thomas Furlong brought both his daughter and son to the club, recognizing it as an opportunity for his daughter to learn chess while giving his son a chance to improve his skills. The sibling dynamic creates friendly competition that benefits both children. For Francesca Furlong, a newer member of the club, the most appealing aspect is straightforward: playing with other kids her age provides enjoyment and social connection alongside skill development.

The club's success at the Port St. Lucie library location has led to expansion plans. The Atlantis Chess Club will soon launch two additional programs at the Fenn Center in Fort Pierce, allowing more young people across the region to participate. Baptiste's simple goal of helping kids learn chess has resonated with families seeking enriching activities that encourage strategic thinking and patience. As the club continues to grow, it demonstrates how one young person's passion for a traditional board game can inspire community engagement and provide meaningful learning opportunities for dozens of other children.

Jupiter Island faces critical erosion threatening to split island at Peck Lake

Nearly the entire coastline of Jupiter Island is critically eroded, with a new state report warning the ocean could break through at Peck Lake and potentially split the island in half.

Jun 5, 4:42 PM ET

A 2025 Florida Department of Environmental Protection report warns that Jupiter Island's 11.5-mile shoreline has eroded so severely that the ocean could break through at Peck Lake, potentially splitting the island in half. The threat stretches from Blowing Rocks Preserve, where private development is already at risk, to Peck Lake, where a breach could turn a thin strip of land into an open inlet. A storm previously breached Peck Lake in the early 1960s before the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers filled it back in with sand. Mark Perry with the Florida Oceanographic Society said a future breach could upend ecosystems that depend on a delicate balance of fresh and saltwater to survive.

The report identifies multiple sites at risk, including the Joseph Reed Mound, a 3,000- to 4,000-year-old archaeological site already half lost to erosion, along with Blowing Rocks Preserve, Hobe Sound Wildlife Refuge, and St. Lucie Inlet Preserve State Park. Perry noted there are over 33 threatened and endangered species in those areas. Jupiter Island Commissioner Marshall Field said the town completed a major beach renourishment project in April 2025, dumping enough sand to fill nearly 400 Olympic-sized swimming pools. About $3 million in town taxpayer dollars goes toward beach renourishment annually. Field said the town will continue this work as long as funding remains available.

Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties enforce summer fertilizer ban starting June 30

Residents in Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties will be prohibited from using any fertilizer containing phosphorus or nitrogen starting June 30.

Jun 5, 5:36 PM ET

Starting June 30, Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties will enforce a ban on any fertilizer containing phosphorus or nitrogen. The restriction applies to both residential lawns and landscaping projects. According to Jordan Skaggs with Environmental Resources in St. Lucie County, the ban targets nutrient-rich runoff during the rainy season that can cause algae blooms in waterways like the Indian River Lagoon and St. Lucie River.

Harmful algae blooms create significant environmental problems. Skaggs explained that the blooms can shade out beneficial plants that fish and wildlife depend on. At certain densities, these blooms can release toxins that affect human health, wildlife health and overall ecosystem health. Grantly Ricketts, director of University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences St. Lucie County Extension, recommends composting as an alternative during the ban. Ricketts described composting as being like a small trash can, calling it "the easiest and most practical way or means to add nutrients to your landscape."

Palm Beach County rental assistance program overwhelmed as demand far exceeds funding

Palm Beach County receives nearly 8,000 calls for housing assistance per month, but emergency rental assistance funding can only help a fraction of those in need.

Jun 5, 6:03 PM ET

Palm Beach County's Community Services Department has nearly $2 million in combined grant funding for its Emergency Rental Assistance Program, designed to help residents who can't pay rent. However, demand far exceeds available resources each month. "We're doing what we can to prioritize and serve those who are most needy but the demand far outweighs the resources that are available," said program director Dr. James Green. Applications open at the beginning of each month but fill quickly. According to Green, there are nearly 8,000 calls for housing assistance per month based on data combined with 211. The county currently has about 800 applications in the queue for rental assistance, typically receiving about 600 applications within the first couple of hours they're open. At the time of reporting, the county's assistance portal was marked closed due to high demand.

Rental prices have pushed many residents to financial crisis. According to the Department of Housing and Urban Development, the average rent for a one-bedroom in Palm Beach County is $2,020 a month. Green attributed the recent surge in applications partly to rising costs of living, including housing, gas, and food. For residents unable to access the county's program, 211 offers another option, connecting people with nonprofits and other assistance programs in the area. Green urges anyone in need to call 1-833-CSD-WILL to check for availability.
