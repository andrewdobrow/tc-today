Yesterday, 3:52 PM ET

Jupiter's town council has approved a fire assessment fee designed to help finance the town's new fire rescue department, which will launch on October 1. The per-property charge will contribute approximately 8 percent of the department's total operating costs. Property taxes will cover 84 percent of expenses, while the remaining 8 percent will come from EMS transport fees. The new assessment replaces the current county fire fee that residents see on their tax bills.

Finance Director Scott Reynolds emphasized that the funding structure will deliver meaningful financial relief to property owners, a goal the town has pursued for three years. "The immediate savings for property owners, we've been talking about this for three years, starting at $25 million so right out of the gate, a $10 million savings to the residents," Reynolds said. To help residents understand their individual situation, the town has created an online calculator where people can estimate their potential savings under the new system.

Mayor Jim Kuretski cast the only vote against the funding structure, expressing concerns about inconsistencies in how the assessment applies to different property types. Single family homes are assessed based on total square footage, including garages and porches, while townhomes and condos are assessed differently. This discrepancy prompted Kuretski to question the fairness of the calculation method across the community.

The town mailed information letters about the fee to 30,000 homes, though Town of Jupiter spokesperson Mariana DiNunzio acknowledged the change has caused some confusion among residents. "I think there's been some confusion because it's new. The town hasn't had to have this, a non ad-valorem assessment," DiNunzio said. Some residents have also expressed concerns about the fee's potential to increase in the future, questioning what might happen as time passes. The town's online calculator provides residents with a tool to better understand how the new assessment will affect their individual properties and to see what financial benefits they can expect from the transition to a municipal fire rescue department.

Palm Beach police arrested Ti'Miyah De'Asia Allen, 19, of West Palm Beach, after she allegedly posed as a parking official and defrauded drivers on Worth Avenue. Investigators say Allen impersonated an employee of ParkMobile and the Palm Beach Parking Authority while targeting unsuspecting visitors and stealing their credit card information.

According to the arrest affidavit, Allen used a common approach to carry out the scheme. She would tell drivers looking to pay for parking that they could pay her approximately $1 instead of the standard $5 per hour plus a 40-cent transaction fee. To make the scam appear legitimate, she carried what looked like a ParkMobile payment device and displayed a fake badge. She would approach vehicles either before drivers had paid for parking or while they were in the process of paying. One victim reported that a fraudulent charge of $2,500 to a company called Health Aid appeared on their credit card after an interaction with Allen on June 5. Police noted that the town was "deprived of parking fee revenue that would have otherwise been legitimately paid."

The investigation revealed that elderly residents were specifically targeted in the scheme. Police say all of the victims were over 65 years old, making them particularly vulnerable to the fraud. This pattern of targeting seniors raised additional concerns for investigators about the nature and scope of the criminal activity.

Allen's arrest history suggests this was not her first fraudulent scheme. Police say she was involved in a similar operation last year while employed at a Wawa location on Belvedere Road, where she allegedly voided customer transactions for profit. Based on the evidence gathered in the current investigation, Allen has been charged with three crimes: falsely impersonating an officer, organized scheme to defraud, and unlawful use of a badge or other indicia of authority. The multiple charges reflect the coordinated nature of the scheme and the deliberate use of fake credentials to carry out the fraud.

The organization is hosting free mammography screening events on July 11 in Jupiter and July 12 in West Palm Beach. "The Promise Fund is here to make screenings accessible for everyone, especially those who might otherwise go without care," Lahoz said. Treatment is often easier to manage, and people are able to focus more on their health, their families, and their everyday lives."

Promise Fund serves uninsured, underinsured and underserved women throughout Palm Beach, Broward and Martin counties. The nonprofit has reached more than 145,000 women across South Florida through education and outreach initiatives. To date, more than 27,000 women have accessed screenings, diagnostic services and care through the organization's programs. Additionally, 286 women who received cancer diagnoses have gotten assistance navigating their treatment. Promise Fund currently operates four co-located screening programs across Palm Beach and Broward counties and is working to secure funding to expand services into Martin County.

Yesterday, 9:23 PM ET

Congressman Mike Haridopolos announced Wednesday that a $1 million federal grant will help fund a major sewage system upgrade in the Ixora Park community in Indian River County. The Member Community Project Funding is part of a larger $20.8 million initiative to retrofit the sewage infrastructure serving over 230 homes that currently depend on a failing sewer system. This aging system poses a significant risk of contaminating local environmental resources and water supplies.

The Ixora Park community is located adjacent to the County South Relief Canal, which flows directly into the Indian River Lagoon, recognized as one of the most diverse estuaries in the country. According to Keith Drewett of the Clean Water Coalition of Indian River County, the lagoon's environmental condition has deteriorated substantially over the past two decades. "It's dramatically different now from what it was twenty years ago in terms of fishing, swimming. Nobody swims in the lagoon anymore," Drewett said. The organization emphasizes that the upgrade is essential given the area's unique environmental challenges, particularly the high water table and heavy rainfall that causes water to flow underground through the subsurface layer.

The sewage retrofit project will replace aging sewer infrastructure and focus on protecting water quality by reducing the discharge of nitrogen and phosphorus into the Indian River Lagoon. Congressman Haridopolos stressed the importance of the federal investment, stating that it "ensures health and safety to the community by rehabilitating infrastructure for over 230 homes, while also protecting a designated federal resource, the Indian River Lagoon." Beyond environmental protection, the congressman highlighted additional community benefits from the project.

The infrastructure improvements will have economic advantages for local property owners. Haridopolos noted that while replacing sewer systems involves significant costs, the upgrades provide dual benefits. "By replacing those, which aren't cheap, it improves not only the quality of the water, which we all want to be out on the water, but the other part of it, you also improve the taxable value of your home," he said. This combination of environmental restoration and economic improvement positions the sewage system upgrade as an investment in both the community's health and its long-term property values.

Florida Attorney General Reaffirms Parental Authority Over Religious Education Programs

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier issued an opinion supporting release time religious instruction programs, affirming that parents hold primary responsibility for directing the education and religious upbringing of their children.

Yesterday, 8:39 PM ET

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier reaffirmed parental authority over children's religious education in a recent opinion addressing release time religious instruction programs. Responding to a question raised by Senator Clay Yarborough, Uthmeier concluded that when parents choose to add religious instruction as part of their child's education, public schools must respect and accommodate that decision. The opinion was directed to school districts across Florida and recognizes a foundational American principle: parents, not government, bear the primary responsibility for directing the education and religious upbringing of their children.

The opinion references more than a century of U.S. Supreme Court precedent establishing parental authority. In Society of Sisters, the Court declared that "the child is not the mere creature of the State." Wisconsin v. Yoder protected parents' ability to direct their children's religious education from government interference, and Mahmoud v. Taylor recognized that parents retain primary responsibility for their children's upbringing, especially regarding matters of faith and conscience. Release time religious instruction programs allow students to leave campus during part of the school day to receive religious instruction off school property with parental permission. The Supreme Court upheld such programs more than 70 years ago in Zorach v. Clauson. Uthmeier's opinion states that school districts cannot frustrate parental decisions simply because administrators prefer a different approach.

Noble uses the Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocol combined with his own proprietary method to help patients recover from joint replacement surgery without ever touching an opioid. The ERAS protocol combines robotics for minimally invasive surgery, targeted numbing, and a timed schedule of non-opioid medications taken before, during, and after surgery.

Patient April Stubbs recovered from knee replacement surgery without opioids using this approach. "I never had to touch the more serious pain medications. At Reprieve Recovery in Jupiter, Chief Marketing Officer Daniel Warren said patients are often unaware of the physical effects of opioid medications taken during surgical recovery. Warren noted that patients may experience withdrawal symptoms after taking opioids for two weeks without understanding why. "If a physician's able to provide recovery time or recovery medication regimen that exposes the patient to a lot less risk of developing a substance use disorder, then that's definitely a step in the right direction," Warren said.

A new Change Research poll showing David Jolly leading Byron Donalds 47% to 42% in the 2026 congressional race warrants significant scrutiny, according to poll analyst Steve Vancore. While the poll demonstrates transparency by disclosing its client, recruitment sources, weighting variables, post-stratification targets and limitations, and employs reasonable sample sizes with appropriate weighting on age, gender, race, education, 2024 Presidential vote and region, several methodological problems undermine its reliability.

The survey's opt-in sample, including recruitment through Facebook and Instagram ads, risks overrepresenting voters motivated enough to respond during a period of high Democratic enthusiasm. Most problematically, the ballot test presented voters with descriptions and photos of both candidates rather than asking a clean, uninformed question. Jolly received more favorable framing and more words, while Donalds' description emphasized endorsements, corporate ties and combative language. Vancore concluded the poll should not be dismissed outright given its relative transparency compared to most surveys, but the combination of opt-in recruiting, English-only fielding, self-described voter behavior and narrative ballot testing all point toward taking these findings with substantial skepticism.
