Martin County Commissioner seeks to relocate Brightline station away from downtown Stuart

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi called on county staff to open discussions with Brightline leadership about potentially moving the planned train station away from its previously agreed-upon downtown Stuart location. Ciampi asked staff to begin conversations with Brightline about changing the location from the current site, which is a parking lot across from the courthouse...

Yesterday, 6:50 PM ET

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi called on county staff to open discussions with Brightline leadership about potentially moving the planned train station away from its previously agreed-upon downtown Stuart location. Ciampi asked staff to begin conversations with Brightline about changing the location from the current site, which is a parking lot across from the courthouse in downtown Stuart, and suggested revisiting the fairgrounds as an alternative site.

The commissioner's comments come after the FEC sent a letter to then-Stuart mayor Christopher Collins explaining it does not support a Brightline station in Stuart. The FEC said the proposed station violates U.S. Coast Guard rules because it would require the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge to be down while the train stops at the station. Ciampi said if a station is deemed too close by the Army Corps of Engineers or the U.S. Coast Guard, then it comes off the table for him. He noted that moving it further south to the fairgrounds would eliminate that issue entirely.

Community members had mixed reactions to the commissioner's suggestion. Paul Gedwed said the opinion is well-thought-out because there is a lot of congestion in the proposed location. However, Steven Vitale, owner of the Colorado Inn, sees value in keeping the station downtown, saying most people are going to want to go to downtown Stuart and questioning why a train station would be built that people cannot walk to. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, said her downtown store will benefit regardless of where the station is built, as long as it is in Martin County.

The county said Ciampi's comments are not official direction for staff to make changes. The topic would need to return to the board for a discussion and vote before staff can take action on exploring a new location. In the meantime, the county is still waiting to hear back on its grant application to fund the Brightline station before the project can move forward.

Riviera Beach pauses parking program after council member ticketed without parking

Yesterday, 10:52 PM ET

Riviera Beach has temporarily halted its paid parking program at Ocean Walk and Singer Island following a controversial citation issued to a city council member. Mayor Douglas Lawson announced the pause during last Wednesday's city council meeting after Council Member Dr. Glen Spiritis was charged for parking despite never actually using a spot.

"From my understanding Dr. Spiritis was charged for driving into the parking lot and he said he immediately drove out," Lawson explained. The incident highlighted problems with the new system, which launched in October of last year and requires visitors to scan a QR code before parking. Since its introduction, the program has generated significant backlash from multiple groups in the community. Lawson acknowledged the adjustment period, saying "It's been up and down, it's a change for our community. It's the first time that we're doing paid parking. A lot of other communities across the state are actually doing the parking, so we're learning through the bumps and bruises trying to make sure that we get it right."

The program has faced complaints from business owners, customers, and residents unhappy with the shift from free to paid parking. One longtime visitor, Daneya Davis, expressed her frustration with the unexpected change. "I go out, chill at the water, get a sense of calmness," Davis said. "All of a sudden you had to pay so it was kind of shocking." The pause has also provided relief to Kerry Lutz, who filed a lawsuit challenging the program's legality. Lutz claims the system violates federal standards and questioned the necessity for paid parking in the first place.

Lawson committed to addressing the issues that prompted the pause. He said parking officials will conduct a comprehensive review of the program's fees and system to examine concerns about handicap parking accessibility and timing issues. The city plans to issue refunds to anyone who received wrongful citations. However, Lawson emphasized that the pause is temporary. "No absolutely not a stop. We're going to continue the system, continue the process but we are pausing the system to make sure that it's done at the most effective, efficient way," he said. The mayor stressed that the city needs paid parking to generate additional revenue but wants to implement it properly. The city is awaiting a response from One Parking, the company operating the system.

Martin County Commissioner pushes to relocate Brightline station away from downtown Stuart

Yesterday, 6:50 PM ET

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi is calling for discussions between county staff and Brightline leadership about moving the planned train station from its current downtown Stuart location. The station was previously agreed to be built on a parking lot site across from the courthouse in downtown Stuart. Ciampi suggested the fairgrounds as a potential alternative location, citing parking and other congestion concerns with the downtown spot. "I would ask our staff to begin a conversation and discussions with Brightline leadership for the possibility of changing the location from where it is currently, which creates parking issues and other issues," Ciampi said.

The commissioner's proposal comes after the Florida East Coast Railway sent a letter to then-Stuart Mayor Christopher Collins expressing opposition to a Brightline station in Stuart. The FEC stated that the proposed station would violate U.S. Coast Guard rules because the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge would need to remain lowered while the train stops at the station. Ciampi explained his reasoning for exploring alternatives, saying that if regulators like the Army Corps of Engineers or U.S. Coast Guard determine the station location is problematic, the downtown site becomes unviable. He argued that moving the station further south to the fairgrounds would eliminate this regulatory obstacle entirely.

Community members expressed divided opinions about the relocation proposal. Paul Gedwed, a resident, supported the idea as reasonable given the congestion issues at the proposed downtown location. However, Steven Vitale, who owns the Colorado Inn, disagreed, contending that most visitors would want to access downtown Stuart and questioned why the county would build a train station people cannot walk to from their destination. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, took a more pragmatic approach, indicating her downtown business would benefit either way and urged officials to move forward with construction regardless of the final location, as long as it remains in Martin County.

County officials emphasized that Ciampi's comments do not constitute formal direction to staff. Any action on exploring alternative locations would require the full board to discuss and vote on the matter before staff can proceed. The county is currently awaiting a decision on its grant application to fund the Brightline station project. Until that funding is secured, the relocation discussion remains secondary to obtaining the necessary financial resources to move forward with construction.

Palm Beach County faces up to $324 million budget loss if property tax reform passes

Palm Beach County commissioners are wrestling with this year's budget while bracing for what could be significant revenue losses if Florida voters approve a property tax reform referendum.

Yesterday, 6:28 PM ET

Palm Beach County commissioners are grappling with budget pressures while preparing for potential significant revenue losses if Florida voters approve a property tax reform referendum. Estimates show the county could lose $221 million in revenue in the first year if the referendum passes, with losses climbing to $324 million the following year. Commissioner Maria Marino expressed the frustration many commissioners feel about the situation. "We're under attack with the property taxes," Marino said. Commissioners are already under pressure to close a budget gap, with Commissioner Marci Woodward calling on departments to find savings. "Any departments that can bring us lower numbers, because they found a different source, I would like to say thank you and keep working on that," Woodward said.

A major point of discussion was the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office budget, which includes a request for more than 8.5 percent more funding than last year. Commissioner Gregg Weiss noted that all new revenue dollars went to support the Sheriff's Office needs last year, and this year requires all new revenue dollars plus an additional $13 million. Lowering property taxes is part of a broader push by Gov. Ron DeSantis to make Florida more affordable. However, commissioners were told that raising taxes on commercial and non-homestead properties, as well as increasing the millage rate, are possibilities. Commissioner Joel Flores said he would like to keep the millage rate at 4.5 for this year before deciding the direction for the following year.

Palm Beach County School Board raises safety concerns about AI data center near Saddle View Elementary School

The Palm Beach County School Board is raising significant safety and health concerns about a proposed artificial intelligence data center complex that would be built just 1,200 feet from Saddle View Elementary School.

Yesterday, 5:15 PM ET

The Palm Beach County School Board is raising significant safety and health concerns about Project Tango, a proposed hyperscale AI data center facility planned just 1,200 feet from Saddle View Elementary School. In a formal letter to Mayor Baxter and county commissioners following their June 3, 2026, meeting, School Board members outlined multiple potential risks to students and staff. These include industrial noise and vibrations that could disrupt the learning environment, environmental health impacts from the facility's operations, public safety risks including hazards from lithium battery fires, emergency response capabilities in the event of an incident, and infrastructure strain on local utilities and services.

The School Board is formally requesting access to any existing public records, impact studies, or reports the county has received regarding the project, specifically seeking information about potential noise levels from facility operations, environmental impact assessments, public safety and emergency response planning, projected energy and water usage, and long-term economic effects on the community. While expressing concerns, the School Board indicated a willingness to work with county officials if the project moves forward, hoping to "work collaboratively with the Board of County Commissioners to identify and implement mitigation strategies" should Project Tango ultimately be approved.
