Independence Day stands out as one of the most hazardous holidays on American roads, though advance preparation can make a critical difference in safety outcomes. Last Fourth of July, 579 people lost their lives in vehicle crashes across the nation. The concerning part is that nearly 4 in 10 of those deaths involved impaired driving, a rate substantially higher than the typical 30% seen during the rest of the year. Research shows that deadly crashes increase by 44% specifically on Independence Day compared to other days.

Federal statistics reveal that teenagers account for about 10% of these holiday fatalities. The spike in dangerous driving during this period has prompted increased awareness campaigns focused on prevention. Rideshare services like Uber and Lyft have reported surge demand as people increasingly turn to these apps to serve as their designated drivers. Safety advocates recommend that celebrants plan ahead by using rideshares, taxis, or public transportation rather than driving themselves after consuming alcohol or becoming fatigued from holiday activities.

Residents in downtown West Palm Beach demonstrate they are taking these safety messages to heart. Scott explained his Independence Day plans involve paddle boarding but noted he will arrange for a designated driver to transport his group to and from the location. Pamela shared that she plans to use Uber for her holiday celebration travel. Carlos captured the sentiment simply by saying, "Definitely want to have a fun time and make it home in one piece."

For those planning to use rideshare services during the busy holiday weekend, several money-saving strategies can help reduce costs during peak demand periods. Timing matters significantly, so leaving early or waiting until surge pricing subsides can result in lower fares. Walking a few blocks away from congested areas makes pickups easier and may reduce wait times. Additionally, comparing prices across different rideshare platforms before booking allows users to find the best available rates. By combining smart planning with these practical tips, holiday celebrants can enjoy Independence Day while prioritizing safety for themselves and others on the road.

Stephen McCoy, a disabled and retired veteran, hired Seabreeze Roofing and Sheet Metal in Boynton Beach to replace his aging roof before hurricane season. He made six payments to the company totaling more than $17,000 and said the work was mostly satisfactory. However, two holes remained unfinished on the back of his home, and when McCoy tried to get them fixed, the contractor stopped responding to his calls, texts and emails. Screenshots showed he had attempted to reach the company multiple times starting in January, including a follow-up email from a worker on February 5 who promised the owner would call. That call never came.

The situation worsened when McCoy received letters from Gulfeagle Supply, a material supplier, warning that a nearly $7,000 lien had been filed against his home. According to the lien document, Gulfeagle Supply had provided materials to Seabreeze Roofing for McCoy's property but had not been paid. McCoy found himself caught in the middle of a payment dispute between the contractor and the supplier. "They're basically saying they never paid them the $6,900, so now they took a lien out on my house," McCoy said. The prospect of dealing with attorneys or court proceedings added to his anxiety and financial burden.

After months without resolution, McCoy turned to WPTV for help. Investigative reporter Kate Hussey visited Seabreeze Roofing and spoke directly with the owner about the unpaid materials and the lien on McCoy's home. Although the owner declined to comment on camera, citing legal reasons, the conversation prompted immediate action. Within an hour, Hussey returned to McCoy's home with a final waiver of lien, signed by Gulfeagle Supply and notarized, officially removing the lien from his property. McCoy was stunned by the swift resolution. "Oh my God, how did you do that? Are you serious?" he asked.

The breakthrough also led to a commitment from the contractor to complete the unfinished work. About an hour after receiving the lien waiver, McCoy sent WPTV a photo showing crews back on his roof finishing the job. McCoy expressed his relief and gratitude, saying the lien and incomplete repairs had caused him immense stress. "I've been going through hell," he said. "You just don't know how much of a burden this was off of me." After months of frustration and unanswered messages, the intervention finally provided McCoy with the resolution he had been seeking.

Florida manufactured home owners can now apply for storm-hardening and repair funding starting in August

Senate Bill 594 took effect on July 1st, making $167 million in state affordable housing funding available to mobile and manufactured home owners seeking emergency repairs and storm-hardening improvements. Yesterday, 6:54 PM ET

Senate Bill 594 took effect on July 1st, making $167 million in state affordable housing funding available to mobile and manufactured home owners seeking emergency repairs and storm-hardening improvements. The law represents a significant shift in how Florida addresses the vulnerability of older manufactured homes that cannot withstand modern storms. The change comes after an investigation revealed that nearly 700,000 of Florida's more than 1 million mobile and manufactured homes were built before current federal safety standards, leaving them at risk during severe weather events.

The push for this funding stems from an EF-3 tornado that destroyed 138 homes in the Spanish Lakes Country Club Community of Fort Pierce, killing six residents. Following that disaster, state lawmakers expanded access to SHIP funding, which traditionally supported affordable housing programs. Now, eligible manufactured homeowners can use these funds for lot rent, rehabilitation, emergency repairs, and storm-hardening retrofits. Many residents living in these homes are seniors on fixed incomes who cannot afford the substantial costs of upgrades, which often range from $10,000 to $15,000 per home.

Martin County Human Services will launch an online portal in August where residents can submit applications. Applicants can either complete the application online or request a paper version by calling the county office. Julie Stellatos with Martin County Human Services emphasizes that funding operates on a first-come, first-served basis, making it crucial for homeowners to gather necessary paperwork quickly. About 75 percent of the county's annual SHIP funding, typically between $1 million and $1.5 million, can now be directed toward manufactured home assistance.

The application timeline varies across counties. Okeechobee County is updating its local housing assistance plan to comply with the new law and plans to offer programs once funding becomes available. St. Lucie County is awaiting final state guidance before announcing its specific application process. Indian River County will present the legislative changes to its Board on August 18th, with a September 30th deadline to submit its plan to the state. Residents can prepare now by visiting the Florida Housing Finance Corporation website, selecting their county or city, and checking income eligibility requirements and available funding amounts. Stellatos notes that while individual counties follow different timelines, they generally operate on a calendar established by the Florida Housing Coalition.

1:16 PM ET

Ashley Moody is celebrating the one-year milestone of what she describes as the largest tax cut in American history. President Donald Trump signed the Working Families Tax Cut Act into law on July 4, 2024, and Moody has been a vocal supporter of the legislation. As the first anniversary approaches, she is drawing attention to the tangible financial benefits that have reached Floridians and Americans across the country.

The measure eliminated federal taxes on three major categories of income: Social Security benefits, tips earned by service workers, and overtime pay. Moody has made clear her commitment to this tax relief approach. "It's not the government's money. It's your money, and you should be allowed to keep as much of it as possible. That is why I supported no tax on Social Security, no tax on tips, and no tax on overtime," she said in a statement released Thursday. She emphasized her intention to continue pursuing tax cuts and fiscal responsibility in her future role. "In the year since the tax cuts became law, it is clear that we delivered for real relief for Floridians, and as your U.S. Senator, I will continue to look for ways to cut taxes, eliminate waste and support economic growth here in the Sunshine State."

The numbers released by Moody's office demonstrate the widespread impact of the legislation. Nationally, Americans have claimed $82 billion in individual relief, with 97 percent of taxpayers experiencing a reduction in federal taxes during the most recent filing season. Specifically, 7.5 million Americans paid no federal tax on tips, 29 million avoided taxes on overtime earnings, and 35 million seniors claimed a larger tax deduction. Florida residents have fared particularly well under the law, receiving the largest average federal tax refunds in the nation. The average refund for Florida filers reached approximately $4,500, significantly exceeding the national average of $3,300.

The legislation also created new financial opportunities for younger Americans and their families. More than 5.5 million child-owned investment accounts have been opened since the law took effect, with 1.4 million of those accounts currently eligible for a $1,000 Treasury contribution. Parents of babies born between 2025 and 2028 can take advantage of this benefit if they choose to participate in the program. These provisions represent an effort to build long-term financial security for the next generation of Americans.

Alligator Alcatraz immigration detention facility closed after one year of controversy and questions about $1 billion cost

The controversial Everglades immigration detention facility opened by Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis and President Donald Trump on July 1, 2025 closed in June after detaining roughly 21,000 people, leaving questions about who will pay the estimated $1 billion cost.

Yesterday, 5:55 PM ET

One year after opening on July 1, 2025, Florida's Alligator Alcatraz immigration detention facility has closed after becoming one of the most controversial detention camps in the country. President Donald Trump announced the opening alongside Gov. Ron DeSantis, describing it as a place to house what he called the most dangerous migrants. The state used emergency powers to commandeer an old landing strip belonging to Miami-Dade County and built the facility in eight days using emergency funding and no-bid contracts through the Florida Division of Emergency Management. Trump said FEMA would pay for the facility. Using ICE's own data, investigations uncovered that about 70 percent of detainees had no criminal record, contradicting government claims that only criminals would be housed there.

The facility faced multiple federal lawsuits challenging its operations and legality. The American Civil Liberties Union sued over detainees' access to legal counsel, citing restrictions on attorney visits and monitored phone calls. Friends of the Everglades, led by Director Eve Samples, joined the Miccosukee Tribe and other environmental groups in a separate lawsuit claiming construction violated the National Environmental Policy Act. On June 16, ICE confirmed all detainees had been removed in preparation for hurricane season. DeSantis said 21,000 detainees were deported through the facility. A stalled $608 million FEMA grant left Florida taxpayers covering costs estimated at more than $1 billion. Both lawsuits remain active.
