With only bushes now marking the boundary between the public path and private land, the problem has escalated during peak summer months.

However, not everyone views the problem with the same urgency. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that."

Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring lower-cost alternatives. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. Riviera Beach has earned a significant national recognition by becoming one of just 10 communities across the United States selected to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city stands alone as the only Florida community honored with this distinction this year. The prestigious award recognizes cities that successfully bring together residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address community challenges and create meaningful change. City leaders presented Riviera Beach's accomplishments at the National Civic League conference in Denver, where judges announced the city as a first-time award winner.

Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the recognition, stating "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He credited the award to years of dedicated work aimed at revitalizing the city and improving residents' quality of life. "They're seeing the changes; they're seeing the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do," Lawson said. City leaders emphasize that the award reflects their commitment to strengthening civic health, building trust between residents and government, and ensuring community members have a direct voice in shaping the city's future.

The city's application highlighted several programs designed to increase civic participation. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy, while the Citizens Leadership Academy provides civic education with a 98 percent completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program offers mini-grants to help longtime homeowners make necessary repairs to their properties. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for making the achievement possible. "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents," Miller-Anderson said. "Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, ranks among the nation's most respected civic honors. City officials say the designation sends a powerful message that Riviera Beach is a community where collaboration produces results and residents actively shape the city's direction. The award also positions Riviera Beach for future opportunities and continued growth. Other cities receiving the 2026 award include Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

The cost of preparing a Fourth of July cookout for ten people has increased 4 percent compared to last year, according to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey. Families can now expect to spend $73.82 for a holiday meal, a reflection of broader inflationary pressures affecting grocery prices across the country and in Florida specifically.

Several key items driving the price increase stand out in the survey data. Ground beef prices have climbed 5.5 percent, while hamburger buns rose 7.7 percent. Florida's recent frost dealt a significant blow to the strawberry crop, pushing prices up 12.4 percent. Ice cream, a popular Fourth of July dessert, increased 5.3 percent. These increases represent the primary cost drivers for traditional cookout menus. However, not all items on holiday shopping lists have become more expensive. Potato prices have actually declined this year, offering some relief for families planning to serve potato salads or baked potatoes. Corn prices have varied throughout the season, but locally grown corn from the Glades can provide the best value for shoppers.

To manage rising grocery costs, budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends a strategic approach to shopping. Consumers should compare prices across different stores to identify the best deals for items on their shopping lists. Woroch emphasizes that reducing food waste is crucial for families trying to control expenses, and creating a meal plan serves as an effective first step in this process. "It's really important to shop savvier than ever, it is comparing the stores to see who has the best deals for the groceries on your list, the biggest thing for families is cutting down the food waste, and that begins with a meal plan," Woroch said.

Shoppers interviewed about their holiday plans highlighted the variety of cookout traditions families enjoy. Some plan to prepare hamburgers with coleslaw and chips for lunch, followed by a dinner of burnt ends and mashed potatoes with fresh broccoli. Others described simpler menus featuring hamburgers, beef, steak, and baked potatoes. Despite the inflationary pressures affecting prices this Fourth of July, these traditional cookout favorites remain popular choices for holiday celebrations, even as families adjust their budgets to accommodate higher costs.

Beth Clark, 48, was diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma three months ago. Less than 24 hours before her reconstruction surgery, her insurance company, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, denied coverage for the procedure.

10:13 AM ET

Beth Clark, 48, was diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma three months ago and decided to undergo a double bilateral mastectomy with reconstruction. Less than 24 hours before her reconstruction surgery, her insurance company, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, denied coverage for the procedure. According to her denial letter, the surgery involved a surgical mesh called GalaFlex, which the insurer considers "investigational" with "no or not enough proof it improves health." GalaFlex is a synthetic surgical mesh popular among plastic surgeons and FDA-approved for some uses in the body, but it is not approved for breast reconstruction and remains an off-label, investigational treatment. In a 2023 letter to health care providers, the FDA stated that the treatment's "efficacy and safety for breast cancer patients have not been determined."

Dr. Alicia Billington, Clark's plastic surgeon, uses GalaFlex mesh on most of her breast reconstruction patients and believes insurance companies should reconsider their coverage policies. Billington has been a vocal critic of insurance denials, sharing Clark's case on social media and calling for policy changes. Last year, after investigative reporting shared breast cancer patients' stories, Cigna became the first and only major insurer to start approving GalaFlex for breast reconstruction. Anthem responded by stating the mesh "does not currently meet the standards required for coverage by our medical policy." Despite the denial, Clark received her reconstruction surgery with GalaFlex mesh more than a week ago, and she and Billington continue fighting the denial.

3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has been named one of 10 communities nationwide to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the only Florida city selected for the honor this year. The award was announced during the National Civic League conference in Denver and recognizes communities that bring residents, local government, businesses and organizations together to solve local challenges and create lasting change. Mayor Douglas Lawson said the recognition reflects the city's long-term effort to revitalize Riviera Beach and improve quality of life for residents. "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars," Lawson said. "They're seeing the changes; they're seeing the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do."

Riviera Beach's application highlighted three civic programs: the Riviera Beach Youth Council, which involves high school students in civic leadership and advocacy; the Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education initiative with a 98 percent completion rate; and the Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program, which provides mini-grants to longtime homeowners for critical repairs. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for the honor. "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents," Miller-Anderson said. She noted that winning the award reinforces the city's positive momentum and brings back community pride.

Secretary of State Cord Byrd asks court to keep Paula Stark off ballot after incomplete filing

Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking courts to keep Paula Stark off the ballot after the St. Cloud Republican failed to submit required paperwork by the qualifying deadline.

5:56 PM ET

Paula Stark's incomplete qualifying materials were timestamped less than 30 minutes before the noon deadline on June 12, and she is now suing to get on the ballot. In the state's response to her Leon Circuit Court lawsuit, Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking the courts to keep Stark off the ballot and cast doubt on her claims about an alleged paperwork mix-up. Five state employees gave sworn written court declarations stating that Stark's treasurer and partner, Joel Davis, never submitted the crucial Form 6 to them nor was he accidentally handed it back when he submitted the rest of the paperwork in the final hour of the qualifying window. Stark was one of 12 candidates who didn't properly turn in Form 6, a public disclosure of financial interests. Half of them corrected the problems and still qualified. Reeve also noted Stark's years of paperwork problems, including a campaign finance report filed over one month late, audits that found inconsistent amounts transferred between campaign and office accounts, and unpaid fines. The state revoked Stark's Friends of Paula political committee in November over $16,000 in unpaid fines from missed campaign finance deadlines. California company proposes elevated self-driving transit connecting West Palm Beach to airport

Glydways will present a proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for an elevated network of autonomous vehicles connecting PBIA to the convention center and nearby Brightline and Tri-Rail stations.

Yesterday, 5:49 PM ET

A California-based autonomous transit company is proposing an elevated network of self-driving vehicles connecting downtown West Palm Beach to President Donald J. Trump International Airport. Glydways, which has projects underway in Atlanta and California, will present the proposal to Palm Beach County commissioners Tuesday for initial consideration. The company describes the system as a modern alternative to traditional transit such as light rail, streetcars and people movers.

Small autonomous electric vehicles carrying four to six passengers would travel along a dedicated elevated guideway built above existing roads. The system would bypass traffic congestion entirely, potentially cutting the trip between downtown and the airport to less than 10 minutes. Glydways estimates travel times of seven minutes or less between the convention center and airport and roughly one to two minutes between Tri-Rail and the convention center. While Glydways has not disclosed a specific price tag, Senior Vice President Brian Gettinger said the system would cost a small fraction of a traditional light rail system while offering comparable passenger capacity. He referenced previous South Florida light rail discussions that carried estimates of 400 million to 500 million dollars per mile. Funding could potentially come from Palm Beach County, the City of West Palm Beach, the Florida Department of Transportation, federal transit grants and public-private partnerships. Tuesday's meeting is not expected to approve construction, but rather determine whether the county should formally evaluate the proposal.

Fourth of July cookout costs rise 4 percent as ground beef and buns see largest increases

According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, a holiday meal for 10 now costs $73.82, up 4 percent from a year ago.

A few minutes ago

The Fourth of July cookout tradition is getting more expensive this year. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, a holiday meal for 10 now costs $73.82, up 4 percent from a year ago. Inflation is driving the increases across multiple items. Ground beef prices have risen 5.5 percent, while buns are up 7.7 percent. Strawberries, impacted by a Florida frost, have seen the largest increase at 12.4 percent. Ice cream prices are also up 5.3 percent.

Not all cookout staples are more expensive this year. Potato prices have decreased, offering savings for those planning potato chips or salads. Corn prices have fluctuated, though locally grown corn from the Glades can provide the best deals. Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends families shop more strategically by comparing prices across stores to find the best deals on their grocery lists. She emphasized that cutting down on food waste through meal planning is the biggest opportunity for families to manage costs during the holiday season.

Breast cancer patient's insurance denies reconstruction surgery hours before procedure

Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield denied coverage for Beth Clark's breast reconstruction surgery less than 24 hours before the scheduled procedure, citing GalaFlex mesh as investigational.

10:13 AM ET

Beth Clark, 48, was diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma three months ago and underwent a full double bilateral mastectomy. Less than 24 hours before her scheduled reconstruction surgery, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield denied coverage for the procedure, which involved GalaFlex surgical mesh. The denial letter stated the treatment is "investigational" and "there is no or not enough proof it improves health." GalaFlex is a synthetic surgical mesh approved by the FDA for some uses in the body but not for breast reconstruction, making it an off-label, investigational and experimental treatment. In a 2023 letter to health care providers, the FDA stated its "efficacy and safety for breast cancer patients have not been determined."

Dr. Alicia Billington, Clark's plastic surgeon, uses GalaFlex mesh on most of her breast reconstruction patients and argues it produces fewer complications than other options. "This product is giving my patients the least amount of complications, and other plastic surgeons as well," Billington said. Despite Anthem's denial, Clark received the reconstruction surgery with GalaFlex mesh a little more than a week ago after Billington found a mechanism to make it happen. Last year, Cigna became the first and only major insurer to start approving GalaFlex for breast reconstruction, following investigative reporting by Katie LaGrone and photojournalist Matthew Apthorp as part of their "Patient No More" series.
