DeSantis signs $117.6 billion Florida budget with $1.6 billion in vetoes hitting Indian River State College and local projects

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed Florida's $117.6 billion state budget Monday in Tampa, using his line-item veto power to cut approximately $1.6 billion from the spending plan lawmakers approved. The budget takes effect July 1 and represents the fourth consecutive year DeSantis has reduced overall state spending through vetoes. The governor framed the action as fiscal responsibility, n...

10:57 AM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed Florida's $117.6 billion state budget Monday in Tampa, using his line-item veto power to cut approximately $1.6 billion from the spending plan lawmakers approved. The budget takes effect July 1 and represents the fourth consecutive year DeSantis has reduced overall state spending through vetoes. The governor framed the action as fiscal responsibility, noting that Florida is one of few states actually cutting budgets rather than simply discussing it. He highlighted the state's roughly $18 billion in reserves and progress on debt reduction. "The debt has been cut in half, the reserves are good, the rainy day fund is flush," DeSantis said, adding that these are accomplishments "very few are able to actually accomplish."

The $1.6 billion in vetoes was distributed broadly across state priorities. About $800 million targeted direct line-item spending. Florida's corrections system faced some of the largest reductions, with tens of millions cut from prison repairs, new housing units, capital improvements, and pay adjustments. The governor argued that some corrections spending was tied to new debt he did not want the state to assume. Water and resiliency projects also took a significant hit, with more than $170 million cut from environment, water, agriculture, resiliency, and parks-related items. This included local stormwater, wastewater, flood mitigation, and infrastructure projects throughout Florida.

Education spending also experienced notable reductions. The vetoes included $15 million for Catholic school security alongside millions in cuts to college infrastructure and programs. Affected institutions included Palm Beach State College, College of the Florida Keys, Indian River State College, and Florida State University's College of Nursing. DeSantis said the final budget still funded major priorities including education, transportation, environmental restoration, law enforcement, and tax relief.

House Minority Leader Fentrice Driskell criticized the vetoes, claiming Democratic projects were cut at roughly twice the rate of Republican projects. She questioned the governor's priorities, suggesting he was more focused on cutting the budget than considering the people affected by the reductions. "What is this governor's priority?" Driskell asked. "Because he seems to be more obsessed with talking points and saying that he cut the budget rather than actually caring about the people who are going to be impacted by those cuts." DeSantis has previously stated that he considers lawmakers' support of his policy agenda when reviewing budget requests. Over eight years, DeSantis said his vetoes have totaled more than $10 billion, far exceeding cuts by recent predecessors combined.

A few minutes ago

Governor Ron DeSantis has signed HB 359, legislation sponsored by Rep. Adam Anderson that extends the deadline for law enforcement to execute search warrants on encrypted phones, computers, and other digital devices from 45 days to one year. The new law is designed to modernize criminal justice procedures and provide law enforcement with realistic time frames to investigate digital evidence in cases involving child sexual abuse material, child exploitation, human trafficking, homicide, and other serious crimes where digital evidence is essential to securing convictions. Anderson said the change reflects modern realities in which digital devices are becoming more frequent tools of crime. "Protecting Florida's children and putting dangerous criminals behind bars should never be derailed by outdated laws that can't keep pace with modern technology," Anderson said.

The legislation builds on reforms enacted following the 2023 Moschella v. State decision. Law enforcement agencies across the state argued that the previous 45-day deadline remained inadequate due to increasingly sophisticated encryption technology, limited forensic resources, and significant laboratory backlogs. State Attorney Bruce Bartlett, who serves in Pinellas County, praised the bill, noting that the volume of child sexual abuse material law enforcement investigates has risen exponentially. The new law gives law enforcement adequate time to access digital evidence without making repeated extension requests on existing warrants, reducing unnecessary court proceedings. It does not expand authority to seize devices or create new search powers for law enforcement.

The bill would have funded $50 million annually in corrections capital projects for 40 years and mandated construction of a new 600-bed corrections hospital in Gilchrist County using bond funding. Department of Corrections Secretary Ricky Dixon has previously called for higher officer salaries, noting Florida lags behind neighboring states. In April, Dixon said Alabama pays officers $35 an hour after 18 months, compared to Florida's proposed $24 an hour.

Jay Collins sues to remove James Fishback from Florida Governor's race over residency requirement

Gubernatorial candidate Jay Collins filed a lawsuit challenging GOP Primary opponent James Fishback's eligibility for office, arguing Fishback does not meet a seven-year Florida residency requirement.

A few minutes ago

Gubernatorial candidate Jay Collins filed a lawsuit in Leon County Circuit Court challenging GOP Primary opponent James Fishback's eligibility for office, arguing he does not meet Florida's seven-year residency requirement. The legal complaint notes that Fishback registered to vote in Washington, D.C., in November 2020 and voted in city elections, requiring him to establish legal residency in the District of Columbia and declare he did not claim voting residence in any other state or territory, including Florida. The lawsuit documents that Fishback later purchased a home in Washington and claimed it as his principal residence to receive a homestead tax deduction. According to documents filed with the lawsuit, he still claimed that deduction as recently as February 2024. Though Fishback corrected the form in April, requiring additional tax payments and penalties, the lawsuit argues this does not resolve whether he maintained Florida residency for the required seven-year period before running for Governor.

Collins, Florida's current Lieutenant Governor, also named Secretary of State Cord Byrd as a plaintiff, contending the Division of Elections should not have granted Fishback qualified candidate status earlier this month. The lawsuit alleges Fishback made false statements on his candidate oath. Collins said the law must be enforced before voters receive ballots for the Republican Primary in August. Eleven Republicans have qualified for Governor this year, including U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds and former Florida House Speaker Paul Renner. Fishback, who lists Madison as his current residence, claims he is a fourth-generation Floridian and has repeatedly shown driver's licenses to verify his Florida residency. Collins has requested an expedited hearing on whether Fishback should be removed from the ballot.

Ron DeSantis has vetoed more than $8.2 million from the budget for Hillsborough County, including cuts to an advanced metering project, flood relief, a scholarship readiness pilot and more.

3:00 PM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis vetoed more than $8.2 million in funding for Hillsborough County projects from the Fiscal Year 2026-27 budget. His largest veto was a $2 million funding item from Sen. Michael Owen for advanced metering infrastructure in the county. Advanced metering provides utility users with more accurate usage information and the ability to monitor for water leaks. The $2 million was the full funding request and would have covered a small portion of an overall $30 million project affecting about 15,000 residents. DeSantis also slashed $1.5 million for a flood relief project on South Howard Avenue, where flooding is prevalent, which had been requested by Karen Gonzalez Pittman. Nearly $879,000 earmarked for a Hillsborough County Schools pilot program to enhance Bright Futures scholarship eligibility and readiness, requested by Rep. Traci Koster, was also cut. The funds would have supported a pilot project with SchoolLinks, a platform offering real-time scholarship eligibility tracking.

Additional vetoes included $800,000 from the Children's Network of Hillsborough's CHANCE program supporting victims of human trafficking and $750,000 from Tampa Bay Watch for shoreline protection and water quality improvements. DeSantis also cut $675,000 for a fallen firefighters memorial and $260,000 for Hillsborough County Fire Rescue emergency management. Other vetoed items included $500,000 for intersection improvements at 30th Street and Sligh Avenue in Tampa, $265,000 for an AMSkills accelerator program for trades education, $250,000 for Hillsborough College's artificial intelligence program, and $200,000 for University of South Florida neuromusculoskeletal research.
