Medicare fraud scheme linked to Delray Beach targets same seniors twice through new Texas company

A Russian national connected to a Delray Beach medical equipment company accused of Medicare fraud allegedly continued submitting bills to the federal health program through a new Texas business after the original company's payments were suspended. Mike and Connie Parrett were among dozens of Medicare recipients who contacted WPTV after Sunshine Senior Solutions submitted fraud...

Yesterday, 4:40 PM ET

A Russian national connected to a Delray Beach medical equipment company accused of Medicare fraud allegedly continued submitting bills to the federal health program through a new Texas business after the original company's payments were suspended. Mike and Connie Parrett were among dozens of Medicare recipients who contacted WPTV after Sunshine Senior Solutions submitted fraudulent claims in Connie's name for medical equipment she never requested or received. More than a year later, the Parretts received another Medicare statement, this time billing Mike for services through a company called Centurion Superior Medical in Austin, Texas.

Federal investigators have connected Centurion to the same individuals behind Sunshine Senior Solutions. A criminal complaint names Nika Machutadze, a Russian national, as someone who allegedly used Centurion to bill Medicare in 2025 after the government suspended payments to Sunshine Senior Solutions in late 2024. Business records show Machutadze listed as an owner of both companies. Mike Parrett's Medicare statement revealed that two of six charges from Centurion were denied, though all charges appeared during 2025. The bills primarily involved catheters spread across multiple invoices, mirroring the original fraudulent charges his wife had received.

Dr. David Maimon, a cyberfraud expert who has studied the Sunshine Senior Solutions case, explains that criminals typically maintain lists of victim identities and frequently share these lists with other fraudsters, making seniors vulnerable to repeated targeting. Maimon advises potential victims to freeze their credit and consider purchasing identity protection services. He highlighted the Justice Department's newly created National Fraud Enforcement Division, which focuses specifically on Medicare fraud investigations, as evidence that the government is treating these crimes with greater urgency.

Machutadze was charged with conspiracy to commit money laundering in January 2026 and arrested in Texas the following month. He remains in custody as his case proceeds through federal court in West Palm Beach. The case illustrates how medical equipment fraud schemes can operate across multiple states and target the same victims repeatedly, even after initial fraudulent activity has been detected and suspended by federal authorities.

Yesterday, 8:20 PM ET

Treasure Coast Square Mall has remained a community landmark for many years, but recent discussions about its future have reignited concerns among both shoppers and business owners. In 2023, Martin County initiated a study examining potential redevelopment options that would transform the property. The proposed concepts include residential apartments, an amphitheater, and green spaces designed as an outdoor shopping environment. County officials characterized the study as a preliminary planning exercise rather than a committed development plan.

Business owners operating inside the mall are closely monitoring the redevelopment discussions and considering what changes might mean for their operations. Jessica Henry, owner of Plantitude and Flame, expressed hope that any future redesign would incorporate open concept stores while allowing existing businesses to continue operating. Ron Gustafson, who runs MVP Sport Cards, reports that his store has thrived in its current location and notes the business model has been successful. However, Gustafson has experienced persistent water damage problems and believes the mall requires significant improvements. He stated that the property shows signs of neglect but could recover with proper management and investment.

Customer opinions about the mall vary, though most express openness to improvements. Shopper Liz Permar called it a fun destination, while another customer, Kimberly Grucza, described it as an acceptable if not ideal location. Both shoppers welcomed the idea of updates and renovations. Permar supported the concept of a facelift, and Grucza indicated she would appreciate modernization efforts. Not everyone supports all aspects of the proposed redevelopment plan, however. Grucza voiced concerns specifically about the apartment component, noting that the surrounding area already contains numerous residential developments and does not need additional housing options.

County and mall leadership have maintained cautious stances on the proposal's future. Martin County Administrator Don Donaldson reiterated that the 2023 study was created as a planning exercise and remains in that capacity. Treasure Coast Square management similarly stated that whether any plans will be implemented remains uncertain. The redevelopment options outlined in the county study continue to be discussed among stakeholders, while shoppers and business owners await clarification about what changes the mall may experience in the coming years.

Following this preparation match, the English national team has relocated to Palm Beach Gardens for additional training sessions before moving to their Kansas City base camp this weekend. The team also has another warm-up game scheduled in Orlando before the World Cup tournament begins. England's opening World Cup match is set for next Wednesday against Croatia.

Manager Thomas Tuchel emphasized the team's commitment to progressing during their time in Palm Beach Gardens. The training facilities in Palm Beach Gardens employ specific techniques to optimize playing conditions. At Gardens District Park, staff water the fields moments before the team arrives for practice. Andrew Keller, Palm Beach Gardens Director of Agronomy, explained the reasoning behind this preparation. The moisture on the grass helps the ball move more quickly across the field rather than catching on the grass and rolling, allowing players to practice at speeds that replicate actual stadium conditions. The Cornell Art Museum at Old School Square in Delray Beach has launched Persistence of Matter, a summer exhibition celebrating artists who repurpose discarded materials into compelling artwork. The show aligns with the museum's commitment to blending artistic creativity with meaningful community engagement, according to Marusca Gatto, Cultural Arts Director for the Delray Beach Downtown Development Authority. Guest curators Mavis Benson and Pamela Lambie assembled a diverse group of artists working across multiple mediums, all using reclaimed objects as their primary materials.

The exhibition features works ranging from sculptures to installations to mixed media pieces. Among the highlighted artists is Autumn Kioti, whose creations draw inspiration from both natural and urban environments. Another featured artist, Mike Silverman, has created the Self-Reflection series using salvaged hard drive platters and printed circuit boards to construct reflective sculptures. Silverman explained that his work encourages viewers to examine their own relationship with technology and waste in fresh ways. "Every piece invites viewers to see themselves and their relationship to technology and waste in a new light," he said.

As sustainability becomes an increasingly urgent concern for communities, Persistence of Matter serves as an artistic exploration of how discarded materials maintain value and purpose. The exhibition demonstrates how everyday waste can be transformed into beautiful and thought-provoking art, fostering important conversations about environmental responsibility through innovative creative practice. The show remains on view through September 10, 2026, and includes a range of public programming designed to deepen visitor engagement.

The museum offers several ways to participate in the exhibition beyond simply viewing the artwork. Visitors can attend artist talks, participate in salon discussions, and watch film screenings throughout the run. The Cornell Art Museum maintains regular hours Wednesday through Sunday, opening at noon on Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday until 5 p.m., 7 p.m., and 7 p.m. respectively. Saturday hours run from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Sunday from noon to 5 p.m. For more information about the exhibition or special programs, interested visitors can call 561-654-2220.

Tampa Electric to cut customer rates 11% starting August 1, ending storm surcharge early

Tampa Electric announced Tuesday it will remove a storm recovery surcharge from customer bills effective August 1, a month earlier than originally planned.

4:15 AM ET

Tampa Electric customers will see an 11% to 12% reduction in monthly electric costs starting August 1, when the utility removes a storm recovery surcharge from bills a month earlier than originally planned. The change will lower bills by roughly $20 per 1,000 kilowatt-hours of electricity used. The surcharge was approved by the Florida Public Service Commission in March 2025 to allow Tampa Electric to recover more than $400 million in restoration costs following Florida's 2024 hurricane season. Originally expected to remain through August with removal beginning in September, the utility now says those costs will be fully recovered sooner than anticipated.

The surcharge stemmed from Hurricanes Helene and Milton, which brought extensive flooding and widespread damage from downed trees to West Central Florida. Tampa Electric deployed approximately 9,500 out-of-state utility workers during recovery efforts. Customers enrolled in the Budget Billing program may not see the full reduction immediately, as the program is designed to smooth monthly bill fluctuations over time. Tampa Electric said those customers will experience more gradual adjustments as the surcharge rolls off. The utility encouraged customers experiencing financial hardship to contact them about payment arrangements and assistance programs. The rate reduction affects approximately 870,000 customers across West Central Florida.

Palm Beach County faces nearly $400 million budget cuts as property tax reductions loom

Palm Beach County officials are preparing for what could be the largest budget cuts in county history, with funding for 30 departments potentially dropping from $609 million to approximately $210 million over the next two years.

Yesterday, 4:33 PM ET

Palm Beach County officials are bracing for what could be the largest budget cuts in the county's history, as proposed property tax reductions threaten to eliminate nearly $400 million from county operations over the next two years. County Administrator Joe Abruzzo announced during a budget meeting that if the property tax reduction passes, the county's budget for 30 departments could drop from $609 million to approximately $210 million, a loss of more than 65% of current funding. The Palm Beach County Fire Department alone stands to lose just under $200 million in funding under the proposed tax structure. The cuts would affect virtually every county service, including parks, libraries and roads. A major concern centers on the proposed $250,000 homestead exemption, which may not represent the final figure. Abruzzo warned commissioners that the exemption could potentially increase to $500,000, further deepening the budget shortfall.

County leaders are examining millage rates and identifying cost-saving opportunities, with particular attention on the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office budget, which has increased more than 216% over the past 20 years. To help offset the budget shortfall, county officials discussed various revenue-generating options, including charging for parking at county beaches. Commissioners agreed they do not want residents to pay too much to park but reached agreement on implementing beach parking fees as one measure to help bridge the funding gap. Mike Caruso, the Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller for Palm Beach County, suggested increasing the limit on how much the county is allowed to invest in Israel bonds from 15% to 20%, which would earn the county an additional $3 million a year in interest income. Commissioners agreed the maximum millage rate will be set at 4.5 in July, with final budget approval scheduled for September.

England's national soccer team trained in Palm Beach Gardens after a 1-0 win over New Zealand in Tampa, using facilities at Gardens District Park.

Yesterday, 6:06 PM ET

England's World Cup team trained in Palm Beach Gardens at Gardens District Park after securing a 1-0 win over New Zealand in a pre-World Cup friendly in Tampa over the weekend. Harry Kane scored the only goal with a header. England has another tuneup scheduled in Orlando before the tournament begins. Manager Thomas Tuchel spoke about the team's preparation, saying "We expect to push tomorrow physical and from intensity and style of play, ball speed and everything, want to take the next step." Tuchel also praised the quality of the facilities. Star defender Marc Guehi echoed those comments, saying "Great, fantastic. Everyone is super friendly and welcoming to us, felt at home straight away here, and in West Palm Beach. The quality of the pitch, really good so far."

Guehi also addressed the challenge of adjusting to Florida's summer heat, saying "First day was extremely hot. We've been doing better getting used to the weather, getting used to the conditions, definitely a big step for us." At Gardens District Park, the fields were watered moments before the team came out for practice. Palm Beach Gardens Director of Agronomy Andrew Keller explained that the moisture on the grass helps the ball skip across the field rather than catching on the grass, allowing the team to play faster and replicate stadium conditions. England plans to leave South Florida for its Kansas City base camp this weekend. The team's first World Cup match is next Wednesday against Croatia. Portugal's national team is also scheduled to train on the same fields after England's departure.

Deloitte volunteers support Florida nonprofits on annual Impact Day across Tampa, Orlando, Jacksonville and Miami

Professionals from Deloitte offices across Florida volunteered with local charities and nonprofits on June 5 as part of Impact Day, an annual day of service that helps create positive impact for local communities and residents.

4:30 AM ET

On June 5, Deloitte professionals from offices across Florida volunteered with local nonprofits as part of Impact Day, an annual day of service. Deloitte's Tampa and Orlando offices mobilized approximately 500 volunteers across 19 service projects. Working alongside organizations including Feeding Tampa Bay, Hillsborough County Pet Resource Center, Girls Inc. Sarasota, Ronald McDonald House, Habitat for Humanity of Tampa Bay Hillsborough, Goodwill Industries, Children's Cancer Center, 1Voice Foundation, Quantum Leap Farm and Disabled American Veterans, Deloitte professionals took part in warehouse sorting, food pantry support, landscaping, painting, camp assistance, pet enrichment and disaster kit assembly.

In Orlando, teams continued a multiyear relationship with Zebra Youth, painting a mural and improving the organization's space. Another Orlando effort mobilized 75 volunteers across three shifts to support The WASH Foundation, sorting and recycling soap and hygiene products to expand access to basic sanitation resources for vulnerable populations worldwide. In Jacksonville, approximately 100 Deloitte professionals volunteered at the Jacksonville Humane Society, the American Heart Association and St. Johns Riverkeeper, participating in river cleanups, animal care support and CPR and AED certification training. In Tallahassee, the Deloitte team sorted donations at The Salvation Army to support families facing immediate hardship. In Miami, Deloitte professionals spent Impact Day with The Posse Foundation, leading workshops on time management, wellness, active listening and goal setting.
