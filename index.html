By Saturday, June 6, the situation escalated significantly when crews returned to the same area to battle a much larger 120-acre brush fire. Officials believe the fire rekindled from the previous day's incident. Shifting winds and dense vegetation contributed to rapid fire growth, prompting a large-scale response from MCFR, FFS and the Martin County Sheriff's Office.

The fire reached approximately 125 acres in total size and is 99% contained as of Sunday morning. Florida Forest Service remains in the area monitoring conditions to ensure no further flare-ups occur. Officials noted early indications suggest it may have been a holdover fire, meaning a lightning strike could have smoldered underground for days before igniting visible flames. The Florida Forest Service is continuing to investigate the cause.

Yesterday, 9:13 AM ET

Orion Jean Baptiste, a 9-year-old Port St. Lucie resident, has started a new junior chess club to encourage other young people to embrace the game. Baptiste first developed his interest in chess at age 5 while playing with family members, and he now wants to share that passion with his community. His goal is straightforward: to help kids learn chess and develop a greater appreciation for board games. The Atlantis Chess Club meets every Friday at Morningside Branch Library, where more than 36 junior members have already signed up. The club will soon expand with two additional programs launching at the Fenn Center in Fort Pierce.

Baptiste expressed concern about young people's focus on technology at the expense of traditional games. "Everybody's more focused on like tech stuff," he said. "I'm hoping that more people have respect for board games." Diana Cowan Kempton, the club's program director, sees chess as a valuable tool for developing young leaders. According to Kempton, the game will help build members who are more focused, patient, strategic, and thoughtful when making decisions. The club welcomes players of all experience levels, from complete beginners to those already skilled at the game who want to continue improving.

The club has attracted families seeking educational opportunities for their children. Thomas Furlong brought his two children to join, viewing it as an ideal setting where his daughter could learn the game and his son could advance his skills. He also appreciated the friendly competition between siblings. His daughter, Francesca Furlong, is one of the club's newest members and particularly enjoys the social aspect of playing with other children her age.

The Friday meetings at Morningside Branch Library remain open to any junior players from Port St. Lucie and surrounding areas of the Treasure Coast. Baptiste's initiative demonstrates how one young person's enthusiasm can create opportunities for many others in the community to explore a classic game that develops critical thinking skills and strategic planning abilities. The expanding program shows strong community support for chess instruction among young people in the region.

With less than a week until the qualifying deadline on June 12, twenty-five Florida House district races have no challenger from one of the two major political parties. While most of these uncontested seats are in strongly partisan districts where a challenge would be unlikely to succeed, five races stand out as genuinely competitive. These five districts, which include two Republican-held seats and three Democratic-held seats, have voter margins narrow enough that the lack of opposition from the other party raises serious questions about campaign strategy and resource allocation.

The five competitive uncontested districts are scattered across Florida. House District 12 in Jacksonville, which favored Trump by 10 points in 2024, has only Republican Terrance Freeman running to succeed term-limited Representative Wyman Duggan. House District 44 in Orange County is a solidly Democratic district where Rita Harris is running unopposed for her third term, having won reelection in 2024 with 65 percent of the vote. House District 60 in Pinellas County covers parts of St. Petersburg and Pinellas Park and leans Democratic by 7 points. After incumbent Lindsay Cross announced her retirement, lawyer Lindsay Polega-Quigley filed to run and currently faces no opposition. House District 89 in Palm Beach County represents the closest race, with a Democratic lean of just 1 point. Democratic Representative Debra Tendrich won the seat in 2024 by only 2 points and is running unopposed. House District 87 in Jupiter, which Democrat Emily Gregory flipped in a March upset, remains an important race as both parties assess their November prospects.

The Florida Democratic Party responded to questions about the unchallenged races by emphasizing their investment in year-round organizing and candidate recruitment. The party said over 1,300 Democrats have expressed interest in running over the past eighteen months. Republican Party of Florida Chair Evan Power expressed confidence that his party would expand its legislative supermajority, stating that "Floridians have rejected the radical left agenda" and that the Republican Party would "field quality candidates across Florida that will drive us to big victories."

Analysis by Matt Isbell of MCI Maps, based on 2024 presidential voting data, shows these competitive districts have voter divisions that suggest both parties could potentially mount successful campaigns. The August 18 primary election will be followed by the November general election. The qualifying deadline is June 12.

Summer fertilizer ban begins June 1 in Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties

Jun 5, 5:36 PM ET

Starting June 1, Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties are implementing a ban on fertilizers containing phosphorus or nitrogen. The restriction applies to anyone treating lawns or landscaping in these three counties through September 30.

According to Jordan Skaggs with Environmental Resources in St. Lucie County, the ban targets nutrient-rich runoff during the rainy season that can cause algae blooms in waterways like the Indian River Lagoon and St. Lucie River. "It can shade out beneficial plants to fish and wildlife," Skaggs said. "At certain densities, harmful algae blooms can release toxins that can affect human health, wildlife health and overall ecosystem health." Grantly Ricketts, director of University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences St. Lucie County Extension, recommends composting as an alternative during the ban. "It can be like a small trash can," Ricketts said. "That is the easiest and most practical way or means to add nutrients to your landscape."

Florida dive operators fight federal shark feeding ban proposal

A federal proposal to ban shark feeding off Florida's coast is drawing sharp criticism from the state's shark diving industry, which warns the measure could devastate conservation-driven tourism businesses.

Jun 5, 5:55 PM ET

The Safe Florida Seas Act, H.R. 3831, cleared the U.S. House and would prohibit shark feeding in federal waters within 200 miles of Florida, with an exception for harvesting. Supporters call it a common-sense safety measure to reduce human-shark interactions. However, a coalition of dive operators argues the bill unfairly targets their work, which includes veteran-owned small businesses and nonprofits focused on education and conservation, while leaving fishing practices untouched. Nathan Campbell, owner of Emerald Charters, says Congress has not asked dive operators to present data or explain their practices.

Operators distinguish their methods from commercial fishing, saying they avoid surface chumming and instead take bait underwater during dives. This approach, they argue, is less likely to condition sharks to approach boats for food, unlike some harvesting operations that release hundreds of gallons of chum at the surface. Dr. Matt Ajemian, an associate research professor at Florida Atlantic University, says shark diving in Florida has a near perfect safety record. Campbell points to Hawaii's 2006 ban on baited shark diving as a cautionary tale, noting that shark incidents have increased there since the ban and that at least one in four fishing trips ends with a shark biting off part of the catch, according to the Western Pacific Regional Fishery Management Council. Campbell is urging senators to delay action, gather research, and involve all stakeholders in shaping new rules.

However, demand far outweighs available funds each month. Program director Dr. James Green told WPTV that the county typically receives about 600 applications within the first couple of hours applications open, and receives nearly 8,000 calls for housing assistance per month combined with 211. For those who cannot access the county's program, 211 is another option available 24/7 that connects residents with nonprofits and other assistance programs in the area. Green is calling on people with extra housing to partner with the county to help neighbors in need.
