Port St. Lucie woman sentenced to 3 years in prison for running illegal med spa out of backyard shed

Yesterday, 10:35 PM ET

Rosa Mena, 50, of Port St. Lucie has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation for operating an unlicensed medical spa out of a shed behind her home. Mena ran the business, called Miracle Hand and Spa, from her residence in the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive. She was convicted on multiple charges including three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, and fraud charges.

The investigation began in May 2025 when a victim reported partial facial paralysis after receiving 62 units of Botox injections from Mena. The victim, who had been referred to the business by coworkers, paid $325 for the services. When the paralysis persisted, Mena offered to inject vitamins to reverse the issues and refunded the money. The victim requested to see Mena's medical license, and Mena provided an altered phlebotomy certificate that had actually expired in February 2024. Mena also claimed to be a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

Port St. Lucie Police, along with PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax, and the Florida Department of Health, executed a search warrant at Mena's residence. Detectives discovered extensive medical equipment including medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines, and injection devices for fillers and Botox. The equipment and evidence confirmed Mena had been operating a full medical spa operation without proper licensing or medical credentials.

The case highlights the dangers of unlicensed medical procedures and the consequences for those who operate illegal healthcare businesses. Port St. Lucie authorities worked across multiple agencies to investigate and prosecute Mena, who now faces three years behind bars followed by five years of supervised probation. The charges included use of a two-way communication device to facilitate a felony, fraud-swindle to obtain property valued at $300 or more, and fraud-swindle to obtain property under $20,000.

The town's Emergency Operations Center could face up to 9 feet of floodwater.

Commissioner Marshall Field said the findings confirm what town leaders already knew, which is why they requested the assessment. The vulnerability assessment will create a roadmap for adaptation, allowing the town to prioritize improvements. Field said Jupiter Island plans to compete for state and federal funding to address vulnerabilities and will apply for a funding window that opened June 1. He noted that evacuation orders would be issued well before floodwaters threaten roadways and the town has a backup facility on the mainland to maintain operations during emergencies.
