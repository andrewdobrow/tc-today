A major waterfront development in Florida is using provisions of the state's Live Local Act to proceed without complying with local zoning requirements. The law allows developers to bypass municipal zoning codes when projects meet certain criteria related to affordable housing.

The development is described as a large-scale project on waterfront property. By invoking the Live Local Act, the developers can move forward with plans that would otherwise not comply with existing local land use regulations.

Local officials have limited ability to stop the project under the state law, which was designed to encourage housing construction. The development represents one of the first major uses of the Live Local Act to override local zoning control on the Treasure Coast.

Teens from anti-drug coalitions across the country joined grieving parents and federal law enforcement at the Fentanyl Free America Summit in Orlando this week. The summit drew students who traveled from around the country to learn about the resurgence of fentanyl so they can return home with a message about one of the country's deadliest drug threats. Hannah Kahn, 18, attended the summit alongside other teen advocates. Jasmine Figueroa, 19, said she has a relative who is addicted to methamphetamine and that seeing how that has destroyed his life encouraged her to prevent that from happening to anybody else.

The students are members of their community anti-drug coalitions who came with questions and answers about how fentanyl spreads and why young people may not recognize the danger. Kahn said people are not seeking out fentanyl necessarily because it is just laced within other drugs. The teens had pointed advice for adults on how to talk to young people about drugs. Figueroa said it is not a taboo topic and adults should tell them what cocaine is because they are going to get educated one way or another. She said adults might as well be the first ones to educate young people and tell them the truth about how things are.

This year's summit welcomed a powerful new partner, the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration. DEA Administrator Terrance Cole spent time with victim families and said the poison is being manufactured and distributed out of Mexico by the Mexican cartels. Cole said the president has designated Mexican cartels as foreign terrorists, which opens different avenues for the DEA to target them. For parents who have lost children, the summit is deeply personal. Mari Rodriguez's daughter, Imam, died of fentanyl poisoning in 2023. Rodriguez said her daughter made the mistake of buying a Percocet pill that had fentanyl in it and it took her life. Rodriguez said they are killing our children, our youth, and our future. She added that the U.S. should have been working on this since 2009, when the first fentanyl was found on our streets, because these monsters are not stopping.

For the teens at the summit, prevention comes down to open conversation and making Narcan as common as any household product. Kahn said a lot of people have that stereotype that Narcan is only for responders and EMS but people have to think about it as a household product, just like Benadryl. While deaths from the synthetic opioid are down, tracking how widespread fentanyl has become is nearly impossible in part because of Narcan, an over-the-counter nasal spray that is increasing survival rates.

1:15 PM ET

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is sticking to encouraging low-altitude military flyovers after a fighter jet buzzed a Florida beach during a show this week, raising new scrutiny after the Pentagon has dismissed a series of safety reviews of such flights. Video spreading widely on social media shows a jet from the Navy's Blue Angels flying so low over a crowded beach in Pensacola on Wednesday that chairs and tents went flying, sand kicked up and children held their hands over their ears. The U.S. Navy said in a statement shortly afterward that it was conducting a thorough safety review. Then on Thursday morning, Trump administration officials heaped praise on the maneuver. Hegseth wrote on his personal X account that the flyovers will continue until morale improves. The Pentagon's top spokesman, Sean Parnell, wrote carry on patriots on social media alongside a photo showing a Blue Angels jet with a wingtip just feet above the heads of beachgoers. The White House tweeted a cartoon showing people on a beach taking photos of a Blue Angels jet, with the words freedom and it's okay to love America.

It is at least the third time that Hegseth and others have voiced support for military aviators performing maneuvers that have drawn public scrutiny and military investigations. In two prior cases, Hegseth's remarks led to the end of the safety investigations. In the Florida flight, acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao said the Navy had no problem with the flight and there would be no reprimands or firings. Jeff Guzzetti, an aviation safety consultant who used to investigate crashes for both the National Transportation Safety Board and the Federal Aviation Administration, said a cavalier attitude like that can only lead to accidents. Former Transportation Department Inspector General Mary Schiavo said the flyovers probably do not violate military rules because the Pentagon does not have the same restrictions on flying low over people that the FAA imposes on civilian flights, but that does not mean they are a good idea.

Demolition is underway at Murray Middle School in Martin County, marking the start of a major reconstruction effort following tornado damage caused by Hurricane Milton. The demolition phase alone carries a price tag of $3.3 million, though officials have not yet finalized the total cost for the entire project. The reconstruction will be funded through a combination of local capital improvement funds, sales tax revenue, and Public Education Capital Outlay. Students who were displaced from the affected classrooms are continuing their education in portable classrooms while construction proceeds.

The project extends beyond simply rebuilding what was lost in the storm. According to Kirstie Gernaine, coordinator of public information and community relations for the Martin County School District, Murray Middle School was already scheduled to receive construction work including a new administration area, a new media center, and a brand new art lab. When Hurricane Milton's tornadoes destroyed buildings 18, 10, and nine, the district was able to incorporate reconstruction of those structures into the planned improvements. The comprehensive project will also include a complete overhaul of the school's parking lot.

The school district has emphasized that parents can be assured their children will remain safe during the construction period. Barriers have already been installed around the work zone to prevent students from entering the construction areas and to maintain a secure learning environment for those still on campus. The safety measures are designed to allow normal school operations to continue while the extensive construction work takes place around them.

The Martin County School District hopes to complete all renovations by December 2027. The extended timeline reflects the scope of work needed to both repair tornado damage and complete the previously planned facility improvements. By combining the storm recovery effort with already scheduled upgrades, the district is working to transform the campus challenges into an opportunity for comprehensive modernization that will serve students for years to come.

Sheriff Richard Del Toro said traffic is the top concern among residents and described the crackdown as a quality of life issue. Del Toro is using extra deputies from the sheriff's school resource division to boost enforcement while the school year is on break and placing top-performing deputies in areas of concern.

Deputies Christopher Rodrigues and Cheyenne Benning are among those targeting high-problem corridors, including Midway Road, U.S. Highway 1 and Indian River Drive. Benning said she recently had someone pass her on I-95 doing 105 mph. Rodrigues said he stopped a girl doing 72 mph on Midway Road yesterday, caught someone doing 98 mph on U.S. Highway 1, and clocked a top speed of 73 mph on Indian River Drive, where the limit is 25 mph. Benning said they see a lot of excessive speeding in St. Lucie County.

In the past four weeks, deputies conducted 1,162 traffic stops, wrote 470 citations and issued 1,728 written warnings. The enforcement push is part of a broader strategy Del Toro put in place when he took office. He eliminated the agency's dedicated traffic unit and made traffic enforcement the responsibility of every deputy. Since 2025, the sheriff's office has issued more than 26,000 citations, a 155% increase.

Del Toro said he has a family that drives on these roadways and wants to make this a safer community. "This is a quality of life issue," Del Toro said. "I have a family that drives on these roadways. I care about my loved ones. I care about everyone else's loved ones. I want to make this a safer community." Residents say the problem is hard to miss. Ashley, a St. Lucie County resident, described what she sees on local roads as insane and said nobody slows down. Jessica, another local driver, echoed those concerns and said drivers are careless and cut people off.

JetBlue is ending its direct flights between New York's JFK Airport and Vero Beach Regional Airport, citing low passenger demand for the route that launched in December. The airline announced that service between the two airports will end Sept. 9, just nine months after the route began operating with much fanfare and optimistic passengers. JetBlue said in a statement that demand for this new route has not materialized as the airline had hoped. JetBlue will continue serving Vero Beach through its Boston route and has not announced whether it plans to add any replacement routes from Vero Beach or if it will adjust frequency on the remaining Boston service.

All affected customers will be notified directly by JetBlue and will receive full refunds. Passengers with upcoming reservations on the discontinued route are encouraged to contact JetBlue directly or check their email for rebooking options and refund information. The JFK-Vero Beach route was seen as a major win for the Treasure Coast when it launched in December 2025, providing direct access to one of the nation's busiest airports and potentially boosting tourism to the area. The inaugural flight brought enthusiastic passengers who called the service a game changer for connecting family and friends between New York and the Treasure Coast.

The route's cancellation comes as a disappointment to local officials and residents who had hoped the direct connection would strengthen ties between the two regions. The discontinuation of the JFK route after such a short period highlights the challenges smaller regional airports face in sustaining air service to major hubs. While JetBlue's Boston service will continue to provide some connectivity for Vero Beach travelers, the loss of direct access to JFK eliminates what many saw as a crucial link to the New York metropolitan area. The airline has not indicated whether it might reconsider the route in the future or explore other destinations from Vero Beach Regional Airport.

The route had been viewed as important infrastructure for the region when it first began operations, with hopes that convenient access to JFK would help strengthen economic and personal connections between the Treasure Coast and the New York metropolitan area. The short lifespan of the route underscores the difficulties regional airports encounter when trying to establish and maintain service to major transportation hubs.

Fri, 17 Jul 2026 15:30:25 -0400

Motorists across the Treasure Coast could encounter delays during the week of July 17-24 as construction and maintenance continue on several major roadways.

The Florida Department of Transportation's latest weekly traffic report highlights multiple full road and ramp closures expected to have the greatest impact on drivers.

Major closures to know about

Southbound I-95 exit to Bridge Road closing overnight

The southbound Interstate 95 exit ramp to County Road 708, also known as Bridge Road, at Exit 96 will be closed from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Sunday, July 19, through Thursday, July 23.

Motorists will be directed to exit at Kanner Highway, travel south on Pratt-Whitney Road and then head east on Bridge Road.

The closure is needed for roadway construction. FDOT has scheduled July 26-30 as contingency dates in case the work is delayed by weather or other unforeseen conditions.

Martin Highway ramps closing at I-95

Two ramps serving Martin Highway at I-95 will close overnight from Sunday, July 19, through Thursday, July 23.

The northbound I-95 exit ramp to Martin Highway at Exit 110 will be closed from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Drivers will continue north to Becker Road at Exit 114, travel west on Becker Road and use southbound I-95 to return to the Martin Highway exit.

The northbound I-95 entrance ramp from Martin Highway will also be closed during the same hours. Motorists will be detoured east on Martin Highway, north on Newfield Parkway and west on Becker Road to reach the northbound I-95 entrance ramp.

FDOT said only one ramp closure will occur at a time.

Orange Avenue exit closing in Fort Pierce

The northbound I-95 exit ramp to westbound State Road 68, or Orange Avenue, will be closed from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Sunday, July 19, through Thursday, July 23.

Drivers will be detoured to the eastbound Orange Avenue exit, north on Jenkins Road and west on Loop Road before returning to westbound Orange Avenue.

The work includes paving and lighting installation. Contingency dates are scheduled for July 26-30.

Emergency repairs could cause major delays on I-95

Southbound I-95 between Kanner Highway and Bridge Road will have two lanes closed beginning at 4 p.m. Monday, July 20, and Tuesday, July 21. The lanes are scheduled to reopen by 5:30 a.m. each morning.

FDOT said the closures are needed to complete an emergency repair near mile marker 98. Drivers are encouraged to consider alternate routes because the early closure time could affect the evening commute.

Port St. Lucie neighborhood roads may close for several days

Extended closures are planned along several roads near the SW Port St. Lucie Boulevard widening project. Closures may begin at 7 a.m. Monday, July 20, and continue until 5 p.m. Friday, July 24, on portions of SW Jaslo Avenue, SW Jacoby Avenue, SW McCullough Avenue, SW McCall Road and SW McHord Avenue.

The roads will close intermittently for drainage and utility installation. Adjacent roads will not be closed at the same time, and access to homes and businesses will be maintained throughout the work.

Detours will use nearby roads including SW Paar Drive, SW McCain Street, SW McIntyre Street, SW Kabane Street and SW McDevitt Avenue. FDOT is also providing advance notice that SW Paar Drive between SW McIntosh Street and SW Port St. Lucie Boulevard may close from July 27-31.

Martin County traffic impacts

Northbound and southbound I-95 between Bridge Road and the Martin-St. Lucie county line will continue to have two lanes closed overnight for milling, resurfacing and signage work.

U.S. 1 between Fischer Street and Decker Avenue may have two lanes closed intermittently from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Sunday through Friday for sign installation.

A1A between Shore Village Terrace and the Jensen Beach Causeway may be reduced to one lane at times Monday through Friday for pavement widening and drainage work. Flaggers will maintain two-way traffic through the work zone.

Martin Highway between Stuart West Boulevard and Citrus Boulevard may have intermittent overnight lane closures Tuesday through Thursday for milling and resurfacing.

In Stuart, two westbound lanes on Monterey Road between Ocean Boulevard and Monterey Commons Boulevard may close from 9 p.m. to 6 a.m. Tuesday through Thursday.

St. Lucie County traffic impacts

Port St. Lucie Boulevard between Seafury Lane and Lennard Road may have daytime lane closures Monday through Friday. Two lanes may close overnight from Sunday through Friday for drainage work, milling and repaving.

U.S. 1 between Jennings Road and Golden Oak Trail may also have lane restrictions during daytime and overnight hours.

SW Port St. Lucie Boulevard between Jordin Avenue and Alcantarra Boulevard will have intermittent lane closures from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Monday through Friday.

Up to two lanes may close overnight on I-95 between Glades Cut-Off Road and the area just south of Okeechobee Road for milling and repaving.

Midway Road between Florida's Turnpike and Selvitz Road will continue to have intermittent weekday lane closures as part of the widening project.

North Causeway marine restrictions continue

Boaters traveling near the North Causeway Bridge in Fort Pierce could encounter partial or full channel closures as crews continue removing sections of the former drawbridge.

Partial channel restrictions may occur during daylight hours on weekdays through late 2026. At least 45 feet of the channel is expected to remain open during partial closures.

Intermittent full channel closures lasting up to six hours may occur through July for the removal of steel bascule bridge spans. Vessels requiring full channel access must provide the bridge tender with 120 minutes of advance notice.

The new North Causeway Bridge remains open to vehicle traffic, although nearby roads including U.S. 1, Old Dixie Highway, Juanita Avenue and Harbortown Drive may have lane restrictions.

Indian River County traffic impacts

One lane may remain closed on the 17th Street bridges between Indian River Boulevard and Indian Bay Drive as bridge rehabilitation continues.

I-95 near the new Oslo Road interchange may have one or two lanes closed overnight Sunday through Thursday for bridge work, milling and resurfacing.

On 90th Avenue between Sebastian Boulevard and 87th Street, one lane may close overnight Sunday through Friday for roadway construction.

Drivers near Sebastian Inlet should expect intermittent lane and shoulder closures on A1A from July 20-31. Flaggers will direct traffic through the work zone.

Sections of the north and south parking lots at Sebastian Inlet State Park remain closed during construction of the replacement bridge, although the park, boat ramp and portions of both parking areas remain open.

Additional I-95 closures coming the following week

FDOT is also providing advance notice of several closures scheduled to begin Sunday, July 26. All four I-95 ramps at Midway Road are expected to close separately overnight through July 31 for paving. Only one ramp will close at a time.

Detours will use St. Lucie West Boulevard, U.S. 1 and nearby interstate exits. Additional ramp closures are also planned later at Martin Highway and Kanner Highway.

Construction schedules may change because of weather or other unforeseen conditions. Drivers should allow additional travel time, follow posted detours and use caution around workers and equipment.

Real-time interstate traffic conditions are available through Florida 511. Updated lane closure information is available through FDOT District Four.
