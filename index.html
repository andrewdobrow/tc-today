A Palm Beach County man led Martin County deputies on a high-speed chase through Indiantown after driving more than 100 mph on State Road 710, one of the area's most dangerous roads. Deputies spotted 33-year-old Markell Tillman driving aggressively at speeds exceeding 100 mph, forcing other vehicles off the roadway. When deputies attempted to stop him, Tillman fled.

Tillman weaved through traffic in an attempt to lose deputies before turning onto SW Martin Highway toward Palm City, where he continued to drive recklessly. Deputies spotted Tillman again traveling eastbound on Martin Highway near Allapattah Road. Deputies used stop sticks to disable his vehicle near I-95.

Tillman then bailed out of the vehicle and attempted to flee on foot before deputies caught up with him and took him into custody. He was arrested on multiple charges including fleeing and eluding, aggravated fleeing with injury or damage, operating a vehicle exceeding the speed limit by 50 or more mph, driving while license suspended or revoked, obstruction without violence, and operating a vehicle at 100 or more mph.

Tillman also had an out-of-county warrant at the time of his arrest. He was booked into the Martin County Jail.

Yesterday, 2:32 PM ET

The Florida Department of Health and Safe Kids Treasure Coast are providing a limited supply of free pool alarms to qualifying residents in Martin County. The initiative aims to add an extra layer of safety for households with residential pools.

Safe Kids Treasure Coast is asking residents to fill out a survey to determine eligibility for the program. The coalition emphasized that a pool alarm is one layer of protection and should always be used alongside close adult supervision, pool barriers and water safety education to help prevent drowning. The alarms are being offered while supplies last, making it important for interested residents to apply promptly.

Residents should be aware that a 9-volt battery is required to operate the alarm but is not included with the free device. This means households receiving an alarm will need to purchase a battery separately before the alarm can be activated and provide protection. The coalition stresses that while pool alarms serve as an important safety tool, they should not replace other critical drowning prevention measures such as constant supervision and proper barriers around pool areas.

For more information about the free pool alarm program or to inquire about eligibility requirements, residents can call 772-462-3501. The partnership between the Florida Department of Health and Safe Kids Treasure Coast reflects ongoing efforts to reduce drowning incidents in the community through increased access to safety equipment and education about water safety practices.

Yesterday, 5:39 PM ET

Villa Del Sol residents in St. Lucie County are returning home after nearly two years of displacement from their condos. Residents were notified last month they could return after an inspection in August 2024 found structural damage in buildings 3, 4, and 5, which were deemed unsafe. WPTV received emails sent to residents from the Board of Directors telling them they could return. WPTV also obtained a letter from engineers sent to St. Lucie County's Building Department requesting they lift occupancy restrictions following their special inspection.

Katie Johnson was the first to share with WPTV that residents had been told they could come back. She said she almost didn't believe it and that it was like too good to be true. Johnson described the work still underway at the property, noting that crews are still moving dirt back under the buildings. She reflected on the effort required to bring residents home, saying it's hard work. Johnson also said she is thinking of neighbors who cannot return and feels bad that some were hit harder than others with the situation.

Robert Segedin took WPTV inside his family's home and said he was excited to return but was thinking about those who cannot. He said their hearts were broken and they wanted to spend the rest of their lives at Villa Del Sol. Eric Johnson is among those not coming back. He was one of the first residents WPTV met at Villa Del Sol and is now living in Georgia. He said it's been a terrible two years and the financial toll has been severe.

Eric Johnson said his family had to drain all their savings accounts and 401ks just trying to make ends meet while this was going on. He is filing for bankruptcy and filing his condo for foreclosure. He said it's been a real struggle and that while he'd like to say they are finally starting to climb out of it, it still feels like they're kind of still falling and waiting to start climbing back up. The displaced residents have faced significant financial hardship during their time away from their homes, though some are now able to move back into the property.

Three young gymnasts from Flip City Gym and Cheer secured a top 15 finish at AAU Nationals in Knoxville, Tennessee last month and qualified for the Junior Olympics in Iowa next month. Skylar Rose-Daris, Victoria Supan, and Charli Rine competed as a unit on the national stage after finding their way to gymnastics through different paths.

Head Coach Candice Lazuka described the season as strong, with the team competing locally and traveling before advancing to states and regionals. At AAU Nationals in Knoxville, the gymnasts came close to a perfect score. Lazuka said the team scored a 9.9, noting that a perfect 10 is hard to achieve.

Registration is now open for Port St. Lucie City University, a free nine-session program designed to help residents better understand how their local government operates.

Classes begin Wednesday, Aug. 12, and will be held from 5:30 to 8:30 p.m. in the Council Chambers at Port St. Lucie City Hall, located at 121 SW Port St. Lucie Blvd.

Participants will learn about City departments, municipal services and the decision-making processes that shape the community. The program also gives residents opportunities to meet City staff, ask questions and gain a closer look at the work involved in operating one of Florida's largest cities.

Residents who complete the course will receive a certificate of completion and may also choose to become a City Ambassador, helping share information about City programs and services throughout the community.

City University is intended to encourage civic engagement while strengthening connections between residents and local government. Since the program began in 2016, more than 600 residents have participated.

Enrollment is limited to 160 participants.

Residents who are unable to attend the in-person classes can also access City U on Demand, which offers a more flexible way to learn about Port St. Lucie government and municipal operations.

Vero Beach parents sue city over infant son's remains allegedly disturbed and reburied without consent

2026-07-16

Adam Chrzan and Tanya Crovo have filed an amended lawsuit against the City of Vero Beach alleging their infant son's remains were disturbed during maintenance work at Crestlawn Cemetery and reburied without their knowledge or consent. The couple's son, identified in court documents only as A.C., died in August 2007 at 37 days old and was buried at the city-owned cemetery. The parents say they did not learn about what allegedly happened until April 2025, nearly 18 years after their son's burial.

According to the complaint, sometime between the 2007 burial and April 2025, heavy equipment being used for maintenance or irrigation work near the burial site breached the grave, damaging or destroying the casket, its contents, and the child's remains. The lawsuit alleges that someone acting in an official capacity for the City of Vero Beach then directed cemetery workers to collect the remains, place them into a new casket, and reinter the child without notifying the parents or obtaining their consent. The amended complaint accuses the city and cemetery of tortious interference with a burial plot and human remains, negligence, negligent infliction of emotional distress, intentional misrepresentation, and fraudulent concealment.

This amended complaint comes after a judge dismissed the family's original lawsuit. The dismissal was not a finding that the allegations were false but rather a ruling that the legal claims as originally pleaded could not proceed against the city under Florida law. The parents have now refiled with different legal arguments in an attempt to overcome the previous dismissal. A judge has not yet ruled on the amended complaint. The City of Vero Beach has declined to comment on the amended complaint.

In court filings responding to the original lawsuit, the city disputed the allegation that irrigation work could have caused the damage described by the parents. The city submitted a survey stating there are no irrigation lines adjacent to the burial plot of A.C. The case highlights the legal protections cities have under Florida law and the challenges families face when seeking accountability for alleged mishandling of cemetery operations. The amended complaint represents the parents' continued effort to hold the city responsible for what they describe as the unauthorized disturbance and reburial of their infant son's remains.
