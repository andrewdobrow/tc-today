A 40-year-old man died Sunday morning following a head-on collision on State Road 710 in Martin County, the Florida Highway Patrol reported. The crash took place around 2 a.m. near the intersection of State Road 710, commonly called Beeline Highway, and Southwest Silver Fox Lane.

According to FHP investigators, a 2015 Kia Optima traveling northbound on SR-710 drifted across the center line into the southbound lane. The vehicle then struck a 2021 Toyota Tundra head-on, with the left front of the Kia colliding with the left front of the truck. The impact caused the Kia to ignite immediately. The sedan came to rest on the grass shoulder facing north after the crash, while the Tundra remained in the southbound lane, positioned facing southeast.

The Kia driver, the 40-year-old man, was pronounced dead at the scene. The truck's driver, a 41-year-old Indiantown resident, was airlifted by Lifestar helicopter to a hospital for treatment of injuries sustained in the collision. The identities of both drivers have not been released by authorities.

The Florida Highway Patrol continues to investigate the circumstances surrounding the crash. The agency has not disclosed details about what may have caused the Kia to cross into the opposing lane.

Yesterday, 6:43 PM ET

Families planning Fourth of July celebrations will notice higher prices at the grocery store. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, a holiday meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, representing a 4% increase from the previous year. Inflation is the primary driver behind these rising costs, with several key cookout staples experiencing significant price jumps.

The survey reveals which items have become noticeably more expensive. Ground beef prices have increased by 5.5%, while hamburger buns have risen 7.7%. Strawberries have seen the steepest increase at 12.4%, largely due to damage from a Florida frost that affected the harvest. Ice cream prices are also up 5.3%. These items represent some of the most popular choices for Fourth of July menus across the country.

Not all grocery prices have climbed, however. Potato prices have actually decreased this year, offering savings for those planning to serve potato chips or potato salad alongside their grilled items. Corn prices have been more volatile, but locally grown corn from the Glades can provide better pricing options for shoppers. These modest savings can help offset some of the increases seen in other categories.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends several strategies to manage cookout expenses. She emphasizes the importance of shopping strategically by comparing prices across different stores to find the best deals for items on your shopping list. Woroch also highlights that reducing food waste is critical for families looking to keep costs down. The best way to minimize waste begins with careful meal planning before heading to the grocery store. By taking time to plan what you'll actually need and use, families can stretch their cookout budgets further while still enjoying a festive holiday celebration.

Mr. Crain, a tile worker by trade, left his mark throughout the facility with craftsmanship visible from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. 11:20 AM ET

Several X accounts were shut down after prominent members of Governor Ron DeSantis' communications team alleged that Layla Collins, wife of Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins, had been operating them anonymously. Christina Pushaw, a Communications Specialist in the Governor's Office, and Jeremy Redfern, Deputy Chief of Staff to Attorney General James Uthmeier, both conducted online research connecting the accounts to the Second Lady. The two accounts in question were @FSUdude01670 and @bobnamy1.

The allegations center on evidence linking the accounts to Layla Collins' contact information. Robert Salvador, CEO of Promota, shared research showing that @FSUdude01670 appeared connected to a phone number and email similar to ones known to be associated with Layla Collins. Florida Politics obtained screenshots verifying the connection through two-factor authentication records. The screenshots showed that access attempts to the account triggered messages sent to an email address with most letters hidden and to a phone number displaying only the final digits. Redfern stated plainly that the FSUDude account was directly tied to her personal cell phone number.

Pushaw analyzed the content of these accounts to strengthen the case against Collins. She noted that the account's posts fell into two distinct categories: promoting Jay Collins and attacking individuals who refused to endorse him, including the Governor himself. "A regular supporter of Jay Collins is probably not going to be talking trash about the governor like that," Pushaw posted. She also questioned whether someone serving on an important state board, particularly one appointed by DeSantis, would have time to consistently maintain and post from burner accounts. Pushaw pointed out that Collins was appointed by the same governor whose administration she was now criticizing.

Both accounts were deactivated shortly after Pushaw and Redfern publicly raised their concerns. Florida Politics requested comment from both Jay and Layla Collins but had not yet received a response at the time of reporting. Both Pushaw and Redfern have previous experience in DeSantis' communications office, having previously served as Press Secretary. Redfern currently works for Attorney General Uthmeier, who has endorsed U.S. Representative Byron Donalds for Governor. DeSantis appointed Jay Collins as Lieutenant Governor last year. The timing of the account deactivations after the public accusations suggests the allegations may have prompted action from Collins or her representatives.

Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce historic estate into arts destination

The Peacock House Art Foundation is reshaping Fort Pierce's cultural landscape by operating out of the historic Crain Estate, a property that now serves as both a museum and active arts center. Yesterday, 2:48 PM ET

The Peacock House Art Foundation is reshaping Fort Pierce's cultural landscape by operating out of the historic Crain Estate, a property that now serves as both a museum and active arts center. The nonprofit preserves the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, the original owners who filled the estate with their travels and personal touches. Jaxson Crain was a tile worker whose skill is evident throughout the property, with his tilework visible from the ground to the walls to the ceiling. The foundation has committed to maintaining the estate's original character rather than commercializing it, keeping approximately 90 percent of the home exactly as it was during the Crains' time there. Visitors can explore the lush gardens and distinctive architecture through guided tours offered every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

The couple's extensive travels shaped the estate's unique character and collections. They journeyed around the world nine times, acquiring one-of-a-kind items for their Florida home, including a gazebo shipped directly from India that features small peacock details. The property also showcases the smoking and bourbon room, featuring rich paneling, masculine bathroom colors, and ornate stained glass that opens onto a patio bar. According to art director Krystal Masters, the Crains' worldly acquisitions and personal style created a distinctive home that continues to captivate visitors today.

The property gets its name from the peacocks that have inhabited the grounds for decades. The Crains introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, initially bringing seven birds to the estate. From that original flock, the population has grown to over 100 peacocks. Among them is Bob, a celebrated resident believed to be 60 years old and recognizable by his distinctive limp. Staff hand-feed Bob daily using his own bowls, and the peacocks have become memorable highlights of tours, especially for young visitors who get to feed the birds directly. The Crains' animal collection once extended further, as local lore suggests that Jaxson Crain traveled to South America and returned home with a baby monkey carried in his pocket.

Today, the foundation operates an artist-in-residency program that draws artists from around the world, providing them with simple yet elegant accommodations on the property. The art lab offers accessible classes for both children and adults of all skill levels, with Masters leading one-on-one instruction designed for complete beginners. The lab features diverse mediums including sculpting and watercolor painting. Masters emphasizes that art serves as a therapeutic outlet, allowing students to disconnect from their phones and create something meaningful. She finds fulfillment in watching students leave feeling accomplished and fulfilled, understanding that art provides emotional and creative benefits beyond technical skill development.
