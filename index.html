Riviera Beach named only Florida city to win 2026 All-America City Award

Riviera Beach has been selected as one of only 10 communities nationwide to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the sole Florida city honored this year. The award was presented during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where city leaders showcased their efforts to strengthen community involvement and collaboration. The...

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has been selected as one of only 10 communities nationwide to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the sole Florida city honored this year. The award was presented during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where city leaders showcased their efforts to strengthen community involvement and collaboration. The recognition celebrates communities that unite residents, government, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and drive meaningful change.

Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the recognition, describing Riviera Beach as "one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He emphasized that the award reflects a sustained commitment to revitalize the city and enhance residents' quality of life. Lawson noted that community members are witnessing the transformation and reimagining of Riviera Beach that leaders have pursued for years. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents directly for the honor, stating that the award belongs to the entire community because residents show up for one another through events, projects and challenges. She added that the designation reinforces the city's positive momentum and brings back community pride.

The city's application highlighted three key civic engagement programs. The Riviera Beach Youth Council enables high school students to participate in civic leadership and advocacy. The Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education program, boasts a 98 percent completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program provides mini-grants to longtime homeowners for necessary repairs. City leaders emphasized that these initiatives represent a broader commitment to treating civic engagement as an ongoing partnership between government, residents, faith groups, schools and community organizations. Miller-Anderson noted that the city is implementing innovative programs to ensure residents feel they are part of shaping their community's future.

The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, is considered one of the nation's most respected civic honors. Being selected positions Riviera Beach for future opportunities and continued growth. City officials said the designation sends a message to the nation that Riviera Beach is a place where collaboration produces results and residents remain deeply engaged in determining their community's direction. The other nine cities receiving the award are Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

A dispute over a missing fence at a Juno Beach public beach access point has created tension between condo residents and summer beachgoers, with a potential fix that could cost the town thousands of dollars. Residents at the Colony Condos say people using the JB-7 public walkway have been cutting through their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. Without the barrier, only bushes now separate the public access path from private land, leaving residents frustrated by what they describe as repeated trespassing incidents.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, said the problem intensifies during summer months when beachgoers arrive to use the public access point. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Hill explained that residents previously confronted trespassers directly but have since shifted to calling police after encountering hostile responses from beachgoers. She said the fence once made the boundary between public and private property clearly visible.

The issue reached the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where officials acknowledged the town is not legally obligated to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring lower-cost alternatives rather than a full replacement. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. Hill indicated that residents hope the town will reinstall the original fence to restore their landscaping and private parking areas. The council agreed to address the fence question during upcoming budget discussions scheduled for July 15.

Not all beachgoers perceive the situation as problematic. Michael House, who walks to JB-7 from a public parking lot blocks away, said he has not witnessed the issues residents describe. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that." His perspective highlights the divide in how different users view the public beach access point and whether a fence replacement is truly necessary to address the concerns raised by condo residents.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football program is working hard during the summer months with ambitious goals for the 2026 fall season. Last year, the team finished with a 10-2 record and won the district championship, but a second-round playoff loss has motivated them to aim higher. The players and coaching staff believe a state title is within reach if they can extend their playoff run beyond where they finished last season.

Head coach Jimmy Robertson highlighted the strength of the senior class as a major advantage heading into the fall. Most of the seniors have been with the program for four years, with many coming up through the middle school system. This continuity means the senior leaders already understand the program's culture and have experienced success throughout their time with the Scots. Robertson expressed confidence that the senior class could lead the program to its best season yet.

The team's approach to improvement centers on raising the level of competition. Senior Jack Williams explained that the program intentionally scheduled tougher opponents this fall to push themselves and attract attention to the school. Rather than seeking easy wins, the Scots want to test themselves against strong teams. Senior Jack Whelche emphasized that true success cannot be measured by an undefeated regular season alone. He said the priority must be advancing further in the playoffs, as winning in the regular season means little if the team continues to exit in the second round. The players have set a clear goal of progressing deeper into postseason play than they did last year.

The summer training period has become a source of motivation for the roster. Senior Edmyer St. Jean described the daily workouts as something meaningful to work toward, knowing that the effort invested now will translate to success when the season begins. The team is using the challenging summer schedule as fuel for their ambitious state title aspirations. With veteran leadership, a demanding schedule planned for the fall, and a commitment to postseason success, the Scots are preparing themselves for what they hope will be a breakthrough season in 2026.

Secretary of State seeks to keep Republican candidate off ballot after incomplete qualifying papers

Yesterday, 5:56 PM ET

Paula Stark submitted her qualifying materials just 30 minutes before the noon deadline on June 12, and now Secretary of State Cord Byrd is asking Leon Circuit Court to keep the St. Cloud Republican off the ballot. Five state employees provided sworn written declarations stating that Stark's treasurer and partner, Joel Davis, never submitted the required Form 6 and did not receive it back when he turned in the rest of the paperwork during the final moments of the qualifying window. More than three weeks after the deadline, Stark still has not provided Form 6, according to a court disclosure by Election Records Division Bureau Chief Erica Ikay Reeve.

Byrd challenged Stark's claims about a paperwork mix-up in his court response, suggesting she could have prevented the problem with more care. "Had Candidate Stark or Treasurer Davis exercised the slightest degree of care or prudence in the matter by questioning why the Form 6 was allegedly handed back, the mistake would have been discovered while Treasurer Davis was at the Division," Byrd wrote. He also noted that waiting until the last moment to file created unnecessary risk. Form 6 is a public disclosure form for financial interests. Reeve noted that Stark was one of 12 candidates who failed to properly submit Form 6, but half of those candidates corrected the problem and still qualified for the ballot.

Beyond the missing Form 6, Reeve documented a pattern of campaign finance problems. Stark's treasurer filed a campaign finance report more than one month late, reported excessive contributions, and has an incorrect address on file. In the 2022 and 2024 election cycles, audits found inconsistent transfers between accounts and balance problems. The state revoked Stark's Friends of Paula political committee in November over 16,000 dollars in unpaid fines from missed campaign finance deadlines, with a remaining fine referred to debt collection for nonpayment.

The timing of this decision matters significantly. Byrd noted that ballots must be mailed to overseas military and their families by July 4, with vote-by-mail ballots due out between July 9 and 16. If Stark is removed from the ballot, the House District 47 primary would become an open contest between Democrats Jorge Figueroa and Anthony Nieves. This would represent a major advantage for Florida Democrats, as the traditionally blue district representing parts of Osceola and Orange counties saw no other Republican candidates file to run. Byrd argued that reversing course at this stage would be costly and could create confusion in election results.

Misty Dawn White, 46, died in a house fire at her Hobe Sound home on Monday. Authorities discovered her body after flames consumed the residence. The sheriff's office has indicated that an electrical problem may have sparked the blaze, though the investigation into the cause remains ongoing.

White's fiancé, Mark, contacted her longtime friend Nina Fenn to inform her of the tragedy. Fenn, who had known White for eight years, learned of the death through that phone call. The community has rallied to support Mark during this difficult time. Friends organized a fundraiser to help him rebuild after the fire destroyed numerous belongings that he and White shared. Fenn described what she witnessed when she returned to the scene as "completely a state of devastation."

Fenn spoke about the person White was and how she wanted her to be remembered. "Misty was a Christian woman. She loved gardening and plants. She plays the piano," Fenn said. "She was always the first to lend a hand." Fenn also noted that White had a deep love for children and people in general. The loss carries additional weight for Mark, who had previously experienced profound tragedy. According to Fenn, Mark lost his only child, Jacob, in a tragic incident. "Mark lost his one and only child, Jacob, and lost him tragically," Fenn said. "So, I just am trying whatever I can to support him."

The timing of White's death adds another layer of heartbreak for those who knew her. White's birthday was approaching on July 4, meaning she passed away just days before turning a year older. "Misty's birthday is the Fourth of July. So, she passed just a few days before her birthday," Fenn said. Despite the tragedy, Fenn emphasized that White's memory will continue to live on. "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts," Fenn said.

Yesterday, 6:16 PM ET

The warehouse retailer has announced plans for a Port St. Lucie location, marking the first Costco on the Treasure Coast. The store would serve St. Lucie County and surrounding areas. Port St. Lucie residents currently travel to Palm Beach County or other locations to shop at Costco. The new store represents a major retail addition for the city and the broader Treasure Coast region.

This expansion comes as Port St. Lucie continues significant infrastructure improvements to accommodate growth. The city is working on major road projects designed to handle both current congestion and future development. A $95 million Midway Road Widening Project is underway to expand the roadway and improve accessibility throughout the area. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the city recognizes the need to plan ahead. "This is a way for us to try to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years," Samples said.

The Midway Road project is being completed in phases. Currently, crews are working on the first phase, which widens the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, extending from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027. Beyond the road widening, the Florida Department of Transportation is improving the Turnpike bridge and designing a partial interchange to address what Samples described as "a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie." The partial interchange is still in the design phase. In addition to roadway improvements, the project will add bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks. Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to donate 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to support the interchange construction.

While the ongoing construction has been noticeable to residents, many recognize its value. Resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged the inconvenience but supported the work. "They're still doing construction over there," she said. "It was like you couldn't get away from it." However, Scheumeister added, "We definitely needed it to be expanded. It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." The combination of the Costco development and these infrastructure improvements positions Port St. Lucie for continued growth while addressing the transportation challenges that rapid expansion creates.
