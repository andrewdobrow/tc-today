Florida property tax fight intensifies as DeSantis signs local government restrictions

Governor Ron DeSantis has signed new laws that restrict how cities, counties and special districts can increase property tax revenue. 6:02 PM ET

Governor Ron DeSantis has signed new laws that restrict how cities, counties and special districts can increase property tax revenue. The legislation ties local governments more closely to the rollback rate, which is designed to generate roughly the same tax revenue as the previous year. The measures also require larger voter approval to exceed this rate. Florida Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia defended the restrictions, stating that "government at all levels needs to start thinking about spending cuts." Supporters frame the changes as taxpayer protection, arguing that local governments have allowed budgets to expand too rapidly as property values have increased.

The new restrictions represent a significant challenge for local governments across Florida, including cities in Martin, St. Lucie and Indian River counties on the Treasure Coast. Local officials are concerned that when combined with a proposed constitutional amendment, these restrictions could create substantial budget shortfalls for cities and counties. Amendment 3, scheduled for the November ballot, would expand homestead exemptions on non-school property taxes. The exemptions would increase to $150,000 in 2027 and then to $250,000 in 2028. Supporters of the amendment claim it would provide meaningful financial relief to homeowners. Critics counter that the tax savings for some property owners could result in reduced services, higher fees, or taxes shifting to other areas.

The property tax debate has intensified as external campaigns emerge to oppose the amendment. A new outside campaign is actively urging voters to reject Amendment 3, signaling that this issue will be among Florida's most closely watched ballot battles this fall. The combination of legislative restrictions on revenue increases and the proposed constitutional amendment expansion of homestead exemptions has created overlapping challenges for local budget planning.

The property tax fight comes amid a volatile week in Florida politics. The state also closed the controversial Everglades immigration detention facility known as "Alligator Alcatraz," which had become one of the most debated projects of DeSantis' administration. The facility, which served as emergency immigration detention capacity, is now empty. However, the closure does not resolve ongoing disputes about the project's cost, detainee treatment, hurricane safety and environmental impact. Environmental groups have questioned whether the state properly assessed effects on the Everglades and Big Cypress region. DeSantis stated he expects the federal government to reimburse Florida for the project costs, which have reached hundreds of millions of dollars.

A 49-year-old Port St. Lucie resident and a 15-year-old were arrested Wednesday evening following an incident that began as an Orbeez toy gun prank near SW Morelia Lane and SW Acapulco Terrace. Gregory Allen Davis and his fiancée called 911 after what they believed to be shots from a BB or pellet gun struck their vehicle while they were driving. The couple then began following the vehicle suspected of firing at them as Port St. Lucie police deployed their first responder drone to the scene. While his fiancée remained on the phone with the dispatcher, Davis exited his vehicle and approached the teens, shouting orders for them to exit their vehicle, actions captured on witness video. Davis, who was armed with a 9mm handgun, then ordered the teens to the ground at gunpoint. Officers arrived moments later.

Upon arrival, officers discovered that the teens had been using an Orbeez toy gun in what was meant to be a harmless game. The three teenagers told police they believed the vehicle they targeted belonged to a friend. According to the Port St. Lucie Police Department, investigators reviewed the circumstances and determined that Davis "had multiple opportunities to safely disengage while officers were actively responding" to the scene.

Davis faced charges of aggravated assault with a deadly weapon without intent to kill and false imprisonment of a child to commit aggravated abuse. The 15-year-old was charged with shooting or throwing a missile at or into an occupied vehicle. Both witness video and police body camera footage documented portions of the confrontation.

The Port St. Lucie Police Department used the incident to issue a broader warning to the community about the dangers of toy firearm pranks. "This incident serves as an important reminder: Pranks involving Orbeez guns, airsoft guns, BB guns, or other toy firearms are not harmless," the department stated. "They can create fear, provoke dangerous reactions, and result in criminal charges." The warning underscores how situations involving imitation firearms can quickly escalate and lead to serious legal consequences for all parties involved, regardless of the original intent behind the prank.

Anne Bendel of West Boca Raton has encountered repeated obstacles while trying to claim a state grant to help cover storm-fortification costs for her home. She first reached out to WPTV in May when her My Safe Florida Home application stalled due to missing paperwork from her insurance company. After that issue was resolved, program representatives told her in May that her application was complete. However, just one week later, the program requested additional documentation, specifically a permit of completion for a large impact window. Bendel provided the requested permit, but staff then raised concerns about the dates listed on the document.

Bendel spent $8,000 of her own money on the storm-fortification work. The My Safe Florida Home program offers grants up to $10,000 to help homeowners like her strengthen their homes against storms and secure insurance discounts. Despite submitting all required permits, insurance documentation, and proof of completed work multiple times, Bendel says new requirements emerge each time the program marks her application as complete. The frustration has been compounded by the fact that she is managing the entire process using only her phone, without access to a computer or assistance from others. "It's not easy for me because I don't have a computer, I'm doing this on my phone, and I have nobody to help me. It's gut-wrenching," she said.

Bendel expressed her concerns about the program's fairness and the moving target of requirements. "I did the work, I had it completed, I got the permits, I had the insurance they asked for. Everything is done, but it seems like when everything is done they find some other little task to send you," she said. She describes herself as persistent and thorough, but the application process has severely tested her patience. The repeated requests for clarification or additional documents have shaken her confidence in the program's promises to support homeowners.

WPTV contacted the Florida Department of Financial Services in Tallahassee to inquire about Bendel's case and the current status of her grant application. As of the time of reporting, her application remains under review with no timeline provided for when a decision might be made. The My Safe Florida Home program has successfully assisted thousands of homeowners across Florida with storm-hardening improvements, but Bendel's experience highlights potential frustrations some applicants may encounter during the grant approval process.

One kilogram brick tested at 16 percent fentanyl and also contained carfentanil, which is multiple times more potent than fentanyl. Miles Aley, special agent in charge of the DEA's Miami Division, said the cartels obtained chemicals and chemical expertise through the dark web. He noted that fentanyl and carfentanil have become a top agency priority, pointing out that while overdose deaths dropped from 110,000 to 70,000, that figure remains far too high. Aley warned that new drugs are appearing that are resistant to Narcan, the over-the-counter nasal spray that reverses opioid overdoses. Next month, the DEA will host its inaugural Fentanyl Free America Summit in Orlando to raise public awareness about the dangers of the latest fentanyl crisis.

Gov. DeSantis signs law protecting parents from wrongful child removals when rare conditions mimic abuse

Florida will delay child removals when parents report diagnoses that cause symptoms resembling abuse, including Ehlers-Danlos syndrome and osteogenesis imperfecta.

4:09 PM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed HB 47, which revises Florida's protective investigation procedures when a child has certain medical diagnoses with symptoms that can resemble physical abuse but are caused by genetic or nutritional conditions. The law applies to children diagnosed with Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, rickets, osteogenesis imperfecta, and vitamin D deficiency, which cause fragile bones, easy bruising, and other injuries that can be mistaken for abuse. The measure, championed by Broward County Democratic Sen. Barbara Sharief, Weston Democratic Rep. Robin Bartleman and Shalimar Republican Rep. Patt Maney, cleared both chambers of the Legislature unanimously in March.

Under the bill, the Department of Children and Families can delay immediately referring certain criminal allegations to law enforcement if a parent reports one of those diagnoses or requests a qualifying medical exam. The law, effective July 1, requires child protection teams to consult with qualified physicians or advanced practice registered nurses and mandates that DCF request relevant medical records. Parents have the right to seek a second medical opinion within a set time frame, and healthcare providers must furnish requested records to DCF within 14 days. The legislation was inspired by the case of Michael and Tasha Patterson, who lost custody of their 8-week-old twin sons in 2022 due to a previously undiagnosed case of Ehlers-Danlos. During committee hearings, Diana Sullivan and Sarah Mischler detailed how DCF removed their children before rare medical conditions were properly diagnosed, with Mischler spending more than $100,000 fighting for 11 months to keep her 7-month-old son.

Former Haitian National Team members, including former captain Jean-Marc Alexandre, hosted a clinic for top Haitian youth players in Boca Raton from June 25-28.

10:58 AM ET

L'Union Fait La Force is a premier soccer development program run by former Haitian National Team members, including former team captain Jean-Marc Alexandre. Alexandre is an Atlantic High School and Lynn University graduate who played on Real Salt Lake's 2009 MLS Cup-winning team. The program hosted a clinic from June 25-28 in Boca Raton that brought together young Haitian soccer players including Nohe Chevry and Luis Etienne for high-level training. Alexandre said the clinic's purpose is to give young players a pathway to the youth national team, especially those not living in Haiti.

Chevry moved to Palm Beach County from Haiti in 2022 with his family due to safety concerns in the country. He said joining L'Union Fait La Force Training Center gave him access to coaches with pro contracts and national team experience. Etienne said the opportunity provides exposure for players hoping to earn spots on the national team. The clinic took place days after a Wednesday night match between Haiti and Morocco. Chevry said his goal is to play in the World Cup and potentially win it.

Veteran Says He Got Help at West Palm Beach's Hanley Center After Years of Drug Addiction

Chris Claflin, a Navy pilot who flew jets in combat and graduated from Harvard Business School, says the Hanley Center in West Palm Beach helped him overcome an eight-year drug addiction.

5:00 AM ET

Chris Claflin is a Harvard Business School graduate and former Navy pilot who flew jets in combat. After years of professional success, his life unraveled when he became addicted to drugs for eight years. Claflin traced the onset of his addiction, in part, to unresolved trauma from his childhood. He is now more than a year sober and working to become a certified counselor after receiving treatment at the Hanley Center in West Palm Beach.

Claflin credits the care he received at the Hanley Center with changing his life for the better. He shared a message for others struggling with addiction, particularly veterans: "You can't do this on your own. It's not something you can figure out. Once you get so far down the rabbit hole, you can't pull yourself out on your own." The Hanley Center serves as a resource for supporting the mental health and well-being of service members and others facing similar challenges.

Lucky Shuck in Jupiter featured in Where's Walter visit

The Lucky Shuck restaurant in Jupiter was revealed as the location for a Where's Walter segment, highlighting the waterfront dining spot founded by a professional athlete.

12:25 PM ET

The Lucky Shuck in Jupiter was featured in a Where's Walter visit on Friday. The restaurant, accessible by both car and boat, was founded by a professional athlete and has become one of Jupiter's dining destinations on the waterfront. Chef John, who has been at the restaurant since its inception, showcased their oyster selection, which features about five different types daily. Their house specialty is Jemma's Pearls, named in honor of Joe Namath's granddaughter and given to the football legend for his birthday. The restaurant goes through approximately 40 cases of oysters per week, with each case containing roughly 100 oysters, totaling 4,000 oysters weekly.

The visit held special meaning for Shining a Light reporter TA Walker, who shared that The Lucky Shuck was the location of his first date with his now-husband, Charlie. The waterfront setting and fresh seafood selection create an ambiance that draws diners to watch vessels drift by while enjoying their meals. The Where's Walter feature marks the beginning of a weekly series exploring different corners of the Jupiter area.
