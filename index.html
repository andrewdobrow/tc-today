2026-07-18

Martin County is facing a $2.1 billion transportation funding shortfall over the next 20 years, a gap that could determine how residents travel for decades to come.

The Martin County Metropolitan Planning Organization estimates the county will need approximately $2.8 billion for transportation improvements through 2050, but only $701 million is currently available. That means the county can fund just about one quarter of its identified needs. Nearly all of the available funding comes from state and federal sources, with only $10 million coming from local revenue. The cost analysis was emailed to county and Stuart officials last August and outlines transportation needs across roads, transit, aviation, freight, and other infrastructure categories.

Among the most pressing unfunded needs are nine roads the county identified as requiring widening as traffic grows. The total cost to widen those roads is approximately $213 million, money that is not currently available, leaving all nine projects out of the county's draft Cost Feasible Plan. Those roads include SW Martin Downs Boulevard, SR-714/SW Martin Highway, SW 96th Street, SW Bridge Road, CR-714/SW Martin Highway, SW Allapattah Road/CR-609, SE Green River Parkway, SW Indiantown Avenue, and SE Bridge Road/CR-708. Without funding for these widening projects, the county will be unable to address growing congestion on these corridors.

State Road 710 stands out as among the county's highest priorities and has been the subject of repeated safety concerns. About $300 million in projects are planned for the corridor, reflecting the urgent need for improvements on this route. The massive funding shortfall highlights the challenge facing Martin County as it tries to balance transportation demands with limited financial resources over the coming decades.

The Florida Department of Health has confirmed five local cases of cyclospora since May, including two in Palm Beach County, two in St. Lucie County and one in Martin County. The cases come as reports of contaminated produce continue to spread across the country, with many South Florida residents changing what they eat and what they avoid.

The latest concern centers around lettuce linked to outbreaks of E. coli and cyclospora, including lettuce connected to Taco Bell locations. Health officials said thousands across the U.S. have been affected, with confirmed cases also reported locally from the Palm Beaches into the Treasure Coast. The outbreaks can cause severe stomach illness and intense diarrhea. Some families are steering clear of leafy greens altogether, with Dakota and Brynlee Rhodes visiting Delray Beach saying they are trying to stay away from lettuce. Their mother Jenny Rhodes said she misses her salads and berries but believes it is better to be careful. In downtown West Palm Beach, George Lorenz said he has heard about the outbreak and is trying to stay away from contaminated produce. Shoppers in Boynton Beach have been loading carts with alternatives, with Hendriette Peckens saying she bought oranges, bread and apples and planned to cook Alaskan salmon for dinner.

Samantha Barone, dietitian for the bariatric program at Delray Medical Center, said consumers should focus on produce with protective outer layers. Barone recommends sticking to melon and things that have a harder skin that you can peel. She also said the trick is to still wash them before cutting into them and peeling them, which includes foods like bananas, melons and avocados. Barone recommends washing all fruits and vegetables thoroughly before eating them and says beans and nuts are safe alternatives that still provide fiber and protein. She emphasized that the outbreaks are not an excuse to abandon healthy eating entirely.

Barone's biggest warning is to avoid bagged lettuce if possible. Instead, she recommends buying whole heads of lettuce, peeling away the outer layers and avoiding berries, especially raspberries, because they are difficult to clean properly. She recommends cleaning produce using a one to three part mixture of distilled white vinegar and water, then soaking, massaging and cleaning off produce. She also suggests making sure to cook vegetables when possible.

A Boca Raton man faces charges of solicitation to commit human trafficking and unlawful use of a two-way communication device after allegedly attempting to solicit videos from someone he believed to be a 14-year-old girl, according to Boynton Beach police. Maximiliano Buitrago, 51, was arrested following an undercover operation conducted by the department's Human Exploitation Unit.

Investigators say Buitrago responded to a Craigslist advertisement offering summer babysitting services that had been posted by undercover detectives. According to police, he contacted the supposed minor and asked, "Are you trying to make cash any other way? What else can you do?" During their communications, Buitrago repeatedly acknowledged that the girl was 14 years old, investigators say. He allegedly offered her money for videos, claiming they would be viewed by clients overseas.

On July 16, Buitrago arranged to meet with the girl at a park in Boynton Beach. When he arrived at the location, police took him into custody. Officers searched his vehicle and found cards that read, "Make money on TikTok with pics and short videos," which also included an offer to "get paid cash." Following his arrest, Buitrago confessed to investigators, according to police. He is currently being held at the Palm Beach County jail.

Boynton Beach police believe there may be additional victims and are asking the public for help in the investigation. Anyone who may have received one of the cards found in Buitrago's vehicle or who communicated with him is encouraged to contact BBPD Detective Joseph at 561-742-6158. The arrest highlights ongoing efforts by law enforcement to combat human trafficking and the exploitation of minors through undercover operations targeting predators who use online platforms to identify potential victims.

4:28 PM ET

JetBlue is canceling its flight route between Vero Beach Regional Airport and New York's JFK Airport, with the last flights operating on Sept. 9. The airline cited low demand as the reason for ending the route, which launched last December to excitement from Treasure Coast residents hoping for convenient travel options to the Northeast. Brandon Dambeck, interim airport director, said the decision reflects how the airline industry operates and that carriers adjust their service based on passenger demand. JetBlue was not seeing the ridership numbers it had hoped for on the JFK route.

Not everyone agrees the route received enough time to establish itself. Charlie Ahern, who regularly uses JetBlue flights at Vero Beach Regional Airport, said he believes the route would have grown over time. "I think if they hung in there, it would have worked. I think it would have built up pretty good," Ahern said. He emphasized that the convenience of flying out of Vero Beach is difficult to match. "It's easy. It's close," Ahern said. Meanwhile, the airline's Boston route from Vero Beach continues to perform well, with Dambeck noting that Boston is seeing great demand. Grece Ezzi flew into Vero Beach on JetBlue from Boston on Friday for the first time.

All affected customers will be notified directly by JetBlue and will receive full refunds. Passengers with upcoming reservations on the discontinued route are encouraged to contact JetBlue directly or check their email for rebooking options and refund information. JetBlue has not announced whether it plans to add any replacement routes from Vero Beach or if it will adjust frequency on the remaining Boston service.

Dambeck said the door is not closed on the route returning in the future. "They'll evaluate continuously. So just because it's gone now doesn't mean that it never will come back," Dambeck said. The cancellation represents a setback for local travelers who had welcomed the direct connection to New York when it launched less than a year ago, though the continued success of the Boston route demonstrates ongoing viability for JetBlue service at the regional airport.

2:26 PM ET

Major League Baseball is restricting iPad usage in dugouts to prevent the tablets from running artificial intelligence to help make decisions. The tablets have access to video and league-provided data, and also included a custom tab where teams could access other programs. MLB made the custom tabs inaccessible to teams starting Wednesday night, when the second half of the season started. MLB executive vice president of baseball operations Morgan Sword wrote in a June 11 memo to general managers, assistant GMs and video coordinators that the custom tab had expanded the use of dugout iPads beyond their originally intended purpose to include recommendations regarding substitutions, pitch calling, and other in-game decisions traditionally made by players and coaches.

A review by the competition committee found clubs had been compliant with the regulations. Sword wrote that instituting the prohibition at the start of the second half was intended to provide clubs that had relied on the custom tab with appropriate lead-time to make any necessary adjustments. MLB started a pilot program allowing use of iPads in dugouts with restrictions late in the 2015 season and expanded their use in 2016 under a deal with Apple. Video was eliminated in the 2020 COVID season following the Houston Astros' sign-stealing scandal, then returned in 2021.

A Leon County jury ordered Republican state Rep. Fabian Basabe to pay $450,000 after finding him liable for sexual harassment, battery and defamation. The civil judgment came at the end of a trial brought by former legislative aide Nicolas Frevola, former intern Jacob Cutbirth and Frevola's mother. Basabe represented himself in court despite having no legal training. The trial was repeatedly interrupted by procedural mistakes and violations of the judge's orders.

Circuit Judge J. Lee Marsh told Basabe directly that if an attorney had done half of what he had done, they would have been sanctioned. Basabe told jurors he represented himself because he wanted to demonstrate that he had nothing to hide. However, after the verdict, he acknowledged that proceeding without an attorney was a mistake. The judge's comments underscored the severity of Basabe's courtroom conduct during the proceedings.

Basabe continues to deny the underlying allegations against him. He has not been criminally charged, and an outside ethics investigation conducted for the Florida House previously reached an inconclusive finding. Despite the civil judgment, Basabe maintains his innocence regarding the claims of sexual harassment, battery and defamation brought by the plaintiffs. His political opponents quickly called for his resignation following the verdict.

Basabe is seeking reelection this fall. The verdict could renew questions about whether House leaders will pursue disciplinary action against him. The case represents a significant legal setback for the Republican lawmaker as he campaigns to retain his seat. The $450,000 judgment reflects the jury's findings on multiple claims including sexual harassment, battery and defamation brought by two former staff members and the mother of one of them.
