Yesterday, 5:18 PM ET

On August 12, 2025, three people died in St. Lucie County when their minivan was crushed beneath a semi-truck. The victims were Herby Dufresne, 30, Rodrigue Dor, 54, and Faniola Joseph, 37, who had traveled to Florida from Indiana to purchase inventory for her shop. The truck driver, Harjinder Singh, allegedly made an illegal U-turn in a marked no-turn zone, causing the collision. Now Joseph's daughter, Angeline Dowdian, 22, has filed a wrongful death lawsuit against Singh, his employer White Hawk Carriers, and freight broker C.H. Robinson. The lawsuit claims negligence and unsafe practices contributed to the tragedy. Singh also faces criminal traffic homicide charges in Florida.

Angeline, represented by attorney Dax Bello of Stuart Tillman, Fox, Bianchi & Kane, and co-counsel Mark Brumer of Brumer & Brumer, describes the profound impact of her mother's death. Living abroad at the time of the crash, Angeline had not seen her mother in person for six years, though they spoke daily through video calls. The day of the accident, her mother left a voice message that Angeline says she listens to every morning. "She sent me a voice message. She said, 'Angeline, where are you?' This is the first message I go to every morning," Angeline said through tears. Her mother was both parents to her since her father rejected her at birth. Visa restrictions have prevented Angeline from visiting her mother's grave. Joseph had hoped to see her daughter become a nurse and would have turned 38 on May 26, 2026.

The lawsuit alleges that C.H. Robinson, a multibillion-dollar freight broker, knowingly continued business with White Hawk Carriers despite serious safety violations. Bello argues this was not an accident but a predictable outcome. A transportation expert has characterized White Hawk's violations as "very egregious." The case is strengthened by a May 14 Supreme Court ruling in Montgomery v. Caribbean Transport, the same broker named in this lawsuit, which clarified that federal law does not protect freight brokers from state negligence claims when they place unsafe drivers on roads. According to Justice Brett Kavanaugh's concurring opinion, "Some carriers are known to be less safe, and some truck drivers are known to be unfit." Bello maintains that brokers have a responsibility to review publicly available safety records before hiring drivers.

White Hawk Carriers has closed, though Bello warns the company may reopen under a different name, a common industry practice. Bello, a former homicide prosecutor, is helping Angeline navigate both civil and criminal proceedings under Marsy's Law, which provides victims' families with rights to be informed and heard. Angeline acknowledges that no legal victory will reverse her loss but hopes the lawsuit prevents other families from suffering similar tragedy. "I don't want nobody suffer like me," she said.

Enrique Rosario Liz has become the first member of Boys & Girls Clubs of St. Lucie County to earn the Southeast Region Youth of the Year award. The St. Lucie High School valedictorian will now compete for the national title at the Youth of the Year competition in New York City on October 1. His achievement marks a significant milestone for the local organization and represents years of dedication to academics, personal values, and community leadership.

Enrique's path to this recognition began eight years ago when he arrived in Port St. Lucie from the Dominican Republic without speaking English. Rather than viewing this challenge as a barrier, he used the support from his school and the Boys & Girls Club to build his skills and confidence. Today, he holds the titles of both county and Florida Youth of the Year. This fall, he will begin his college career at the University of Florida. "I could never have imagined this moment when I was 8 years old," Enrique said after receiving the award. "It's a privilege to share my journey, and I hope it inspires belief in every young person who needs a helping hand and an opportunity."

Will Armstead, CEO of Boys & Girls Clubs of St. Lucie County, highlighted Enrique's character and growth. "Enrique's achievement is historic, but his character impresses me most. He's turned challenges into opportunities and advocated for others, all with humility and determination." Travis Leonard, owner of A&G Concrete Pools and a longtime judge for the Youth of the Year program, reinforced this assessment. "From the start, Enrique stood out. Thanks to his hard work and all those who support him, we finally have a Southeast Region Youth of the Year. I believe he's ready for the national competition, and I'm proud of him and our whole community."

The Youth of the Year program represents the highest honor given by Boys & Girls Clubs of America to recognize exceptional teenagers who demonstrate leadership and positive change in their communities. Enrique's parents expressed their pride in their son's accomplishments. "We're grateful to God for every opportunity and to Boys & Girls Clubs of St. Lucie County for helping Enrique grow as a leader and a person," they said. As Enrique prepares to represent the Southeast Region on the national stage, his journey continues to inspire young people throughout the community.

Yesterday, 1:07 PM ET

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium invites visitors to explore Florida's coastal underwater habitats along Fort Pierce's waterfront. Manager Bill Hoffman explains that the facility's approach of displaying fully live, complex ecosystem models began in 1980 under the Smithsonian, distinguishing it from most public aquariums. "We feel like we're the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast, and we don't want to be," Hoffman said, pointing out that the facility houses more species than many larger exhibits. Located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce, the aquarium operates year-round and welcomes the public to experience marine life up close.

The centerpiece of the exhibit is the Atlantic coral reef tank, which recreates one of Florida's most important coastal ecosystems in vivid detail. Beyond the coral reef, visitors encounter live representations of seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities. Each display demonstrates how organisms interact and depend on one another within their habitats. The collection includes unexpected residents, such as the facility's four-inch barracuda, showing the diversity of marine life in Florida's waters.

A popular attraction for many guests is the touch tank, where visitors handle unusual marine species under the watchful eye of trained staff members. Exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran notes that guests often encounter animals they have never seen before. This hands-on experience creates memorable connections between visitors and the marine world. The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit partners with the Smithsonian Marine Station to raise public awareness about ocean threats while promoting conservation efforts. Hoffman stresses that maintaining healthy ecosystems requires understanding the critical role of countless small organisms, a lesson reinforced through the facility's live displays.

As the exhibit celebrates its 25th anniversary this year, upcoming programming will highlight the facility's mission and history. With growing concerns about ocean health, exhibits like this one in Fort Pierce provide valuable educational opportunities for visitors of all ages. They create connections between people and the fragile beauty and importance of Florida's marine ecosystems. For more information about hours, exhibits, and programming, visitors can check sms.si.edu and the Smithsonian Marine Station's social media channels.

Florida economic outlook downgraded as tourism growth slows amid global instability

A few minutes ago

Florida TaxWatch released its latest economic outlook projecting that Florida will add roughly 2.3 million residents between 2026 and 2035 and continue to outperform the national economy in terms of growth. However, compared to its previous forecast, TaxWatch has downgraded expectations for several key indicators, citing global instability, rising energy costs, and uncertainty surrounding the Strait of Hormuz. Population growth is still expected to climb from 23.8 million in 2026 to just over 26 million by 2035, with daily net migration gradually slowing from about 895 people a day to 689. Despite economic headwinds, Florida continues to attract new residents thanks to its business climate, lack of a personal income tax, and favorable weather.

The labor market presents a less favorable picture. While total employment is expected to grow from 10.1 million workers to 11.3 million over the next decade, TaxWatch now expects unemployment to rise higher and remain elevated longer than previously forecast. The statewide unemployment rate is projected to peak at 4.8% in 2027, then gradually settle near 4.2% in the early 2030s. Income growth expectations have also been lowered. Per-capita personal income is still projected to rise from roughly $88,000 in 2026 to more than $126,000 by 2035, but overall income projections declined by an average of 10.3% compared to the prior forecast.

Tourism represents the biggest downgrade in the outlook. TaxWatch's growth expectations for visitor numbers have fallen by an average of 45.3% since the previous report, making tourism the sector most affected by current headwinds. Visitor counts are still expected to increase each year, but the pace of growth is projected to slow dramatically, bottoming out at just 0.8% in 2027. Despite this slowdown, Florida's broader economic output is expected to perform relatively well. Real GDP growth will slow from 2.5% in 2026 to 1.9% in 2027 before rebounding above 3% for much of the following decade.

The primary uncertainty shaping TaxWatch's outlook centers on Middle Eastern geopolitics. The watchdog group repeatedly points to uncertainty surrounding the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a quarter of the world's seaborne oil passes, as the single biggest factor influencing its projections. If energy markets stabilize, the group suggests many of its more pessimistic assumptions could ease, potentially allowing Florida to avoid a rougher economic ride. The overall message from the report frames Florida as resilient but not immune to broader economic pressures.

Indian River County faces $50 million budget loss from property tax amendment on November ballot

Indian River County leaders warned Friday that a property tax amendment on the November ballot could cost the county around $50 million by 2028.

Yesterday, 4:40 PM ET

Indian River County leaders gathered Friday at the Heritage Center in Vero Beach to discuss a property tax amendment appearing on the November ballot. The amendment would raise the homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. Vice Chair Laura Moss convened the meeting, criticizing the state for dictating local funding decisions. "They've declared war on local government," Moss said. The county operates on a budget of approximately $597 million and predicts it could lose around $50 million in funding by 2028.

Lance Lunceford, president of the Indian River County Tax Payers Association, characterized the amendment as a tax shift rather than a tax cut. He warned that filling the $50 million shortfall without reducing law enforcement and first responder services would be impossible. The shift could increase costs on non-homesteaded and commercial properties, as well as government service fees like impact fees and utility fees. Christen Brewer, president of Indian River Firefighters, said fire rescue could lose $15 to $20 million of its $70 million budget, since about 79.1 percent of fire rescue funding comes from ad valorem taxes. Three projected fire stations may not be built if the budget shortfall occurs.

The new congressional districts will now be used for the 2026 elections. The map, approved by Gov. DeSantis stated the changes were necessary to create a race-neutral map, anticipating that the Supreme Court would make it harder to use race as a factor in redistricting.

On August 12, 2025, three people were killed when their minivan became wedged underneath a semi-truck on the Florida Turnpike in St. Lucie County. Now the daughter of one victim has filed a wrongful death lawsuit.

Yesterday, 5:18 PM ET

On August 12, 2025, Herby Dufresne, 30, Faniola Joseph, 37, and Rodrigue Dor, 54, were killed in St. Lucie County when their minivan became wedged underneath a semi-truck. The truck driver, Harjinder Singh, who worked for White Hawk Carriers, allegedly attempted an illegal U-turn in a clearly marked "No U-turn" zone. Joseph's 22-year-old daughter, Angeline Dowdian, has filed a wrongful death lawsuit against Singh, White Hawk Carriers, and freight broker C.H. Robinson, alleging negligence and unsafe practices. Attorney Dax Bello of Stuart Tillman, Fox, Bianchi & Kane argues the crash was not an accident but "a predictable end that came at the cost of three lives." The lawsuit alleges C.H. Robinson knowingly gave business to White Hawk Carriers despite serious safety violations in its record. The case is supported by a May 14 U.S. Supreme Court ruling in Montgomery v. Caribbean Transport, which clarified that federal law does not shield freight brokers from state negligence claims when unsafe drivers are placed on the road.

Angeline lived in another country at the time of the crash and had not seen her mother in person in six years, though they spoke daily via video calls. She missed a call from her mother on the day of the crash and receives a voice message from her each morning asking, "Angeline, where are you?" Singh faces criminal traffic homicide charges in Florida. White Hawk Carriers has since shut down, though Bello warns the company may reopen under another name.

Humane Society of the Treasure Coast thrift stores house adoptable cats during Palm City shelter renovations

The Humane Society of the Treasure Coast is using its two Stuart thrift stores to house cats while construction continues on adoption, customer service, and intake areas at its Palm City shelter.

Yesterday, 1:53 PM ET

The Humane Society of the Treasure Coast is housing adoptable cats at its two thrift stores while undergoing major renovations at its Palm City shelter. The nonprofit began construction on its adoption, customer service, and intake areas earlier this year, temporarily relocating many of its cats to the store locations. Spokesperson Sarah Fisher explained that the stores have become essential during this period. "With our shelter cages unavailable, the stores have become a lifeline for our animals," she said Thursday.

Senior Manager Kelly Ellis noted that shoppers can now browse unique items for sale while also viewing adoptable cats at both thrift stores. "Every treasure you find here helps provide for our animals," Ellis said, welcoming donations of gently used goods. The organization is also seeking temporary foster homes for other animals while construction continues. The thrift stores are located at 3302 SE Federal Highway in Stuart and 1099 NW 21st Street in Stuart. Store hours and donation guidelines are available at hstc1.org.

Ten candidates qualify for race to succeed Frederica Wilson in Congress

Seven Democrats, one Republican, one independent, and one write-in candidate qualified for the race in Florida's 24th Congressional District, with several well-known candidates competing for the deeply blue seat.

Yesterday, 7:50 PM ET

A race to replace longtime Democratic U.S. Rep. Frederica Wilson in Florida's 24th Congressional District is set with 10 candidates after the qualifying deadline Friday. The contest features seven Democrats, one Republican, one person with no party affiliation, and a write-in candidate. CD 24, redrawn under a mid-decade redistricting plan drafted by Gov. Ron DeSantis' office, covers Miami Gardens in Miami-Dade and Hollywood, Miramar, and Pembroke Park in Broward County. The district is deeply blue, and the winner in November will almost assuredly be a Democrat.

State Sen. Shevrin Jones, Chair of the Miami-Dade County Democratic Party and a former in-line candidate for Senate Democratic Leader, is a favorite in the race. He pointed to legislation he passed, including the Dignity for Incarcerated Women Act, Blue Envelope Program, and Infectious Disease Elimination Program. Also prominent is Miami-Dade Commissioner Oliver Gilbert, a former Mayor of Miami Gardens and immediate past Chair of the County Commission, who reported $250,000 raised in just the first five days of his campaign. Other Democrats include former lawyer Kendrick Meek Jr., son and grandson of two former members of Congress; Miami-Dade Commissioner Jean Monestime; and doctor, lawyer, and repeat congressional candidate Rudolph "Rudy" Moise. Cultural leader Marshall Davis and civil rights lawyer Roderick Vereen also qualified. Republican candidate Te Mayonna Brown is a self-described real estate developer. NPA candidate Andy Daro is a sitting North Bay Village Commissioner. Patricia Gonzalez is a write-in candidate. The Primary Election is Aug. 18, followed by the General Election in November.

Delray Beach struggles to replace Pride memorial amid state restrictions

Ten years after the Pulse nightclub massacre, Delray Beach residents face mounting obstacles to creating a new memorial after the state removed a rainbow-painted intersection that honored the 49 victims.

Yesterday, 5:22 PM ET

A rainbow-painted Pride intersection in Delray Beach was sandblasted away by the Florida Department of Transportation in August 2025, removing a mural that local residents had created and funded themselves to honor Pulse nightclub massacre victims. The state removal was part of a statewide push to regulate road markings and eliminate political and ideological messaging from roads. Similar crosswalks were removed in Orlando, including one outside the Pulse nightclub itself, while Boynton Beach preemptively took down its Pride crosswalk and West Palm Beach removed one in Northwood before later installing a rainbow corner in a nearby park. Delray Beach initially considered legal action but abandoned the idea due to potential costs.

Local advocate Ronnie Dunayer and other residents formed the Delray Beach Pride Memorial Committee and designed a rainbow pyramid monument to honor Pulse victims, proposing Old School Square near the Eagle Scout monument as the location. However, Senate Bill 1134, which passed 25-11 in the Senate and 77-37 in the House and awaits the governor's decision, would ban local governments from funding or supporting diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives. Commissioner Juli Casal stated that private funding and private property could allow the project to proceed without city involvement. Dunayer is reaching out to state leaders and national organizations to find solutions.

Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit in Fort Pierce showcases Florida coastal habitats

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium offers rare close-up encounters with marine life at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce.

Yesterday, 1:07 PM ET

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium invites visitors to explore Florida's coastal habitats in Fort Pierce's waterfront district. Manager Bill Hoffman says the facility's philosophy of displaying fully live, complex models of ecosystems dates back to 1980 under the Smithsonian, making it unlike most public aquariums. Hoffman told WPTV that more species are housed here than in many larger exhibits. The highlight is the Atlantic coral reef exhibit, a sprawling tank that recreates one of Florida's most significant coastal ecosystems. Visitors will also see live models of seagrass beds, mangroves, and hardbottom communities, each demonstrating the intricate relationships between organisms, including the aquarium's four-inch barracuda.

The exhibit's touch tank is another crowd favorite, according to exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran, where guests can handle unusual marine species under staff guidance. The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit operates in partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station, aiming to raise public awareness about threats facing oceans while inspiring conservation. Upcoming programming will tie into the facility's 25th anniversary celebration later this year. The facility is located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce and is open to the public year-round.
