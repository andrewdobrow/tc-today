Lawsuit challenges Florida property tax ballot language as biased and misleading

A voter advocacy group and two registered voters filed a lawsuit Thursday in Leon County challenging the ballot language for Florida's property tax amendment approved by lawmakers for November 2026. 2:27 PM ET

A voter advocacy group and two registered voters filed a lawsuit Thursday in Leon County challenging the ballot language for Florida's property tax amendment approved by lawmakers for November 2026. The complaint contends the ballot summary uses "biased" and "misleading" language, pointing to campaign-style phrases such as "Save Our Homes From Excessive Property Taxes," "protecting small businesses," and "ensuring fairness."

The plaintiffs argue the ballot summary overstates what the amendment actually delivers. They claim it falsely suggests the measure guarantees funding for core services, fully eliminates non-school homestead taxes, and immediately exempts the first $250,000 of a home's value. According to the lawsuit, the initial exemption would actually be $150,000 starting in 2027, not $250,000. The lawsuit also challenges language claiming the amendment would ensure core services funding, arguing it does not guarantee money for police, firefighters, schools, or infrastructure. Additionally, the plaintiffs dispute assertions that the proposal protects small businesses, noting the amendment does not specifically mention them. Instead, it would limit future assessment increases on non-homesteaded property more broadly. The complaint further argues the summary overstates how the amendment would handle non-school property taxes on homesteaded properties, saying the text creates a process allowing local governments to provide additional exemptions rather than requiring full elimination.

Attorney Jamie Cole, representing the plaintiffs, argues that ballot language must be fair, neutral, and accurate. Cole previously succeeded in challenging a similar property tax amendment nearly two decades ago, when a judge in 2007 found the language for a proposed "Super Exemption" misleading and removed it from the ballot. Cole emphasizes that accurate wording matters because many voters read only the ballot summary before voting. The lawsuit asks a judge to declare the current wording unconstitutional and order Attorney General James Uthmeier to rewrite the ballot language within ten days.

On June 2, lawmakers passed legislation establishing the homestead exemption increase, raising it to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028 while creating a framework for full exemption over time. The measure will appear on the November 2026 General Election Ballot and requires approval from at least 60 percent of voters to become law. During the June special session, House Democratic Leader Fentrice Driskell raised similar concerns about the language, characterizing the proposal as misleading. Republican supporters contend local governments can absorb the reductions while providing meaningful relief to homeowners.

A few minutes ago

A wrongful death lawsuit was filed months after a fatal crash on Florida's Turnpike that claimed three lives when a semi-truck driver made an illegal U-turn in St. Lucie County. The lawsuit, filed on behalf of the estate of victim Faniola Joseph, names multiple defendants including the truck driver, trucking company, freight broker C.H. Robinson, and a company manager.

On August 12, 2025, at approximately 2:57 p.m., commercial truck driver Harjinder Singh attempted an illegal U-turn through a median crossover on Florida's Turnpike near Mile Marker 171 in St. Lucie County. The crossover was clearly marked "U-TURN OFFICIAL USE ONLY" according to court documents. The 53-foot trailer swung across and completely blocked all northbound travel lanes. A minivan driven by Herby Dufresne, carrying passengers Rodrigue Dor and Faniola Joseph, struck the left side of the trailer in what the complaint describes as "a classic and fatal underride crash." All three occupants were killed, with Joseph pronounced dead at the scene.

The lawsuit raises significant concerns about carrier selection and driver qualifications. It alleges Harjinder Singh was present in the United States illegally without lawful immigration status or work authorization, yet obtained a Class A California commercial driver's license. The complaint further alleges Singh lacked required English language proficiency under federal regulations that mandate commercial drivers read and speak English sufficiently to understand highway signs and respond to official inquiries. Additionally, an unauthorized passenger identified as Harneet Singh was in the truck cab at the time of the crash, representing further safety violations. C.H. Robinson Company Inc., one of the largest transportation brokers in the United States with headquarters in Eden Prairie, Minnesota, and eight Florida offices, selected White Hawk Carriers Inc. despite the carrier's documented safety issues. Before this selection, White Hawk Carriers already had a prior reportable crash on approximately December 9, 2024, multiple roadside inspection citations for unsafe driving violations, repeated hours-of-service violations with falsified duty status records, and vehicle defects including brake problems. This safety information was publicly available through the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's Safety Measurement System data.

Harpreet Singh, identified as a White Hawk Carriers manager and California resident, is named individually in the lawsuit. The complaint alleges he was personally responsible for hiring, training, and supervising company drivers, including Harjinder Singh, but failed to adequately investigate the truck driver's immigration status, work authorization, English proficiency, and overall fitness before hiring. The lawsuit seeks damages exceeding $75,000 under Florida's Wrongful Death Act, with Joseph's daughter entitled to compensation for her mother's death. This case highlights ongoing concerns about trucking industry safety oversight and the responsibility of freight brokers in carrier selection.

Yesterday, 6:50 PM ET

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi has asked county staff to begin conversations with Brightline leadership about relocating the planned train station from its current proposed location in downtown Stuart. The station had been previously agreed upon to be built on a parking lot site across from the courthouse in downtown Stuart. Ciampi suggested the fairgrounds as a potential alternative location, citing parking issues and other concerns at the downtown site.

The request comes in response to opposition from the FEC, which sent a letter to then-Stuart Mayor Christopher Collins stating it does not support a Brightline station in Stuart. The FEC indicated that the proposed station would violate U.S. Coast Guard regulations because it would require the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge to remain down while the train is stopped at the station. Ciampi explained his reasoning for the potential relocation, saying that if the station is deemed too close to navigational channels by the Army Corps of Engineers or the U.S. Coast Guard, he would prefer to take the downtown location off the table entirely. He argued that moving the station further south to the fairgrounds would eliminate this regulatory issue altogether.

Community response to Ciampi's proposal has been mixed. Resident Paul Gedwed supported the idea, describing it as well-thought-out and noting the significant congestion that would occur at the proposed downtown location. However, Steven Vitale, owner of the Colorado Inn, disagreed with the fairgrounds option. He questioned the logic of building a train station that people cannot walk to, emphasizing that most visitors would want access to downtown Stuart's attractions and businesses. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, took a pragmatic approach, saying she believes her downtown store will benefit from the Brightline project regardless of the station's final location, as long as it remains in Martin County.

County officials clarified that Ciampi's comments do not constitute official direction for staff to pursue changes at this time. The full county board would need to discuss and vote on the proposal before staff can take action to explore alternative locations. The project remains in a holding pattern as the county awaits news on its grant application to fund the Brightline station before moving forward with any construction plans.

1:53 PM ET

The Humane Society of the Treasure Coast is temporarily housing cats at its two thrift store locations while undergoing major renovations at its Stuart shelter. The nonprofit began construction on its adoption, customer service, and intake areas earlier this year, and with shelter cages unavailable, the organization needed a creative solution to continue caring for its animals. According to spokesperson Sarah Fisher, the thrift stores have stepped in as "a lifeline for our animals" during this renovation period.

The decision to use the retail spaces has added a unique element to the shopping experience at both locations. Customers browsing for gently used goods can now also meet adoptable cats available for adoption. Senior Manager Kelly Ellis emphasized that this arrangement benefits both the animals and the organization's mission. "Every treasure you find here helps provide for our animals," Ellis said, while welcoming donations of gently used items from the community. The combination of retail shopping and animal adoption gives shoppers the opportunity to support the nonprofit while searching for quality merchandise.

Beyond the thrift store initiative, the Humane Society is also seeking temporary foster homes for other animals in its care while construction continues. This multi-pronged approach helps the organization maintain its animal care services without interruption. The situation reflects a broader challenge that animal shelters across the country face when buildings require upgrades and improvements. The Treasure Coast's approach demonstrates how creative problem-solving can keep community connection alive by bringing adoptable pets into everyday spaces where residents already shop and gather.

The Humane Society's thrift stores are located at 3302 SE Federal Highway in Stuart and at another location elsewhere in Martin County. Community members interested in visiting, shopping, or adopting can find store hours and donation guidelines on the organization's website at hstc1.org. Those looking to support the animals during the renovation period can do so by shopping at the thrift stores, donating gently used goods, or providing temporary foster care. For more information about how to help, residents can visit the website or contact the organization directly.

A wrongful death lawsuit was filed months after a fatal crash on Florida's Turnpike killed three people when a semi-truck driver made an illegal U-turn in St. Lucie County. The lawsuit names truck driver Harjinder Singh, trucking company White Hawk Carriers Inc., freight broker C.H. Robinson, and company manager Harpreet Singh as defendants. The case represents the estate of victim Faniola Joseph, one of three people who died in the collision.

On August 12, 2025, at approximately 2:57 p.m., Singh attempted an illegal U-turn through a median crossover clearly marked "U-TURN OFFICIAL USE ONLY" on Florida's Turnpike near Mile Marker 171. The 53-foot trailer swung across all northbound travel lanes, completely blocking traffic. A minivan driven by Herby Dufresne, carrying passengers Rodrigue Dor and Faniola Joseph, struck the left side of the trailer in what the lawsuit describes as "a classic and fatal underride crash." All three occupants were killed, with Joseph pronounced dead at the scene.

The lawsuit raises serious questions about the truck driver's qualifications and authorization to operate commercial vehicles. According to the complaint, Singh was present in the United States illegally without lawful immigration status or authorization to work. Despite this alleged status, he obtained a Class A California commercial driver's license in violation of applicable federal and state regulations. The complaint further alleges Singh lacked required English language proficiency under federal regulation 49 C.F.R. 391.11(b)(2), which mandates commercial drivers read and speak English sufficiently to understand highway signs and respond to official inquiries.

The lawsuit also scrutinizes the decisions made by C.H. Robinson, one of the largest transportation brokers in the United States, in selecting White Hawk Carriers to transport the load. Court documents allege the carrier had a documented unsafe federal safety record that was publicly available through the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's Safety Measurement System. This record included a prior reportable crash in December 2024, multiple roadside inspection citations for unsafe driving violations including speeding and improper lane changes, repeated hours-of-service violations with falsified records, and vehicle defects including brake problems and air-brake leaks. The complaint alleges C.H. Robinson failed to adequately vet the carrier despite marketing itself as a company that properly evaluates carriers using federal safety data. A Titan Arum, commonly called the corpse flower, is expected to bloom soon at McKee Botanical Garden in Vero Beach. According to updates from the garden's Instagram account, the rare plant could flower within the next few days or weeks. When it does bloom, visitors will experience one of nature's most distinctive and unpleasant phenomena: the flower emits a powerful stench resembling rotting flesh. The bloom itself will be short-lived, typically lasting only 24 to 48 hours before the flower closes.

The Titan Arum originates from the rainforests of Sumatra and Indonesia, where it faces serious threats in its natural habitat. The species is considered endangered, making specimens like the one at McKee particularly valuable for conservation efforts and scientific study. The botanical garden received this individual plant in May 2026 and has been carefully monitoring its development ever since. The garden has affectionately nicknamed the flower "Princess Pungent" and has kept followers updated through social media posts documenting the plant's progress.

Recent signs suggest the bloom may arrive very soon. According to a Thursday update from McKee Botanical Garden, the flower has grown nine inches taller in recent days. Additionally, one of the plant's three remaining leaves has dropped off, which the garden identifies as an important indicator that blooming time is approaching. These physical changes typically precede the dramatic flowering event that makes corpse flowers so remarkable and sought-after by botanists and curious visitors alike.

The timing could work in visitors' favor. McKee Botanical Garden's annual Waterlily Celebration is scheduled for June 13, which falls within the window when Princess Pungent might bloom. This means guests attending the celebration could potentially witness the full flowering display and experience the distinctive odor firsthand. For those interested in following the plant's progress, McKee Botanical Garden continues to post regular updates on Instagram, allowing people to track when the rare bloom might occur. Whether the flower cooperates with the celebration's timing remains to be seen, but the garden's dedicated monitoring ensures that when bloom time arrives, news will spread quickly to those eager to witness this unusual botanical event.

Law enforcement agencies across Florida are joining forces to combat so-called "teen takeovers" after many events have recently turned violent in several communities. Corpse flower nicknamed Princess Pungent set to bloom at McKee Botanical Garden in Vero Beach

A rare Titan Arum, also known as the corpse flower, is set to bloom at McKee Botanical Garden in Vero Beach in the next few days or weeks.

1:08 PM ET

The rare Titan Arum, also known as the corpse flower, is set to bloom at McKee Botanical Garden in Vero Beach in the next few days or weeks, according to updates from the botanical garden's Instagram. The flower's bloom period typically lasts only 24 to 48 hours, during which it emits the stench of rotting flesh, hence its distinctive name. The plant is native to the rainforests of Sumatra and Indonesia and is considered endangered.

This particular Titan Arum arrived at McKee in May 2026. In a Thursday update, the botanical garden reported that the flower has already grown nine inches taller, and one of three remaining leaves has dropped, a sign that bloom time is approaching. McKee Botanical Garden's annual Waterlily Celebration takes place June 13, which means visitors could potentially see and smell the full bloom during the event.
