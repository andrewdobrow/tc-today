Jul 2, 5:05 PM ET

Stuart's downtown public dock will close July 6 for a $3 million renovation projected to be complete by July 4, 2027. The project will nearly double the dock's capacity, expanding from 18 to 44 slips for motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation includes a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock and a smaller floating platform. The project is funded by a state grant and transportation impact fees.

Stuart Mayor Sean Reed said the upgrades are designed to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms. More than 1,400 boaters use the dock annually. During the closure, the city is working to accommodate businesses relying on the dock. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates from the dock, will be allowed to build a gate at the end of the non-floating section to continue boarding passengers, according to Austin Kuyrkendall, the company's director of operations. Boat captain Parker Fisher said the dock has needed work for years and hopes to see more floating docks added to the facility.

Yesterday, 6:17 PM ET

The School District of Palm Beach County has received an A rating from the Florida Department of Education for the third consecutive year, reflecting strong student performance across all grade levels. The district reported that 143 of its operated schools, representing 84 percent of the total, earned either an A or B rating. This marks significant progress from the previous year, when 120 schools achieved these top two ratings. The state assigns grades to school districts and schools based on multiple factors, including student performance on state tests, graduation rates, and achievement in advanced coursework.

The district's success extends beyond school ratings to overall student achievement. The graduation rate reached a record-setting 93.5 percent, while 86 percent of seniors participated in college-level coursework by taking at least one Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, Cambridge Advanced International Certificate of Education, or Dual Enrollment course. Additionally, 144 recent graduates earned an associate degree while still completing their high school diploma. Superintendent Mike Burke praised these accomplishments, stating that the A rating "reflects our ongoing commitment to sparking academic success and helping every student ignite their future."

One of the most dramatic success stories came from S.D. Spady Elementary School in Delray Beach, which jumped from a C rating to an A rating this year. The school had actually earned an A rating two years prior but narrowly missed a B rating last year. Principal Rona Tata, who has led the school for 15 years after five years as assistant principal, attributed the turnaround to a comprehensive approach. Three years ago, the school modified its early childhood curriculum program, which serves students starting at age 3 through kindergarten. Many of those students are now in third grade and performing at higher levels. Beyond curriculum changes, the school refined its entire academic approach, focusing on challenging both students and staff to raise expectations across campus. Tata said the school's theme last year was "Be the reason you succeed," which resonated with the school community.

Spady Elementary was one of four district schools that improved from a C rating to an A rating this year. The other three schools include Dwight D. Eisenhower K-8, Glade View Elementary, and West Gate Elementary. Community members have expressed enthusiasm about Spady's transformation, noting that achieving an A rating will help attract students to their neighborhood school and demonstrate the district's commitment to educational excellence in the area.

The Martin County School Board is pushing back against a judge's recommendation to reinstate a teacher suspended last year following controversial social media comments about Charlie Kirk. Matt Theobald, a social studies teacher at Spectrum Academy since 2009, sparked controversy with his comments, which labeled Kirk as racist, misogynistic and a neo-Nazi. In a Thursday court filing, the school board argued Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone erred in recommending Theobald get his job back, saying "numerous findings ... are not based upon competent substantial evidence." The board's attorney claims Theobald's Facebook post about Kirk was a "serious offense" and removing him was appropriate.

In an order issued last month, Livingstone cited a recent Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal ruling that said disciplining a teacher for personal Facebook posts made off school grounds, on personal equipment, with no student involvement, violated the teacher's First Amendment rights. The judge also recommended Theobald be awarded back pay and benefits. According to an investigation report, Theobald admitted writing the comments about Kirk but said they were posted on his mother's private Facebook page "within a discussion among personal acquaintances" and were not intended for public view. The school board still has the final say on whether Theobald gets his job back. After the judge's recommendation, Theobald told WPTV the case raises a larger question about where the line is drawn for teachers speaking outside of school.

Riviera Beach named only Florida city to receive 2026 All-America City Award

Riviera Beach earned the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, becoming the only Florida city selected for the honor this year and one of just 10 U.S. communities to receive the award. The prestigious honor recognizes communities that bring residents, local government, businesses and organizations together to solve local challenges and create lasting chang...

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach earned the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, becoming the only Florida city selected for the honor this year and one of just 10 U.S. communities to receive the award. The prestigious honor recognizes communities that bring residents, local government, businesses and organizations together to solve local challenges and create lasting change. The award was announced during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where Riviera Beach leaders presented the city's efforts to strengthen community engagement. Mayor Douglas Lawson called the recognition a proud moment for the city, stating that "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." Lawson said the city earned the award through a long-term effort to revitalize Riviera Beach and improve quality of life for residents.

The city's application highlighted several civic engagement programs: the Riviera Beach Youth Council, which involves high school students in civic leadership and advocacy; the Citizens Leadership Academy with a 98% completion rate; and the Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program, which provides mini-grants to help longtime homeowners make critical repairs. City leaders say the recognition reflects years of work focused on improving civic health, strengthening trust between residents and local government, and creating opportunities for the community to play a direct role in shaping the city's future. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for the honor, saying the award "brings back community pride." The All-America City Award has been presented annually since 1949.

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment.

The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups encourage beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection announced free admission to state parks from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends, and family to explore the outdoors and make new memories at the state's public lands. The initiative applies to day use only at most parks, with overnight accommodations, special events, concessions, and rentals still requiring payment.

Florida's state park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award. The parks offer hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling, horseback riding, and access to historic sites including forts, battlefields, and archaeological landmarks. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks recorded more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated $3.6 billion in statewide economic impact. Three locations are excluded from the free admission weekend: Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park, and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. Visitors can plan trips at FloridaStateParks.org.

A record-breaking number of Americans are hitting the roads this Fourth of July weekend, and South Florida drivers are contending with both massive crowds and heavy rain. AAA estimates that 72.2 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, marking an all-time high for the holiday. Of those travelers, 61.4 million are making the journey by car. Orlando and Miami rank among the top five travel destinations for Independence Day this year, according to AAA data. Friday's wet weather created challenging conditions on the Florida Turnpike south of Orlando as drivers began their holiday trips.

The combination of rain and congestion tested drivers' abilities on the roadways. Driver Ed Budd described the conditions as beautiful until the rain arrived, at which point he observed that everyone seemed to forget how to drive. Despite the challenging weather, most drivers pressed forward with their plans. Driver Jim Macfawn, who said he has considerable driving experience, explained that he simply rolls with the punches when dealing with difficult road conditions. Macfawn also reflected on the scale of holiday travel, expressing surprise at the number of people traveling given current gas prices.

Some drivers experienced very different outcomes on Friday's roads. Carlos Borras and his wife completed a round trip between Orlando and Miami without incident, reporting smooth sailing with no traffic or accidents encountered. Driver Fernando Amado painted a different picture, describing busy conditions that were made worse by the weather. Amado noted that Friday appeared to be the peak day for people heading north, contrary to what some might have expected.

For those still planning to travel, AAA offers guidance on timing. The organization recommends leaving after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to avoid the worst congestion. Saturday's heaviest traffic is expected between 10 a.m., while Sunday will see peak congestion between noon and 6 p.m. According to AAA, your starting point, destination, and departure time all play significant roles in determining your travel experience. With record numbers of vehicles on South Florida roads this weekend, careful planning about when to travel can make a substantial difference in avoiding delays.
