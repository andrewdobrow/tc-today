Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned a significant national recognition by being selected as one of only 10 U.S. communities to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city stands alone as the only Florida municipality chosen for this prestigious honor this year. The award was announced at the National Civic League conference in Denver, where city leaders presented their initiatives focused on strengthening community engagement and collaborative problem-solving.

Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the achievement, stating that "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He credited the award to the city's sustained commitment to revitalization and improving residents' quality of life. "They're seeing the changes; they're seeing the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do," Lawson said. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson emphasized that the honor reflects the community itself. "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents," Miller-Anderson said. "Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

The All-America City Award recognizes communities that unite residents, government, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and drive lasting improvements. Riviera Beach's application highlighted three key programs demonstrating its commitment to civic participation. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in leadership and advocacy work. The Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education program, maintains a 98 percent completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program provides mini-grants enabling longtime homeowners to make essential repairs. Miller-Anderson emphasized the city's broader philosophy: "We're doing a lot of innovative things to make sure that our community is able to say that they are part of what's happening here in the city."

The All-America City Award, presented annually since 1949, ranks among the nation's most respected civic honors. City leaders view this designation as validation of their progress while positioning Riviera Beach for future growth and opportunities. Miller-Anderson noted that the award "brings back community pride" and reinforces positive momentum. Officials believe the recognition signals to the nation that Riviera Beach represents a place where collaboration generates results and residents remain actively engaged in determining their community's direction. The nine other 2026 award recipients include Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

A dispute over a missing fence at a Juno Beach public beach access point has created friction between condo residents and beachgoers, with potential solutions carrying a significant price tag for the town. Residents of the Colony Condos have complained that people accessing the JB-7 public walkway have been cutting across their private property since the town removed the fence in 2023 for safety reasons. With only bushes now marking the boundary between the public path and private land, trespassers have easy access to the residential area.

Colony resident Lori Hill described the problem as especially severe during summer months. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." Hill noted that when the fence was in place, residents had a clear boundary to point out to violators. Without it, confrontations have become routine, forcing residents to call police to address trespassers on their property.

Not all beachgoers see a problem. Michael House, who walks to JB-7 from a nearby public parking area, said he has not witnessed the incidents residents describe. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said, adding that he has never seen anyone crossing the shrub line.

The issue was brought to the Juno Beach Town Council, where officials clarified the town is not legally required to install a fence. However, Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring lower-cost alternatives to a full fence replacement. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. Hill expressed hope that the town would act on the concern. "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking," she said. The council decided to address the fence question during budget discussions scheduled for July 15.

Misty Dawn White, 46, was found dead Monday after a fire broke out inside her home in Hobe Sound. Preliminary findings from the sheriff's office point to an electrical issue as a possible cause. The fire remains under investigation. Her fiancé Mark is now rebuilding his life after losing both his home and his future wife in the tragedy.

Nina Fenn, who had been friends with White for eight years, learned of the death through a phone call from Mark. "We received a call from Mark stating that Misty had passed away in a fire," Fenn said. She later returned to the scene of the fire in the days following the tragedy to process her grief. "It's just a terrible shame and awful loss of life," Fenn said. Friends have since launched a fundraiser to help Mark rebuild after the flames destroyed many of his and White's belongings. "It's completely a state of devastation," Fenn said.

Fenn remembered White as a devoted Christian woman with many passions. "Misty was a Christian woman. She loved gardening and plants. She plays the piano," Fenn said. "She was always the first to lend a hand. She loves kids; she loves her kids. She loves people." The loss carries additional weight for Mark, who has already experienced profound tragedy. "Mark lost his one and only child, Jacob, and lost him tragically. So, I just am trying whatever I can to support him," Fenn said.

The timing of White's death adds further heartbreak for those who knew her. "Misty's birthday is the Fourth of July. So, she passed just a few days before her birthday," Fenn said. Despite the devastating loss, Fenn said White's memory will remain with those close to her. "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts," Fenn said. The community continues to rally around Mark during this difficult time as he faces the dual grief of losing his fiancée and rebuilding his home.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football team is aiming for a state championship this fall after finishing last season with a 10-2 record and capturing their district title. Despite the strong regular season, a second-round playoff loss has motivated the program to push harder toward deeper postseason success in 2026.

Head coach Jimmy Robertson credits the strength of his senior class as a major advantage heading into the season. "Most of these seniors are four-year seniors. I mean, if not the majority of them came from the middle school as well. So they know how things are run. They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had," Robertson said. The veteran leadership from players who have progressed through the program's ranks since middle school provides a foundation of experience and continuity.

To prepare for a more competitive playoff run, the Scots have intentionally strengthened their schedule for the upcoming season. Senior Jack Williams explained the reasoning behind the tougher matchups, saying "We knew that we wanted to take our program to the next level. So we really have a challenging schedule for this year because we wanna compete. That's what we wanna do and we wanna take that next level, and it's by playing against really good teams that we do that and attract new people." This approach aims to both prepare the team for stronger opponents in the playoffs and draw recruits interested in competing against quality programs.

For the Scots, the ultimate measure of success this season centers on postseason advancement rather than the regular season record. Senior Jack Whelche emphasized this priority, noting "A better year is just gonna be going farther in the playoffs. Obviously we wanna go undefeated in the season, but that doesn't matter if we keep losing in the second round of the playoffs. We just gotta go farther than that, that's all that matters." The team is using their demanding summer training regimen as motivation for the work ahead. Senior Edmyer St. Jean described how the daily workouts provide purpose and direction, saying "It just adds up every day as something like to look forward to. Work harder so we can see the results when we come off the season positive and break our record." With a veteran senior class leading the charge through intensive summer preparation, the Scots believe they have the pieces in place to transform their district success into a state championship run.

Jun 30, 11:20 AM ET

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment.

The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups encourage beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

Yesterday, 6:16 PM ET

The warehouse retailer is planning to open its first Treasure Coast store in Port St. Lucie. The store would be the first Costco location serving St. Lucie County and the broader Treasure Coast region, giving local residents access to Costco's warehouse shopping without traveling to other counties.

No specific timeline or location details for the Port St. Lucie Costco have been disclosed. However, the planned store comes as Port St. Lucie pursues major infrastructure improvements to support the city's continued growth. The city is currently working on expanding Midway Road and adding a partial interchange to the Florida Turnpike, projects designed to improve accessibility and reduce congestion in the area.

Port St. Lucie City Council recently approved the first reading of an ordinance to donate 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to help facilitate the Turnpike interchange. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, the interchange addresses an important gap in the current system. "There's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie," Samples said. He emphasized that the interchange is about keeping up with the city's expansion. "This is a way for us to try to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years," Samples said.

The $95 million Midway Road Widening Project is advancing to support this growth. Crews are currently working on the first phase, which widens the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027. FDOT is also improving the Turnpike bridge and designing the partial interchange, which is still in the design phase. The Midway Road project will also add bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks to improve safety and accessibility. City council will vote on final approval to donate the land at an upcoming meeting.

The Fourth of July tradition of cookouts, grilling and picnics comes with a higher price tag this year. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, a holiday meal for 10 now costs $73.82, up 4% from a year ago. The survey points to inflation as the driver behind increased costs. Ground beef is up 5.5%, and buns are up 7.7%. Strawberries, hit hard by a Florida frost, are up 12.4%. Ice cream is up 5.3%.

Not all cookout staples have become more expensive. Potato prices are down this year, offering savings for potato chip and salad lovers. Corn prices have fluctuated, but locally grown corn from the Glades can offer the best deal. Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends shopping strategically to keep costs in check. She said families should compare stores to see who has the best deals for groceries on their list and cut down food waste by beginning with a meal plan.
