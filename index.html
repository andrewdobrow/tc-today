Women in Palm Beach County can access free mammogram screenings at two upcoming events hosted by Promise Fund, a South Florida nonprofit dedicated to improving breast and cervical cancer survivorship. The organization will hold screening days on July 11 in Jupiter and July 12 in West Palm Beach. These events are made possible through partnerships with Florida Mobile Mammography, HealthyMe, the Health Care District of Palm Beach County, Origin Church, the Florida Department of Health and Monarch Health Services.

The first screening event takes place July 11 starting at 8 a.m. at a location on Indiantown Road in Jupiter. The second event is scheduled for July 12 beginning at 9 a.m. in West Palm Beach. Appointments are required to participate in either event. Women interested in attending can register by calling 877-427-7664.

Lissette Lahoz, Promise Fund's Palm Beach County navigator, emphasized the importance of these free screening opportunities. She stated, "The Promise Fund is here to make screenings accessible for everyone, especially those who might otherwise go without care." Lahoz added that early detection can significantly impact treatment outcomes, saying, "Early detection can make a real difference. Treatment is often easier to manage, and people are able to focus more on their health, their families, and their everyday lives."

Promise Fund serves uninsured, underinsured, and underserved women throughout Palm Beach, Broward and Martin counties. The organization has reached more than 145,000 women across South Florida through education and advocacy efforts. To date, more than 27,000 women have accessed screenings, diagnostics and care through Promise Fund's programs, and the nonprofit has provided assistance to 286 women diagnosed with cancer as they navigate treatment. Promise Fund currently operates four co-located screening programs in Palm Beach and Broward counties and is working to establish a similar program in Martin County.

A $1 million federal grant will help fund a major sewage system upgrade in Indian River County, Congressman Mike Haridopolos announced at an afternoon press conference Wednesday. The Member Community Project Funding will go toward a $20.8 million project to retrofit the sewage system in the Ixora Park community, affecting over 230 homes that currently rely on a failing sewer system that risks contaminating environmental resources. The community sits adjacent to the County South Relief Canal, which discharges into the Indian River Lagoon, one of the most diverse estuaries in the country.

Haridopolos said federal support for the project ensures health and safety to the community by rehabilitating infrastructure for over 230 homes while protecting a designated federal resource, the Indian River Lagoon. The project will replace aging sewer infrastructure and is designed to protect water quality by reducing discharges of nitrogen and phosphorus into the lagoon. Keith Drewett of the Clean Water Coalition of Indian River County noted that the upgrade is critical because of the area's environmental conditions, stating "The water table is so high, there's so much rain that's all just flowing underground through the subsurface." Haridopolos said the benefits extend beyond environmental protection, noting that replacing the aging infrastructure improves not only water quality but also the taxable value of homes.

A 19-year-old West Palm Beach resident has been arrested after allegedly impersonating a parking official and defrauding visitors on Worth Avenue. According to Palm Beach police, Ti'Miyah De'Asia Allen posed as a representative of both ParkMobile and the Palm Beach Parking Authority while conducting an illegal scheme targeting drivers in the area.

The scam worked by offering unsuspecting victims a discounted parking rate. Allen would approach people either before they paid for parking or while they were in the process of paying. She would tell them they could pay her approximately $1 instead of the legitimate rate of $5 per hour plus a 40-cent transaction fee. To appear credible, Allen carried what looked like a ParkMobile payment device and displayed a fake badge. According to police, the victims targeted in these encounters were predominantly over 65 years old. When victims provided their credit card information, fraudulent charges appeared on their accounts. In at least one case documented in an arrest affidavit from June 5, a victim experienced a $2,500 unauthorized charge to a company called Health Aid.

Beyond the direct harm to individual victims, police say the scheme also deprived the town of legitimate parking revenue that should have been collected through proper channels. The fraudulent transactions represent money that would otherwise have gone to the municipality's parking fund.

This is not Allen's first brush with law enforcement involving financial schemes. Police say she was involved in a similar operation last year while employed at a Wawa location on Belvedere Road. During that incident, she allegedly voided customer transactions for personal profit. Allen now faces three charges related to the Worth Avenue scheme: falsely impersonating an officer, organized scheme to defraud, and unlawful use of a badge or other indicia of authority. The combination of charges reflects the deliberate nature of her deception and the coordinated effort to present herself as an official authority figure while stealing from vulnerable residents and visitors.

Attorney General Ashley Moody and Senator Rick Scott have announced that Florida will receive nearly $1 billion in federal funding for military installations across the state. The $935 million allocation comes through the Senate National Defense Authorization Act, which was approved this year. The funding will support military personnel, installations, and defense industrial bases throughout Florida. Once President Donald Trump signs the legislation, the money will be implemented in the federal 2027 fiscal year budget.

Moody emphasized the personal significance of the funding to her as a member of a military family. "Supporting our armed forces is not just my job — as a mom in a military family, it is personal to me. These wins are not just allocated dollars; they are a down payment on the future of our military in Florida, aimed at strengthening the state's installations and defense industrial base," she said. Serving on the Senate Armed Services Committee, Moody stated that she has worked to ensure military readiness and support from day one. Scott highlighted Florida's strategic military importance, noting that the state hosts more than 20 military installations and three combatant commands. "I am pleased to see so many critical provisions have been included in this year's version of the bill that will help Florida, our warfighters, and our national security," Scott said.

The funding will support numerous critical projects across Florida's military infrastructure. Cape Canaveral Space Force Station will receive support for the Eastern Range, the nation's busiest launch site, including a new command facility, base support and communications warehouses, security forces, vehicle maintenance facilities, and a missile defense test facility. Tyndall Air Force Base in Panama City will receive funding for gate projects and a fire and crash rescue station. Eglin Air Force Base in Fort Walton Beach will get funding to enhance recruit training capacity and design funding for a hangar and physical fitness complex. Homestead Air Force Reserve Base in South Florida will receive a climate-controlled tactical storage warehouse.

Additional projects include upgrades to First Coast military installations. Naval Air Station Jacksonville will add a power generation grid, while Camp Blanding's Florida National Guard installation will add a multipurpose training range. The Marine Corps Support Facility on Blount Island will modernize its communications infrastructure. Naval Air Station Key West will receive funding to construct a command and control facility.

Hillsborough County faces $343 million budget impact from property tax reform proposal

County officials warn that a property tax overhaul headed to the November ballot could force cuts to public safety, libraries and other services.

5:30 PM ET

Hillsborough County could face a $213 million hit to its countywide general fund and another $130 million squeeze in its unincorporated area fund if voters approve a proposed property tax overhaul in the November 2026 General Election. Budget Director Tom Fesler warned County Commissioners on Wednesday that the proposal would create a $150,000 homestead exemption in Fiscal Year 2027, affecting Hillsborough's Fiscal Year 2028 budget, before increasing to $250,000 the following year. The proposal would also reduce the assessment cap on non-homesteaded properties from 10 percent to 5 percent and require five years of Florida residency to qualify for the expanded exemption. Fesler said about 66 percent of the countywide general fund already goes toward constitutional officers, outside agencies and fire rescue, including the Sheriff. If the proposal passes, ad valorem revenue would no longer cover those services entirely, likely forcing the county to look to alternate revenues.

The library district, which funds Hillsborough's public library system, would see an estimated $21 million impact from the proposed changes. Fesler said the loss in funding would likely affect future library projects, as ad valorem revenues have historically been used to replace and build new libraries. County staff outlined possible options including using reserves, freezing compensation or hiring, reducing nonprofit funding, creating special assessment districts, cutting service hours, raising fees or finding new revenue sources. One possibility would be a public service tax on utility bills, which Fesler estimated could generate about $80 million to $90 million if levied at 10 percent. The constitutional amendment would require 60 percent voter approval.

Florida Attorney General affirms parental rights over religious instruction in schools

Attorney General James Uthmeier issued an opinion stating that public schools must respect and accommodate parental decisions about religious instruction.

8:39 PM ET

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier issued an opinion reaffirming that parents, not government, bear the primary responsibility for directing the education and religious upbringing of their children. The opinion addressed release time religious instruction programs in response to a question raised by Senator Clay Yarborough. Uthmeier concluded that when parents choose to add religious instruction as part of their child's education, public schools must respect and accommodate that decision.

Release time religious instruction programs allow students to leave campus during part of the school day with parental permission to receive religious instruction off school property. The U.S. Supreme Court upheld such programs more than 70 years ago in Zorach v. Clauson, recognizing that accommodating religion reflects the best of the nation's traditions. However, some school districts have erected unnecessary barriers to these programs in recent years, refusing to cooperate or imposing restrictions that make participation difficult or impossible. Jeremy Dys, Senior Counsel and Chair of the Education Practice Group for First Liberty Institute, wrote that Uthmeier's opinion rightly recognizes that school districts in Florida cannot frustrate parental decisions simply because administrators prefer a different approach. Public education should complement parental authority, not compete with it.

Indian River County sewage system in Ixora Park gets $1 million federal grant

The federal funding will help pay for a $20.8 million project to retrofit the sewage system in the Ixora Park community, affecting over 230 homes that currently rely on a failing sewer system.

A few minutes ago

A $1 million federal grant will help fund a major sewage system upgrade in Indian River County, Congressman Mike Haridopolos announced at an afternoon press conference Wednesday. The Member Community Project Funding will go toward a $20.8 million project to retrofit the sewage system in the Ixora Park community, affecting over 230 homes that currently rely on a failing sewer system that risks contaminating environmental resources. Haridopolos said the federal support ensures health and safety to the community by rehabilitating infrastructure while protecting the Indian River Lagoon, a designated federal resource.

The Clean Water Coalition says the upgrade is critical because of the area's environmental conditions, with high water tables and heavy rain flowing underground through the subsurface. The project will replace aging sewer infrastructure and reduce discharges of nitrogen and phosphorus into the lagoon while also improving the taxable value of homes in the area.

Jupiter approves fire assessment fee for new fire rescue department starting Oct. 1

Jupiter's town council approved a fire assessment fee to help fund the town's new fire rescue department, which begins service Oct. 1. The fee will cover about 8% of the total cost of the department.

3:52 PM ET

Jupiter's town council has approved a fire assessment fee to fund the town's new fire rescue department, which begins service Oct. 1. The per-property charge will cover about 8% of the total cost of the department. Property taxes will fund 84% of the department, with the remaining 8% collected through EMS transport fees. The assessment will replace the current county fire fee that appears on residents' tax bills.

Finance Director Scott Reynolds said the move represents significant savings for property owners, citing a $10 million savings compared to initial projections from three years ago. Mayor Jim Kuretski was the lone no vote on the funding structure, raising concerns about how the assessment is calculated across different property types. Single family homes are being assessed based on total square footage including garages and porches, while townhomes and condos are not. The town sent letters to 30,000 homes explaining the fee and has established an online calculator where residents can see their potential savings. Town of Jupiter spokesperson Mariana DiNunzio acknowledged the change has caused some confusion because it is new and the town has not had a non ad-valorem assessment before.

Jupiter surgeon uses opioid-free recovery protocol for joint replacement patients

Andrew Noble, an orthopedic surgeon at Jupiter Medical Center, is using an opioid-free protocol to help patients recover from joint replacement surgery.

6:16 PM ET

Andrew Noble, an orthopedic surgeon at Jupiter Medical Center, is taking a different approach to post-operative pain management by helping patients recover from joint replacement surgery without opioids. He uses the Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocol combined with his own proprietary method. The approach combines robotics for minimally invasive surgery, targeted numbing, and a timed schedule of non-opioid medications taken before, during, and after surgery. "Instead of chasing pain, it's a way of trying to stay ahead of pain, and have less pain afterwards with less need for opioids," Noble said.

Patient April Stubbs recovered from knee replacement surgery without opioids using Noble's approach. "I never had to touch the more serious pain medications. The opioids, never even had to take any," Stubbs said. Daniel Warren, Chief Marketing Officer at Reprieve Recovery in Jupiter, noted that patients are often unaware of the physical effects of opioid medications taken during surgical recovery and can experience withdrawals without being educated about it. Noble said the goal is straightforward: "Get patients through a surgery, better pain control, and having them with a quick recovery and satisfied results without the opioid exposure." Patients can ask their doctor if the ERAS protocol is an option for them.
