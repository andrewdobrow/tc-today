Residents at Colony Condos say the removal of a fence at the JB-7 public beach walkway has created a trespassing problem on their private property. The town took down the fence in 2023 for safety reasons, leaving only bushes to mark the boundary between the public access path and the condo's private land. Since then, residents have reported increased foot and vehicle traffic crossing onto their property during peak summer months.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, described the scope of the problem. She said cars frequently park in private parking spots and beachgoers push through the landscaping with coolers, fishing rods and lawn chairs to access the beach. Hill explained that residents previously tried confronting trespassers directly but found people became hostile in response. Now they call police to handle the situation. She said the fence previously made the property boundary clear and helped prevent these incidents. Residents are hoping the town will reinstall it so they can reclaim their landscaping and parking areas.

The issue was brought before the Juno Beach Town Council, where officials acknowledged the town has no legal obligation to install a fence. However, Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring more affordable options. She proposed investigating whether the town could help pay for a lower-cost solution, such as a split rail fence combined with shrubs. The council agreed to discuss the fence question during its budget deliberations scheduled for July 15.

Not all beachgoers perceive the situation as problematic. Michael House, a regular beachgoer who walks to JB-7 from a public parking lot blocks away, said he has not witnessed the trespassing issues residents describe. He noted that free parking is available and suggested that property damage and littering might be greater concerns than unauthorized foot traffic. His perspective highlights the different experiences residents and visitors have regarding the access point and its impact on the surrounding area.

She said numerous vehicles park randomly in private spots, and people push through bushes to access the beach with coolers, fishing rods, and lawn chairs. Before the fence was removed, Hill explained, the boundary between public and private property was clearly marked. Now without that visual barrier, confrontations between residents and trespassers have become frequent. Rather than engaging directly with intruders, residents have taken to calling police to handle the situations. Hill expressed hope that the town would reinstall the fence to restore order and protect their landscaping and private parking areas.

Not all beachgoers view the situation as problematic. Michael House, who walks to JB-7 from a nearby public parking lot, said he has not observed the trespassing issues residents describe. He noted that free parking is available in the area and suggested that property damage and littering might be separate concerns worth addressing. His perspective contrasts with the residents' account of regular boundary violations.

At a recent town council meeting, a municipal official clarified that the town has no legal obligation to install a fence. He proposed that the town might assist the condo association with a lower-cost fence option or a combination approach using split rail fencing mixed with shrubs. Riviera Beach named only Florida city to receive 2026 All-America City Award

Riviera Beach has been selected as one of just 10 communities across the United States to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the only Florida city honored this year. 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has been selected as one of just 10 communities across the United States to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the only Florida city honored this year. The prestigious award recognizes communities that bring together residents, local government, businesses and organizations to address local challenges and create meaningful change. City leaders presented Riviera Beach's civic engagement efforts during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where the selection was announced.

Mayor Douglas Lawson described the recognition as a proud moment for the city, saying "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." He explained that the city earned the award through sustained efforts to revitalize Riviera Beach and enhance quality of life for residents. "They're seeing the changes; they're seeing the revitalization and the reimagining of Riviera Beach that we set out so many years to do," Lawson said. City leaders emphasized that the honor reflects years of work focused on improving civic health, strengthening trust between residents and government, and creating opportunities for the community to participate directly in shaping the city's future.

Riviera Beach's application showcased several programs designed to increase civic participation and neighborhood investment. The Riviera Beach Youth Council helps high school students become involved in civic leadership and advocacy, while the Citizens Leadership Academy provides civic education with a 98 percent completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program provides mini-grants to help longtime homeowners make critical repairs. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson highlighted the broader commitment behind these initiatives, stating "We're doing a lot of innovative things to make sure that our community is able to say that they are part of what's happening here in the city." She credited residents for the honor, saying "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents. Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

The All-America City Award has been presented annually since 1949 and is considered one of the nation's most respected civic honors. Being named an All-America City signals to the nation that Riviera Beach is a place where collaboration produces results and residents remain deeply engaged in shaping their community's future. City officials say the designation celebrates the city's progress while positioning it for future opportunities and continued growth. The other nine cities receiving the award are Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

The Saint Andrew's Scots football team is working toward a state championship during the 2026 fall season following a strong 2025 campaign that produced a 10-2 record and district title. Despite the successful regular season, a second-round playoff loss motivated the program to aim higher. Head coach Jimmy Robertson emphasized that the team has the leadership in place to accomplish big goals this year.

Robertson highlighted the strength of the senior class, noting that most are four-year players who came through the middle school program together. "Most of these seniors are four-year seniors. I mean, if not the majority of them came from the middle school as well. So they know how things are run. They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had," Robertson said. The continuity and experience within the group positions them as anchors for the program's ambitions.

To elevate the program, the Scots intentionally scheduled more competitive opponents for the upcoming season. Senior Jack Williams explained that playing stronger teams is essential for growth and gaining recognition. "We knew that we wanted to take our program to the next level. So we really have a challenging schedule for this year because we wanna compete. That's what we wanna do and we wanna take that next level, and it's by playing against really good teams that we do that and attract new people," Williams said. The challenging slate represents a deliberate strategy rather than happenstance.

For the Scots, measuring success this year means more than winning games during the regular season. Senior Jack Whelche stressed that playoff advancement is the true marker of improvement. "A better year is just gonna be going farther in the playoffs. Obviously we wanna go undefeated in the season, but that doesn't matter if we keep losing in the second round of the playoffs. We just gotta go farther than that, that's all that matters," Whelche said. Senior Edmyer St. Jean credited summer workouts as a daily source of motivation, stating that the demanding preparation schedule drives the team to work harder and achieve new records when the season begins.

2:38 PM ET

St. Petersburg Mayor Ken Welch has chosen The Burg Bid as the city's preferred redevelopment plan for Tropicana Field and the Historic Gas Plant District. Led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, the $8.1 billion proposal centers on transforming stadium parking and public land into a mixed-use district featuring housing, hotels, offices, retail, cultural institutions, and entertainment venues. The developers would purchase 58 acres of city-owned land for $275 million, with the full land package valued at $405 million when including $130 million in land the city would retain. A $20 million deposit would be required when the purchase and sale agreement is executed.

Unlike previous redevelopment proposals built around a new stadium, The Burg Bid organizes the district around Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre civic and cultural space within 18.5 acres of total public park area. The park would house The Woodson African American Museum of Florida, a Museum of Public Art, a Discovery Center, event lawns, a playground, a splash pad, and a concert lawn. The Woodson museum would serve as an anchor for the district, positioned along a diagonal pedestrian corridor called the Legacy Link that connects City Hall with the Manhattan Casino and the Deuces District. The plan also includes a surf wave attraction, recreation deck, multimodal transportation hub, workforce and small-business center, and Resilience Event Center. CEO Thompson Whitney Blake noted in the proposal that the team designed "an evocative, architecturally jaw dropping permanent home for the Woodson African American Museum of Florida" and created the Legacy Link "that connects City Hall with the Manhattan Casino and the Deuces District."

The proposal heavily emphasizes the history of the former Gas Plant neighborhood, a predominantly Black community demolished decades ago to build Tropicana Field. The plan preserves the original gas tank footprint within Samuel Davis Park and incorporates historic references throughout the Legacy Link. Nine bridges are planned over Booker Creek, echoing the nine bridges that once stood in the historic Gas Plant area. The proposal also calls for restoring Booker Creek itself, which had been reduced to a drainage ditch between parking lots.

The plan includes 3,600 income-restricted housing units over a 15-year buildout, with 1,800 units in the Historic Gas Plant District and 1,800 on complementary sites elsewhere in St. Petersburg. Blue Sky Communities serves as the affordable housing partner. The development would occur in three phases of approximately five years each, with early construction potentially beginning outside the Tropicana Field footprint before stadium demolition. Phase 1 would establish cultural and workforce anchors, Phase 2 would develop the mixed-income neighborhood, and Phase 3 would complete the district. The Rays' lease with the city ends after the 2028 season.

As the Fourth of July weekend began, hundreds of boaters launched into Martin County waterways, prompting local maritime response teams to gear up for the holiday surge. Lt. Brett Harry with the Martin County Maritime Response Unit said his team would position two boats with medics on standby across the county, with one unit covering the north section and another handling the south. The unit anticipates a busy weekend as residents take to the water to celebrate.

The maritime response teams are prepared to handle a wide range of emergencies that may occur on the water. According to Lt. Harry, their scope includes everything from minor incidents like stubbed toes to more serious medical events such as cardiac emergencies and potential spinal injuries. The teams will patrol Martin County waters throughout the holiday weekend to assist any boaters in distress or need of emergency response.

Among those heading out on the water Friday was the Shannon family, who launched from Sandsprit Park in Martin County with their young daughter and puppy. Brendan and Ashley Shannon emphasized that safety remains their top priority when boating. Their approach reflects the kind of responsible preparation that Lt. Harry encourages all boaters to practice during busy holiday weekends.

Lt. Harry stressed several critical safety measures for anyone planning to boat during the Fourth of July celebrations. He urged boaters to keep their focus on the water at all times and to designate a sober driver before launching. Additionally, he recommended that all boats be equipped with life vests, whistles and other flotation devices. By taking these precautions and following basic safety guidelines, boaters can help ensure a safe and enjoyable holiday weekend on Martin County waterways. "Just have fun and be safe," Harry said, capturing the message that emergency responders want residents to remember as they celebrate on the water.

Costco has announced plans to open its first store on the Treasure Coast in Port St. Lucie, bringing its membership-based warehouse shopping model to St. Lucie County for the first time. The warehouse retailer operates stores that require paid memberships and offer bulk merchandise, groceries, and other products at discounted prices. This location will serve the three-county Treasure Coast region, which previously had no Costco presence.

The arrival of Costco to Port St. Lucie comes as the city undergoes significant infrastructure improvements to support growth. The city is working on a major $95 million project to widen Midway Road and add a partial interchange to Florida's Turnpike. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, these improvements are designed to address transportation challenges. "There's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie," Samples said. The interchange project aims to improve accessibility while preparing for future development over the next 20 to 50 years.

The Midway Road Widening Project is currently in its first phase, expanding the road from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, which will widen the road from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027. The project also includes improvements to the Turnpike bridge and a partial interchange, both still in design phases. The city council has already approved the first reading of an ordinance to grant 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to support the interchange. Additional improvements include new bike and pedestrian paths, along with sidewalks throughout the project area.

Local residents have noticed the ongoing construction but recognize its importance. Port St. Lucie resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged that while construction can be disruptive, the expansion is necessary for the community. "We definitely needed it to be expanded," she said. "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." These infrastructure investments, combined with Costco's arrival, signal Port St. Lucie's continued growth and development as a commercial hub on the Treasure Coast.
