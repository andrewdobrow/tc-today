DeSantis targets Medicaid fraud in Southeast Florida with new state enforcement measures

Gov. Ron DeSantis announced comprehensive new Medicaid fraud prevention measures Friday afternoon in West Palm Beach, targeting what state officials describe as sophisticated criminal operations that have drained millions of dollars from Florida's healthcare program. The governor said the state is partnering with the Trump administration and federal agencies to combat fraud tha...

9:42 AM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis announced comprehensive new Medicaid fraud prevention measures Friday afternoon in West Palm Beach, targeting what state officials describe as sophisticated criminal operations that have drained millions of dollars from Florida's healthcare program. The governor said the state is partnering with the Trump administration and federal agencies to combat fraud that is particularly concentrated in southeast Florida, especially in Palm Beach, Miami-Dade, and Broward counties. "Southeast Florida is one of those fraud hotspots in the United States," DeSantis said, noting that while other regions may experience worse problems, all three counties remain vulnerable to organized theft from the Medicaid system.

Shevaun L. Harris, secretary of the Agency for Healthcare Administration, detailed how investigators uncovered more than a dozen fake Medicaid providers operating in West Palm Beach. These entities, created solely to steal from the state program, were discovered through shared addresses across multiple unrelated businesses, suspicious ownership structures, and a complete absence of actual patient care. Harris characterized the problem bluntly: "This is not a paperwork issue or breakdown in process. This is fraud, plain and simple." She added a direct warning to potential fraudsters: "We have more tools, more investigators, and more resolve than ever. Find another state, because our state is closed to fraud."

The state rolled out three major initiatives to address the problem. First, AHCA is launching a pilot program with Ventolin, an identity fraud detection company that uses technology to identify stolen and synthetic identities and hidden ownership structures before criminals can exploit the system. The technology processes data at speeds that traditional tools cannot match, flagging suspicious activity within minutes rather than days. Second, the state has imposed enrollment freezes on high-risk provider categories, including durable medical equipment suppliers and adult day care providers. DeSantis criticized the previous approach of paying fraudulent claims first and investigating them years later. Third, AHCA is requiring all active Medicaid providers to revalidate their credentials and identity, with removal from the program for those who fail to respond.

Harris reported that Florida's fraud prevention efforts have already recovered and prevented 136.45 million dollars in fraudulent spending. In the past two years alone, AHCA has terminated or denied enrollment to more than 3,200 providers. DeSantis credited Dr. Mehmet Oz at the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services for supporting the initiative, while the state also coordinated with Attorney General James Uthmeier's Medicaid Fraud Control Unit through enhanced information sharing. Florida's Medicaid program serves nearly 4 million recipients statewide. The announcement reflects DeSantis's emphasis on fiscal responsibility, as Florida has achieved four consecutive years of state budget reductions while maintaining essential services.

Yesterday, 5:40 PM ET

New data from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development reveals significant rental cost variations across South Florida counties. Indian River County's Fair Market Rent for one-bedroom apartments stands at $1,222, positioning it in the mid-range of regional rates. Comparable figures for nearby areas include Okeechobee County at $1,032 and St. Lucie and Martin areas at $1,467. These figures demonstrate the substantial pricing differences that exist even within the same region of Florida.

Real estate broker Talbot Sutter, president of Sutter and Nugent Real Estate, attributes rental price variations to specific market factors. "A lot of it has to do with school districts, especially Wellington. They have like top A-rated schools, lifestyle — they have so many aspects from equestrian to outdoor activities," Sutter said. Location advantages and available amenities directly influence what landlords can charge and what renters must pay. Sutter works primarily with high-end clients, often executives relocating from New York City, who lease luxury properties before making permanent purchase decisions. He notes that demand for properties with more acreage and land has increased across the rental market.

HUD's Fair Market Rent data serves as a tool for understanding current market conditions across different areas. The agency calculates these figures using surveys, rental listings and market adjustments to reflect rates for similar properties in each region. However, these numbers do not function as rent control limits or restrict what landlords can charge tenants. Rather, they help government agencies calculate housing assistance program benefits and provide market reference points. The broader South Florida rental market continues reflecting significant affordability challenges as residents attempt to balance income growth against rapidly climbing living costs.

For renters seeking to navigate these competitive markets, Sutter recommends conducting thorough research before committing to a lease. "Know the market, do your comps. You have to really, really know the area if you don't want to get ripped off paying rent," Sutter said. Understanding comparable rental prices in specific neighborhoods and ZIP codes can help potential tenants identify fair rates and avoid overpaying. This data provides essential information for renters, policymakers and housing advocates working to address the regional affordability gaps that continue affecting South Florida communities.

1:07 PM ET

The Smithsonian Marine Ecosystems Exhibit at the St. Lucie County Aquarium invites visitors to experience Florida's underwater world through live, working displays of coastal habitats. Located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce, the facility showcases marine life in settings that recreate actual ocean ecosystems rather than displaying animals in isolation. Manager Bill Hoffman explained that this approach to exhibiting fully functional ecosystem models has been the facility's cornerstone since 1980, distinguishing it from most public aquariums. "We feel like we're the best kept secret on the Treasure Coast, and we don't want to be," Hoffman told WPTV. The Fort Pierce location houses more species than many larger aquarium facilities across the country.

The centerpiece of the exhibit is the Atlantic coral reef tank, a large display that brings one of Florida's most important coastal ecosystems to life. Beyond the coral reef, visitors encounter live recreations of seagrass beds, mangrove habitats, and hardbottom communities. Each display demonstrates how different organisms depend on and interact with one another within their environment. Even smaller details capture attention, such as the facility's four-inch barracuda, which shows the diversity of marine life at different life stages.

A major draw for guests is the interactive touch tank, where visitors can handle marine species under the supervision of trained staff members. Exhibit educator Chelsie Corcoran noted that guests will likely encounter animals they have never touched before. This hands-on experience allows people of all ages to develop a personal connection with marine life.

The exhibit operates in partnership with the Smithsonian Marine Station and focuses on educating the public about ocean conservation and environmental threats. Hoffman stressed that healthy ecosystems depend on numerous small organisms working together, a concept that the live displays help visitors understand. The facility remains open to the public year-round and plans upcoming programming tied to its 25th anniversary celebration later this year. For more information, visitors can check sms.si.edu and the Smithsonian Marine Station's social media channels. By bringing Florida's coastal habitats to visitors in their home region, the exhibit connects people to the importance and fragility of these marine environments.

2:06 PM ET

Ron DeSantis is challenging the Republican Party of Florida's decision to cancel a planned debate at the upcoming Sunshine Showdown event. DeSantis questioned why the party should be involved in structuring debates at all, arguing that candidates and media should drive the process instead.

DeSantis said the party announced it would hold a debate but never publicly released the qualifying criteria or held a vote to establish requirements. According to what he heard secondhand, the criteria included polling at 10% support in the party's own survey, raising more than $10 million, and securing more than 10,000 donors. DeSantis pointed out that he would not have met these standards when he ran for governor in 2018, arguing that trying to engineer outcomes through such requirements is counterproductive. Since Representative Byron Donalds meets the threshold, the party chose to allow candidates to speak individually rather than participate in a debate format, despite calls from Lieutenant Jay Collins and former House Speaker Paul Renner for a traditional debate.

DeSantis emphasized that an open process where candidates can directly address voters is superior to attempting to predetermine results. He stressed that the party should prioritize what benefits Republican voters rather than pursuing its own agenda or answering to other interests. He called it ridiculous for the party to unilaterally set debate structures, noting that the party's role should be limited. DeSantis argued that debates should be candidate-driven affairs, not controlled by people whom voters have never elected to make such decisions. He suggested that candidates work directly with television stations, radio broadcasters, and other media outlets to arrange multiple opportunities for public appearances and debates.

DeSantis also noted the timing of his criticism, pointing out that qualifying closed at noon on Friday and many voters were not yet actively following the race. His comments highlight a broader debate within Republican circles about how primary elections should be structured and who should control the process of determining which candidates get significant media exposure before voters cast their ballots.

Jim Moir, executive director of the Indian Riverkeeper, recommended natural solutions including planting aquatic vegetation, reducing fertilizer use, and removing sediment that fuels algae growth. "We're going to end up in a cycle that we can't break unless we go back to square one and start with a natural remediation," Moir said.

Lawsuit challenges Florida property tax ballot language as biased ahead of November vote

A lawsuit filed Thursday in Leon County challenges the property tax amendment ballot language approved by Florida lawmakers for November, arguing it uses campaign-style phrases and overstates what the proposal would accomplish.

Yesterday, 2:27 PM ET

A lawsuit filed Thursday in Leon County challenges the ballot language for a property tax amendment Florida lawmakers approved for the November 2026 General Election. The complaint, filed by a voter advocacy group and two registered voters, argues the language is "biased" and "misleading," claiming lawmakers used campaign-style phrases including "Save Our Homes From Excessive Property Taxes," "protecting small businesses" and "ensuring fairness." Attorney Jamie Cole, representing the plaintiffs, said ballot amendments should be neutral and accurate. "A ballot amendment is supposed to be fair, neutral, and not biased, and it's supposed to be accurate," Cole said. The lawsuit asks a judge to declare the wording unconstitutional and order Attorney General James Uthmeier to rewrite it within 10 days.

The lawsuit disputes several claims in the ballot summary. It challenges language suggesting the amendment guarantees funding for core services like police, firefighters, schools or infrastructure, and disputes claims that it protects small businesses when the amendment does not specifically mention them. The complaint also argues the summary overstates the proposal by suggesting it immediately exempts the first $250,000 of a home's value, when the initial exemption would be $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. On June 2, lawmakers passed legislation establishing this exemption schedule. The amendment requires approval from at least 60 percent of voters to become law.
