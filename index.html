Yesterday, 9:09 AM ET

A record-breaking number of Americans are hitting the roads this Fourth of July weekend, creating significant congestion across South Florida. According to AAA, 72.2 million people are expected to travel at least 50 miles from home between June 27 and July 5, with 61.4 million of those trips made by car. The combination of this unprecedented travel volume and Friday's heavy rainfall has made conditions particularly challenging on major highways like the Florida Turnpike south of Orlando. The wet weather has complicated driving conditions, with one driver commenting that "it starts raining and then everyone forgets how to drive."

Orlando and Miami have emerged as top-five travel destinations for this Independence Day holiday. Despite the crowded roads and adverse weather, many drivers continued with their plans. Some, like Carlos Borras and his wife who completed a round trip between Orlando and Miami on Friday, had surprisingly positive experiences. Borras reported that he "didn't see any traffic, no accidents or anything, so it's been great." However, not all drivers shared this experience. Fernando Amado described busy conditions worsened by the weather, noting that more people seemed to be hitting the road on Friday than he expected.

The timing of travel significantly impacts the driving experience, and AAA has identified specific windows when roads are less congested. The organization recommends that drivers still planning to travel should depart after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to minimize time spent in traffic. The worst congestion is projected between 10 a.m. and noon on Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday. Where travelers are coming from, their destination, and departure time all play crucial roles in determining how smooth their journey will be.

As Americans navigate this record holiday travel period, the sheer scale of movement on the roads is striking. One driver acknowledged the enormity of the situation, expressing surprise that people could manage to afford all the gas needed for such widespread travel. Those who must travel during peak hours should prepare for delays and exercise extra caution, particularly during wet weather conditions. The combination of record-breaking travel numbers and unpredictable weather means South Florida roads will remain congested throughout the holiday weekend.

Last month, the Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance to give 7 acres of land to the Florida Department of Transportation to assist with a new Turnpike interchange. Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, said there is a significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie. The interchange is designed to accommodate growth that has already occurred and growth expected over the next 20 to 50 years.

FDOT is constructing the $95 million Midway Road Widening Project to address congestion and safety. Crews are currently working on the first phase, widening the road from two lanes to four lanes from Selvitz Road to Jenkins Road. The second widening phase from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road is still in the design phase, with construction anticipated to begin in Summer 2027. FDOT is also working on improving the Turnpike bridge and adding a partial interchange, both still in the design phase. The project will also add bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks. City council will vote on final approval to give the land at an upcoming meeting.

Serena Williams withdrew from her doubles match with sister Venus at Wimbledon on Saturday due to a right knee injury sustained during her singles return earlier in the week. The 44-year-old lost to 20-year-old Maya Joint of Australia 6-3, 6-7 (6), 6-3 on Tuesday. "I'm heartbroken to have to withdraw from doubles. Coming back to compete again has been a gift, and the opportunity to play alongside Venus once more meant the world to me. The post included images of four syringes showing fluid drained from her knee after her singles match, along with a video of her walking with strapping on her right leg and what appeared to be one of her daughters holding a cane for her. The Williams sisters were scheduled to play Camila Osorio and Solana Sierra in the only first-round doubles match not scheduled earlier in the week, which had been pushed back to allow Serena more recovery time. Venus, 46, played in the mixed doubles tournament Friday with Kevin Krawietz, losing in straight sets to Tereza Mihalikova and Lloyd Glasspool. The pair are six-time Wimbledon women's doubles champions. With the U.S. Open approaching, Serena hinted at future competition, saying "stay tuned to a city near you."

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn more about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The center's Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations during the programs may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

Peacock House Art Foundation transforms Fort Pierce historic estate into arts destination

The Peacock House Art Foundation has established itself as a cultural hub in Fort Pierce, revitalizing the historic Crain Estate and creating a space where local and international artists converge. The nonprofit organization preserves the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, the original owners who filled their home with treasures collected during nine trips around the world. Krystal Masters, the art director, leads guided tours of the property's lush gardens and distinctive architecture every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, showcasing how the estate remains approximately 90 percent unchanged from the Crains' era. The craftsmanship of Jaxson Crain, a tile worker by trade, is evident throughout the facility, visible on floors, walls, and ceilings. Among the estate's preserved spaces is the smoking and bourbon room, featuring dark wood paneling, masculine color schemes, and decorative stained glass that opens onto a front patio bar. The couple's worldly acquisitions include a gazebo shipped from India, complete with peacock designs carved into its structure.

The peacocks themselves are central to the property's identity and history. The Crains are believed to have introduced the birds to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, starting with seven birds that have multiplied to over 100. Bob, a 60-year-old peacock who walks with a limp, has become something of a celebrity at the estate, receiving daily hand feedings from his own set of bowls. Visitors, particularly children, find memorable moments interacting with the peacocks during tours. The property once housed monkeys as well, with one account describing how Jackson Crain traveled to South America and returned home with a baby monkey tucked in his pocket.

The foundation operates an artist-in-residency program that attracts creative professionals from around the world, offering simple yet elegant accommodations on the property. Masters emphasizes that the foundation has deliberately avoided commercializing the estate while maintaining its original character. The art lab provides classes for children and adults of all skill levels, with most instruction delivered through one-on-one sessions. Beginners arrive with no experience and leave with completed artwork. Masters teaches various mediums including sculpting and watercolor, with workshops like "Paint Your Pet in Watercolor and Rainbow" drawing participants seeking creative outlets.

For Masters, art serves a therapeutic purpose beyond simple creativity. She describes it as a form of stress relief that allows people to disconnect from their devices and leave feeling fulfilled. The foundation's commitment to accessibility means anyone can participate in workshops featuring different artistic techniques and projects. This approach has created a welcoming environment where the historic estate functions as both a cultural destination and a healing space for the community.
