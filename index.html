Martin County Commissioner calls for talks to move Stuart Brightline station away from downtown

6:50 PM ET

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi is asking county staff to begin discussions with Brightline about relocating the planned train station from its current downtown Stuart location. During a public meeting, Ciampi suggested the fairgrounds as a potential alternative site and expressed concerns about parking and other issues at the originally proposed location. "I would ask our staff to begin a conversation and discussions with Brightline leadership for the possibility of changing the location from where it is currently, which creates parking issues and other issues," Ciampi said.

The county and Brightline had previously reached an agreement to build the station on a parking lot across from the courthouse in downtown Stuart. However, Ciampi's proposal follows a letter from the FEC to then-Stuart Mayor Christopher Collins stating the agency does not support a Brightline station in Stuart. The FEC cited a conflict with U.S. Coast Guard regulations, explaining that the proposed station would require the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge to remain down while trains stop. Ciampi indicated this regulatory issue could be resolved by moving the station further south. "If a station is deemed too close by the Army Corps of Engineers or the U.S. Coast Guard or whoever is involved, then it comes off the table for me. If you move it further south to the fairgrounds, that issue comes totally off the table," he said.

Community response to the relocation suggestion has been mixed. Resident Paul Gedwed supported the commissioner's position, calling it a well-reasoned approach given the congestion at the proposed downtown site. Steven Vitale, owner of the Colorado Inn, disagreed with moving the station away from downtown, questioning the logic of building a train station that residents cannot easily walk to. "Most of the people are going to want to go to downtown Stuart, so why would we want to build a train station that people can't walk to," Vitale said. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, expressed less concern about the location, stating her downtown store would benefit either way and urging officials to finalize the decision as long as the station remains in Martin County. "Just get it done, wherever they decide to put it as long as it's in Martin County," Freitas said.

County officials clarified that Ciampi's comments do not represent official direction for staff action. The board must hold a formal discussion and vote before staff can pursue exploring an alternative location. The county continues to await a response to its grant application to fund the Brightline station, which is necessary before the project can advance.

6:15 PM ET

A controversial proposal to build a downtown parking garage in Lake Worth Beach is moving forward after a commission vote during a quasi-judicial hearing Monday night. The nearly $10 million project would transform an existing surface parking lot near K Street and First Avenue South into a four-and-a-half-story structure designed to add hundreds of parking spaces in the downtown core. City leaders say the garage is needed to address growing parking demands as redevelopment continues, though some residents question whether the project is necessary and have raised concerns about potential taxpayer costs.

Vice Mayor Mimi May said the city has been discussing a centralized parking area since the 1960s, and recent parking studies have shown a need for additional parking downtown. Funding sources include city general fund dollars, federal American Rescue Plan Act funds, Community Redevelopment Agency contributions, and a $1 million loan from the Wiener Museum of Decorative Arts. May said everything has been allocated and budgeted with no future taxpayer payments required. The project includes relocating two historic city-owned buildings and adding public art, a mural and a green wall. The proposal heads to a second and final reading on June 30, with construction potentially beginning and the garage opening as early as 2028.

5:31 PM ET

Nick Greene is sharing his enthusiasm for soccer and discussing why America's relationship with the world's most popular sport continues to develop. Greene, who wrote "How to Watch Soccer Like a Genius," examines the game through the perspectives of agronomists, architects, computer scientists and other professionals. "Soccer is one of my favorite sports, if not my favorite sport," Greene said during a recent interview. "You have this game that is the simplest game, you just need a ball and you don't even need grass."

Greene believes Americans are already embracing soccer more than many people recognize. He points to several key moments in the 1990s that sparked domestic interest in the sport. The 1994 World Cup held in the United States, the establishment of Major League Soccer, and the 1999 U.S. Women's World Cup victory all played important roles in growing the sport's popularity at home. These milestones marked turning points in how Americans viewed soccer and created momentum for its expansion.

The sport has undergone significant transformation since its earliest modern era, Greene explained. Soccer evolved from a game focused on individual enjoyment to one emphasizing sophisticated team strategies. "The first modern players in the 1860s and 1870s were aristocratic gentlemen in England, and they weren't used to sharing and they played, as one player famously said, 'purely for my own pleasure,'" Greene said. This shift reflects how the sport has become more collaborative and strategically complex over time, changing both how players approach the game and how fans understand it.

Greene's book takes an innovative approach by incorporating expert perspectives from various fields to help readers appreciate soccer's complexity and appeal. His expert-driven method offers new ways for audiences to recognize tactical elements they might otherwise miss during matches. As soccer continues gaining traction in the United States, understanding the sport's strategic depth becomes increasingly important for American viewers. Greene wonders about soccer's future trajectory in America, questioning whether the goal is maintaining steady growth or elevating soccer to become a major American spectacle. Through his book, Greene hopes readers will develop a deeper appreciation for soccer's nuances and strategic elements that have made the sport compelling to billions of fans worldwide.

The Delray Beach Public Library is providing free STREAM summer programs for kids and teens at its downtown location on Atlantic Avenue and Swinton Avenue. These programs focus on Science, Technology, Reading, Art, Engineering, and Math, and are open to all Florida residents. The library, which has served the community since 1913, welcomes more than 193,000 visitors annually to its 36,000-square-foot facility.

Young adult librarian Natalie Baron leads sessions including ColorSpace: Artistic Excavations, where teens explore art history spanning from cave paintings to ancient pottery. Teens can participate in lab-based activities like collaborative 3D puzzle builds and select themed book boxes. Mandy Fulop, head of the children's department, is launching the STREAM Into Summer Literacy Program. This program encourages young readers to set reading goals with librarians, track their progress, and take home an Unearth a Story bag that includes a free STREAM book and related activities. Registration for programs is available at delraylibrary.org.

In partnership with the School District of Palm Beach County, the library provides additional support for youth during the summer months. Free lunch is served daily from 11:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m., and snacks are available from 2 p.m. to 4 p.m. for children up to 18 years old. Family Nights on select Tuesdays invite children ages 5 and older to explore STREAM topics together. Saturday Science Discovery programs for ages 9 to 12 feature hands-on archaeology activities including Piece Together History, trash bag archaeology, carbon dating, and fossil hunts.

The library is hosting special guest speakers on Thursday afternoons throughout the summer. Malachi Fenn from the Florida Public Archaeology Network will visit on June 11, followed by Sea Turtle Adventures beach cleanup on June 25, American Shark Conservancy's SharkSmarts on July 2, and FPAN Beyond Artifacts on July 16. According to Kae Jonsons, director of development and community relations, the library's free, high-quality education programs help bridge summer learning gaps and keep families engaged in accessible, safe spaces within walking distance for many residents. For a complete program lineup and registration details, visit delraylibrary.org.

5:44 PM ET

Democratic congressional candidate Elijah Manley has accused opponent Dale Holness of making antisemitic comments during a meeting of candidates challenging U.S. Rep. Debbie Wasserman Schultz in Florida's 20th Congressional District primary. The alleged remarks occurred while the candidates discussed whether Black candidates should unite behind a single alternative to Wasserman Schultz. According to Manley, Holness stated that "the Jews are coming for all the seats" and claimed they had fought him throughout his career, including during his time on the County Commission. Manley, who converted to Judaism six years ago, said Holness referenced Wasserman Schultz, lobbyists Ron Book and Bernie Friedman, and former Senate Minority Leader Lauren Book as part of a political conspiracy.

Holness flatly denied the allegations, calling them "an absolute lie." He emphasized his strong relationships with Jewish community leaders and said he has consistently supported Jewish elected officials and business owners. Holness noted that he considers Friedman a close friend, that Book has donated to his campaigns, and that he recently attended fundraisers for U.S. Rep. Jamie Raskin. He also pointed to his record of supporting mayors and commissioners in his former city. "Elijah is making up stories in order to get attention," Holness said. Wasserman Schultz declined to comment on the dispute.

Stephen Johnson, another attendee at the meeting, provided a different account of what transpired. Johnson said he never heard Holness raise the subject of Jewish Americans and recalled only bringing up Jewishness himself when questioning whether Black church members would accept Manley given his Jewish faith and LGBTQ identity. The meeting included four Black candidates for the district: Manley, Holness, former U.S. Rep. Sheila Cherfilus-McCormick, and rapper-turned-community leader Luther Campbell. Florida Politics contacted other attendees for their recollections but received no additional responses.

The controversy reflects broader tensions in the race following Wasserman Schultz's decision to run in the newly drawn CD 20 district. That district was originally created in 1992 as a majority Black seat and has been represented by Black lawmakers since its inception. Wasserman Schultz's district was dismantled under a new congressional map signed by Governor Ron DeSantis. Manley has already qualified for the ballot, while Cherfilus-McCormick told local media she intends to do the same. Holness also expects to qualify and predicted all candidates from the meeting would appear on the ballot.

The Florida Supreme Court denied an emergency petition Wednesday to halt implementation of a new congressional map for the 2026 midterm elections, clearing the .

6:24 PM ET

The Florida Supreme Court denied a petition from voting rights groups seeking to halt implementation of a new congressional map for the 2026 midterms. In a 6-1 decision, the justices said they lacked jurisdiction over the matter while an appeal remained pending in a lower court. Equal Ground Protection Fund and other groups argued that using the map this year would harm voters if it were later determined to be illegal. However, a Leon County circuit judge had already rejected a request for an injunction, and the 1st District Court of Appeal declined to reverse that decision. The new map, signed into law last month by Gov. Ron DeSantis, cuts in half the number of congressional districts where Democrat Kamala Harris won a majority of votes in 2024, from eight to four. Under the new lines, 24 districts were carried by Republican Donald Trump.

Justice Jorge Labarga was the sole dissenter, noting that candidate qualification for Congress ends this week at noon. He criticized the lower courts for delaying appellate review for a second time in fewer than three years, recalling similar delays on a 2022 congressional map challenge. Labarga wrote that even more Floridians' votes were at stake this time. Justice Adam Tanenbaum responded in a concurring opinion, arguing it would be improper for the high court to rule on the case's outcome at an early stage by granting an injunction. DeSantis celebrated the decision on social media, saying the ruling ensures the map will be in place for the 2026 election.

The Palm Beach County School Board is raising significant safety and health concerns about a proposed artificial intelligence data center complex that would be built just 1,200 feet from Saddle View Elementary School.

5:15 PM ET

The Palm Beach County School Board is raising significant safety and health concerns about Project Tango, a hyperscale AI data center facility planned just 1,200 feet from Saddle View Elementary School. During their June 3, 2026, meeting, School Board members outlined multiple concerns in a formal letter to Mayor Baxter and county commissioners. The potential risks identified include industrial noise and vibrations that could disrupt the learning environment, environmental health impacts from facility operations, public safety risks including hazards from lithium battery fires, emergency response capabilities in the event of an incident, and infrastructure strain on local utilities and services.

The School Board is formally requesting access to existing public records, impact studies, and reports the county has received regarding the project, specifically seeking information about potential noise levels from facility operations, environmental impact assessments, public safety and emergency response planning, projected energy and water usage, and long-term economic effects on the community. While expressing these concerns, the School Board indicated a willingness to work with county officials if the project moves forward. The letter states that as the governing body responsible for student and staff education and welfare, the School Board has a fundamental responsibility to provide safe learning environments that promote student achievement.

Palm Beach County faces up to $324 million in losses if property tax reform passes

Palm Beach County commissioners are wrestling with this year's budget while bracing for what could be significant revenue losses if Florida voters approve a property tax reform referendum.

6:28 PM ET

Palm Beach County commissioners are grappling with the current budget while preparing for potential significant revenue losses if Florida voters approve a property tax reform referendum. Estimates show the county could lose $221 million in revenue in the first year if the referendum passes, with losses climbing to $324 million the following year. Commissioner Maria Marino expressed the frustration many commissioners feel about the situation, saying "We're under attack with the property taxes." The commissioners are already under pressure to close a budget gap, with Commissioner Marci Woodward calling on departments to find savings wherever possible.

A major point of discussion centered on the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office budget, which includes a request for more than 8.5% more funding than last year. Commissioner Gregg Weiss noted that all new revenue dollars from the previous year went to support the sheriff's office budget needs, and this year requires all new revenue dollars plus an additional $13 million. The property tax reduction effort is part of a broader push by Gov. Ron DeSantis to make Florida more affordable. Commissioners were told that raising taxes on commercial and non-homestead properties, as well as increasing the millage rate, are possibilities. County commissioners insist they have been running a tight ship on spending and acknowledge things could get much tighter in the years ahead.

Riviera Beach man accused of dragging FHP trooper during I-95 traffic stop in Boynton Beach

Devontea Stubbs, 34, of Riviera Beach faces multiple charges after a Florida Highway Patrol trooper was dragged following a traffic stop at an Interstate 95 exit ramp in Boynton Beach on Sunday.

2:48 PM ET

A Riviera Beach man faces multiple charges after a Florida Highway Patrol trooper was dragged during a traffic stop on an Interstate 95 exit ramp in Boynton Beach on Sunday. FHP said the incident started at 3:41 p.m. when a trooper spotted a beige Honda Accord with an expired tag near the Boynton Beach Boulevard exit. The trooper pulled over the vehicle on the exit ramp, and the driver, identified as Devontea Stubbs, 34, told the trooper his friend was the registered owner. Stubbs said he was unaware of the expired tag and did not have a driver's license. The trooper later learned that Stubbs had his license suspended 11 times since 2014, including two indefinite cancellations, and had a possible statewide extradition warrant for Palm Beach County in a burglary case.

When the trooper asked Stubbs to step out of the vehicle, Stubbs refused and remained seated with his hands on the steering wheel. As the trooper opened the driver's side door and reached into the car, Stubbs rapidly shifted the vehicle into drive. The Honda accelerated, spinning its tires and leaving skid marks as it exited the shoulder, traveling about 15 feet before the trooper could exit. Stubbs continued southbound on Boynton Beach Boulevard at speeds over 75 mph in a 35 mph zone, ignoring red lights and colliding with a Toyota. The pursuit continued to Congress Avenue, where Boynton Beach police arrested Stubbs with K-9 Ash at approximately 4:40 p.m. at the Sealofts at Boynton Village apartments. Stubbs faces charges of aggravated fleeing and eluding with injury or damage, aggravated battery on a law enforcement officer, resisting arrest, driving while license suspended, burglary and petit theft.

Cornell Art Museum in Delray Beach opens upcycled art exhibit through September

The Cornell Art Museum at Old School Square has opened Persistence of Matter, an exhibition featuring artists who turn discarded materials into sculptures and installations.

Yesterday, 9:45 AM ET

The Cornell Art Museum at Old School Square has opened its newest summer exhibition, Persistence of Matter, spotlighting artists who turn discarded materials into striking works of art. Guest curators Mavis Benson and Pamela Lambie selected artists whose sculptures, installations, and mixed media pieces are all crafted from reclaimed objects. Featured creators include Autumn Kioti, whose work draws on natural and urban influences, and Mike Silverman, whose Self-Reflection series uses discarded hard drive platters and printed circuit boards to create reflective sculptures. According to Delray Beach Downtown Development Authority Cultural Arts Director Marusca Gatto, the show underscores the museum's mission to marry creativity with community impact.

The exhibition runs through Sept. 10, 2026, with special programs including artist talks, salon discussions, and film screenings. Museum hours are Wednesday from noon to 5 p.m., Thursday and Friday from noon to 7 p.m., Saturday from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Sunday from noon to 5 p.m. For event details or more information, call 561-654-2220.

Martin County commissioner calls for Brightline station location review

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi asked county staff to discuss moving the planned Brightline station from downtown Stuart to the fairgrounds.

6:50 PM ET

Martin County Commissioner Ed Ciampi called on county staff to open discussions with Brightline leadership about potentially moving the planned train station from its previously agreed-upon downtown Stuart location. Ciampi suggested revisiting the fairgrounds as an alternative site, citing parking issues and other concerns with the current plan. The station was previously planned for a site that is currently a parking lot across from the courthouse in downtown Stuart. Ciampi's push for relocation comes after the FEC sent a letter to former Stuart Mayor Christopher Collins explaining it does not support a Brightline station in Stuart because the proposed station violates U.S. Coast Guard rules requiring the St. Lucie River Railroad bridge to be down while the train stops.

Community members expressed differing perspectives on Ciampi's suggestion. Resident Paul Gedwed supported the idea, saying it is well thought out because of congestion in the proposed location. However, Steven Vitale, owner of the Colorado Inn, questioned moving the station away from downtown, arguing that most people will want to go to downtown Stuart and asking why a train station would be built where people cannot walk. Lisa Freitas, owner of Lisa Gay Fashion, said her downtown store will benefit regardless of where the station is built. The county clarified that Ciampi's comments are not official direction for staff, and the topic would need to return to the board for a discussion and vote before staff can explore a new location.

Palm Beach Gardens is welcoming Portugal's national soccer team at North County District Park ahead of the World Cup, which kicks off tomorrow.

4:10 PM ET

On Wednesday, media members covering Portugal's national team gathered at North County District Park for a debriefing ahead of the squad's arrival. Marco Carvalho, the team's media coordinator for the past three decades, led the session and emphasized the importance of collaboration. "We have to work with the media, with all the media to have a great World Cup," Carvalho said. Tim Ford, sports and special facilities director, has overseen preparations for Portugal's temporary home. The team will train on fields adjacent to those used by England over the past week. "Each one we host, we get to know the intricacies of what each team is looking for and each time it seems we tightened down and dial in more," Ford said. Security measures will be heightened when Portugal arrives. The team requested that video footage of their training center at the back of the complex not be published until Saturday, allowing them to see the facility privately during their first practice.

Pulse nightclub survivor's mother continues fight for answers 10 years after massacre

Christine Leinonen, whose son Christopher was among 49 people killed in the Pulse nightclub massacre, is still seeking accountability a decade later. Christopher and his partner Juan were both killed in the early morning hours of June 12, 2016.

5:27 PM ET

Christine Leinonen moved to a small house near Polk City to support her son Christopher as he pursued his bachelor's and master's degrees in psychology at UCF. He was in the process of pursuing a doctorate when, on the morning of June 12, 2016, he and his partner Juan went to SeaWorld. That night, they planned to go home and relax, but in the early morning hours, Leinonen learned through Facebook that there had been a shooting at Pulse nightclub. Christopher was 32 years old. Juan was 22 and was also shot multiple times. Both were among the 49 people killed. It took Leinonen 33 hours to learn her son was dead when a random officer told her he died on the dance floor.

A decade later, Leinonen continues fighting for answers and accountability. She has been outspoken on gun reform and wrote a book called Control the False Narrative about her pursuit of a lawsuit over what she believes were response failures, permit problems, and code violations the night of the shooting. In May, demolition crews tore down the Pulse nightclub, raising concerns among some that potential evidence of critical issues and violations would be lost. Orlando Mayor Buddy Dyer declined to comment when pushed for answers about calls for accountability. A permanent memorial is expected to open in fall 2027.
