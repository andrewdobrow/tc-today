A few minutes ago

Spirit Airlines is liquidating its assets through bankruptcy court, putting its planes, airport gates, and potentially its West Palm Beach headquarters up for sale. The airline's closure means travelers have lost another budget carrier option and raises uncertainty about the future of affordable air travel.

According to Clint Henderson, managing editor at The Points Guy travel website, Spirit's tangible assets hold significant value. "The assets themselves, the planes, the gates at airports, potentially the headquarters, those can potentially be valuable assets," Henderson said. For many passengers, Spirit's disappearance represents a genuine setback. "A lot of people flew Spirit because it was so cost-effective, and everything is so expensive these days it gave them an option to travel," one traveler told WPTV on Thursday.

A local entrepreneur from the Treasure Coast named Leroy Gillead has submitted a proposal to purchase Spirit's entire operation and rebrand it as Florida Air Express, positioning it as a new low-cost airline headquartered in West Palm Beach. However, Henderson views this proposal skeptically. "This seems like a pie in the sky thing. If they can get the money together, maybe they'll establish a new low-cost carrier, but right now they don't fly any routes at all," Henderson said. He believes a more realistic outcome is that Spirit's assets will be broken up and sold separately to the highest bidders, which are likely to be major airlines. "It would be much more likely in the end that you'll see the assets of Spirit divvied out to the highest bidder, and that's probably going to be major airlines," he explained.

The bidding deadline is scheduled for July. Henderson noted that low-cost carriers are becoming increasingly scarce in the industry, and he recommends that travelers invest extra effort in searching for competitive fares. As for Spirit's iconic big yellow aircraft, they will almost certainly be repainted in the colors of their new owners. The George Snow Scholarship Fund has reached a significant milestone, distributing more than $7.5 million in scholarships and support services to 470 students across Palm Beach and Broward counties in 2026. The Boca Raton-based nonprofit, which was founded over 40 years ago, continues to expand its reach and impact in the region. President Tim Snow announced the record-breaking achievement to WPTV, highlighting the organization's commitment to making higher education more accessible for deserving students.

Since its founding, the organization has awarded more than $45 million to over 4,100 students, establishing itself as the largest scholarship provider in Palm Beach County. Snow explained that the fund's core mission is to address the financial gap between traditional financial aid packages and what students and families can realistically afford. The organization accomplishes this through four-year scholarship commitments that provide stability and predictability as students work toward their degrees.

The fund's support extends well beyond monetary awards. Vice President of Development Jay Brandt noted that mentorship, professional development, and educational resources form essential parts of the program. The organization produces the College Compass podcast to help guide students through their academic journeys. Scholar Services Coordinator Garvey Antoine manages the Doyle-Whelchel Scholars Boutique, a unique initiative that provides professional, formal, and casual clothing to help scholars feel confident during interviews, internships, and campus activities. The boutique accepts donations of new or gently used clothing, footwear, and accessories from the community.

Among this year's recipients is Zya Chow, a Wellington Community High School graduate who earned both the Royal Palm Hearing Aid Scholarship and the Davidowitz Family Caregiving Youth Scholarship. Chow plans to attend Georgetown University and expressed her gratitude for the support. Her story exemplifies the fund's dedication to recognizing and supporting students who have demonstrated exceptional character and commitment despite facing various challenges. As education costs continue to rise across the country, comprehensive support systems like those offered by the George Snow Scholarship Fund have become increasingly important for helping underserved communities in South Florida access quality higher education opportunities.

2:17 PM ET

The Florida Education Association has thrown its support behind Democrat David Jolly in the race for governor, joining forces with the Florida AFL-CIO and other public-sector unions in the endorsement. The FEA, which represents 120,000 educators working in Pre-K-12 schools and higher education institutions throughout Florida, made the announcement through a press release highlighting Jolly's record on education and labor issues.

FEA President Andrew Spar explained the union's reasoning, stating that Floridians are exhausted by unfulfilled promises from elected officials while dealing with a persistent cost-of-living crisis and inadequately funded schools. Spar pointed to families struggling to afford basic necessities including rent, childcare, and groceries. He also noted that recent anti-worker legislation has eroded constitutionally protected rights while students have been neglected in favor of partisan disputes. According to Spar, the upcoming election represents an opportunity for voters to reject out-of-state billionaire interests and choose a governor committed to prioritizing Florida's children, families, and workers.

Jolly, a former member of Congress, expressed appreciation for the union's backing and stated his commitment to Florida's educators. "Florida's teachers and education professionals dedicate their lives to our children and the future of our state," Jolly said. He added that he and his running mate Gwen Graham, whom he selected as his choice for Lieutenant Governor, are dedicated to strengthening public schools, supporting teachers, and ensuring all Florida students have opportunities to succeed. The FEA highlighted specific concerns motivating the endorsement, including the redirection of billions of dollars to what it characterizes as an unaccountable voucher program, restrictions on academic freedom in higher education institutions, and insufficient teacher compensation.

Jolly is competing in a competitive gubernatorial race among multiple candidates. The Republican primary field includes Lieutenant Governor Byron Donalds, investor James Fishback, and former House Speaker Paul Renner. The FEA's endorsement recognizes what the union views as Jolly's consistent advocacy for teachers and education professionals, his support for traditional public schools, and his broader commitment to strengthening communities across the state.

Florida initial jobless claims fall below 6,000 for first time since early May

The number of new unemployment claims has decreased for a second week in a row in Florida, with 5,981 filings last week.

A few minutes ago

Florida's new unemployment claims have declined for a second consecutive week, according to data released by the Department of Labor for the week ending June 20. The state recorded 5,981 initial filings, down 962 from the previous week's 6,943 claims. This marks the first time since early May that Florida's numbers have fallen below 6,000. The decline follows a similar drop the previous week, when claims fell from 6,732 to 6,943, a decrease of 939 filings.

The downward trend aligns with national patterns. Across the country, initial filings totaled 207,133 last week, representing a 6.1% decline or 13,509 fewer claims than the prior week. This decrease exceeded economist projections, which anticipated only a 0.7% drop. Year-over-year, claims fell from 227,516 in the comparable week of 2025. The improvement in jobless claims comes as FloridaCommerce reported the state's unemployment rate stabilized at 4.8% in May after four consecutive months of increases. Florida's workforce numbered 11.14 million, with 531,000 residents unemployed. The state's jobless rate remained higher than the national figure of 4.3%, which held steady from April. FloridaCommerce will release June unemployment figures in a few weeks.

Port St. Lucie city council approved funding for a three-year treatment plan to address water quality issues in the Sawgrass Lakes community after a fish kill wiped out 1,100 fish.

Yesterday, 5:54 PM ET

Port St. Lucie city council approved funding Monday for a $1.3 million three-year treatment plan to address water quality issues in the Sawgrass Lakes stormwater tract. The city will use a solution called TryMarine, administered by vendor SOLitude, to treat the water and conduct ongoing water quality testing.

The treatment follows a fish kill that wiped out around 1,100 fish in the stormwater tract several weeks ago. Bob Shonce, HOA president of North Panther Trace at Sawgrass Lakes, said neighbors have been concerned about the water quality for over five years. Most of the community voted in March to try the TryMarine treatment after city staff listened to resident concerns. Kevin Matyjaszek, utility systems director for Port St. Lucie, said the stormwater tract has muck and sediment issues that lower oxygen levels and water quality, making it susceptible to algae growth. He said TryMarine could help reduce the muck and bring oxygen levels back. Jim Moir, executive director of the Indian Riverkeeper, said while TryMarine may help reduce sediment, the city also needs to address the underlying source of the muck to prevent future problems.

Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League urges pet hurricane plans as storm season continues

Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League is urging South Florida residents to create a disaster plan for their pets before the next storm hits during hurricane season.

Yesterday, 1:34 PM ET

With hurricane season in full swing, Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League is urging South Florida residents to create a disaster plan for their pets before the next storm hits. Dr. Alyssa Comroe, CEO of Peggy Adams, said the safest place for pets during a hurricane is right alongside their owners. The shelter's expert team recommends ensuring pets wear a collar with current identification and have an updated microchip registered with correct contact information.

The organization also recommends introducing pets to a designated safe room ahead of time, such as a walk-in closet or downstairs bathroom, and conducting practice runs so the hurricane isn't their first time there. Pet owners should prepare an emergency kit with a three-day supply of food and water in airtight containers, bowls, a sturdy carrier, bedding, towels, medications for two weeks, vaccination records, first-aid supplies, litter and box for cats, leashes, harnesses, cool packs, wipes, waste bags, and multiple forms of visible ID. Peggy Adams recommends keeping a printed list of local pet-friendly shelters and hotels in an accessible place and keeping recent photos of pets, ideally with their owner, to help with identification if separated. Conducting hurricane drills that include pets' daily routines inside the safe room can also maximize comfort during an actual storm. Florida's hurricane season runs June through November.

Citizens Property Insurance places more reinsurance through capital markets than traditional carriers

Florida's state-backed insurer placed $803.2 million through insurance-linked securities and collateralized deals compared to $565.8 million with traditional reinsurers.

2:29 PM ET

Citizens Property Insurance is increasingly turning to capital markets rather than traditional reinsurers to protect against catastrophic losses. According to Reinsurance News, Citizens placed $565.8 million with traditional reinsurers and $803.2 million through insurance-linked securities (ILS) and collateralized deals. For every dollar of newly placed traditional reinsurance, Citizens placed about $1.42 through capital-market protection. ILS transforms insurance risk into financial instruments that investors can buy and sell. Instead of traditional reinsurers holding risk on their own books, investor money gets set aside to cover specific catastrophe losses, with investors paid for taking on that risk.

The cost of this reinsurance protection ultimately reaches Florida consumers through their insurance premiums. The Florida Office of Insurance Regulation stated in a recent market stability report that reinsurance is a "direct and significant cost to consumers." Some protection comes from European firms including Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re and Lloyd's of London. Swiss Re estimated its own claims from Hurricane Ian at about $1.3 billion, with damage occurring in Florida but part of the bill landing in Zurich. While reinsurance pricing has softened recently, the basic structure remains unchanged: Florida still needs willing participants willing to take on Florida hurricane risk.

Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League urges Treasure Coast pet owners to prepare disaster plans for hurricane season

The animal rescue organization is encouraging South Florida residents to create hurricane plans for their pets, including emergency kits and safe room preparations.

Yesterday, 1:34 PM ET

With hurricane season in full swing, Peggy Adams Animal Rescue League is urging South Florida residents to create a disaster plan for their pets before the next storm hits. Dr. Alyssa Comroe, CEO of Peggy Adams, said the safest place for pets during a hurricane is right alongside their owners. The organization recommends several key steps for pet safety, including ensuring pets wear a collar with current identification and have an updated microchip registered with correct contact information.

Pet owners should introduce pets to a designated safe room ahead of time, such as a walk-in closet or downstairs bathroom, and conduct practice runs. The organization also recommends preparing a pet emergency kit with a three-day supply of food and water in airtight containers, bowls, a sturdy carrier, bedding, towels, medications for two weeks, vaccination records, first-aid supplies, litter and box for cats, leashes, harnesses, cool packs, wipes, waste bags, and multiple forms of visible identification. Pet owners should keep a printed list of local pet-friendly shelters and hotels in an accessible place and maintain recent photos of pets, ideally with their owner, to help with identification if separated. The organization recommends conducting hurricane drills that include pets' daily routines inside the safe room. Florida's hurricane season runs June through November.

Valerie Sale, chief of communications for the Bay County Commission, said Commissioners "had not opined" on the use of Flock cameras and had no plans to.
