2026-07-17

JetBlue is ending its direct flights between New York's JFK Airport and Vero Beach Regional Airport, citing low passenger demand for the route that launched in December. The airline announced that service between the two airports will end September 9, just nine months after the route began operating. JetBlue told WPTV that demand for the new route has not materialized as the airline had hoped. The carrier will continue serving Vero Beach through its Boston route, and all affected customers will be notified directly by JetBlue and will receive full refunds.

The JFK-Vero Beach route was seen as a major win for the Treasure Coast when it launched in December 2025, providing direct access to one of the nation's busiest airports and potentially boosting tourism to the area. The inaugural flight brought enthusiastic passengers who called the service a game changer for connecting family and friends between New York and the Treasure Coast. The optimism surrounding the launch contrasted sharply with the route's short lifespan, as the service failed to attract sufficient passenger numbers to remain viable.

Vero Beach Regional Airport has worked to expand its commercial service in recent years, with JetBlue's Boston route continuing to serve travelers seeking alternatives to larger South Florida airports. The Boston service remains operational and provides ongoing connectivity for the region. However, the loss of the JFK route represents a setback for efforts to increase air service options at the smaller regional airport. JetBlue has not announced whether it plans to add any replacement routes from Vero Beach or if it will adjust frequency on the remaining Boston service.

Passengers with upcoming reservations on the discontinued route are encouraged to contact JetBlue directly or check their email for rebooking options and refund information. The airline's statement indicated that all affected customers would receive full refunds for their cancelled flights as the carrier winds down operations on the route over the coming weeks. The decision to discontinue the JFK service comes as airlines continue to evaluate route profitability and adjust their schedules based on passenger demand patterns.

Yesterday, 6:41 PM ET

Demolition has begun at Murray Middle School in Martin County, marking the start of a major reconstruction effort following tornado damage caused by Hurricane Milton. The project includes reconstruction of buildings destroyed in the tornadoes, along with a new administration building, media center, art lab, and a full overhaul of the parking lot. The demolition phase alone costs $3.3 million, though the total project cost has not been finalized. The project is being funded by local capital improvement, sales tax and Public Education Capital Outlay.

Students who were displaced from the affected classrooms will continue learning in portable classrooms until construction is complete. The tornadoes destroyed buildings 18, 10, and 9 at the school. Kirstie Gernaine, coordinator of public information and community relations for the Martin County School District, explained how the hurricane damage merged with existing plans. "Murray Middle School was already slated to get all of this construction work with a new admin area, a new media center and a brand new art lab," Gernaine said. "With Hurricane Milton and those tornadoes destroying the buildings in the back buildings 18, 10, and nine we were able to add these on."

The school district said parents can be assured their students will be safe during construction. Barriers have already been placed around the work zone to keep students out. The district hopes to complete the renovations by December 2027. The project combines planned improvements that were already scheduled with the necessary repairs from the hurricane damage.

The reconstruction effort represents a significant investment in the school's infrastructure, modernizing facilities that were due for upgrades while also replacing the structures lost to the Hurricane Milton tornadoes. The combination of planned construction and storm damage repairs allows the district to comprehensively address both immediate needs and long-term facility improvements in a single project timeline.

Yesterday, 6:22 PM ET

The St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office is intensifying traffic enforcement this summer, deploying additional deputies and launching a new targeting initiative aimed at reducing speeding and reckless driving across the county. Sheriff Richard Del Toro said traffic is the top concern among residents and described the crackdown as a quality of life issue. Del Toro is using extra deputies from the sheriff's school resource division to boost enforcement while the school year is on break and placing top-performing deputies in areas of concern.

Deputies Christopher Rodrigues and Cheyenne Benning are among those targeting high-problem corridors, including Midway Road, U.S. Highway 1 and Indian River Drive. Benning said she recently had someone pass her on Interstate 95 doing 105 mph. Rodrigues said he stopped a driver doing 72 mph on Midway Road, got someone going 98 mph on U.S. Highway 1, and clocked his top speed on Indian River Drive at 73 mph, where the posted limit is 25 mph. Benning said the county sees a lot of excessive speeding.

In the past four weeks, deputies conducted 1,162 traffic stops, wrote 470 citations and issued 1,728 written warnings. The enforcement push is part of a broader strategy Del Toro put in place when he took office. He eliminated the agency's dedicated traffic unit and made traffic enforcement the responsibility of every deputy. Since 2025, the sheriff's office has issued more than 26,000 citations, a 155 percent increase.

Del Toro said he has a family that drives on these roadways and cares about his loved ones and everyone else's loved ones, adding that he wants to make this a safer community. Residents described the danger they encounter daily on local roads. Ashley, a St. Lucie County resident, said nobody slows down and called the situation insane. Jessica, another local driver, said drivers are careless and cut people off. Del Toro characterized the stepped-up enforcement as addressing a quality of life issue for the community.

The closing weeks are bringing new attacks, a ballot challenge and questions about the candidates...

Yesterday, 5:00 PM ET

Republican U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds and Democrat David Jolly have emerged as frontrunners in Florida's governor race as the primary approaches. Donalds holds a commanding advantage in Republican polling and fundraising, while Jolly has consolidated support in the Democratic contest. The closing weeks are bringing new attacks, a ballot challenge and questions about the candidates' vulnerabilities heading toward November.

A Tyson Group survey released this week placed Donalds at 48% among Republican voters, followed by Lt. Gov. Jay Collins at 9%, businessman James Fishback at 8% and former Florida House Speaker Paul Renner at 4%. About 26% of respondents remained undecided. Donalds has announced more than $90 million raised across his campaign operation, with President Donald Trump endorsing him. Collins, who Gov. Ron DeSantis appointed lieutenant governor in August 2025, has not received an endorsement from DeSantis. During a Thursday campaign call, Collins positioned himself as the strongest Republican alternative to Donalds and urged Renner to leave the race, saying it is a two-person race between himself and Donalds.

Fishback faces pressure on two fronts. The Front Line, a Republican group aligned with U.S. Sen. Ted Cruz, announced a $400,000 campaign targeting statements the group characterizes as antisemitic. Fishback denies being antisemitic. Collins is also challenging Fishback's candidacy, alleging he lived and voted in Washington, D.C., and does not meet Florida's seven-year residency requirement for governors. A two-day hearing is scheduled to begin July 21 in Tallahassee. Because ballots have already been printed, a ruling against Fishback would likely involve directing election officials not to count votes cast for him rather than removing his name from ballots. Fishback says he was born and raised in Florida, has lived in the state throughout his life and purchased a Washington condo only to visit his parents.

On the Democratic side, Jolly addressed his past business and political relationship with Clearwater financier Leo Govoni. Federal prosecutors allege Govoni and accountant John Witeck stole more than $100 million from a nonprofit that managed trust funds for people with disabilities. The indictment alleges the scheme operated from 2009 through 2025. Jolly is not a defendant in the case. Jolly previously worked as a vice president at Boston Finance Group, a Govoni-controlled company, before entering Congress. In a campaign video, Jolly said he had no knowledge of the alleged theft. A June Change Research survey commissioned by Freedom Project USA, a group aligned with Democrats, showed Jolly leading Donalds 47% to 42% among registered voters and 49% to 43% among likely voters.

Mon, 13 Jul 2026 18:36:08 -0400

Registration is now open for Port St. Lucie City University, a free nine-session program designed to help residents better understand how their local government operates.

Classes begin Wednesday, Aug. 12, and will be held from 5:30 to 8:30 p.m. in the Council Chambers at Port St. Lucie City Hall, located at 121 SW Port St. Lucie Blvd.

Participants will learn about City departments, municipal services and the decision-making processes that shape the community. The program also gives residents opportunities to meet City staff, ask questions and gain a closer look at the work involved in operating one of Florida's largest cities.

Residents who complete the course will receive a certificate of completion and may also choose to become a City Ambassador, helping share information about City programs and services throughout the community.

City University is intended to encourage civic engagement while strengthening connections between residents and local government. Since the program began in 2016, more than 600 residents have participated.

Enrollment is limited to 160 participants.

Residents who are unable to attend the in-person classes can also access City U on Demand, which offers a more flexible way to learn about Port St. Lucie government and municipal operations.
