Yesterday, 10:19 PM ET

Homeowners in an unincorporated Palm Beach County neighborhood are dealing with unexpected bills for years of unpaid sewer fees following a city audit. Residents along Franwood Drive received notices from the city of Delray Beach demanding back payments, with some bills exceeding $4,000. Candace Etzler received a notice for $3,139, while her neighbor Ruth Stephens was charged $4,494.92. At least a dozen homeowners in the area received similar notices covering up to three years of charges.

The retroactive billing stems from events in 1998, when nearby development disrupted the residents' septic systems. State law required them to connect to the nearest municipal sewer system. The city completed an audit in April 2026 that identified the sewer connections and triggered the back charges. Residents say they were caught off guard because they had previously attempted to pay but were told they did not need to. "We were in total shock because it feels like we're being punished," Stephens said. "We didn't make a mistake; we tried to pay, but they said we didn't have to."

The residents are questioning the calculation method for the back charges, which total $90 per month for 36 months. Etzler expressed frustration with the formula, asking "How can you have this random number of 36 months and at the price of $90 a month." Some homeowners have already paid the fees or enrolled in payment plans out of concern that their sewer service could be disconnected. "I understand having to pay a monthly fee, but my total was $4,494.92, and that's hard for people to just come up with," Stephens said.

City Commissioner Juli Casale defended the billing in a statement, explaining that when a sewer connection is identified, the city must determine what costs were incurred and recover applicable fees. "This is about ensuring that public utility costs are paid by those receiving the service rather than being shifted to the taxpayers," Casale said. County Commissioner Marci Woodward said she has contacted the county attorney to examine the legality of the back charges and the monthly payments going forward. The situation has left residents feeling unfairly treated for circumstances beyond their control.

Yesterday, 6:23 PM ET

Volunteers in Medicine, a free clinic serving low-income families in Martin County, is experiencing increased patient demand following the expiration of enhanced federal subsidies under the Affordable Care Act at the beginning of this year. Dr. Jordan Bromberg, the clinic's medical director, reports that more than 200,000 people across Florida chose not to renew their health insurance through the ACA program. While the clinic has directly served about 20 patients who lost coverage, Bromberg estimates that upwards of 500 Martin County residents may have lost access to health insurance entirely.

The loss of subsidies has created a difficult situation for families already struggling financially. Rokaya McGarry, one of the clinic's recent patients, found herself unable to maintain health insurance after the enhanced assistance program ended. Without coverage, McGarry initially viewed medical care as a luxury she could no longer afford. This perspective changed when she fell seriously ill following her mother's death. "The grief and the everything I had like a bronchitis I felt very weak and in a moment of desperation," McGarry said. "I called and within a day VIM clinic took over." McGarry expressed her relief at receiving care, saying "I can't even express how grateful I am to them because I don't know where I'd be."

The expiration of the enhanced subsidies has removed critical financial protection for vulnerable populations. According to Bromberg, early medical screening can identify conditions before they develop into serious health problems. "There are a lot of things that get screened for that if they're found early can prevent terrible outcomes," he noted. The clinic's ability to catch medical issues at early stages becomes even more important when patients have no other access to healthcare.

Volunteers in Medicine maintains eligibility requirements to serve those most in need. Individuals earning under $39,990 annually or families of four earning under $82,500 per year qualify for the clinic's services. Despite the increase in patient volume, Bromberg indicated the clinic has capacity to serve additional patients from the community. "There are so many more out there that can come and see us," he said. "We have the capacity for more." The clinic continues offering comprehensive medical care at no cost and without conditions attached to those who qualify for assistance.

Martin County manufactured home owners can apply for storm repair funding starting in August

Martin County manufactured home owners will soon be able to apply for state funding to help with emergency repairs and storm-hardening improvements. An online application portal will open in August through Martin County Human Services. Once it launches, approximately 75 percent of the county's annual SHIP funding, which typically ranges from $1 million to $1.5 million, will bec...

Yesterday, 6:54 PM ET

Martin County manufactured home owners will soon be able to apply for state funding to help with emergency repairs and storm-hardening improvements. An online application portal will open in August through Martin County Human Services. Once it launches, approximately 75 percent of the county's annual SHIP funding, which typically ranges from $1 million to $1.5 million, will become available to eligible mobile and manufactured homeowners.

This new program opportunity stems from Senate Bill 594, which took effect July 1st and expands access to $167 million in state affordable housing funding for manufactured home owners. The law makes money available for lot rent, emergency repairs, and storm-hardening retrofits. The legislative change was prompted by an investigation revealing that nearly 700,000 Florida mobile and manufactured homes, about two-thirds of the state's total housing stock, were built before current federal safety standards. These older homes cannot withstand modern storms, and many seniors living in them lack the financial resources to make necessary improvements. Repair estimates often range from $10,000 to $15,000 per home.

Julie Stellatos with Martin County Human Services says residents can complete applications online or request a paper version by calling the county office. Stellatos notes that homeowners have been requesting assistance long before this law passed. Since funding is distributed on a first-come, first-served basis, residents should prepare their documentation as soon as possible. Stellatos explains that all counties typically follow a similar timeline based on a calendar from the Florida Housing Coalition, though the specific schedule varies by county. Indian River County plans to present the legislative changes to its Board on August 18th with a state submission deadline of September 30th. St. Lucie County is still awaiting final state guidance before announcing its application process, while Okeechobee County is updating its local housing assistance plan to comply with the new law.

Residents can begin preparing now by visiting the Florida Housing Finance Corporation website and selecting their county or city. The website provides information about available funding, income eligibility requirements, contact information, and application instructions. Gathering necessary paperwork ahead of the portal opening will help ensure applicants can submit their requests quickly when the system becomes active.
