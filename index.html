Jul 2, 5:05 PM ET

Stuart's downtown public dock will close July 6 for a $3 million renovation project expected to be complete by next Fourth of July. The project, funded by a state grant and transportation impact fees, will expand the dock's capacity from 18 to 44 slips for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation will include a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock and a smaller floating platform. More than 1,400 boaters use the dock annually.

Stuart Mayor Sean Reed said the upgrades will prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms. During the closure, the city is accommodating businesses that rely on the dock. Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates off the dock, will continue running during construction. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, said the city is allowing the company to build a gate at the non-floating section of the downtown Stuart dock for boarding passengers. Kuyrkendall said modernizing and expanding the dock will be beneficial for both the city and the business.

The City of Riviera Beach was named one of just 10 communities in the U.S. to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League, making it the only Florida city selected for the honor this year. The award was announced during the National Civic League conference in Denver, where Riviera Beach leaders presented the city's efforts to strengthen community engagement. Mayor Douglas Lawson called the recognition a proud moment for the city, stating "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars." Lawson said the city earned the award through a long-term effort to revitalize Riviera Beach and improve quality of life for residents.

Riviera Beach's application highlighted several civic participation programs, including the Riviera Beach Youth Council, which helps high school students become involved in civic leadership and advocacy, and the Citizens Leadership Academy, a civic education initiative with a 98% completion rate. The city also highlighted the Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program, which provides mini-grants to help longtime homeowners make critical repairs. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson credited residents for the honor, saying "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents." She added that winning the award brings back community pride and reinforces the city's positive momentum. The All-America City Award has been presented annually since 1949 and is considered one of the nation's most respected civic honors.

The Martin County School Board is opposing a judge's recommendation that would bring back a teacher who was suspended following social media posts about political commentator Charlie Kirk. Matt Theobald has taught social studies at Spectrum Academy since 2009. His suspension came after he posted comments on Facebook that characterized Kirk as racist, misogynistic, and a neo-Nazi. The school board filed court documents Thursday arguing that Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone made errors in her ruling and that "numerous findings are not based upon competent substantial evidence." The board's legal team maintains that Theobald's Facebook post represented a serious violation that justified his removal from the classroom.

The judge's recommendation relied heavily on recent legal precedent from Florida's Fifth District Court of Appeal. That ruling established that teachers cannot be disciplined for personal social media posts created outside of work, using personal devices, and without any student involvement, as such action violates First Amendment protections. In her order from last month, Livingstone applied this standard to Theobald's case. She recommended not only that Theobald be reinstated but that he also receive back pay and employee benefits for the time he has been away from his position.

According to an investigation report reviewed by WPTV, Theobald acknowledged that he wrote the comments about Kirk. However, he stated that he posted them on his mother's private Facebook page as part of a conversation with personal friends. He indicated that he did not intend for the posts to reach a wider audience beyond that private circle. This detail about the limited audience and private nature of the forum became significant to the judge's reasoning in recommending his reinstatement.

The final decision on Theobald's employment status rests with the school board, which has not yet indicated when it will rule on the judge's recommendation. Following the judge's order, Theobald discussed with WPTV how his case highlights broader questions about the boundaries of free speech for teachers outside school settings. The dispute raises important questions about what protections teachers have when expressing personal opinions on their own time and platforms, and where school districts can and cannot restrict their employees' speech. As the Martin County School Board considers its next steps, the case continues to highlight tensions between employee rights and school board authority.

Jun 30, 11:20 AM ET

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during peak sea turtle nesting season through September. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups continue encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection waived admission fees from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, at Florida State Parks to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends and family to enjoy the outdoors, make new memories and support the state's public lands during the significant milestone in the nation's history.

Florida's state parks offer hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling and horseback riding, along with historic sites including forts, battlefields, archaeological sites and other landmarks. The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks saw more than 28 million visitors, generating an estimated $3.6 billion in statewide economic impact. The free admission applied to day use only and excluded Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park. All other fees, including overnight accommodations, special events, concessions and rentals, remained in effect.

A record number of Americans are traveling this Fourth of July weekend, with South Florida drivers contending with heavy rain and congestion on already-crowded roads. Friday's wet weather created difficult driving conditions on the Florida Turnpike heading south of Orlando. Driver Ed Budd described the conditions as "really beautiful until it starts raining and then everyone forgets how to drive."

AAA estimates 72.2 million Americans will have traveled at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, marking an all-time high for the holiday. Of that number, 61.4 million are making the trip by car. Orlando and Miami rank among the top five travel destinations this Independence Day, according to AAA. Drivers reported vastly different experiences on the roads. Carlos Borras and his wife made the round trip between Orlando and Miami on Friday and saw no traffic or accidents. However, driver Fernando Amado said it's been pretty busy, especially with the weather, and noted that Saturday is the day when people are really heading up North. For those still planning to travel, AAA recommends hitting the road after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday to avoid the worst congestion, with the heaviest traffic expected between 10 a.m. Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. Sunday.

2:48 PM ET

The Peacock House Art Foundation is establishing Fort Pierce as an artistic destination by preserving the historic Crain Estate in the Peacock Arts District. This non-profit organization maintains the legacy of Jaxson and Mary Crain, the property's original owners. Jaxson Crain worked as a tile worker, and evidence of his skilled craftsmanship covers the entire facility, visible on floors, walls, and ceilings throughout. Guided tours of the gardens and distinctive architecture are available every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. The house retains much of its original character from the Crain era, including a smoking and bourbon room with wood paneling, masculine-toned bathroom fixtures, and pre-stained glass windows that open to a front patio bar. During their travels around the world nine times, the Crains collected distinctive pieces for their Florida home, including a gazebo shipped from India that features small peacocks in its design. The foundation has deliberately chosen to preserve the estate's authentic character rather than commercialize it, keeping the property approximately 90 percent as it was during the Crains' residency.

The property's name comes from the peacocks that have inhabited it for decades. The Crains are believed to have introduced peacocks to Fort Pierce during the 1970s, initially bringing seven birds to the estate. That original flock has since grown to over 100 peacocks. Among them is Bob, a celebrated bird estimated to be 60 years old and recognizable by his distinctive limp. Bob has become something of a celebrity, living inside the house with his own feeding bowls and receiving hand-feedings daily. For visitors, particularly children, seeing peacocks eat from their hands creates memorable experiences. Interestingly, the Crains once kept monkeys on the property as well, with one story recounting how Jaxson Crain brought a baby monkey back from South America in his pocket.

The foundation operates an artist-in-residency program that draws creative professionals from around the world. The program provides simple yet elegant accommodations on the property for visiting artists. The art lab offers classes for both children and adults regardless of experience level. Art director Krystal Masters teaches one-on-one sessions specifically designed for beginners, allowing students to arrive with no artistic background and leave with a completed piece. The lab provides various mediums and projects, including sculpting and watercolor painting. One workshop offered is called "paint your pet in watercolor and rainbow," which combines creative instruction with popular subjects.

For Masters, art serves a therapeutic purpose beyond simple creativity. She emphasizes that classes help people destress and leave feeling fulfilled, disconnected from their phones. Students working with her report experiencing genuine satisfaction from their artistic accomplishments, with Masters noting that her work inspires her through witnessing the positive impact on her students.
