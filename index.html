Yesterday, 6:54 PM ET

Nearly 140 new Florida laws took effect on July 1, and one of them could significantly help vulnerable homeowners across the state. Senate Bill 594 makes $167 million in state affordable housing funding available to mobile and manufactured home owners for emergency repairs and storm-hardening improvements. The law addresses a critical housing safety issue that affects hundreds of thousands of Floridians living in older homes that cannot withstand modern storms.

The push for this legislation came after an EF-3 tornado devastated the Spanish Lakes Country Club Community in Fort Pierce, destroying 138 homes and killing six residents. An investigation following the disaster revealed that nearly 700,000 manufactured homes across Florida, roughly two-thirds of the state's total, were built before current federal safety standards and are vulnerable to severe weather. Many residents, including Paula Richards from Spanish Lakes, cannot afford the estimated $10,000 to $15,000 needed for storm-hardening work. Lawmakers responded by passing Senate Bill 594 in spring 2026, expanding access to SHIP funding specifically for manufactured home emergency repairs and storm-hardening retrofits.

In Martin County, the application process will launch in August through an online portal. Julie Stellatos with Martin County Human Services explains that about 75 percent of the county's annual SHIP funding, typically between $1 million and $1.5 million, can now assist eligible mobile and manufactured homeowners. Applicants can either complete an online application or call the office to request a paper form. Stellatos notes that people were seeking assistance long before the law passed, and this funding represents a major opportunity. Across the viewing area, other counties are at different stages of implementation. Okeechobee County is updating its local housing assistance plan to comply with the law. St. Lucie County is still awaiting final state guidance before announcing its application process. Indian River County plans to present the matter to its board on August 18, with a September 30 deadline to submit its plan to the state.

Because funding is allocated on a first-come, first-served basis, Stellatos recommends that interested homeowners begin gathering necessary paperwork immediately. Applicants can visit the Florida Housing Finance Corporation website, select their county, and check income eligibility requirements while preparing their applications. The website also provides contact information and application instructions. As Paula Richards said following the tragedy, "Mobile home lives matter too." This new funding represents a concrete response to the needs of Florida's most vulnerable residents living in manufactured homes.

Yesterday, 10:19 PM ET

Homeowners in an unincorporated Palm Beach County neighborhood are dealing with unexpected bills for unpaid sewer fees spanning several years, with some residents facing charges exceeding $4,000. The situation has left residents concerned about potential service disconnection if they cannot pay the amounts demanded by the city of Delray Beach.

Residents along Franwood Drive received notices demanding back payments following a city audit completed in April 2026. The bills include charges for up to three years of sewer service along with new monthly fees going forward. Candace Etzler received a notice for $3,139, and she reported that at least a dozen other homeowners in the neighborhood received similar notices. Her neighbor, Ruth Stephens, was billed $4,494.92. The retroactive billing stems from 1998, when nearby development disrupted the residents' septic systems. State law required them to connect to the closest municipal sewer system as a result. Stephens said residents had previously attempted to pay for sewer service but were told they did not need to do so.

Residents are challenging how the city calculated the back charges. Etzler questioned the methodology behind the 36-month calculation period and the $90 monthly rate the city applied. Stephens expressed frustration about the total amount, saying "I understand having to pay a monthly fee, but my total was $4,494.92, and that's hard for people to just come up with." Some residents have already paid the fees or signed up for payment plans out of concern that they could lose sewer service.

City Commissioner Juli Casale defended the billing approach in a written statement, explaining that when a sewer connection is identified, the city must determine accumulated costs and recover applicable fees from those using the service. She emphasized that the city is ensuring utility costs are paid by those receiving service rather than shifting expenses to other taxpayers. Meanwhile, County Commissioner Marci Woodward indicated she has been communicating with the county attorney's office regarding the legality of both the retroactive charges and the monthly payments being demanded from residents.

A Jensen Beach teenager will represent Team USA at the Sailing World Championship in France this weekend, following years of competitive sailing that started at age four. Harper Aberman's journey into the sport began when her grandparents enrolled her in a four-week summer camp in Pennsylvania. While her cousin disliked the experience, Aberman was immediately drawn to sailing and chose to extend her stay to eight weeks.

After her family relocated to Jensen Beach, Aberman joined the green fleet program at the US Sailing Center of Martin County, where her competitive sailing career truly took off. She describes herself as an adrenaline junkie and is particularly attracted to challenging conditions on the water. "When it's windy, I've always struggled to stay upright and stay out of the water," Aberman said, acknowledging the difficulty of sailing in rough seas.

Despite downplaying her own abilities, Aberman has achieved significant success in youth sailing. She has won numerous tournaments, placed in several national competitions, and has become recognized as one of the biggest names in the youth sailing circuit. Learning that she would represent Team USA came as a surprise during Spring Break. "It was during Spring Break, I got the email. I started screaming, my parents started screaming, and we were like, 'This is not real. We're going to Europe this summer,'" Aberman said.

In preparation for the championship, Aberman has spent recent weeks training in Spain ahead of the Monday start of racing. She emphasized the importance of recovery and dedication leading up to the competition. "We plan on taking the waters Monday for racing, and it's just been a lot of recovery and a lot of time and effort out on the water, but it's definitely going to be worth it. Especially in the venue we're in," Aberman said. The combination of her early introduction to sailing, competitive achievements, and rigorous training has positioned her well for representing her country on the international stage.

Meta complies with Florida youth social media law while TikTok, Google face enforcement action

Yesterday, 5:45 PM ET

Florida's landmark social media law took effect after a major legal victory in November when the 11th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals cleared the way for implementation. HB 3, which bans children under 14 from creating social media accounts and requires parental consent for users ages 14 and 15, passed the Legislature in March 2024 and was signed that same month by Governor Ron DeSantis. The law represented a signature priority for then-House Speaker Paul Renner, who argued that "a child in their brain development doesn't have the ability to know that they're being sucked into these addictive technologies."

Attorney General James Uthmeier moved swiftly to enforce the law after defeating Big Tech's legal challenge. He warned noncompliant platforms of $50,000-per-violation fines that could accumulate into billions of dollars. Meta responded by announcing compliance effective in early May, and Uthmeier confirmed that Meta has suspended hundreds of thousands of accounts belonging to underage users. By contrast, TikTok and Google, which owns YouTube and YouTube Kids, have remained silent about their compliance plans and have effectively ignored the law's requirements.

Uthmeier sued TikTok in June for violating HB 3 by allowing underage accounts to persist and failing to secure parental consent from older teens. "TikTok knowingly deceives parents and allows children to be exposed to harmful and inappropriate content in direct violation of Florida law," Uthmeier stated. He emphasized that "we have zero tolerance for companies that prioritize profit over children's safety." At a subsequent press conference, Uthmeier directed his message toward other holdouts, telling YouTube and other platforms: "You got to wake up. This is real and you can save lives."

Google's continued silence is particularly striking given YouTube's dominance in teens' online activities. Research from Pew found that roughly 90 percent of U.S. teenagers use YouTube, surpassing all other platforms, with about 75 percent using it daily. The exposure risks are well documented: 46 percent of parents with children ages 11 and younger who use YouTube reported their child had encountered inappropriate content on the platform. Even YouTube Kids, which is rated for ages 4 and up, has been found to serve children content on topics like gender ideology and systemic racism, according to congressional testimony. Whether Google, TikTok, Roblox, and other platforms will follow Meta's lead remains uncertain. If they do not comply, Uthmeier indicated that companies "will have to open up your checkbook."

Yesterday, 6:23 PM ET

Volunteers in Medicine in Martin County is treating more patients after enhanced subsidies through the Affordable Care Act expired at the start of 2026, leaving many people without health insurance. The clinic serves low-income families and provides free medical care with no strings attached.

Dr. Jordan Bromberg, medical director of Volunteers in Medicine, said more than 200,000 people in Florida did not renew their health insurance through the ACA this year. He estimates upwards of 500 people in Martin County lost access to health coverage. So far, about 20 patients have turned to the clinic after losing their ACA coverage. Rokaya McGarry is one of those patients. After the enhanced ACA subsidies expired, she could no longer afford health insurance. She fell very ill after losing her mother and began to see medical care as a luxury until it became a necessity. She called the clinic and was seen within a day.

Bromberg emphasized the importance of early medical screening. He said the expiration of the enhanced financial assistance program has left many people without any access to medical care. When health conditions are found early through screening, they can prevent serious outcomes. McGarry described her situation during that difficult time. "Panic set in because you know that the only way that you're going to be helped as far as health is to go to an Emergency," she said. After calling the clinic in her moment of desperation, she expressed deep gratitude for the care she received. "I can't even express how grateful I am to them because I don't know where I'd be," McGarry said.

To qualify for care at Volunteers in Medicine, individuals must earn under $39,990 per year, or under $82,500 for a family of four. Despite the influx of new patients, Bromberg said the clinic has room to serve more people from the community. "There are so many more out there that can come and see us," he said. "We have the capacity for more." The clinic continues to provide medical services at no cost to qualifying patients, making it an important resource for those who have lost insurance coverage and have limited financial means to pay for healthcare.

Yesterday, 5:13 PM ET

Governor Ron DeSantis announced Wednesday that Florida will immediately use a new state law to designate several groups as terrorist organizations. At a Tampa press conference with FDLE Commissioner Mark Glass and Statewide Prosecutor Brad McVay, DeSantis said the state is preparing its first designations under HB 1471, which took effect July 1. The list includes the Muslim Brotherhood, the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR), Antifa, cartel-linked groups including Tren de Aragua and Sinaloa-related organizations, and Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. HB 1471, formally titled "Systems of Law and Terrorist Organizations," authorizes Florida's Chief of Domestic Security to recommend terrorist designations that must be approved or rejected by the governor and Florida Cabinet by majority vote. Designated organizations face public-funding restrictions, possible corporate dissolution in certain circumstances, and penalties tied to material support.

DeSantis framed the announcement as strengthening a December executive order targeting CAIR and the Muslim Brotherhood that was temporarily blocked by a judge in March after civil rights groups sued, arguing it violated constitutional protections. DeSantis said the new law provides better legal footing. The consequences could affect state and local funding, colleges and universities, and Florida's K-12 school-choice scholarship program. Following the announcement, attorneys representing CAIR said they expect another court fight. While the designations are not final until the Cabinet approves them, DeSantis said officials may meet in an emergency session to approve them despite no regular Cabinet meeting scheduled in July.

DeSantis vetoes bill to raise sovereign immunity caps, ending bipartisan push for government liability reform

Governor Ron DeSantis vetoed legislation that would have increased for the first time in 16 years the caps on damages payable to people harmed by government negligence, saying the increase would generate more unmeritorious lawsuits.

Yesterday, 6:15 PM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis vetoed HB 145 on Monday, rejecting legislation that would have raised sovereign immunity caps for the first time in 16 years. The bill, which passed with overwhelming bipartisan support in March, would have increased damage caps from $200,000 per person and $300,000 per incident to $350,000 per person and $500,000 per incident. It would have also allowed local governments to voluntarily settle claims exceeding those caps without legislative approval. DeSantis said the increase would "generate more unmeritorious lawsuits" and opposed the measure on those grounds.

The legislation was sponsored by Sarasota Republican Rep. Fiona McFarland and Lake Mary Republican Sen. Jason Brodeur. McFarland framed the issue as one of equal protection under the law, arguing that claimants face prohibitive obstacles depending on whether the offender was a public or private entity. More than 240 lobbyists registered to speak about the bill, including representatives from counties, cities and their agencies facing budget shortfalls under a proposed property tax amendment on the November ballot. DeSantis defended his veto by noting that the Legislature clears roughly a third of the dozen claims bills it considers annually, arguing the current system works without fundamental changes.

Alligator Alcatraz immigration detention facility in Everglades closes after one year of controversy

The controversial Alligator Alcatraz immigration detention facility in the Everglades closed in June, one year after Florida and the federal government opened it to house immigration detainees.

Yesterday, 5:55 PM ET

One year after its July 1, 2025 opening, Florida's Alligator Alcatraz immigration detention facility in the Everglades has closed following months of controversy. President Donald Trump and Gov. Ron DeSantis announced the facility's opening, describing it as a place to house dangerous migrants. The state used emergency powers to commandeer an old Miami-Dade County landing strip and built the facility in eight days using emergency funding and no-bid contracts through the Florida Division of Emergency Management. Trump said FEMA would cover costs, but a $608 million FEMA grant approved in October was stalled, leaving Florida taxpayers responsible. Total costs exceeded $1 billion according to most estimates. DeSantis said 21,000 detainees were deported through the facility.

The facility faced immediate legal and operational challenges. Using ICE data, investigators found approximately 70 percent of detainees had no criminal record, contradicting government claims. The American Civil Liberties Union sued over detainees' limited access to legal counsel, while Friends of the Everglades filed a separate lawsuit claiming construction violated the National Environmental Policy Act. A district court judge issued a preliminary injunction in August ordering dismantling of the facility, but an appeals court reversed the decision in September. ICE confirmed on June 16 that all detainees had been removed in preparation for hurricane season. Both lawsuits remain active.

A statewide poll conducted by the Florida Chamber of Commerce shows 64% of Floridians say they are "very proud" to be American as the nation marks the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence. Another 12% say they are "somewhat proud," while 12% indicate they are not proud at all. Mark Wilson, President and CEO of the Florida Chamber of Commerce, said, "Two hundred and fifty years ago the Founding Fathers set America on a course that has shaped the world ever since. As America celebrates this milestone anniversary, the Florida Chamber celebrates and takes pride in our nation, in Florida's national leadership, and in the entrepreneurial spirit that defines America to this day."

The poll revealed significant partisan divisions on American pride. Some 89% of Republicans surveyed say they are very proud Americans, compared to 34% of Democrats and 58% of no-party voters. About 25% of Democrats say they're not proud at all of being American, while only 2% of Republicans and 12% of no-party voters say the same. Age also significantly affected responses, with 71% of Floridians over 60 saying they're very proud to be American versus 42% of those under 40. On American history generally, 74% of Florida voters say Americans should be proud of the nation's history, with 91% of Republicans and 51% of Democrats agreeing. The Florida Chamber survey included 604 likely voters between May 1 and May 9.
