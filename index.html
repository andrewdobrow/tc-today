Port St. Lucie residents to receive refunds after $24 million settlement with former trash hauler Waste Pro

Yesterday, 6:03 PM ET

Port St. Lucie residents will receive refunds after the city secured a $24 million settlement with Waste Pro, the former trash hauler involved in a contractual dispute that began in 2021. Both the city and the company filed lawsuits against each other during the pandemic. The city claimed Waste Pro breached its service contract and failed to meet required standards, while the company attributed the problems to labor shortages caused by the pandemic. The city council voted to return all settlement funds to residents through credits applied to property tax bills rather than issuing physical checks.

The refund amounts vary based on property ownership history. More than 57,000 properties with the same owner since 2022 will receive a $364 credit. The city determined that these residents experienced the most significant impact from Waste Pro's service interruptions during the pandemic period. Thousands of other residential properties will receive a $64 credit. This smaller amount corresponds to rate increases that occurred while Waste Pro was in breach of contract, including costs associated with establishing a new waste site, litigation expenses, and transitioning to a new waste management provider.

The city identified 24,676 properties that experienced title changes since 2022 and 13,241 new properties in the city limits. These properties will also receive the $64 credit because the city cannot determine whether title changes resulted from trusts, marriage, divorce, or relocation, and former owners cannot be contacted. However, residents who sold a home and purchased another property within the city during this period may submit documentation proving continuous ownership. These residents can receive the remaining $300 credit on their property tax bill the following year after successfully submitting their claim.

City leadership expressed satisfaction with the distribution plan. Mayor Shannon Martin stated, "From the beginning, I have maintained that the funds should be to benefit the residents who experienced the issues in 2021 and 2022." Councilman Anthony Bonna said the city "reached the sweet spot here" with the approach. Councilman David Pickett emphasized that residents deserved compensation, saying, "They're the ones that suffered who went through the litigation, and now it's time to reward them for the suffering they did." The city will establish a 180-day claims window to process requests for the additional $300 credit. This window will be implemented after city staff and the city's legal department complete their review of the process.

A weekly beach dance gathering in Jupiter has become the subject of ongoing discussion about potential impacts on sea turtle nesting. For the past three years, the event called "Happy Beach Dancing" has drawn dozens to hundreds of participants to beach access 55 on weekends. The gatherings attract people of all ages, families with children, and dogs. Rather than calling for an outright ban, concerned residents are asking organizers to move the events during sea turtle nesting season.

Jupiter's beaches rank among the world's densest loggerhead sea turtle nesting sites, with approximately 150 nests documented in this small area. Record numbers of loggerhead and leatherback sea turtle nests have been documented on Florida beaches this year. According to Joanna Walczak, Vice President of Conservation at the Loggerhead Marinelife Center, the presence of large crowds on the beach can discourage mother turtles from nesting, regardless of the specific activity taking place. Walczak emphasized that the Loggerhead Marinelife Center functions as an educational organization rather than a regulatory agency, and their priority is protecting sea turtle welfare.

Isabel Nylen, a longtime Jupiter resident who has attended several dance events, recognizes the value of both the community gatherings and turtle protection efforts. "I think people come and have a great time. Every age, people come with families, little kids, with dogs. It's open to everybody," Nylen said. She also noted the importance of local conservation practices, observing that area buildings use red lighting to avoid disrupting sea turtles. "People really care in this area," she said. Her daughter, Camila Nylen, stressed that residents bear responsibility for protecting Jupiter's unique beaches. "You got to be super wary and super careful about where you put your stuff and where you walk around," she said.

The Loggerhead Marinelife Center has scheduled a meeting with the dance party organizers for Wednesday to discuss potential solutions. The founder of "Happy Beach Dancing" declined to comment on the controversy. The Town of Jupiter is convening all interested parties to work through this issue collaboratively. The conversation reflects a broader challenge in coastal communities where human recreation and wildlife protection must coexist.

8:25 AM ET

Gov. Ron DeSantis has until June 30 to decide whether to sign, veto, or allow HB 145 to become law without his signature. The Legislature sent the bill to DeSantis on June 15. The legislation would increase the liability caps that the state and local governments must pay in negligence lawsuits.

The bill raises sovereign immunity caps from $200,000 per individual to $350,000 and from $300,000 per incident to $500,000. These limits have remained unchanged since 2010. Sovereign immunity stems from the legal principle that "the king cannot commit a legal wrong." While the state can exempt itself from civil lawsuits, it does allow negligence claims against itself and its subdivisions, including agencies, cities, counties, and public hospitals, but only within established liability limits. When injured parties win awards exceeding these caps, they can petition the Legislature through claims bills to collect the difference. Past cases have involved traffic accidents caused by government workers, medical malpractice at public hospitals, and wrongful convictions resulting in prison sentences.

HB 145 attracted significant attention during the 2026 Regular Session, drawing 242 lobbyist registrations and making it one of the most contested bills. Multiple organizations have requested that DeSantis veto the legislation. Fourteen school superintendents, represented by the Florida Panhandle Area Education Consortium, signed a veto letter warning that the increased caps would create serious financial exposure for districts with constrained local tax bases and narrow budget margins. The Florida Hospital Association also requested a veto, with President and CEO Mary C. Mayhew stating that "every additional dollar diverted to litigation exposure, insurance premiums, settlements or claims administration is a dollar unavailable for patient care, workforce investment, access expansion, and community health priorities."

The Florida Justice Association, representing trial lawyers, has supported the bill, calling it "a small step toward addressing the lack of accountability that allows government entities to avoid fully compensating Floridians who have been injured by government negligence." DeSantis has a history of vetoing legislation backed by the trial bar group, including a 2025 bill to repeal a law preventing wrongful death lawsuits in medical malpractice cases and a 2021 bill to repeal Florida's no-fault automobile insurance system. House sponsor Rep. Fiona McFarland, a Sarasota Republican, expressed satisfaction with the 75 percent increase in individual caps, though an early version would have changed the claims bill process to allow government entities to settle excess claims without legislative approval. That provision was removed before final passage. DeSantis has not publicly commented on the bill, and his office did not respond to requests for comment.

The proposed changes would affect elected officials who take office in upcoming terms rather than those currently serving on the council.

As part of its regular review of municipal leadership positions, the council has begun deliberating on appropriate pay levels for these elected roles. The focus of these discussions centers on establishing compensation structures for future officeholders. This approach means that sitting council members and the current mayor would not see immediate salary increases from this proposal.

Sebastian's government operates under a council-manager system where elected officials work part-time while establishing city policy and direction. The city manager position handles the day-to-day administration of municipal services and maintains direct accountability to the council. The council has not yet determined specific pay increase amounts or approved any formal changes to compensation. The proposal will continue moving through the city's standard legislative process, with additional discussion and a formal vote anticipated at upcoming council meetings. Council members will need to reach consensus on appropriate salary adjustments before implementing any changes to future elected positions.

Upcoming programming will tie into the facility's 25th anniversary celebration later this year. The facility is located at 420 Seaway Drive in Fort Pierce and is open to the public year-round.

After hatching, sea turtles spend their first days swimming away from shore, where floating mats of sargassum provide shelter from predators, food sources, and habitat during one of their most vulnerable life stages.

Three labor unions endorse Vicki Lopez for Miami-Dade Commission District 5

Miami-Dade County Commissioner Vicki Lopez added endorsements from UNITE HERE Local 355 and the South Florida AFL-CIO to her growing list of labor support.

A few minutes ago

Miami-Dade County Commissioner Vicki Lopez announced endorsements from UNITE HERE Local 355 and the South Florida AFL-CIO this week, adding to support from other labor organizations and elected leaders. South Florida AFL-CIO President Jeffrey Mitchell said the umbrella group's 60 local unions, representing 250,000 members, voted unanimously to endorse Lopez's campaign. "The South Florida AFL-CIO proudly voted unanimously to endorse Commissioner Vicki Lopez's campaign and are excited to continue to partner with her as she champions policies and programs that empower working families in Miami-Dade County," Mitchell said.

Lopez, a former Lee County Commissioner and state Representative, was appointed to the Commission in November 2025. She faces Democrat Rob Piper and fellow Republican Joe Sanchez in the Aug. 18 Primary. The County Commission races are technically nonpartisan, and if no candidate receives more than 50% of the vote, the top two vote-getters will advance to a November runoff. Lopez has also received endorsements from AFSCME Florida, SEIU 32BJ, SEIU Local 1991, the South Florida Police Benevolent Association, Metro-Dade Firefighters IAFF Local 1403, Miami Beach IAFF Local 1510, the Miami-Dade chapter of the Florida State Fraternal Order of Police, Miami-Dade Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz, and Miami Beach Commissioners Tanya Katzoff Bhatt, Laura Dominguez, Alex Fernandez and Monica Matteo-Salinas. District 5 covers parts of Miami and Miami Beach, including Brickell, Downtown Miami, Little Havana, Miami River, Mid Beach, The Roads, Silver Bluff, Shenandoah, South Beach and West Flagler.

U.S. Rep. Ritchie Torres of New York backs Shevrin Jones for Florida's 24th District

Democratic U.S. Rep. Ritchie Torres of New York endorsed state Sen. Shevrin Jones in the crowded Democratic Primary race.

10:31 AM ET

Former Miami-Dade Commissioners Betty Ferguson and Barbara Jordan endorsed state Sen. Shevrin Jones in the Democratic Primary for Florida's 24th Congressional District. Ferguson, who served from 1993 to 2004 and was instrumental in incorporating Miami Gardens, said she knows all candidates personally and believes Jones is best suited for federal policymaking. Jordan, who succeeded Ferguson on the County Commission from 2004 to 2020, said Jones has delivered life-changing results for constituents while criticizing rival Oliver Gilbert for breaking commitments to voters about prioritizing their interests over special interests. The endorsements came one day after U.S. Rep. Ritchie Torres of New York threw his support behind Jones's campaign.

Jones launched his campaign to succeed retiring U.S. Rep. Frederica Wilson last week at his family's church in Pembroke Park, calling for a "new generation of leadership" to take on "out-of-touch politicians in Washington" who put themselves and wealthy donors first. CD 24 covers Miami Gardens in Miami-Dade and Hollywood, Miramar and Pembroke Park in Broward County. The district backed former Vice President Kamala Harris with 68.7% of votes in 2024, making the Democratic Primary the decisive race. Six other Democrats have qualified: Gilbert, Marshall Davis, Rudy Moise, Jean Monestime, Roderick Vereen, and Kendrick Meeks. Republican Te Mayonna Brown, no-party candidate Andy Daro, and write-in candidate Patricia Gonzalez also appear on the ballot.
