Stuart's downtown public dock will close July 6 for a $3 million renovation project expected to be complete by next Fourth of July. The project, funded by a state grant and transportation impact fees, will nearly double the dock's capacity from 18 to 44 slips for both motorized and non-motorized vessels. The renovation will include a 7,500 square foot fixed dock, a 1,400 square foot floating dock and a smaller floating platform. Mayor Sean Reed said the upgrades are designed to prevent storm damage during hurricanes and major storms. More than 1,400 boaters use the dock annually.

Tiki Taxi & Cruises, which operates off the dock, will continue running during construction. Austin Kuyrkendall, director of operations for Tiki Taxi & Cruises, said the city is allowing the company to build a gate at the end of the non-floating section for passenger boarding. Boat captain Parker Fisher said the dock has needed work over the years and expressed hope that the project includes more floating docks. Kuyrkendall noted that Stuart's identity and economy are tied to the waterfront and acknowledged that busy weekends create crowding at the current dock.

The cost of celebrating Independence Day with a backyard cookout has increased significantly this year. According to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey, a holiday meal for 10 people now costs $73.82, representing a 4 percent increase from the previous year. The survey attributes this rise directly to inflation affecting grocery prices across multiple categories of food typically served at Fourth of July celebrations.

Several key ingredients for holiday meals have seen notable price jumps. Ground beef, a staple for hamburgers and hot dogs, is up 5.5 percent compared to last year. Hamburger buns have experienced an even steeper increase of 7.7 percent. Strawberries have been hit particularly hard, with prices climbing 12.4 percent due to damage from a Florida frost that affected the crop. Ice cream, another popular summer treat, has risen 5.3 percent in price. These increases across multiple items have combined to push overall cookout expenses higher for families planning their Independence Day celebrations.

Not all food prices have moved upward, offering some relief for budget-conscious shoppers. Potato prices have actually declined this year, providing savings for those planning to serve potato salads or potato chips. Corn prices have fluctuated, though locally grown corn from the Glades region can provide consumers with the best value for their money. These price decreases can help offset some of the increases seen in other categories.

Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommends several strategies to manage cookout costs without sacrificing the celebration. She suggests comparing prices across different stores to identify which retailers offer the best deals on the items on your shopping list. Woroch emphasizes that reducing food waste represents the most significant way families can save money, and this process starts with developing a detailed meal plan before shopping. By planning meals in advance and shopping strategically, consumers can keep their Fourth of July cookout expenses under control despite the inflationary pressures affecting grocery prices this year.

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs through September during peak sea turtle nesting season. Guests can participate in guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The programs are designed to give visitors an opportunity to learn about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups are encouraging beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection is waiving admission fees to state parks from Friday, July 3, 2026, through Sunday, July 5, 2026, to celebrate America's 250th anniversary. DEP Secretary Alexis A. invited all Floridians, their friends and family to explore the state's parks during this significant moment in the nation's history. Florida's award-winning state parks offer hiking, camping, wildlife viewing, swimming, paddling and horseback riding alongside historic sites including forts, battlefields, archaeological sites and landmarks. The Florida State Park system is the only four-time winner of the NRPA's National Gold Medal Award.

Free admission applies to day use only at most locations. Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park, Skyway Fishing Pier State Park and Weeki Wachee Springs State Park are excluded from the promotion. All other fees, including overnight accommodations, special events, concessions and rentals, remain in effect. Over the last fiscal year, Florida State Parks welcomed more than 28 million visitors and generated an estimated $3.6 billion in statewide economic impact. Visitors can plan trips at FloridaStateParks.org.

A record-breaking number of Americans are hitting the roads this Fourth of July weekend, and South Florida drivers are contending with challenging conditions as a result. The AAA estimates that 72.2 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home between Saturday, June 27 and Sunday, July 5, with 61.4 million of those travelers choosing to drive. This marks an all-time high for car travel during the holiday. Friday's rainy weather created difficult driving conditions on the Florida Turnpike south of Orlando, testing drivers' patience and skills as they began their holiday journeys.

The wet weather made an immediate impact on travelers trying to navigate the crowded roads. One driver, Ed Budd, summarized the frustration many experienced, noting that conditions were beautiful until the rain arrived and drivers seemed to forget how to operate their vehicles safely. Despite these challenges, most travelers pushed forward with their plans. Driver Jim Macfawn, drawing on his years of experience on the road, said he simply adapts to whatever conditions come his way and keeps moving.

Orlando and Miami are among the top five travel destinations for Independence Day this year, according to AAA data. Driver experiences on the route between these two cities varied significantly. Carlos Borras and his wife completed their round trip between the two cities on Friday without incident, reporting smooth conditions with no traffic congestion or accidents along the way. However, driver Fernando Amado described a notably busier experience, attributing the heavy traffic to the rainy weather and noting that Friday appeared to be the day when most travelers were heading northward.

For those still planning to travel, AAA offers specific recommendations to help avoid the worst congestion. Travelers should consider departing after 3 p.m. on Saturday or before 11 a.m. on Sunday for the smoothest journey. The most severe traffic delays are expected between 10 a.m. and noon on Saturday and between noon and 6 p.m. on Sunday. AAA notes that the timing of travel, combined with your origin and destination, can significantly influence your overall road experience. As millions of Americans continue their holiday travels, checking weather forecasts and road conditions before departing remains essential for a safer journey.

Fort Pierce residents and visitors gathered along the waterfront Saturday to celebrate Independence Day during the community's 16th annual boat parade. Decorated in patriotic red, white, and blue, the boats created a festive atmosphere as the parade marked another year of this cherished Fourth of July tradition on the Fort Pierce waterfront. The event has grown into a signature celebration for the area, drawing both longtime residents and visitors who want to be part of the holiday festivities.

The boat parade represents one of several ways the Fort Pierce community comes together to celebrate and connect. The waterfront serves as a gathering place for residents throughout the year, hosting various events and activities that bring people together. Saturday's parade continued a tradition that has become an important part of Fort Pierce's cultural calendar, allowing the community to share in the patriotic spirit of Independence Day on the water.

Beyond the boat parade, Fort Pierce offers visitors and residents other attractions and cultural experiences. The Peacock House Art Foundation has emerged as a significant destination, transforming the area into an artistic hub. The foundation preserves the historic Crain Estate, a property that reflects decades of history and craftsmanship. According to Krystal Masters, the art director, the estate was once home to Jaxson and Mary Crain, whose dedication to artistry is evident throughout the facility. Mr. Crain's work as a tile craftsman is visible in detailed tilework that spans from the ground to the walls and ceiling.

The estate remains largely unchanged from the Crains' era, maintaining approximately 90 percent of its original character. The couple traveled the world nine times and collected unique pieces for their Florida home, including a gazebo shipped from India. The property is also known for its peacocks, which have roamed the grounds for decades. The Crains are believed to have introduced the birds to Fort Pierce in the 1970s, beginning with seven peacocks that have since grown to over 100. Among the flock is Bob, believed to be the oldest peacock on the property at around 60 years old. Visitors can take guided tours of the Peacock House's lush gardens and vibrant architecture every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, experiencing the blend of history and artistry that defines this Fort Pierce landmark.
