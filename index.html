Jupiter Island faces up to 9 feet of flooding at emergency center, leaders say they're prepared

Jupiter Island's Emergency Operations Center could see up to 9 feet of floodwater during future storms, according to a new vulnerability assessment by the Florida Department of Environmental Protection. The report identified 103 critical town assets at risk of flooding, including 68 stormwater facilities, 17 historic sites, conservation lands, and key evacuation routes. Commis...

11 hours ago

Jupiter Island's Emergency Operations Center could see up to 9 feet of floodwater during future storms, according to a new vulnerability assessment by the Florida Department of Environmental Protection. The report identified 103 critical town assets at risk of flooding, including 68 stormwater facilities, 17 historic sites, conservation lands, and key evacuation routes.

Commissioner Marshall Field told reporters the findings confirm what town leaders already knew when they requested the assessment. The town's Beach District Director John Duchok said the assessment will set up a roadmap for how to adapt and prioritize improvements. Field said the goal is to document the risks, build a roadmap for improvements, and compete for state and federal funding to fix vulnerabilities.

Field addressed immediate concerns about the island's vulnerability, saying evacuation orders would be issued well before floodwaters threaten roadways. The town has a backup facility on the mainland to maintain operations during emergencies. The 126-page report warns that stormwater systems face some of the greatest risks, noting they can fail during the very flooding events they are designed to manage. Field said there is a funding window that opened June 1 for applications, and the town will take advantage of it.

2 hours ago

Florida TaxWatch, an influential government watchdog organization that has monitored state taxation and spending for 47 years, released a sharp analysis warning against Gov. Ron DeSantis's property tax cut proposal being decided in a special session. The organization called for the issue to be taken up instead by the Taxation and Budget Reform Commission, a constitutionally mandated group that Florida TaxWatch helped establish.

The analysis examined DeSantis's proposal to increase the state's homestead exemption from $50,000 to $150,000 in 2027, then to $250,000 in 2028. While Florida TaxWatch praised the conversation about lowering property taxes and agreed that significant relief is warranted, the report warned that the government cannot solve affordability by simply addressing property tax increases. The organization noted that guardrails are needed to curb local government spending, which it said has far exceeded the growth in population and inflation over the last 10 years.

The TaxWatch analysis drew criticism from former lawmaker Bob Rommel, Florida State Chair of the America First Policy Institute, who questioned on social media where the organization had been for the last seven years while local government spending increased 300 percent above inflation. The analysis itself pointed to Florida TaxWatch's ongoing work on ensuring state and local governments are good stewards of taxpayer dollars, including a report titled Save Our Taxpayers that called for property tax relief to be accomplished equitably.

Billed as an independent and nonpartisan research institute, Florida TaxWatch is often considered a conservative think tank because of its hard-line approach to taxation. The organization regularly produces a list of budget turkeys cautioning against government spending excess and provides information to residents and public officials about local government taxation, expenditures, policies and programs.

Port St. Lucie woman sentenced to 3 years for running illegal med spa out of backyard shed

Yesterday, 10:35 PM ET

Rosa Mena, 50, has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation after operating an illegal medical spa from a shed in her Port St. Lucie backyard. Police began investigating after a victim came forward in May 2025 reporting partial facial paralysis following services at Mena's business, Miracle Hand and Spa, located at the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive.

The victim received 62 units of Botox injections during a session at the makeshift med spa. When the paralysis developed, Mena refunded the $325 fee and asked the victim to return for vitamin injections to reverse the issues. After the paralysis persisted, the victim requested Mena's medical license. Mena provided an altered phlebotomy certificate that had expired in February 2024. Mena also claimed to be a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

A multi-agency investigation by Port St. Lucie Police, PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax, and the Florida Department of Health led to a search warrant at Mena's residence. Detectives found medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines, and injection devices for fillers and Botox. Mena was charged with three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, fraud charges, and use of a two-way communication device to facilitate a felony.

Jupiter Island officials confident despite state report warning of 9 feet of flooding at emergency center

A new state vulnerability assessment identified 103 critical town assets at risk of flooding on Jupiter Island, but local leaders say they are prepared and have been working on these issues for years.

11 hours ago

Jupiter Island leaders say they are "pretty well covered" for future storms following a Florida Department of Environmental Protection vulnerability assessment. The report identified 103 critical town assets at risk of flooding, including 68 stormwater facilities, 17 historic sites, and evacuation routes. The Emergency Operations Center could see up to 9 feet of floodwater, according to the report.

Commissioner Marshall Field said the findings confirm what town leaders already knew, which is why they requested the assessment. Field said evacuation orders would be issued well before floodwaters threaten roadways, and the town has a backup facility on the mainland to maintain operations during emergencies. The town plans to apply for state and federal funding to address vulnerabilities.
