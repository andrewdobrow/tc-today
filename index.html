7:51 AM ET

Families in Indian River County can apply for free or reduced-price meals for students starting July 15. Students at six Indian River County schools can receive free meals during the 2026-2027 school year through federal nutrition programs. Other families in the district may still qualify for free or reduced-price meals based on household size and income criteria. Families are encouraged to complete one application per household to determine eligibility for free or reduced-price school meals.

Families need their child's Student ID number and full formal name as listed in FOCUS. They also need a valid email address and either internet access or the LINQ Connect mobile app. Households receiving Florida Medicaid benefits should have their 10-digit Medicaid case number that begins with the number 1. This is the Medicaid case number, not the Medicaid card number. The case number can be found by logging into MyACCESS or looking at the Notice of Eligibility Review letter from the Florida Department of Children and Families. The case number is printed at the top of the letter.

To apply, families visit www.LINQConnect.com and either sign in or create an account. New users can click Register to create a free account. Families then search for Indian River County Public Schools in Vero Beach and add their students by entering the Student ID and using the formal name as listed in FOCUS, not nicknames. From the website homepage, families click School Services at the top menu and then select Free and Reduced Meal Application. Families using the app go to the homepage and click Free and Reduced Meal Application under Quick Links. Applicants fill in household information and income details and submit the application. Families receive an email when the application is processed.

Families with questions about the meal programs can contact Shawn O'Keefe, the district's nutrition services director, at (772) 564-5041 or shawn.okeefe@indianriverschools.org.

The St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office announced Tuesday that a second person has been arrested in the fatal shooting of Fort Pierce art teacher Andre Leonard Barnes, 66. Dale A. Mincey, 41, Barnes' nephew, was formally charged with first-degree murder Saturday in connection with Barnes' death. Mincey remains in custody at the St. Lucie County Jail and is being held without bond. Deputies said the fatal shooting was premeditated.

The fatal shooting happened July 7 at a residence on the 2700 block of Essex Drive. Deputies responded to the home at around 3:30 p.m. after receiving reports of a person with a gunshot wound. When deputies arrived, they learned a man had shot another man with a rifle. Barnes was taken to a local hospital, where he died. Karen Moye was also at the home when the shooting occurred. She heard a loud bang while she was in the bathroom and came out to find Mincey and called police, an arrest affidavit said. Moye lived in the home with Mincey and Barnes.

Moye, the victim's sister and mother of the suspect, is now facing charges for false report to law enforcement and the commission of a capital felony. Sheriff Richard Del Toro said Tuesday during a press conference that detectives uncovered information indicating Moye knowingly provided false information to investigators and withheld pertinent information related to the homicide investigation. Moye told detectives immediately after the shooting that Mincey and Barnes did not have a good relationship and that Mincey expressed fear in the days leading up to the shooting. Moye said Mincey told her Barnes was trying to kill him and Mincey did not want to be left alone at the house. In a follow-up interview on Monday, Moye gave a detective conflicting accounts of what happened on the day of the shooting. She told detectives she was in fear of Barnes and that something might happen between Mincey and Barnes. When asked why she didn't notify police, she said she thought both of them would get in trouble. During questioning, Mincey told detectives he could not recall what happened the day of the shooting and that he didn't have any issues with Barnes and did not know how Barnes died, though he admitted to owning two firearms.

Barnes worked as an art teacher for the St. Lucie County School District since 2017, serving at West Gate K-8 and Samuel S. Gaines Academy. Barnes leaves behind three adult children. Former student Caila Coleman, who said Barnes was her 7th-grade teacher in Tallahassee, remembered him as a pillar of the community and a father figure. The fatal shooting remains an active investigation. Deputies are encouraging anyone with information that has yet to be shared with investigators to contact the St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office, or to remain anonymous contact the Treasure Coast Crime Stoppers.

Yesterday, 4:27 PM ET

A 28-year-old man died Tuesday after being struck by a semi trailer truck while fleeing from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents on State Road 16 in Florida. The man was one of four people who ran from the scene during an encounter with ICE and Homeland Security Investigations agents in the parking lot of a local gas station and convenience store, according to the Florida Highway Patrol.

The man ran across State Road 16 and into the path of the tractor trailer. He was struck in the right lane and sustained fatal injuries on scene, according to a statement from the Florida Highway Patrol. The agency confirmed it was not involved in the incident but has since opened an investigation into the death. Details about the victim, including their name, nationality and immigration status, were not immediately available. The three other individuals who fled have not been located or identified by authorities.

ICE and the Department of Homeland Security have not yet commented on the incident. The death comes as ICE faces increasing scrutiny for multiple fatal encounters. On Monday, Joan Sebastian Guerrero, a 26-year-old Colombian man living in Maine, was fatally shot by ICE agents during a traffic stop. Senator Angus King said Guerrero was not the target of an immigration operation. Days earlier, Lorenzo Salgado Araujo was fatally wounded during a traffic stop involving ICE in Houston. Like the shooting in Maine, Salgado Araujo was not the person ICE was trying to find.

The Trump administration ordered Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents to pause most traffic stops on Tuesday following the deadly incidents. Florida Highway Patrol officials continue to investigate the circumstances surrounding the death on State Road 16. The incident remains under investigation and is being treated as a developing story.

David Grubitch, a mitigation specialist and spokesperson with the Florida Forest Service, said this is their peak season for brush fires. The summertime heat is drying out fine dead fuels, making them receptive and ready to ignite when lightning strikes occur. Grubitch explained that rain often extinguishes these fires immediately, but sometimes they travel underground and surface days later.

Grubitch said the public plays a crucial role in helping the Florida Forest Service do its job. He stressed that residents should never assume someone else has already called in a brush fire. If you see something, say something, as it can make all the difference in how quickly crews are able to get a fire under control. Martin County saw two brush fires this week, including one sparked by a lightning strike. Both are now fully contained and being monitored by the Florida Forest Service.

Yesterday, 5:53 PM ET

The Causeway Cove development project, located off Seaway Drive, includes five high-rise buildings, including a 17-story building standing 192 feet tall, more than four times the city's height limit. Plans also call for two hotels and more than 70,000 square feet of retail space. The project includes more than 900 apartments and condos, with 365 of those designated as affordable housing. The development qualifies under the Live Local Act, a state law passed in 2023 that allows developers to bypass height and density restrictions as long as they set aside 40% of units for affordable housing for at least 30 years.

Neighbors packed the chamber Monday as the Fort Pierce Planning Board reviewed the plans. Buster Brewster, a boater at Causeway Cove Marina, said he worries the project will change the character of the area and take away from the beauty of the place. Dennis Fein raised concerns about traffic, saying nobody thought this out and there will be massive traffic jams with nobody able to get on or off the island. City Planning Director Kev Freeman said developers must add traffic mitigation efforts and any required intersection or access improvements. City Commissioner Michael Broderick said the city has no authority over whether the project is approved, stating the project is regulated by the state of Florida in its entirety and the city has been eliminated from the process. City commissioners are scheduled to hear a review of the project as the next step.
