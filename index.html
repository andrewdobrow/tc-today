Juno Beach faces fence dispute at JB-7 beach access that could cost thousands

A missing fence at a Juno Beach public beach access point has created conflict between condo residents and summer beachgoers, with repairs potentially costing the town significant money. Yesterday, 1:35 PM ET

A missing fence at a Juno Beach public beach access point has created conflict between condo residents and summer beachgoers, with repairs potentially costing the town significant money. Residents at the Colony Condos report that people using the JB-7 public walkway have been crossing through their private property since the town removed the old fence in 2023 for safety reasons. With only bushes now separating the public access path from private land, the boundary between public and private areas has become unclear to visitors.

Lori Hill, a Colony resident, explained that the problem intensifies during summer months when beachgoers arrive in large numbers. "Lots of cars that just come at random, and then they just park on the spots there," Hill said. "Bushes are pushed back that they cut through those with their coolers and their fishing rods and lawn chairs and everything, and then they just park there all day." She noted that residents previously tried confronting trespassers directly but stopped after encounters became hostile. Now, the residents call police to handle the situation instead.

Not all beachgoers acknowledge a significant problem. Michael House, who regularly walks to JB-7 from a nearby public parking lot, said he has not observed the trespassing issues described by residents. "There's plenty of free parking. If they're damaging property and you know littering and stuff like that might be a different issue," House said. "I have never noticed anyone hopping the fence or the shrubs or anything like that."

The issue reached the Juno Beach Town Council at a recent meeting, where officials clarified that the town has no legal obligation to install a fence. Vice Mayor Pro Tem DD Halpern suggested exploring more affordable alternatives rather than reinstalling the original fence. "I understand fencing is expensive, but I'd like to see maybe we can help them with a lower-cost fence or some combination of split rail and shrubs," Halpern said. Hill expressed hope that the town would take action to restore the boundary. "We're really hoping that the town will get back on board and just put it back up, so that we can have our landscaping and our private parking," Hill said. The council agreed to address the fence question during its budget discussions scheduled for July 15.

The Martin County School Board filed a court brief Thursday challenging an administrative law judge's recommendation to reinstate Matt Theobald, a social studies teacher at Spectrum Academy since 2009. Theobald was suspended last year after posting Facebook comments that labeled conservative activist Charlie Kirk as racist, misogynistic and a neo-Nazi. In the filing, the school board argued that Administrative Law Judge Jodi-Ann Vanessa Livingstone made numerous findings that are not based upon competent substantial evidence. The board's attorney claims Theobald's Facebook post about Kirk was a serious offense and that removing him was appropriate.

Livingstone cited a recent Florida Fifth District Court of Appeal ruling that said disciplining a teacher for personal Facebook posts made off school grounds, on personal equipment, with no student involvement, violated the teacher's First Amendment rights. She recommended Theobald be awarded back pay and benefits. According to an investigation report, Theobald admitted writing the comments about Kirk but said they were posted on his mother's private Facebook page within a discussion among personal acquaintances and were not intended for public view.

Yesterday, 3:42 PM ET

Riviera Beach has earned a prestigious national honor as one of only 10 communities across the United States selected to receive the 2026 All-America City Award from the National Civic League. The city stands as the sole Florida recipient of this year's recognition. The award celebrates communities that unite residents, local government, businesses, and organizations to address community challenges and create meaningful, lasting improvements. City leaders presented Riviera Beach's civic engagement efforts at the National Civic League conference in Denver, where judges announced the city as a first-time winner. Mayor Douglas Lawson expressed pride in the recognition, stating that "Little old Riviera Beach is one of the biggest, brightest, shining stars."

Lawson credited the award to years of sustained effort aimed at revitalizing the city and enhancing residents' quality of life. He noted that residents are witnessing the community transformation that city leaders envisioned. The honor reflects an extended commitment to strengthening civic health, building trust between residents and local government, and enabling the community to directly influence the city's future direction. Councilwoman KaShamba Miller-Anderson attributed the award to residents' dedication and involvement. "This award is a tribute to the heart and determination of our residents," Miller-Anderson said. "Every event we host, every project we complete, and every challenge we overcome happens because our community shows up for one another."

The city's application emphasized multiple initiatives designed to boost civic participation and neighborhood development. The Riviera Beach Youth Council engages high school students in civic leadership and advocacy work. The Citizens Leadership Academy provides civic education with a 98% completion rate. The Neighborhood Reclaim Home Rehabilitation Program awards mini-grants to longtime homeowners for essential repairs. City leaders described these programs as part of a larger commitment to treating civic engagement as an ongoing partnership involving government, residents, faith organizations, schools, and community groups. Miller-Anderson emphasized the city's innovative approach, saying the programs ensure residents feel connected to what is happening in their community.

The All-America City Award has been presented annually since 1949 and ranks among the nation's most respected civic honors. Officials in Riviera Beach believe the designation celebrates the city's progress while positioning the community for future opportunities and continued development. The award communicates to the nation that Riviera Beach is a place where collaboration produces results and residents actively shape their community's future. The complete list of 2026 All-America City Award recipients includes Chelsea, Massachusetts; Franklin, Tennessee; Harlingen, Texas; Montgomery, Alabama; Morrisville, North Carolina; Norfolk, Virginia; North Charleston, South Carolina; Roanoke, Virginia; and Woodburn, Oregon.

The Saint Andrew's Scots are working through a rigorous summer training program as they aim for a state championship during the 2026 fall football season. Last year, the team finished with a 10-2 record and won their district title, but an early playoff exit in the second round has motivated the program to reach higher goals this year.

Head football coach Jimmy Robertson emphasized the strength of this year's senior class, noting that most of them have been with the program since middle school. "Most of these seniors are four-year seniors. I mean, if not the majority of them came from the middle school as well. So they know how things are run. They had a lot of success in their career, and hopefully their senior year is the best season that we've had," Robertson said. This continuity and experience within the senior leadership gives the program a strong foundation for advancement.

To compete at the next level, Saint Andrew's has intentionally scheduled more challenging opponents for the upcoming season. Senior Jack Williams explained the reasoning behind this strategy, saying the team wants to test itself against quality competition to attract new players to the program. Senior Jack Whelche emphasized that advancing further in the playoffs is the real measure of success for the Scots. While winning the regular season is important, Whelche stressed that it means nothing without postseason success. "A better year is just gonna be going farther in the playoffs. Obviously we wanna go undefeated in the season, but that doesn't matter if we keep losing in the second round of the playoffs," he said.

The demanding summer workout schedule serves as daily motivation for the team as they build toward the season. Senior Edmyer St. Jean noted that the daily grind of training gives players something to anticipate and pushes them to work harder so they can see positive results when the season begins. The Scots are collectively embracing the challenge ahead, with their veteran senior class leading the charge to take the program to new heights and ultimately compete for a state title.

Jun 30, 11:20 AM ET

Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach is offering educational and interactive programs during sea turtle nesting season through September. Available programs include guided Turtle Walks, the Hatchling Discovery Program, Hatchling Releases, Tidal Tots and Plastic Free July Community Action Day. The center designed these experiences to give visitors an opportunity to learn about sea turtle conservation along one of the world's busiest sea turtle nesting beaches.

The Turtle Walk program allows participants to observe the nesting process while learning about sea turtle biology, conservation and ongoing research efforts. Educational demonstrations during the programs may include the use of red-light headlamps, amber lighting, night scopes and research tag scanning equipment. The center's education and research departments work together during nesting season to collect and analyze data related to nesting activity and hatchling success. Sea turtle nesting season in Florida typically runs from March through October. Conservation groups encourage beachgoers to turn off beachfront lighting at night, properly dispose of trash and avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings. Registration information and program details are available through Loggerhead Marinelife Center.

Yesterday, 2:38 PM ET

St. Petersburg Mayor Ken Welch has chosen The Burg Bid as the city's preferred development plan for transforming Tropicana Field and the Historic Gas Plant District into a mixed-use neighborhood. Led by Blake Investment Partners and Related Group, the $8.1 billion proposal centers on creating housing, hotels, offices, retail, cultural institutions, and entertainment spaces across the stadium site and surrounding public land. The development team will purchase 58 acres of city-owned land for $275 million, with a $20 million deposit due when a purchase and sale agreement is finalized. Including $130 million in land the city will retain, the full land package is valued at $405 million.

The plan departs significantly from previous stadium-focused proposals by organizing development around Samuel Davis Park, a 13-acre civic space that would anchor the district. This central park would be part of 18.5 acres of public green space and would house The Woodson African American Museum of Florida, a Museum of Public Art, a Discovery Center, playgrounds, splash pads, event lawns, a concert venue, and public art installations. A Legacy Link pedestrian corridor will connect City Hall with the Manhattan Casino and the Deuces District. Additional amenities include a surf wave attraction, recreation deck, multimodal transportation hub, workforce training center, and a Resilience Event Center. The plan also addresses environmental restoration by reviving Booker Creek, which once served as a central feature of the Historic Gas Plant neighborhood before becoming a drainage ditch between the stadium and parking lots.

The proposal incorporates significant respect for the community history of the Gas Plant District, a predominantly Black neighborhood that was demolished to make way for Tropicana Field. The plan preserves the original gas tank footprint within Samuel Davis Park and weaves historic references throughout the district. Nine planned bridges over Booker Creek reference the nine bridges that once existed in the Historic Gas Plant area, symbolizing restoration of physical connections across the neighborhood. CEO Thompson Whitney Blake stated the team created "an evocative, architecturally jaw dropping permanent home for the Woodson African American Museum of Florida" and designed a Legacy Link that "rests on respect for the community that once existed here, informing and educating the future generations."

The development will occur in three five-year phases, with construction potentially beginning outside the Tropicana Field footprint before the stadium's demolition. The Tampa Bay Rays' lease with the city ends after the 2028 season. Phase 1 will establish cultural and workforce anchors including the Woodson and the Collaboratorium, a workforce training and business development center. Phase 2 will develop mixed-income neighborhoods and strengthen connections with surrounding districts. Phase 3 will complete the district as a regional cultural and events destination, including the 4,000 to 5,000-seat Resilience Event Center, which would also function as a hurricane evacuation shelter. The plan calls for 3,600 income-restricted housing units over the 15-year buildout, with 1,800 units in the Historic Gas Plant District and 1,800 at complementary sites throughout St. Petersburg.

Misty Dawn White, 46, was found dead Monday after a fire broke out inside her home in Hobe Sound. Preliminary findings from the sheriff's office suggest an electrical issue may have caused the fire, though investigators continue their examination of the incident.

Nina Fenn, who had been friends with White for eight years, learned of the tragedy through a phone call from White's fiancé Mark. "We received a call from Mark stating that Misty had passed away in a fire," Fenn said. Fenn visited the scene in the days following the fire to process her grief and described what she witnessed. "It's completely a state of devastation," she said. Friends have organized a fundraiser to help Mark rebuild his life after the flames destroyed many belongings that he and White shared.

Fenn reflected on White's character and the qualities that made her special to those who knew her. "Misty was a Christian woman. She loved gardening and plants. She plays the piano," Fenn said. "She was always the first to lend a hand. She loves kids; she loves her kids. She loves people." The loss carries added significance because White's birthday was approaching on the Fourth of July. "Misty's birthday is the Fourth of July. So, she passed just a few days before her birthday," Fenn said.

The tragedy compounds existing grief for Mark. According to Fenn, Mark previously lost his only child, Jacob, in a separate tragedy. "Mark lost his one and only child, Jacob, and lost him tragically. So, I just am trying whatever I can to support him," Fenn said. Despite the profound loss, Fenn said White's memory will remain alive in the hearts of those closest to her. "She's always been a good soul to me. She'll always be in our hearts," Fenn said.

Yesterday, 6:16 PM ET

The warehouse retailer is bringing its first store to the Treasure Coast with a planned location in Port St. Lucie. This marks the first Costco presence in the region and reflects the city's ongoing growth and development initiatives.

Port St. Lucie is experiencing significant infrastructure improvements to support this expansion and other commercial development. The city is working on a $95 million Midway Road Widening Project that aims to address congestion and safety concerns. According to Scott Samples, strategic communications manager for Port St. Lucie, "There's a pretty significant gap between the entrances and exits to the Turnpike currently in Port St. Lucie." The project is designed to accommodate both current needs and anticipated growth over the next several decades. Crews are currently in the first phase of the Midway Road project, widening the roadway from two lanes to four lanes between Selvitz Road and Jenkins Road. The second phase, which will extend from Jenkins Road to Glades Cut Off Road, remains in the design phase with construction expected to begin in Summer 2027.

Beyond the road widening work, transportation planners are also enhancing access to Florida's Turnpike. The Port St. Lucie City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance last month to dedicate 7 acres of city land to the Florida Department of Transportation to support a partial interchange on the Turnpike. Samples noted that this interchange is essential for accessibility and growth management, stating "This is a way for us to try to accommodate the growth that's already occurred, but also the growth that will occur over the next 20 to 50 years." The partial interchange is still in the design phase. The city council will vote on final approval of the land dedication at an upcoming meeting. FDOT is also working to improve the Turnpike bridge as part of these broader improvements.

The infrastructure investments extend beyond major roadways. The Midway Road Widening Project will add bike and pedestrian paths along with sidewalks to improve connectivity throughout the area. While construction activity has been noticeable to residents, the improvements are widely viewed as necessary for the city's future. Local resident Erin Scheumeister acknowledged the disruption but expressed support, saying "We definitely needed it to be expanded" and noting that "It'll be a lot better and easier to get to work." These transportation enhancements position Port St. Lucie to accommodate major retail developments like Costco while supporting the community's continued growth trajectory.

Yesterday, 6:43 PM ET

The cost of hosting a Fourth of July cookout for 10 people has increased to $73.82 this year, representing a 4 percent rise from 2023, according to the American Farm Bureau Federation's Summer Cookout Cost Survey. The increase reflects broader inflationary pressures on holiday meal staples. Ground beef prices have climbed 5.5 percent, while buns have increased 7.7 percent. Strawberry prices surged 12.4 percent following a Florida frost that damaged crops. Ice cream prices also rose 5.3 percent compared to the previous year.

Not all cookout ingredients have become more expensive. Potato prices decreased this year, offering savings for shoppers planning potato chips or salads. Corn prices have fluctuated locally, though produce from the Glades can provide better deals. Budgeting expert Andrea Woroch recommended strategies to manage holiday meal expenses, including comparing prices across stores to find the best deals for items on shopping lists and reducing food waste through meal planning.
